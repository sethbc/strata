# 3.7 decisions (rhythm)

The other releases: [README](README.md).

The design's decisions, its calls and its "Cost if wrong" list are in `docs/design/3.7-design.md` (its "Amended 2026-11-16" block rules where the body differs, and its lines are marked where the build differs). What follows was decided while it was built: by the maintainer, by the plan where the design met 3.6's merged code, and as rulings while the packages were built, reviewed and landed (the plan is removed at the release; git history keeps it). 3.7 has one numbered series, the plan's thirty-seven rulings (its section "The design against the merged code"), written plan ruling N; 3.7's notes and comments that cite a bare "ruling N" beside a 3.7 feature mean the plan's (older lines keep their own release's numbers, as `docs/decisions/3.4.md` to `3.6.md` give them). Decided by the maintainer:
- **The design's nineteen decisions** (2026-11-15), each asked with its alternatives and listed in the design's "Decided in the brainstorm"; three not as recommended (6, 16 and 19). If wrong: each decision's cost is in the design's "Cost if wrong". By number:
  1. A slice's end is the scan mode's (one shot once, wrap repeats, bounce back and forth).
  2. Overlapping slices are a choice per voice (one at a time, or each note its own).
  3. Tempo follow is a choice per voice: off, stretch or repitch.
  4. One envelope per voice, fired by its own voice's hits, routed through the matrix.
  5. Slices are made automatically (a sensitivity or a count) and corrected by hand (add, delete, nudge).
  6. Neighbor conditions are in (`nei`, `!nei`), not as recommended.
  7. Four random sequences, each with its own clock (a division or a voice's hits).
  8. Transients come from a pass of their own over whatever a slot holds, run only when a slot is sliced by transients.
  9. The delay line grows to 2.7 s, and a division that does not fit is halved.
  10. A file's settings follow the file, kept by Strata in its data folder; a take keeps its settings until its slot is recorded over or cleared.
  11. Up to 64 slices a buffer.
  12. The next scene is drawn from three chances (next, previous, random; the rest repeats), and the pattern chain takes the same rule.
  13. A grid's position row becomes slice keys, the strip's four bookmark keys picking the bank.
  14. Slices are edited in a view of their own, opened by K3 on BUFFER's `slices` row.
  15. Layouts are sketched in text, not in a browser.
  16. Fill has a key on the 256 and on the 128h (row 8 column 4), not as recommended.
  17. The playback of the whole design was accepted as the basis of the document.
  18. Layouts, menus and storage may change before 4.0: nothing shipped in 3.x is a reason to pick the weaker design.
  19. A of B has its opposite too, not as recommended.

The onsets pass (`lib/engine/StrataOnsets.sc`, package 1b):
- **Each hop's energy is summed in pairs with `copySeries`, not taken from `integral`** (unnumbered, 1b's build and review): design §3.3 and its fact file (`docs/design/3.7-facts/buffers.md` §1) give `squared`, `integral`, then `copySeries` at the hop ends, but sclang's `Signal.integral` returns one Float, the whole Signal's sum, not a running sum (`prSignalIntegral`, `lang/LangPrimSource/PyrSignalPrim.cpp`, the same in 3.13.0 and 3.14.1), so there are no ends to take. `hopEnergies` squares a chunk and adds its even and odd elements eight times over, so each of a chunk's 64 sums is one hop's: C primitives only, three Signals a level, none per sample (#131), and no running total to round. The check compares it with each hop's own `copyRange(...).squared.integral` on noise. If wrong: none in what it finds; a faster primitive would change only `hopEnergies`.
- **The mean before a hop counts as at least half `onsetFloor`, and a strength is the rise in level** (unnumbered, 1b's review, the controller's ruling): the mean of the four hops before a hop is floored at half the floor so the divide after silence is finite (a hop above the floor there is a candidate, as the 2x rule would make it anyway); a candidate's strength is log2 of its ratio less 1 (the 2x threshold), then scaled so the strongest is 1. With the plain ratio, one hit after near-silence (a ratio of about 3.6e5) made every candidate inside a texture print `0.000`, so 2b's sensitivity would have been all or one; design §3.3 says "its strength is the rise". Detection is unchanged. If wrong: a strength scale that spreads the candidates too evenly, changed in `onsetChunkStrengths` alone.
- **A chunk's lock time on a CM3 is left to the device check** (1b's review): the pass yields between chunks, and how long one chunk holds sclang on the slowest norns is #6's to measure. If wrong: a smaller chunk.

The delay on the beat (package 1d, `lib/delay_sync.lua`):
- **A scene's apply ends with an open hook, `scene_applied`** (unnumbered, 1d's review, the controller's ruling): `S.lib.scenes.apply_entries` runs every `S.register_scene_applied({name, fn})` hook once, right after it clears `S.scene_recall_in_progress`, each contained. The delay holds its `setDelay` while a scene applies and its hook sends one with all four values, so a recall's send is never a mix of old and new values and never deferred (`docs/notes/lua/engine-sync.md`): an instant recall's and a fade's landing go at once; a morph's waits as every morph write does. No other seam marks the end of an apply: `scene_recalled` runs at an instant recall and a fade's first step only, and a field's own `set` cannot know it is the last. If wrong: a three-line hook removed, and the delay's recall send deferred.
- **An identical `setDelay` is still sent** (1d's review, its Minor 1 not taken; filed as #741, for after 3.7.0): while synced under an LFO, a matrix routing or a fade, an action's send goes out even when its values equal the last; a skip for identical values would break the rule that an action's send always goes out, and the round robin's slot is affordable. If wrong: a few redundant messages a second.
- **Ping-pong's first repeat of a centered sound is louder on the left** (1d's review): the input is summed into the left line with no 0.5 factor, the design's choice (§6), and the FX user page says so. If wrong: one factor.

- **A recording sets its slot's gain to 1 at its preparation, not at its command** (unnumbered, the final review's engine Minor 2, design §4.4): a synced take's slot keeps its normalize gain through the arm wait, until `bufferFadeWait + recordPrepMargin` before the start; a take armed by level is prepared at its command, as before, since its record synth is sent then and writes a 10 ms pre-roll from before the crossing, which a boosted slot would feed back into. A synced take stopped in its wait never touches the gain. If wrong: one line moved back to `recordSlot`.
