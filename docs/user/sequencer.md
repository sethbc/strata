# Sequencer

The pattern sequencer: each voice's own length, division, swing and direction, what a step can hold (a condition, probability, ratchets, step locks, notes, a gate), fill, Euclidean fill, chaining scenes' patterns and following the clock's transport. Back to the [README](../../README.md).

The patterns themselves are 16 steps per voice, each off or at a level of 1-15 (its accent's strength). They are edited on the [SEQ page](pages/seq.md) and the [grid](grid.md). K2 on SEQ, the grids' play keys and the arc's button start and stop them.

## Each voice's clock

Every voice steps through its pattern on its own clock (PARAMETERS > the voice's extras):
- **seq length** (1-16 steps, default 16): the voice plays steps 1 to its length, then starts again at step 1. A shorter length takes effect at once while the voice is still inside it; when it has already passed the new length, the voice finishes its round first. On the grid, the steps past a voice's length show dimly (level 1) if they are set
- **seq division** (1/32, 1/16t, 1/16, 1/8t, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 or 1 bar per step; default 1/16): how long each of the voice's steps is. Voices at different divisions and lengths drift against each other and meet again, polyrhythms from one start
- **seq swing** (50-75 %, default 50): every second step the voice plays comes late, by (swing - 50) / 50 of a step: 50 is straight, 66 is close to a triplet feel, 75 puts the step three quarters of the way through its pair. Each voice swings its own division, and swing never drifts against the clock

- **seq direction** (forward, reverse, ping-pong, random; default forward): the order the voice takes its steps. Forward goes 1 up to the length and starts again at 1; reverse from the length down to 1 and starts again at the length; ping-pong up to the length and back down to 1 without playing either end twice (1 2 3 4 3 2 1 2 ...); random picks any step of the length each time
- **seq misstep** (0-100 %, default 0): the chance that, at each step, the voice moves one step up or down (either way, at random, wrapping around at the ends) instead of where its direction would take it. It wraps around at the ends of the pass it is in: while a shorter length waits for the pass's end, a misstep can still reach the steps past it. When the voice stands past its length (a shorter length has just taken effect), a misstep goes to the nearest step inside it: up wraps to step 1, down lands on the length's last step (length 6 cut to 4: from step 6, up plays 1 and down plays 4). In ping-pong a misstep is a ping-pong step taken its own way: one that meets an end turns there and ends the pass, as an ordinary step does (from the top, a misstep up plays the step below it), and one that does not lets the voice go on the way it was going. A misstep never brings in a waiting length by itself: the length takes effect where an ordinary step from the step the voice now stands on would bring it in. So on the way down a misstep up leaves it to the turn at step 1; on the way up a misstep down onto a step inside the new length lets the voice turn there (8 cut to 4 on the way up at step 5 plays 6 7 8, then 4 3 ...; with a misstep down to 4, it plays 4, then turns: 3 2 ...); and a misstep that turns at an end ends the pass there, and the length takes effect at that turn as at any other

A **pass** is one run through the voice's length: its length in steps taken (forward, reverse, random), or from one end to the other (ping-pong, where each end ends a pass). A length change that does not fit the pass played so far waits for its end; one that fits takes effect at once, and a voice that stands past it finishes the pass from there with its ordinary steps (reverse cut from 8 to 4 after 8 7 6 plays 5, then 4 3 2 1), while a misstep from there goes to the nearest step inside (seq misstep, above); in ping-pong a longer length, or a shorter one the pass can still turn at on its way up (it has not gone past it), takes effect at once, and otherwise the pass finishes its leg (up to its old top, or down to 1) and then turns at the new length (8 cut to 4 after step 5 plays 6 7 8, then 4 3 2 ...). A direction change while the patterns play ends the pass where the new direction wraps, so the pass still ends where the new direction starts over, even though it turns back over steps it has played: reverse for three steps (8 7 6), then forward, plays 7 8 and starts the next pass at 1; forward for three, then reverse, plays 2 1 and starts the next pass at 8. A change to ping-pong or random counts on in the pass. Each row of the grid shows its own voice's playing step. All the voices start together when the patterns start, each at its direction's first step (step 1, or its length in reverse). **Division reset** (PARAMETERS > sequencer) starts every voice again at the next bar, so voices that have drifted apart line up again: each at its direction's first step (step 1 in forward and ping-pong, its length in reverse, a new draw in random), on its first pass. Until that bar SEQ's status line shows `[reset at bar]`.

## What a step holds

A set step plays its voice at its level: a running voice gets an accent (Karplus re-plucks its string, a voice in triggered grain mode fires one grain), a stopped one is started; what else it does depends on the voice's step mode (below). Beyond its level, a step can hold:
- **a condition** (below; none when not set): the step plays only when it holds
- **probability** (0-100 %, 100 when not set): the chance the step plays each time around, drawn once for the whole step (only when its condition holds)
- **ratchets** (1-4 hits): the step plays that many times, evenly spaced through it. Hits are never closer than 20 ms apart: at fast tempos and short divisions a step plays fewer of them
- **step locks** (position, pitch, size): the voice plays that buffer position, grain pitch or grain size for this step only, and goes back to its own value at the next step that plays without the lock. The voice's own setting never changes. Turning the param by hand (an encoder, the arc or a grid fader) while a step lock holds it ends the lock at once. Every step lock also ends when the patterns stop, the voice's pattern is cleared or replaced (a scene recall, the chain), the voice's length is shortened past the step that set it, or, once the step is turned off with no other step of the voice set, when the voice comes around to its step 1 again. Step locks have nothing to do with a voice's lock (`[L]`)
- **a note** (a scale degree, -14 to 14): the step plays the voice that many tones of the global scale above (or below) its own pitch, for this step only: a synth voice's freq, a buffer voice's rate, an input voice's grain pitch (on top of the step's pitch lock when it has one). Degree 0 is the voice's own pitch. While the scale is off every semitone counts, so degree 3 is three semitones up; in another tuning a degree is one of its tones (in 19 equal steps, a nineteenth of an octave with the scale off). A buffer or input voice's pitch is the note a MIDI note plays at its rate (rate 1 is middle C, or for a buffer voice the file's key when it has one: [A File's Settings](files.md))
- **a chord** (up to 8 notes): the step plays them all together. The first is the voice's own note, moved for the step as a single note moves it; the others are extra notes from the note pool, each at its degree from the same pitch (an input voice's on top of the step's pitch lock too). Every hit of a ratchet accents every note. A chord ends where a step lock ends: at the voice's next step that plays (the notes it shares with the chord change pitch where they sound, the rest end), when the patterns stop, the pattern is cleared or replaced, the voice's length is shortened past the step, or the voice comes around to its step 1 with no step left set. A chord takes only free room: notes past the voice's **note cap** (VOICE) or the **note pool** are not played, at any step, so a chord keeps its first notes and a note cap of 1 or a pool of 0 plays its first note alone, as 3.4 did. A step never takes a note or its place from MIDI: it never ends, replaces or re-pitches a note whose key is held down, on any voice, while a MIDI note may take a sequenced note's place (and plays its own pitch at once). While a key held down holds the voice, the chord plays as extra notes beside it as far as there is free room (at a note cap of 1 or an empty pool the step then adds no note). A latched MIDI note (its key lifted) gives way: the next step's first note is the voice's own again, re-pitching and accenting it as 3.4's steps did, so a latched note under a pattern plays every step even at a note cap of 1
- **a bookmark** (1-4): a buffer voice jumps to that bookmark as the step plays, but only when the bookmark lies inside the voice's loop (a jump outside it would reset the loop)
- **a gate** (0.05-16 steps): this step's gate length, for a gate or pulse voice (Step modes, below)
- **a slice** (1-64): a buffer voice plays that slice of its buffer at the step, as its own note for the step, or on a voice set to `overlap` in a note of its own while an earlier slice still rings (a chord plays inside the slice); a slice the buffer lacks plays the step as if it had none ([Slices](slices.md))

Scenes keep every step's attributes with the patterns, and recall them as they recall the patterns.

## Conditions

A step's **condition** (`cond` on SEQ) decides, each time the voice reaches it, whether it plays. Each has its opposite, written with a `!`, which plays exactly when the condition does not:
- **A:B** (B from 2 to 8, A from 1 to B): the step plays on the A-th of every B passes: `1:2` on passes 1, 3, 5 ..., `2:2` on 2, 4, 6 ..., `3:4` on 3, 7, 11 .... `!A:B` plays on all the others
- **first**: only on the voice's first pass since the patterns started
- **pre**: only when the voice's last conditioned step played (a conditioned step is a set step with a condition or a probability under 100 %; whether it played is its outcome). Before any conditioned step, `pre` does not play and `!pre` does
- **nei**: only when the last conditioned step of the voice above played (voice 1's neighbor is voice 7). Voices run on their own clocks: on a beat they share, the voice above counts only if its step came first. A pattern start plays the voices in order, so there the voice above comes first; a single voice restarted (a division reset's bar) can fall behind it
- **fill**: only while fill is on (below)

The condition is checked first and the probability drawn only if it holds. The passes count from 1 when the patterns start, at a division reset's bar and when a voice restarts; a scene recall and a chain link keep counting. A step the patterns skip when the norns falls behind checks nothing.

## Fill

**Fill** turns on the steps marked `fill` and turns off those marked `!fill`, for as long as it is on. It is on while it is latched (SEQ's `fill` row, K3 there; PARAMETERS > sequencer > fill, which a MIDI controller can map; the `fill` action of crow and OSC) or while a grid key holds it (FL, the strip's second key: the 256's and 128h's row 8 column 2, the 128v's column 8 row 2, on every page: on at the press, off at the release). A key's release ends only its own hold: a latched fill stays on until it is turned off. A held key also lets go when its grid is unplugged; a page change leaves it held. SEQ shows `f` at the end of the selected voice's row while fill is on. Fill always starts off: no PSET or scene turns it on.

If a step fails to play one of Strata's own voices (a bug), the patterns stop, show stopped, and the error appears in maiden. Other things a step plays through (MIDI out and other integrations, in later versions) never stop the patterns: one that fails is named in maiden once and left out until the patterns start again, and SEQ's status line names it until then (`[targets off]`).

## Step modes

Each voice has a **step mode** (SEQ's `mode` row, or PARAMETERS > the voice's extras > **seq gate**), which says what its steps do to it:
- **pulse** (the default): while the patterns play and the voice has a step, it is heard only while a step's gate is open, so a pattern is heard as a rhythm. A voice with no step sounds as it always does; give it a step and it pulses from the next start (or at once, while they play); stop the patterns and every pulse voice sounds again. Only the steps inside the voice's length count: a voice whose only steps lie past its length sounds on. SEQ marks a pulse voice's row `P`.
- **gate**: the voice is heard only while a step's gate is open, with the patterns stopped too (silent while MAIN still shows it running). This is 3.8's **seq gate** on, so a scene or PSET saved with the gate on comes back as gate. SEQ marks it `G`.
- **accent**: a step only accents a voice that keeps sounding, the swell as strong as the step's level. This is 3.8's seq gate off, so a scene saved in 3.8 comes back with its voices in accent, and new steps on them swell as before until you choose another mode. No mark.
- **move**: a step adds no swell and plucks no string, and the voice keeps sounding. It is still a hit for what listens to one (the envelopes, a one-shot scan, a voice in triggered grain mode), and MIDI out and the other note targets get it at full velocity. SEQ marks it `M`.

In gate and pulse each hit opens the gate (in 5 ms) for **seq gate length** (0.05-16 steps, default 0.5) or the step's own gate, then it closes over **seq gate release** (0.005-4 s, default 0.1). A ratchet's hits share the step's gate length between them. The gate opens and closes a chord's notes together. A gate longer than the gap to the next hit is reopened by it, so long gates tie. A MIDI note opens the gate too, and so does each key added to a MIDI chord on it, with no accent of its own beyond its note's. A gated voice's notes on MIDI out and the other targets last its gate length, a pulse voice's too. The delay and reverb tails ring on after it closes. **seq gate floor** (0-100%, default 0%) is how much of the voice a closed gate lets through: at 25% a gated voice plays at a quarter of its level between its steps and at its whole level while a step's gate is open; at 0% a closed gate is silent, as before. Scenes switch the mode at a transition's end and fade the gate's length, release and floor; the chain carries the patterns and never the mode, so a chain of scenes saved in 3.8 plays in each voice's mode as it is now (pulse, in a fresh session). A voice preset leaves the mode and the floor as they are, as it leaves every seq setting.

## Moving a voice

A step can hold two **values**, A and B (each 1 to 15, or none), and each voice has two **move targets** that say what they move (PARAMETERS > the voice's extras): **seq move A** (`auto`, the default, is position on a buffer voice and pitch on any other; or `position`, `pitch`, `size`, `cutoff`) and **seq move B** (`off`, the default, or one of the four). A value moves its target in fourteen equal moves:
- **position**: from the start of the voice's loop (1) to its end (15), as a position lock sets it: the scan goes on from there.
- **size** and **cutoff**: across the grain size's or the voice filter's whole range, on its own scale (1 the smallest, 15 the largest).
- **pitch**: from seven degrees below the voice's own pitch (1) to seven above (15), on the tuning and scale a step's note uses; 8 is the voice's own pitch. It moves what a step's note moves: a synth voice's frequency, a buffer voice's rate, an input voice's grain pitch. A synth voice's grains read it from the voice's ring, so it is heard a moment later and smeared across the grains as the position spread is wide, as a step's note on a synth voice is.

A step's note and chord count their degrees from the pitch value the voice stands on (the step's own, or the one held), so with a value of 10 (two degrees up) a chord of notes 0 and 2 plays degrees 2 and 4; the sum is kept within fourteen degrees either way. A step's pitch lock adds to a pitch value on a synth or buffer voice (the lock moves the grain pitch); on an input voice the two move the same setting, so the lock wins for its step and the value comes back after it. While a MIDI key you hold plays the voice's own note, a pitch value gives way: a held one ends when the key lands, steps set none while the key is down, and releasing the key brings nothing back until the next step that sets one. (A MIDI note sets the voice's own pitch, so the next value counts from the last key you played.) A position value does not give way.

A value **stays**: the step that sets it moves the voice there, and the voice stays there through the steps that hold no value, until a later step sets the same target again. It ends, and the setting goes back to the voice's own, when the patterns stop, when you turn the setting by hand, when the voice's pattern is replaced (a clear, a randomize, a scene recall, the chain), when you change a move target, the voice's step mode, its source or its type (a morph's switch of the source too), when the voice jumps (for a position value: a cell of the grid's position page, a bookmark, a step's bookmark, which wins over its own step's value), when the voice comes around to its step 1 with no step left set, or when its length is shortened past the step that set it. A step that a condition or its chance holds back sets nothing, so the last value holds. Where A and B name the same target, A moves it and B does nothing. A step lock on the same setting wins for its own step, and when the lock ends the value comes back (unless the value ended meanwhile: a hand edit under the lock leaves the hand's setting). The row a value holds shows `[S]` on VOICE and MAIN for as long as it stays.

Values work in every step mode, and a step's level stays its strength in all of them: in **move** a step adds no swell, so a move voice is heard through its values, and its level comes back as the swell when you change it to another mode. A step turned on in move (a grid tap or SEQ's K3) is given a random value A where it has none. Choosing move from the grid or SEQ (in a later step of this work) gives every set step inside the voice's length a random value A where it has none, so the mode is heard at once, and choosing cutoff there opens a closed voice filter (its mix from 0 to 1, `filter on`); a scene, a PSET or the parameter itself does neither. Scenes keep both targets and switch them at a transition's end; a voice preset leaves them as they are, as it leaves every seq setting.

## Lanes

What a step plays is read from six **lanes**: its hits (on, off and the level), value A, value B, its chance, its repeats and its gate. Each lane's sixteen cells are the steps' own settings: step 3's chance is the chance lane's cell 3. The hits lane runs at the voice's length, direction and misstep. Each of the other five can be given a **length of its own**, 1 to 16 (from the grid and SEQ in a later step of this work); with none, it **follows**: it is read at the step the hits play, so a pattern plays as it always has.

A lane with its own length is **free**: it keeps its own place, which starts at cell 1 whenever the voice starts (the patterns start, the voice restarts, a division reset's bar) and moves on by one cell at every step the voice takes, played, silent or skipped at a late wake, and wraps at its length. It always runs forward, whatever the voice's direction or misstep. So with the hits on all 16 steps and value A 5 long, the values of A's cells 1 and 3 are set at the 1st, 3rd, 6th, 8th, 11th, 13th, 16th, 18th, ... steps it plays, and the pattern shifts against itself every bar. A free chance lane of 2 with a chance of 0 in its cell 2 holds back every second hit; a free gate lane gives each hit its own cell's gate. A step's repeats share the cell their step read.

**A following lane and a free one of the same length differ** wherever the hits do not walk 1, 2, 3, ... from the top of the lane: in reverse, ping-pong or random, with a misstep, after a late wake, and when the voice's length is not the lane's. A free lane of 16 on a voice of 16 steps playing forward with no misstep reads what a following one reads from the voice's start (a length given while the patterns play starts its lane at cell 1 at the next step, wherever the hits are); in reverse it still reads 1, 2, 3, ... while the hits go 16, 15, 14, ...; on a voice of 12 steps it carries on to its cells 13 to 16 before it wraps.

A step's condition, its locks, its note, its bookmark and its slice are no lanes: they always play with the step played. Conditions count the hits' passes, whatever the lanes' lengths, so `1:2` on step 1 of a 4-step voice plays every other pass with a 5-step lane beside it. Shortening a lane below its place sends it back to cell 1; a longer length keeps its place. The lengths belong to the pattern: scenes keep them (a scene saved before them has none, so its lanes follow) and switch them at a transition's end, the chain carries them with the patterns, and a clear or a randomize keeps them, as it keeps each step's settings; a paste (the grid's PC) and a switch of [Variations](#variations) bring their pattern's own. A voice preset leaves them as they are.

## Variations

Each voice holds **four patterns, A to D**. A pattern here is the whole of it: which steps are on and how strong each is, every step's settings and the lanes' own lengths ([Lanes](#lanes)). A voice plays A at first. The first switch to a letter you have never used gives it a copy of the pattern playing, so the voice plays on unchanged and you make the variation by changing it. A letter you have used keeps what you left in it: one you cleared stays empty, and switching to it silences the voice's steps. A switch stores the playing pattern under its letter and loads the other whole, so whatever ends when a pattern is replaced ends then: a value or step lock the voice was holding, a step held on the grid, the step editor, the Euclidean row.

**When a switch happens.** With the patterns stopped, a switch is at once. While they play, a letter you pick waits for the next bar (the bars the chain counts, four beats each from the patterns' start) and plays from that bar's first step; picking the waiting letter again switches at once, picking another letter waits in its place, and picking the letter playing takes the wait back. Stopping the patterns drops a waiting letter. A scene recall or a chain link on the same bar wins: it loads the scene's own letters, and the letter that waited is dropped.

**An order of letters.** A voice can hold an order of up to eight letters, such as A A B A. While it is on, the voice takes the order's next letter at every bar, from the first again after the last (when the script falls behind by whole bars, the order lands where those bars put it, switching once); a letter the same as the one playing changes nothing (an order of one letter never touches the pattern, so the step editor stays open). Starting the patterns starts the order at its first letter, which the first bar plays, and so does turning an order on while they play, from the next bar. Turning an order on drops a waiting letter, and switching a letter by hand turns the order off (it is kept, to turn on again). Scenes keep each voice's order and whether it is on; where the order stands and a waiting letter are never saved. Switching letters and setting an order from the grid and SEQ come in later steps of this work.

Scenes keep each voice's letters: the one playing and the patterns of the others it has used, switched at a transition's end; a scene saved before them plays its own pattern on letter A, with no other letter used. The chain carries them with the patterns, and the undo of a chain start puts them back. A clear, a randomize or a fill changes only the letter playing.

## Euclidean fill

A Euclidean fill spreads a number of hits as evenly as possible over the voice's length (3 hits over 8 steps: steps 1, 4 and 7), turned by a rotation (0 to the length - 1, wrapping around; the hits go from 0 to the length). It writes the voice's first length steps (hits at level 15, the rest off) and keeps the steps past the length and every step's attributes, so you can edit the steps by hand afterward. SEQ's `euclid` row shows the fill until something else replaces the whole pattern (a clear, a randomize, a paste, an undo, a scene recall or a chain link); it then shows `-`, and the next turn starts from 0 hits.

## Chaining

**Chain** (PARAMETERS > sequencer) plays a run of scenes' patterns one after the other: while the patterns play, every **chain bars** bars (1-16, default 4) a saved scene of **chain from** (default 1) to chain from + **chain scenes** - 1 (default 2) gives its patterns, its step attributes, its lane lengths ([Lanes](#lanes)), each voice's letters ([Variations](#variations)) and each voice's length, division, direction and misstep (empty slots, and a damaged scene that fails its check, are never picked). SEQ's status line shows the scene the chain linked last and the bars to its next link (`[S3 4 bars]`). Starting the patterns with the chain on loads the range's first saved scene at once (a quick ALT+K2 on SEQ within 5 seconds brings back the patterns it replaced, with their steps, their lane lengths, the letters and each voice's length, division, direction and misstep), and so does the first link after the chain is switched on while they play (it waits for the next bar). After that, which scene comes next is drawn from three chances: **chain next** (0-100 %, default 100), the next saved scene of the range (after the last, the first); **chain previous** (default 0), the one before it (before the first, the last); and **chain random** (default 0), any other saved scene of the range. When the playing scene has been cleared (a scenes load can do that), the chain goes on to the next saved scene after its slot, whatever the chances; with the playing scene the only saved one in the range, a next or previous move links it again and a random one keeps it. What the three leave over (100 less their sum; a sum over 100 is scaled down to 100) keeps the scene playing for another round of bars, linking nothing. At the defaults the chain plays in order. Nothing else of the scene is applied: the sound, the sources and which voices run stay as they are. PSETs store the chain settings; scenes do not.

## Following the clock's transport

**Follow transport** (PARAMETERS > sequencer, or SETUP's `transport` row: `follow` or `off`; default on) lets an external clock start and stop the patterns (on the internal clock SETUP's row reads `follow [int]`, since nothing follows): with norns' clock source set to MIDI or Link (with PARAMETERS > CLOCK > link start/stop sync on), a MIDI start or a Link start plays every voice from its first step at once (step 1, its length in reverse, a random step in random), on the clock's first beat, whether the patterns played or not (a running chain starts again from its first scene), and a stop stops them as K2 does. On the internal clock nothing follows: PARAMETERS > CLOCK > reset neither starts nor stops the patterns. Strata never starts or stops the clock itself: its play keys start the patterns only.

When the clock starts again without the patterns following it (PARAMETERS > CLOCK > reset on the internal clock, or a MIDI or Link start with follow transport off), or its count jumps for another reason (a new clock source chosen in PARAMETERS > CLOCK, for example), while they play, they keep their own count: the next 16th waits for the clock's next 16th, every voice moves by that same amount (at most a 16th later than it would have played, never earlier), and the voices, the chain's bars and the ratchets stay together, with no step played twice. The next start of the patterns counts from the clock again. While they play on their own count, SETUP's status lines show how far it is from the clock's (`count offset: +3.50 beats`).

If the norns is busy for a moment (a long load, for example) and the patterns fall behind the clock, each voice plays the one step the clock has reached and skips the ones it missed, rather than rushing through them, so the patterns stay in time with the clock. A step that comes a little late (less than a 16th) still plays.
