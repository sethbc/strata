# BUFFER

Where the eight sample buffers are recorded, filled, cleared and saved, where a buffer's waveform and the voices playing it show, and where the selected voice's source is chosen, so it all works without a grid. Back to [Screen Controls](../controls.md).

**Left column.** Twelve fields, four at a time (the list scrolls as the field cursor moves; a brighter mark on the dividing line shows where you are). A small `>` marks the field E3 edits:
- `v1`...`v7`: the selected voice (picked on MAIN) and its source: `synth`, `buf 1` ... `buf 8`, `input`, or `v1` ... `v7` for another voice's sound or its own ([Voices as sources](../sources-and-buffers.md#voices-as-sources)).
- `rec`: what a recording takes: the input (`in`), the master before its effects (`pre`) or after them (`post`, Strata's own output only), or one voice (`v1`-`v7`).
- `dur`: the record duration, up to the buffer length (16 s, or up to 300 s on the high tier). With sync on it becomes `len`, the length in beats (`16b`); when that many beats would not fit in a buffer at the current tempo it shows how many do, with `max` (`8b max`), and the recording is that many beats long, so a loop recorded that way wraps on the beat (`<1b max` when not even one beat fits: the recording is then a whole buffer).
- `lvl`: the record level.
- `fb`: the record feedback: 0% replaces what the buffer held; above 0 the buffer keeps playing while it records, and each pass keeps the old audio at that level (an overdub).
- `loop`: `on` records over and over the first `dur` seconds until you stop it.
- `thr`: `off`, or a level (`-24dB`) the source must reach before the recording starts (it keeps the 10 ms before).
- `sync`: `off`, or start on the next `beat` or `bar` of the clock.
- `bm`: the bookmark the selected voice last jumped to and its position (`2 0.25`), or `-` before it has jumped to one. E3 steps through bookmarks 1-4 and jumps the voice to each one it lands on (from `-`, the first step either way is bookmark 1; turning past 1 or 4 does nothing); ALT+E3 jumps the voice back to the bookmark shown, after its grains have moved on. With quantize actions on beat or bar ([Parameters](../params.md#sequencer-group)) the jump waits for the next beat or bar (the header shows `v1 bm 2 at bar`), and E3 steps on from the bookmark that waits. Dim when the voice is not playing a buffer. A bookmark is set on the VOICE page (see [VOICE](voice.md)) or with a grid's store key.
- `start`: for a file longer than a buffer, how far into the file the buffer starts; `-` when the file fits. A file above 48 kHz fills a buffer with fewer of its seconds (8 of 16 at 96 kHz), so its start reaches its last 8 s. It shows hundredths below 10 s (`9.05s`), tenths from 10 s (`12.1s`), whole seconds from 100 s (`123s`), minutes and seconds from 1000 s (`20:34`), then whole minutes (`123m`) and hours (`17h`) for very long files. ALT+E3 moves it by the last digit shown, E3 by that or a tenth of a second, whichever is more; the file reloads from there a moment after you stop turning.
- `fric`: the arc scrub's friction: 100% stops where you let go; lower lets a fling glide on and slow down.
- `slices`: how the buffer's file is cut into slices: `off`, `eq 12` (equal parts) or `tr 12` (at its transients), with the number of slices; `--` for an empty buffer. E3 does nothing here: K3 opens the slice editor (below).

The fields are also in PARAMETERS: `rec`, `fb`, `loop`, `thr`, `sync` and `len` (the record beats) in the **record** group, `dur` and `lvl` in the **buffers** group, `fric` in **positions**, the voice's source in its granular section and its bookmarks in its extras. `start` is no parameter: it is remembered with the buffer's file (see [Audio Sources and Buffers](../sources-and-buffers.md#files)). Below the fields, `in` is a meter of the live input: eight segments, about 6 dB each from -48 dB, showing the louder of the two channels; the highest segment reached stays lit for 1 s. The meter shows the live level at each visit (it never stops listening: the same level can drive a modulation source on any page).

**Right column.** At the top, the waveform of the buffer under the cursor: brighter inside the selected voice's loop when that voice plays this buffer, a flat dim line when the buffer is empty. Over it, while the buffer holds audio, a line for each running voice playing the buffer at the place its grains start (the selected voice's brightest), with a short line under the waveform for the width its grains spread over; while the buffer records without emptying, a dotted line shows where the recording writes. Just above the waveform, a dot marks each column where a slice starts. Under the waveform, small ticks mark the selected voice's four bookmarks (the last one recalled brighter). An empty buffer, or one recording a take that replaces it, shows none of these.

Below, the eight buffers, three at a time (the list scrolls as the cursor moves; a thin bar at the right edge shows where you are). Each row shows the buffer's number, its name (the file name, shortened, or `take <n>` for a recording; `-` when the buffer is empty; the norns screen has no accented letters, so `café` shows as `cafe`, and a character it cannot show at all as `?`, while the file keeps its own name) and its length: in seconds below a minute (`16.5s`), in minutes and seconds from a minute on (`1:05`, up to `5:00` on the high tier). While it records, the length gives way to the recording's state: `ARM` while it waits for a beat, a bar or a level, `REC` for a take that replaces the buffer, `DUB` for an overdub, `LOOP` for a loop recording. An empty buffer shows no length. The row under the cursor is bright and also lists the voices playing that buffer (`v2 v6`; `+` when more do than fit). Buffers holding audio are mid, empty ones dim.

A take shows `REC` until the engine reports what it captured (a moment after the recording ends; an overdub or loop stopped with K3 shows `DUB` or `LOOP` until then, and if no report comes within 5 s it ends anyway with the length last shown): then it becomes `take <n>` (an overdub or loop into an empty buffer is `take <n>` as soon as it has a length), or `-` if nothing was captured (a stop right after the start; a take or file the engine reports late, such as a slow read at start, is still picked up, unless the buffer was cleared, loaded or recorded into meanwhile). A loaded file shows its name at once. On the high tier a file longer than 16 s is checked first, which can take a few seconds on the norns: meanwhile the buffer keeps playing what it held, its row shows the new name with `...` for the length, and the new length shows once the file is in. If the check takes longer than 30 s, the hint row says `buf <n> still checking` once and the row keeps waiting for the engine (loading, clearing or recording into the buffer ends the wait). If the file cannot be loaded (missing, unreadable, an unsupported format, empty, or damaged so that it stops decoding part way), the buffer goes back to what it held before (the earlier file and its audio, or empty; if the file disappears in the moment between the checks and the read, as when a drive is unplugged, the buffer is left empty), the hint row shows `buf <n> load failed` for 1.5 s, and the file is not reloaded at the next start.

If a finished take cannot be written to the card (a full card, a folder that cannot be made), the hint row shows `buf <n> take not saved` for 1.5 s: the buffer still plays the take, but it is not restored at the next start (ALT+K3 can still save it).

K2, ALT+K2 and ALT+K3 act when released (since 3.7 they follow the other pages: ALT+K2 is the random draw, K2 held clears): let go within half a second for the first action, hold them half a second or more for the second.

- **E2** - Move the cursor to a buffer (1-8). It stays there when you change pages
- **ALT+E2** - Move the field cursor
- **E3** - Change the field under the field cursor (ALT+E3 in finer steps)
- **K3** - On the `slices` field, open the slice editor for the buffer under the cursor (`buf <n> empty` when it holds no audio). On every other field, record into the buffer under the cursor with the record settings, whether or not a voice plays it; press again to stop early and keep what was captured, or, while it waits to start (`ARM`), to cancel it. A take (feedback 0, loop off) silences the voices playing that buffer until it is done; with sync on it does so only just before its beat or bar, so canceling it before then leaves the buffer as it was (its name and audio), and a cancel in that last moment leaves it empty. An overdub or loop recording keeps the voices playing. While a file is still loading into the buffer (`...` or the first moment after a load) an overdub or loop recording does not start: the hint row shows `buf <n> loading`, and K3 again once the length shows records over the new file (a take starts at once and replaces the load). The arc's button does the same
- **K2** - Released quickly, open the file browser at `~/dust/audio` and load the file you pick into the buffer under the cursor; held, clear the buffer under the cursor
- **ALT+K2** - Released quickly, load a random sound file into the buffer under the cursor from the folder of the buffer's file (else the folder you last chose a file from, else `~/dust/audio`); the hint row shows `buf <n>: <name>` (`no files in <folder>` when the folder holds no sound file, `cannot open <folder>` when it cannot be opened; the folder is named by its path inside `~/dust/audio` (`tape/`, `audio/` for `~/dust/audio` itself), or by its own name when that path would not fit the screen or the folder is elsewhere). Held, fill every empty buffer with a different random file from that folder (`filled <k> slots`, `filled 1 slot`, or `no files in <folder>` / `cannot open <folder>`). It never opens the file browser, even if K1 is let go before K2
- **ALT+K3** - Released quickly, save the buffer under the cursor to `~/dust/audio/strata/<date-time>-buf<n>.wav`; held, save only a loop region of it to `<date-time>-buf<n>-loop.wav` (trim; the buffer is left as it is): the selected voice's loop if that voice's source is this buffer, else the loop of the lowest-numbered voice whose source it is (running or not), else the whole buffer, still named `-loop.wav` and flashed as a loop save. For 1.5 s the hint row shows `saving buf <n>` (`saving buf <n> loop`), then, once the file is written, `saved buf <n>` (`saved buf <n> loop`), or `buf <n> save failed` when it could not be written (a full card, a folder that cannot be made: a file already there is kept). It shows `buf <n> busy` while the buffer is recording or waiting for its take, or `buf <n> empty` when the buffer holds no audio; nothing is saved in those two cases
- While K2, ALT+K2 or ALT+K3 is held, the hint row names what letting go will do, and changes after half a second: `K2 up: load` then `K2 up: clear`; `K2 up: random` then `K2 up: fill`; `K3 up: save` then `K3 up: save loop`
- Otherwise the hint row reads `K2:ld/clr K3:rec` (`K3:edit` on the `slices` field of a buffer that holds audio, `K2:ld/clr` alone there for an empty one, `K3:stop` while the buffer under the cursor records or waits to, `K3:...` while a take that has ended waits for the engine; K3 does nothing then). With K1 (ALT) held it reads `K2:rnd/fill K3:sv/trim`

## The slice editor

K3 on the `slices` field opens the slice editor for the buffer under the cursor. It takes the screen, the encoders, K2, K3 and the arc until K2 closes it (the page shows again); it closes by itself if the buffer is cleared or recorded over with a take. Its title reads `buf <n>` and `slices`. Under it the buffer's waveform spans the whole screen; above the waveform a tick marks the start of each slice (the selected one brightest) and a bright line spans each slice a voice playing the buffer sounds now; a line over the waveform shows where the selected voice's grains start, while it plays this buffer. Below, three lines of two fields: `mode` and `count` (or `sens`), `slice 5/12` and `at 2.410s` (the selected slice and where it starts; `analyzing` while the transients are being found), `tempo` and `bars`, then, scrolled to, `key` and `norm`.

The editor opens on the slices:
- **E2** - Pick a slice (its start marker)
- **E3** - Move its start by 1 ms; ALT+E3 by 0.1 ms. The first slice's start (the buffer's start) never moves
- **K3** - Add a slice start where the selected voice's grains start, when it plays this buffer; otherwise in the middle of the selected slice. A buffer holds at most 64 slices (`64 slices max`); a start too close to another is not added (`too close`)
- **ALT+K3** - Delete the selected slice's start (never the first)
- **ALT+E2** - Go to the fields
- **K2** - Close the editor

On the fields:
- **ALT+E2** - Pick a field: `mode`, `count`/`sens`, `tempo`, `bars`, `key`, `norm`
- **E3** - Change it:
  - `mode`: `off`, `equal` (`count` equal slices) or `transients` (a slice at each transient whose strength reaches the sensitivity: `sens`, 0-100%, 100% takes every one). Changing it cuts the slices again: starts you added, moved or deleted by hand are then dropped, and the hint row says `edits dropped`.
  - `count` or `sens`: changing it cuts the slices again. Starts you added, moved or deleted by hand are then dropped, and the hint row says `edits dropped`. In `off` the count is kept for when you pick `equal`.
  - `tempo`: the file's tempo, 20-300 BPM in 0.1 steps; from `--` (none) the first turn up sets the clock's tempo, and turning below 20 clears it.
  - `bars`: how many 4-beat bars the buffer holds at the file's tempo; turning it sets the tempo that makes it exactly 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32 bars (a count whose tempo would fall outside 20-300 BPM is passed over).
  - `key`: the note the file is in (`--` unset, then C-2 to G8, as norns names notes: 60 is C3); from `--` the first turn up sets C3, and turning below C-2 clears it. While this field is picked, a note played on a MIDI controller sets it, any note, even one outside the slices of a voice that plays them.
  - `norm`: `off`, or on, showing the gain the buffer plays at (`+6.0dB`).
- **E2** - Back to the slices
- **K2** - Close the editor

The hint row reads `K2:back K3:add E2:sel E3:move` (with ALT `K3:delete E2:rows E3:fine`), and on the fields `K2:back E2:slices E3:edit` (with ALT `E2:row E3:edit`).

The arc's rings on this page, and in the slice editor, are described in [Arc](../arc.md). Buffers, recording, saving and restoring are described in [Audio Sources and Buffers](../sources-and-buffers.md).
