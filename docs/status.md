## Limits to keep in mind

- Lua's 200-local limit applies to each function: the locals probe in `test/unit/structure_test.lua` (which `test/run.lua` runs) measures the free locals at the end of each file's main chunk and of each module's constructor function, and requires 30 in each (`strata.lua`'s main chunk uses 5). Group new constants into tables, and split a module that runs short.
- An LFO whose home routing (its own destination, the matrix's slot 16 + n) is not fast plays at most 10 Hz (`LFO.lua_max_hz`), and the matrix sums it with the R routings, at most 12 sends a 48 Hz step in all (576 a second); one homed on a fast destination runs in the engine, up to 50 Hz. A modulation writer's sends share 150 messages per 100 ms (`ENGINE_BUDGET`), counting exactly the messages that reach the engine; a recall's or a morph crossing's discrete switches (voice starts and stops, source switches) go at once outside the count (ruling 93: about 60 messages a voice start, 417 for a crossing that starts all seven, and a crossing at most once per 0.3 s, ruling 94). Notes add their own: at most 16 `noteExpression` a 48 Hz step (768 a second), counted in the budget's window and never deferred. The budget is a number the hardware soak (#6) is to confirm.
- scsynth's real-time pool on norns is 8192 KB. With six engine LFOs, scatter on every voice and destination and sixteen looping envelope nodes running and no extra note, 3.8's worst cases so far (every voice on the costliest voice def, Karplus; measured after its package 0b on SuperCollider 3.14.1, with 3.13's figure where it differs) leave 5160 KB free on standard at the tightest, synth -> buffer -> synth (5190 on 3.13; eight recordings 5310 / 5360), and, with spectral freeze on every voice and the FDN reverb, 4510 KB on high at synth -> buffer -> synth (4520 on 3.13: 926-936 KB above the 3.5 MB floor, about 820-840 counting the spectral writers' late allocations), as the engine check measures them (`docs/notes/engine/memory.md`). With the note pool full (16 extra notes) the tightest case leaves 2950 KB on standard (all-buffer; 2980 on 3.13) and 2390-2400 KB on high (synth -> buffer -> synth), against the full-pool floor of 1280 KB; the third pool case (every pool slot's voice synth live and cutting, a type switch under a full held chord) leaves 3450-3480 KB on standard and 2870-2890 KB on high. The pool cases read 80 to 90 KB lower when other check areas run before memory in one sclang; these are the memory-first figures, as CI's shard runs it. `\strataGrainBuf` is 91.8 KB of its 94 KB limit (90.4 to 92.7 between runs), `\strataGrainRing` 73.3 (since the grain's controls, 1c, whose window bank and controls also lower the worst cases: 4990 KB free on standard and 4320 on high at synth -> buffer -> synth, 736 above the high floor; with the note pool full 2450 on standard and 2030 on high; the third pool case 3300 and 2690), an envelope node 6.7 KB (sixteen at most), each mix stage 39.6 KB of 42 (since plain sends, 1a), and every voice def is under one 52 KB limit: Karplus, the costliest voice def, is 49.7 KB (48.9 on 3.13; its lowest pitch 11.7 Hz at 48 kHz; the additive type, the largest of 3.8's five new ones, is 47.3 KB at sixteen partials, the others 17 to 32 KB), and the costliest note is a buffer note (`\strataGrainBuf`). The delay's line is two buffers (ordinary RAM, 2 MB) and holds 5.4 s; `\strataDelay` takes about 8 KB of the pool. The engine counts its fixed buffers once (`fixedBufferBytes`: the delay's line, and the output rings, window bank, wavetables and shimmer's rings later packages allocate). Each later engine package of 3.8 measures what it adds against the floors (3072 KB standard, 3584 KB high, 1280 KB with the pool full); a new UGen with a delay line or table in the voice or grain SynthDefs, a node per voice or a larger pool needs that budget re-measured, the high synth -> buffer -> synth case first.
- The largest scenes file the build can write (sixteen scenes of full macros, routings and step attributes, chords included) is 6898 tables: 3.7.0's 7474 less the six tables a voice that scene format 9's one `values` table replaces (16 scenes × 7 voices × 6), plus the six LFO home routings a scene in the `matrix` block (16 × 6), under a quarter of the data file reader's cap of 32768 (`lib/data_files.lua`); a later block with tables per step or per voice re-measures it, since a file over the cap is refused whole.
- sclang's garbage collector fails under millions of small allocations (#131, closed as a platform limit of norns' SuperCollider 3.13), and when a live frame points at freed memory ([#211](https://github.com/sethbc/strata/issues/211), fixed in 3.5; it also failed 3.14.1, and crashed #671's single-sclang runs): sclang 3.13 and 3.14.1 free a function's frame as it returns while a method frame that a kept closure holds keeps its caller, so the engine calls such methods from functions through `areaCall` (`docs/notes/engine/structure.md`, Must not break and Areas: the area hooks, `strataAreas` and the listed calls, which a structure test checks). Measured on 2026-11-13, the waveform area: the fix passed 96 of 96 and 48 of 48 runs on 3.13 and 24 of 24 on 3.14.1, where the code before failed 7 of 48 (3.13, 210910d1, a scratch runner) and 5 of 31 (3.14.1); every area in one sclang on 3.14.1, 6 of 6 against 2 of 6. Since #689 the structure test holds the rule for every function in the engine (the commands, the handlers and each function literal sclang compiles as one). Unsure: norns' own classes and the class library, which the rule cannot reach. If the collector's count-error lines, or sclang ending on signal 11 or 8 or hanging after them, are seen again in any shard, it is a regression of the rule, not noise to rerun: run the area under an instrumented sclang (structure.md, Areas, says how) and route the call it names through `areaCall`. The other rules that work around the collector are in AGENTS.md and `docs/notes/engine/files.md`; the device check is #6 section 6, and the 3.4 command rates' soak is #6's too.
- The ii bus has one leader, the rig's `bus leader` setting (off, strata leads, teletype leads, kept in `io.data`): Strata sends ii messages only while it leads through a connected crow, and a Teletype always leads, so a bus with both is the player's to set as `teletype leads` (`docs/user/io/ii.md`). The queue sends four lines each 48 Hz step and holds 48 (a note's off is never dropped); above the bus's rate (about 190 lines a second) the ports take turns, a note sent or dropped whole (its pitch with its trigger) and a chord to one port thinned to one note a step, so every voice's ports are played but each gets only some of its notes (#710, `docs/notes/lua/io/ii.md`). Leaving the lead sends at most 14 offs and 15 lines at once (`II.cleanup_sends`, `II.leave_lines`, inside crow's queue of 16, which no document lets us measure; #6 measures it): offs past the fourteenth are owed and lowered at the next lead, a release line past the fifteenth is dropped, and at a script's end the owed offs are lost, so those gates stay high until their port next plays (build ruling 47).
- Offs to outside targets are timed by the targets core's one 48 Hz step hook, so an off lands up to 1/48 s after its note's end; a step note ends 0.03 s before its gate (`TARGETS.step_gap_seconds`, build ruling 46), so at very fast ratchets its low can fall in the same tick as the next high.
- AGENTS.md has 7,524 words of its 8,000 (476 left): 7,195 after 3.8's package 0a moved the module table and other detail to the notes, then 0b's 40, 0e's 82, 0c's 74, 0d's 73, 1a's 49 and 1c's 11. 3.8's packages name about 760 words of additions in all (431 still to come), so each landing reports the count. A release that adds rows or rules makes room first, as #696 did for 3.6 and each of 3.7's and 3.8's package 0a did.
