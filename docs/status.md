## Limits to keep in mind

- Lua's 200-local limit applies to each function: the locals probe in `test/unit/structure_test.lua` (which `test/run.lua` runs) measures the free locals at the end of each file's main chunk and of each module's constructor function, and requires 30 in each (`strata.lua`'s main chunk uses 5). Group new constants into tables, and split a module that runs short.
- An LFO that runs in Lua plays at most 10 Hz (`LFO.lua_max_hz`) and sends at most 12 values a 48 Hz step with the matrix's (576 a second); one on a fast destination runs in the engine, up to 50 Hz. A modulation writer's sends share 150 messages per 100 ms (`ENGINE_BUDGET`), counting exactly the messages that reach the engine; a recall's or a morph crossing's discrete switches (voice starts and stops, source switches) go at once outside the count (ruling 93: about 60 messages a voice start, 417 for a crossing that starts all seven, and a crossing at most once per 0.3 s, ruling 94). Notes add their own: at most 16 `noteExpression` a 48 Hz step (768 a second), counted in the budget's window and never deferred. The budget is a number the hardware soak (#6) is to confirm.
- scsynth's real-time pool on norns is 8192 KB. With six engine LFOs and scatter running on every voice and destination and no extra note, 3.5.0's worst cases leave 4650 KB free on standard at the tightest, eight recordings (4670 KB at synth -> buffer -> synth), and, with spectral freeze on every voice and the FDN reverb, 3980-4000 KB on high (396-416 KB above the 3.5 MB floor, about 284-304 KB counting the spectral writers' late allocations), as CI's memory shard measures them on SuperCollider 3.13. With the note pool full (16 extra notes) the tightest case leaves 2540 KB on standard and 2070-2080 KB on high in that shard, and 2420 and 1960 KB in a full run of every area on 3.14.1, against the full-pool floor of 1280 KB; a state the pool cases do not reach (seven Karplus notes cutting at once) would cost about 56 KB more. `\strataGrainBuf` is 73.3 KB of its 88 KB limit, `\strataGrainRing` 63.2, each mix stage 41.3 KB of 42 (with 3.5's `t_gate`), and Karplus, the costliest note, 80.9 KB of its 83 (`docs/notes/engine/memory.md`). 3.7's Wave 1 adds 9.3 KB to every `\strataGrainBuf` (82.6 KB) and sixteen envelope nodes (6.7 KB each): the tightest case, high synth -> buffer -> synth, then leaves 3820 KB, 236 above 3584 (nearer 130 counting the spectral writers' late allocations), and the pool cases 1750 KB or more (`docs/notes/engine/memory.md`, 3.7: Wave 1 together). A new UGen with a delay line or table in the voice or grain SynthDefs, a node per voice or a larger pool needs that budget re-measured.
- The largest scenes file 3.7 can write (sixteen scenes of full macros, routings and step attributes, chords included) is 7474 tables: 3.4's 7394 and 3.7's `sequences` block, five tables a scene (3.5's scene keys and 3.7's step keys are numbers and strings, which add none), under a quarter of the data file reader's cap of 32768 (`lib/data_files.lua`); a later block with tables per step or per voice re-measures it, since a file over the cap is refused whole.
- sclang's garbage collector fails under millions of small allocations (#131, closed as a platform limit of norns' SuperCollider 3.13), and when a live frame points at freed memory ([#211](https://github.com/sethbc/strata/issues/211), fixed in 3.5; it also failed 3.14.1, and crashed #671's single-sclang runs): sclang 3.13 and 3.14.1 free a function's frame as it returns while a method frame that a kept closure holds keeps its caller, so the engine calls such methods from functions through `areaCall` (`docs/notes/engine/structure.md`, Must not break and Areas: the area hooks, `strataAreas` and the listed calls, which a structure test checks). Measured on 2026-11-13, the waveform area: the fix passed 96 of 96 and 48 of 48 runs on 3.13 and 24 of 24 on 3.14.1, where the code before failed 7 of 48 (3.13, 210910d1, a scratch runner) and 5 of 31 (3.14.1); every area in one sclang on 3.14.1, 6 of 6 against 2 of 6. Since #689 the structure test holds the rule for every function in the engine (the commands, the handlers and each function literal sclang compiles as one). Unsure: norns' own classes and the class library, which the rule cannot reach. If the collector's count-error lines, or sclang ending on signal 11 or 8 or hanging after them, are seen again in any shard, it is a regression of the rule, not noise to rerun: run the area under an instrumented sclang (structure.md, Areas, says how) and route the call it names through `areaCall`. The other rules that work around the collector are in AGENTS.md and `docs/notes/engine/files.md`; the device check is #6 section 6, and the 3.4 command rates' soak is #6's too.
- The ii bus has one leader, the rig's `bus leader` setting (off, strata leads, teletype leads, kept in `io.data`): Strata sends ii messages only while it leads through a connected crow, and a Teletype always leads, so a bus with both is the player's to set as `teletype leads` (`docs/user/io/ii.md`). The queue sends four lines each 48 Hz step and holds 48 (a note's off is never dropped); above the bus's rate (about 190 lines a second) the ports take turns, a note sent or dropped whole (its pitch with its trigger) and a chord to one port thinned to one note a step, so every voice's ports are played but each gets only some of its notes (#710, `docs/notes/lua/io/ii.md`). Leaving the lead sends at most 14 offs and 15 lines at once (`II.cleanup_sends`, `II.leave_lines`, inside crow's queue of 16, which no document lets us measure; #6 measures it): offs past the fourteenth are owed and lowered at the next lead, a release line past the fifteenth is dropped, and at a script's end the owed offs are lost, so those gates stay high until their port next plays (build ruling 47).
- Offs to outside targets are timed by the targets core's one 48 Hz step hook, so an off lands up to 1/48 s after its note's end; a step note ends 0.03 s before its gate (`TARGETS.step_gap_seconds`, build ruling 46), so at very fast ratchets its low can fall in the same tick as the next high.
- The last re-review of MIDI out's moved device left three rare Minors, each with a fix sketched by that re-review, for the review before 4.0: a synth moved away and back to its own port in one menu visit keeps a note whose off falls while it is away; a note sent to an old port left empty while SYSTEM > DEVICES is open has its off go to the device moved in after it (a spare off, as the user page and the notes say); a played MPE note's expression, while the menu is open, still reaches the device that took its port until a note starts or ends there.
- AGENTS.md has 7,917 words of its 8,000 after 3.6.0 (83 left): a release that adds rows or rules makes room first, as #696 did for 3.6.
