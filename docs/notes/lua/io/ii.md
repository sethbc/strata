## Must not break

- **One door, one leader.** Every ii message in the script goes through `M.send`, `M.send_off` or `M.send_direct`, and each returns false and sends nothing unless `M.leads()`. The flag `leads()` reads is the module's own: set when the rig's `ii_bus` becomes `strata` with a crow there, or a crow is set up under `strata`, and cleared only at the end of the leaving sequence (the rig tells its listeners after its value has changed, so the rig cannot be what `send` reads while the lead is given up). Under `off` and `teletype` no line starting `ii.` reaches crow but Teletype's three handlers and `ii.reset_events(ii.self)`; a test drives both settings and holds it.
- **Text only.** Each message is one line, `ii.<device>.<command>(<args>)`, each argument written by `S.lib.io_crow.number` and joined by `, ` (`()` with none), sent with `S.lib.io_crow.send_now`, as crow receives `ii["<device>"]["<command>"](args)` (`docs/design/3.6-facts/crow-ii.md` §2). Teletype's handlers are lines of crow-side Lua calling `tell` with a fixed event name (`strata_tt1` to `strata_tt3`), received through `S.lib.io_crow.on_event`: no function is ever assigned through the `crow` proxy.
- **The queue is bounded and in order.** `send` adds a line to the queue (true), or is refused when 48 (`II.queue_max`) or more lines wait, offs included, or while the queue is pressed by the turns' rule (next bullet): dropped and counted (`M.dropped()`, false). A line `send` answered true for is never taken out of the queue while Strata leads (nothing admitted is ever displaced), so a W/ tape row that shows it is true. The bound exactly: a `send` joins only while fewer than 48 lines wait; an off joins unless the newest waiting entry of its line is already an off; so between two sends of a line at most one off of it waits, and the queue holds at most 48 plus the offs that joined since (at most one a line after each of its sends). The LFO clock's step sends the first `II.sends_per_step` (4) through `send_now`, about 20 in each 0.1 s crow window, so a chord to several devices is spread over a few steps and never lands on crow's own 16-message leader queue at once (design §6.2, crow-ii.md §2). Notes are delayed only by this rate. `send_direct` never queues: it is for a device's `release`, which runs when no step will come, and its lines count in the leaving sequence's total (below).
- **Under sustained overload the lines take turns** (#710, the controller's second ruling; the first, displacing the busiest key's oldest line, was withdrawn because it sent no voice's gate at all). A send is admitted or refused whole, at once, and nothing admitted is displaced, so the bound above and the off rules below hold untouched. A send's **turn** is its line: its key (device, command, first argument) once its pair has had an off this lead, else its pair (device and command), so a command with no off of its own (`play_note`, `cv`) takes one turn for all its first arguments. The turn is fixed when the send joins (`entry.turn`). Each turn keeps `waiting` (its sends in the queue), `served` (the LFO step its latest send went to crow), `asked` (the step of its latest send) and `wanting` (refused since it was last served, ordered by `state.refusals`). The queue is **pressed** from any refusal until it next empties at the end of a step. With room and not pressed, every send is admitted, so below the bus's rate nothing changes. While pressed a send is admitted only (1) while its turn has fewer than its **share** waiting, `max(1, floor(II.queue_max / (II.share_factor × asking)))` (`share_factor` 2, leaving the owed offs their room), and (2) when the room left (`queue_max` minus the lines waiting) is more than the wanting turns with nothing waiting that were served less recently than it. Never served comes before any service; between two never served, the one refused first. So a line just served never takes the place of one still waiting for its first. **Asking** counts the turns whose latest send came within the last `II.asked_steps` (48, a second of LFO steps): `ask` counts a turn in at its send, and each step's `age_turns` counts out the turns whose `asked` is 48 or more steps old (and takes them out of `wanting`), forgetting those with nothing waiting. The turns are forgotten whenever the queue is emptied (taking the lead, the leaving sequence, crow leaving). The measure is the whole-system test's flood: chords of eight on seven voices to four devices at 300 BPM with ratchets of four, for 48 s. Every voice's ER-301 port and TELEXo output first go high within 2 s of the flood's start (0.354 s measured), and on each device the most-sent line is sent at most 3 times as often as the least-sent (1.28 measured). `test/unit/io_ii_test.lua`'s flood reaches every port within 34 steps (22 measured).
- **The leaving sequence runs in this order, with the flag still set**, when the rig leaves `strata` with a crow there and at the cleanup step (144): `S.lib.io_targets.end_target(name)` for each registered device, so the offs of notes still sounding join the queue; the queue's offs (`send_off`'s) sent at once in order, at most `II.cleanup_sends` (14), the offs past them owed (below), the sends waiting dropped (not counted in `dropped()`, which counts the queue's overflow); each device's `release`, whose `send_direct` lines go only while the sequence's whole total stays at most `II.leave_lines` (15, design §3.4's "inside crow's 16"; registration order, so Just Friends' `mode(0)` comes first) and are dropped past it (false); then the flag cleared and anything a release queued dropped. The targets' own cleanup step (142) has already ended every note, so at cleanup their offs are what waits.
- **An off is queued exactly when its line owes one** (build ruling 24). A lost off is one nothing puts right: the ER-301's off is `tr(port, 0)` and it has no `release`; the TELEXo's are `tr(n, 0)` and, in `envelope`, `env(n, 0)`, and its `release` sends only `env_act(n, 0)` (the 2e brief), so a lost low leaves the gate high (Just Friends and W/ synth send no off: each shapes its own notes). A line is a key: the device, the command and the first argument (the port or output, as `S.lib.io_crow.number` writes it), so `ii.er301.tr(3, 1)` and `ii.er301.tr(3, 0)` are one line; each queued entry keeps its key and whether it is an off beside its text, and nothing is parsed back out of a line. **The queue learns which lines have offs, and keeps state only for those**: a (device, command) pair is learned at its first `send_off` of a lead, and before that its `send`s keep nothing, so `play_note`, `cv`, `mode` and the like leave no state however many first arguments they carry (`M.lines_kept()` counts the lines kept, for tests). The pair's first off is always owed (lowering a line never raised is harmless). The pair's sends already waiting are counted on their lines when it is learned, and every later one as it joins, so every line's count of waiting sends is exact from then on (an early send left uncounted would, as it drains, use up a later send's count, and the line would read low with nothing waiting while that send still waits; a short count fails loudly at the drain). Its lines without state start unknown. For a learned pair the module keeps per line whether it is high on the device (true when a `send` of it is sent to crow, false when an off of it is, unknown when the line has no state yet: it may have gone high before its pair was learned, as a second voice's port does when two notes start before either ends) and how many of its `send`s wait. `send_off` owes an off unless the line is known low with no `send` of it waiting; an owed off joins the end of the queue whatever its length (it never displaces a line and is never dropped), or adds nothing when the newest waiting entry of its line is already an off; true either way. An off that is not owed (its line known low and its on refused) adds nothing, returns false and is not counted. So a dropped `send` is the only loss, its off is then not sent when its line is known low (an unknown line still gets its harmless low), an off is never sent before a `send` of its line queued ahead of it, and the learned pairs and the line state are cleared when the lead ends and when it is next taken (a crow that leaves keeps them until then, below). The state is bounded by the lines that have offs (ER-301 ports, TELEXo outputs for `tr` and `env`); an unknown line costs at most one extra, harmless low a lead. The re-review's case holds: one note's `tr(12, 1)` sent, a second note's waiting in a full queue, then the off: it is owed (the line is high), joins as the 49th, and lowers the gate.
- **Past fourteen offs at the leaving sequence, the offs are owed.** `II.cleanup_sends` stands as the design gives it (from crow's own 16-message leader queue, which no document lets us measure), and `II.leave_lines` (15) bounds everything the sequence sends at once, its offs and the releases' lines. The fourteen count waiting offs, in queue order, whatever their lines: the ones already queued and those the leaving sequence's `end_target` adds (a TELEXo output in `envelope` owes two, so five voices on an ER-301 port and a TELEXo envelope output already owe fifteen, and a line can have an off waiting before its next send and another after it). Each off past the fourteenth is kept as owed (`state.owed`, by line key, the first kept, in order; `M.owed()` counts them, for tests). A release line past the total is dropped: the TELEXo's `env_act(n, 0)` then leaves that envelope set up on the module, with its gate low.
- **Owed offs are queued by the next lead.** `take_lead` sets the flag, forgets the line state, runs every device's `ready`, then queues each owed off as an off (its pair learned, never refused and never dropped; nothing added when the line's newest waiting entry is already an off) and empties the store. A low on a line already low is harmless by the queue's own rule. The store lives in the module, so it does not survive a script load: at cleanup the offs past the fourteenth stay high until that port or output next plays.
- **A crow that leaves while Strata leads** owes the offs waiting in the queue, empties the queue (the sends waiting never reached the device, so every line's waiting count goes to 0 and its `high` keeps only what crow got), keeps the line state and clears the flag, setting `state.owing`: until the next lead, an off that comes (a note ending while crow is away, a route change, a stop) is owed unless its line is known low (`high == false`). Nothing is sent and no device released: there is nothing to send through. Its return under `strata` makes every device ready again and then queues the owed offs, so a gate high when the cable came out goes low after the devices' setup. The section's `[--]` is drawn by crow's own `request_redraw()`, which its remove handler calls after the `on_gone` hooks (a test pins it).
- **The lead is taken once**: `take_lead` returns at once while the flag is set. norns keeps one crow, and a second one plugged in calls `norns.crow.add` again with no remove (crow-ii.md §1), so its setup runs the hook while Strata leads: no device is made ready twice and the queue is kept.
- **The group stays whole** (build ruling 15): each device's `add_params` runs through `contained_call`. The module compares the params it added (the paramset's count before and after) with its registered `param_count`: when fewer (it raised, or miscounted), the rest are hidden separators `<device>_unused_<n>` (`params:hide`, as the keyed options hide their text param) and one line names the device and both counts (`strata: ii device a added 1 of its 3 params: 2 hidden in their place`), so the next device's params stay inside the group; when more, the group cannot be mended (norns takes the next `n` params), and one line says so (`... added 4 params, more than its 2: the out ii group ends before the last 2`). A device that raised also prints `contained_call`'s own failure line. The other devices' params are added as if nothing happened.
- **Teletype is answered only under `teletype`.** The three handlers go at each crow setup and at once when the rig turns to `teletype` with a crow there; leaving `teletype` with a crow there sends `ii.reset_events(ii.self)`, what `crow.reset()` runs to put crow's own handlers back (crow-ii.md §1). An event that arrives under another setting does nothing. No line names `call4` or a `query` (a query handler that does not return a number crashes crow inside its interrupt path, crow-ii.md §3).
- **The devices are the registry's**, read at each use (`S.registry.ii_devices`, plan ruling 8): their `ready` and `release` run in registration order, each through `S.lib.ui_frame.contained_call`, so one that raises prints a line and the next still runs.
- The module never sets a rig value: a layout state reaches the bus row as a user does (`S.lib.pages_io.go_to_row`, the row by its key, then E3) and raises when it cannot (plan ruling 26).

## The bus setting

| `ii_bus` | Strata sends | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| `off` (default) | nothing | nothing |
| `strata` | the devices' messages, while a crow is there | nothing |
| `teletype` | the three handlers, at each crow setup | `CROW.C1` to `C3` |

A change with a crow there: leaving `strata` runs the leaving sequence; leaving `teletype` sends `ii.reset_events(ii.self)`; arriving at `strata` takes the lead (each device's `ready`); arriving at `teletype` sends the handlers. From `strata` to `teletype` the sequence runs first.

## The queue's states

Per line (a key: the device, the command and the first argument), kept only for a learned pair. `high`: unknown (nil), true or false; `w`: its sends waiting; "off waiting": the newest waiting entry of the line is an off. While Strata leads:

| The line | `send` admitted | `send` refused (48 or more wait, or pressed past its turn) | `send_off` | A send of it drains | An off of it drains |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| its pair not learned | queued, no state | dropped, counted | the pair learned (its waiting sends counted), queued | sent, no state | (cannot happen) |
| unknown, `w` 0 | queued, `w` + 1 | dropped, counted | queued, unless an off waits | (cannot happen) | `high` false |
| unknown, `w` > 0 | queued, `w` + 1 | dropped, counted | queued, unless an off waits | `high` true, `w` - 1 | `high` false |
| high | queued, `w` + 1 | dropped, counted | queued, unless an off waits | `high` true, `w` - 1 | `high` false |
| low, `w` 0 | queued, `w` + 1 | dropped, counted | refused (false), nothing | (cannot happen) | `high` false |
| low, `w` > 0 | queued, `w` + 1 | dropped, counted | queued, unless an off waits | `high` true, `w` - 1 | `high` false |

The leading flag's changes:

| Event | What happens |
|---|---|
| The leaving sequence (the rig leaves `strata`, or cleanup), the flag still set | `end_target` for each device (its offs join by the table above); the waiting offs in order: the first 14 sent, the rest owed; the waiting sends dropped; the releases' lines while the total is at most 15, the rest dropped; the flag cleared; the queue, pairs and lines forgotten |
| crow leaves while Strata leads | the waiting offs owed; the queue dropped, every `w` to 0; `high` kept; the flag cleared; `owing` set |
| Strata takes the lead | the flag set; pairs and lines forgotten; each device's `ready`; the owed offs queued (never refused or dropped); the store emptied; `owing` cleared |

While Strata does not lead, `send` and `send_direct` return false and send nothing; `send_off` returns false, and while `owing` it owes the off unless its line is known low. At crow's leaving, by the line's state: high, its note's off comes later and is owed (crow away) or is the new lead's first off (crow back); unknown or a pair never learned, its off is owed (a needed or a harmless low); an off waiting, owed at once; low with a send waiting, that send never went, so its off is not owed.

## Teletype's calls

Each event goes to `S.lib.io_actions` (`docs/notes/lua/io/actions.md`), which checks its voice, scene, macro and mode and prints one line for anything out of range; this module checks what only it knows: `k`, the velocity (0 to 127) and the macro value (0 to 16383), each with one `strata: teletype call3 ignored: ...` line.

| Event | Arguments | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| `strata_tt1` | `n` | `recall_scene(n)` |
| `strata_tt2` | `v, x` | `run_voice(v, x)` |
| `strata_tt3` | `1, v, x` | `retrigger(v, x / 127)` |
| | `2, v, x` | `freeze(v, x)` |
| | `3, v, x` | `mute(v, x)` |
| | `4, m, x` | `macro(m, x / 16383)` |
| | `5, _, x` | `patterns(x)` |

## The section

IO's `ii` section has one `rig` row, `bus leader`, on `ii_bus`, reading `off`, `strata leads` or `teletype leads` (the rig spec's display labels, `S.lib.io_rig.text`; `docs/notes/lua/io/rig.md`). Its title shows `[--]` without a crow, else `<n> dropped` once the queue has dropped any (the count is the session's). Layout states: without a crow; a crow with the bus off; Strata leads; Teletype leads; messages dropped.
