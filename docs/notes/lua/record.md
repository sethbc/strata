## Must not break

- `engine.recordBuffer` is sent with the nine arguments of AGENTS.md's table: the first eight from the record params (`record_source - 1`, feedback / 100, threshold as `10^(dB / 20)` or 0, loop 0/1, `delay` from `sync_delay`), the defaults sending 3.2's take; the ninth, `sent`, is `util.time() % 1000` at the send, last, so the engine starts a synced take `delay` after the send however long the command waits for a busy sclang (#565; norns' `util.time()` is the wall clock, as sclang's `Date` is).
- The record params are PSET-only (never scene fields). The length is worked out in one place, `S.lib.record.take_length()`, which `record_buffer` sends and BUFFER's `dur`/`len` field shows, so the two never disagree (#321): `record_duration` capped at `S.performance.buffer_seconds` without sync; with sync `record_beats` beats at the tempo, or, when they need more than a slot holds, the whole beats that fit (a whole slot only when not one beat fits). Neither caller computes the cap itself.
- **A synced take's length and delay use the tempo the clock runs from now on** (#567): `take_length` and `sync_delay` read `S.lib.params_setup.beat_seconds_now()`, never `clock.get_beat_sec()`, which on the internal clock and Link reads the old tempo until the clock has ticked twice after a change (up to about 0.9 s going to 3 BPM), so K3 or BUFFER's `len` right after a tempo change would use it. Under MIDI or crow the helper reads the clock's tempo (`docs/notes/lua/params.md`).
- Only reports of the slot's newest recording (`records_sent`) act in the `recordState` handler. A replace take's timer starts at the "recording" report when it can be armed (sync or threshold), else at the send; a playable recording never settles (`finish_buffer_take`) and becomes `take <n>` at "ended". `state.started_at` is set at the "recording" report (and at the send for a recording that cannot be armed).
- A replace take's "ended" settles the slot at once when it already reports a length (`settle_buffer_slot`): the engine sends the take's length before "ended", so that length is the take's, whether the take's timer had run out or not (#457).
- **A canceled take follows the engine's state table** (`docs/notes/engine/record.md`, The state table, #311): "canceled" for a replace take shows the slot empty and keeps what the take replaced in `kept_state`; the engine's next length report decides (audio: the take had not prepared the slot, which is put back; 0 s: it stays empty, `lib/buffers.lua`'s `buffer_state_changed`). So a replace take remembers what it replaces (`state.previous`, from `S.lib.buffers.state_before_take`, taken before anything in `record_buffer` changes the slot), and one sent with a delay leaves `seconds` alone: the engine keeps the slot playing, and reports its length, until 0.21 s before the start. A load sent while the take is armed, also after K3 has asked the engine to cancel it but before its "canceled" report arrives (the take never recorded), keeps that `previous` for the length the engine reports after the cancel to decide (`buffer_state_before_load`, `docs/notes/lua/buffers.md`).
- **A playable recording is never sent while a load awaits the engine** (`confirm_since` set): `record_buffer` flashes `buf <n> loading` and sends nothing, before it cancels or changes anything (#455). A replace take is sent: it supersedes the load and is the same take on both sides. Whether a recording is replaced for its rate is decided from a file the engine confirmed (`loaded` and its `sample_rate`): a slot that shows no audio is empty to the engine, whatever file was last named for it.
- `S.finish_buffer_take` stays the tests' handle on the local `finish_buffer_take`; its upvalue `BUFFER_SETTLE` is the table shared with `lib/buffers.lua` (`S.lib.buffers.BUFFER_SETTLE`).
- `S.buffer_record_clocks[slot]` holds the recording timer, then the finishing fallback, and also `lib/buffers.lua`'s notice for a long load the engine is still checking (`start_check_notice`, #317), and every path that ends a recording or a check (`settle_buffer_slot`, `load_buffer`, `clear_buffer`, a report that ends the check, cleanup) cancels it through `S.lib.record.cancel_timer(slot)`.
- `record_buffer` checks its slot only through `S.lib.buffers.slot_index` (#402): a whole float is that slot, anything else sends nothing.
- A dub into a slot with no file (`path` nil: an empty slot) is `take <n>` from the report that gives it a length (`slot_reported`), its path coming at "ended": a failed load during it puts back a named slot, never a loaded one named `empty` (#400). A dub over a file keeps the file's name until "ended".
- `S.lib.record.state(slot)` is nil when the slot is not recording, and still nil when `record_buffer` calls `S.lib.buffers.recording_started` (before it sets `recording`), so the call passes the take's kind: `recording_started(slot, playable)` (#244).
- A playable recording's "ended" report makes the slot `take <n>` on the scratch path with `start` 0 and no `sample_rate` or `file_seconds`; its "canceled" report leaves the file's three as they were (`docs/notes/lua/buffers.md`, States).
- Every poll decoder floors its slot (`math.floor(value % NUM_BUFFERS) + 1` in `record_state_changed`, `buffer_load_failed` and `buffer_save_result`; `math.floor(value / BUFFER_STATE_STRIDE)` in `buffer_state_changed`). matron hands every poll value to Lua as a float (`lua_pushnumber`), so on Lua 5.3 an unfloored slot is `3.0`: the take becomes `take 3.0` on `scratch/buf3.0.wav` and `set_buffer_file_param` raises on `buf3.0_file` (#300). LuaJIT has one number type and hides it; the stubs deliver floats (`poll.fire`), and the `buffer slots` invariant (`lib/buffers.lua`) checks that a take is named for its own slot on a whole-numbered scratch path.

## Owns

`lib/record.lua` (`S.lib.record`; included after `lib/buffers.lua`, whose `BUFFER_SETTLE` it reads at include time):
- `record_buffer(slot)` (global), the take timers (`S.buffer_record_clocks`), `finish_buffer_take` (`S.finish_buffer_take`);
- `S.buffers[slot].records_sent` (counted here; the field lives in `lib/buffers.lua`'s table);
- `S.lib.record.take_length()`: the next recording's length at the tempo the clock runs from now on (`beat_seconds_now`), `{seconds, beats, capped}`: sync off `{seconds = min(record_duration, slot seconds)}` (no beats); sync on, with `fit = floor(slot seconds / beat seconds + 1e-9)`, `record_beats <= fit` gives those beats (`capped` false), `fit < 1` gives a slot's seconds with `beats` 0 (shown `<1b max`), else `fit` beats (`capped` true: 26 beats, 15.6 s, at 100 BPM on a 16 s slot); the seconds never pass the slot (a rounding error when a slot is a whole number of beats). A capped synced take is a beat or less shorter than the slot, so a loop recorded that way wraps on the beat (a deviation from the 3.3 design's "capped at `S.performance.buffer_seconds`");
- `S.lib.record.state(slot)`: nil when idle, else `{number, kind = "take" | "playable", phase = "armed" | "recording", started_at (nil while armed), seconds, loop, previous}` (`previous`: for a replace take, the state it replaced, as `apply_buffer_state` takes it; nil for a playable recording); `any_recording()`, `any_armed()`;
- `add_params()` (the `strata_record` group, registered after `"buffers"`), the `recordState` poll (start step `record_poll`, cleanup step `record`);
- `S.lib.record.slot_reported(slot, seconds)` (`buffer_state_changed` calls it for a recording slot; it marks a playable recording loaded once it has a length, names a dub into a slot with no file `take <n>` then, #400, and returns true when it changed the slot), `S.lib.record.cancel_timer(slot)` and `S.lib.record.stop_all()` (`lib/buffers.lua`'s cleanup step calls it).

`lib/buffers.lua` keeps the slots, loads, clears, saves, the polls' decoding, `settle_buffer_slot` and `confirm_buffer_audio`; it exposes `S.lib.buffers.display_changed()` and `S.lib.buffers.recording_started(slot, playable)` (`record_buffer` calls it after sending).
