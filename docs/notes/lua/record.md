## Must not break

- `engine.recordBuffer` is sent with the nine arguments of AGENTS.md's table: the first eight from the record params (`record_source - 1`, feedback / 100, threshold as `10^(dB / 20)` or 0, loop 0/1, `delay` from `sync_delay`), the defaults sending 3.2's take; the ninth, `sent`, is `util.time() % 1000` at the send, last, so the engine starts a synced take `delay` after the send however long the command waits for a busy sclang (#565; norns' `util.time()` is the wall clock, as sclang's `Date` is).
- The record params are PSET-only (never scene fields). The length is worked out in one place, `S.lib.record.take_length()`, which `record_buffer` sends and BUFFER's `dur`/`len` field shows, so the two never disagree (#321): `record_duration` capped at `S.performance.buffer_seconds` without sync; with sync `record_beats` beats at the tempo, or, when they need more than a slot holds, the whole beats that fit (a whole slot only when not one beat fits). Neither caller computes the cap itself.
- **A synced take's length and delay use the tempo the clock runs from now on** (#567): `take_length` and `sync_delay` read `S.lib.params_setup.beat_seconds_now()`, never `clock.get_beat_sec()` or `clock_tempo_now()`. On the internal clock it reads the `clock_tempo` param, since `clock.get_tempo()` reads the old tempo until the clock has ticked twice after a change (up to about 0.9 s going to 3 BPM), so K3 or BUFFER's `len` right after a tempo change would use it. On Link, MIDI and crow it reads `clock.get_tempo()`: the param is a whole BPM refreshed once a second, so a fractional Link session's takes would be off (`docs/notes/lua/params.md`). One residual remains on the internal clock: `sync_delay`'s `clock.get_beats()` extrapolates from the last published beat length, so for one new-tempo tick right after a change a synced start can land off the beat by one tick × (new beat ÷ old beat − 1), about 42 ms from 120 to 60 BPM; only a scene recall that changes the tempo, pressed together with a record key, can hit it.
- Only reports of the slot's newest recording (`records_sent`) act in the `recordState` handler. A replace take's timer starts at the "recording" report when it can be armed (sync or threshold), else at the send; a playable recording never settles (`finish_buffer_take`) and becomes `take <n>` at "ended".
- **A playable recording has a fallback too** (#904), as a replace take has: a dub with a length (loop off) runs the slot's clock in the role `"take"` for its length (from the send, or from its "recording" report when it can be armed), and when that has run out (where the engine ends it) the fallback starts; K3 starts it at once (a loop, which has no end, only so; a K3 while it waits leaves it as it is). `BUFFER_SETTLE.fallback_seconds` (5 s) later, with no "ended" or "canceled" report, `end_playable` ends it as a recording that ran (take `<n>`, loaded when its last known length is above 0), on the scratch path unless the take is unsaved: it reads `take_unsaved` as `confirm_buffer_audio` does for a replace take (one flag, one rule), so the slot is on no path, plays, is not restored, and ALT+K3 saves it. One whose phase is still `"armed"` ends as recorded into too, but the fallback sets `take_unsaved` first, so it is an unsaved take: Lua knows only that no report came. It does not know whether the dub started (a synced dub records at its beat while the poll is missing), nor whether a take was written: the likely case is that the engine got `stopRecord` while armed and canceled it, which writes no take, and the scratch file `buf<n>.wav`, overwritten only by each completed take, may still hold an older take of the slot, which a restore of the scratch path would bring back under this name (`takeWriteFailed` cannot rescue that: no write was tried). Keeping the file's name and path over audio that may have changed would lose the dub at the next load; with no path the slot plays, is not restored, and ALT+K3 saves it: whichever way the engine went, the cost is at most a save. A dub that reported "recording" writes (or will write) its scratch file, so it keeps the scratch path, unless that write's failure came first: `takeWriteFailed` while the slot still records sets `take_unsaved`, which the fallback's settle reads (the slot on no path); one that comes after the settle finds the scratch path and drops it (`take_write_failed`, `docs/notes/lua/buffers.md`). The report, a load, a clear or cleanup ends the fallback first; a report that comes after it finds no take and changes nothing, and K3 is never answered by `stopRecord` alone for good. `state.started_at` is set at the "recording" report (and at the send for a recording that cannot be armed).
- **A replace take stopped while armed that no report ends never started** (#974, as #904 settled the armed dub): its fallback (`finish_buffer_take`'s) cancels it as its "canceled" report would and settles the kept state from the last known length (`S.lib.buffers.settle_kept_state`): the slot as it was before the take, never `take <n>` on the scratch path, which may hold an older take. That length is only as good as the reports: without the `bufferState` poll none is ever known (`seconds` stays 0), so the fallback leaves the slot empty even where the engine kept the file (a synced take stopped before its preparation; the cost is a load), and a length report still on its way at the fallback (5 s) leaves the one before it, so a slot the engine had emptied would get its file back. A take whose phase is "recording" settles from its length as before.
- A replace take's "ended" settles the slot at once when it already reports a length (`settle_buffer_slot`): the engine sends the take's length before "ended", so that length is the take's, whether the take's timer had run out or not (#457).
- **A canceled take follows the engine's state table** (`docs/notes/engine/record.md`, The state table, #311): "canceled" for a replace take shows the slot empty and keeps what the take replaced in `kept_state`; the engine's next length report decides (audio: the take had not prepared the slot, which is put back; 0 s: it stays empty, `lib/buffers.lua`'s `buffer_state_changed`). So a replace take remembers what it replaces (`state.previous`, from `S.lib.buffers.state_before_take`, taken before anything in `record_buffer` changes the slot), and one sent with a delay leaves `seconds` alone: the engine keeps the slot playing, and reports its length, until 0.21 s before the start. A load sent while the take is armed, also after K3 has asked the engine to cancel it but before its "canceled" report arrives (the take never recorded), keeps that `previous` for the length the engine reports after the cancel to decide (`buffer_state_before_load`, `docs/notes/lua/buffers.md`).
- **A playable recording is never sent while a load awaits the engine** (`confirm_since` set): `record_buffer` flashes `buf <n> loading` and sends nothing, before it cancels or changes anything (#455). A replace take is sent: it supersedes the load and is the same take on both sides. Whether a recording is replaced for its rate is decided from a file the engine confirmed (`loaded` and its `sample_rate`): a slot that shows no audio is empty to the engine, whatever file was last named for it.
- `S.finish_buffer_take` stays the tests' handle on the local `finish_buffer_take`; its upvalue `BUFFER_SETTLE` is the table shared with `lib/buffers.lua` (`S.lib.buffers.BUFFER_SETTLE`).
- **The slot's one clock knows its role** (#870): it is a clock helper's (`S.lib.clocks.run`, `docs/notes/lua/clocks.md`), started only through `S.lib.record.run_slot_clock(slot, role, seconds, action)`, whose one local `set_slot_clock(slot, handle, role)` keeps the handle and the role (`"take"`, the timer that ends a replace take or a dub's length; `"fallback"`, a take's settle or a playable recording's end with no report; `"notice"`, `lib/buffers.lua`'s notice for a long load the engine is still checking, `start_check_notice`, #317) and sets `S.buffer_record_clocks[slot]` to its id, which stays an id (the tests' `wake_buffer_clock` and the cleanup-order test read it so, plan ruling 10). `S.lib.record.clock_role(slot)` reads the role; the "recording" report starts a take timer (a replace take's, or a dub's with a length) unless the role is already `"take"` or `"fallback"`, never reading "a clock" as "the take timer". Every path that ends a recording or a check (`settle_buffer_slot`, `load_buffer`, `clear_buffer`, a report that ends the check, a new clock in the slot) ends it through `S.lib.record.cancel_timer(slot)` (the first three through `end_take`, which calls it), which stops it (`S.lib.clocks.stop`: never canceled while its wake may be posted, as the engine's "ended" report can arrive when the take timer runs out); only the cleanup step (`stop_all`) cancels at once.
- **A take's record lives exactly while the slot records it** (3.9, #903): `takes[slot]` (what `S.lib.record.state` reports) goes where the take ends or is superseded, never only hidden by `M.state`'s `recording` gate: a replace take's settle (`settle_buffer_slot`, from a report or the fallback), a load and a clear (each through `S.lib.record.end_take(slot)`, which also ends the slot's clock), a playable recording's end (`end_playable`) and a cancel (`cancel_recording`). The cleanup step keeps a running take's record (no take ends there: the engine finishes or frees it, and the slot still records). The `"buffer slots"` invariant (`lib/buffers.lua`) holds the other side: a slot records only with a take and finishes only a replace take.
- `record_buffer` checks its slot only through `S.lib.buffers.slot_index` (#402): a whole float is that slot, anything else sends nothing.
- A dub into a slot with no file (`path` nil: an empty slot) is `take <n>` from the report that gives it a length (`slot_reported`), its path coming at "ended": a failed load during it puts back a named slot, never a loaded one named `empty` (#400). A dub over a file keeps the file's name until "ended".
- `S.lib.record.state(slot)` is nil when the slot is not recording, and still nil when `record_buffer` calls `S.lib.buffers.recording_started` (before it sets `recording`), so the call passes the take's kind: `recording_started(slot, playable)` (#244).
- A playable recording's "ended" report makes the slot `take <n>` on the scratch path, or on none when the take is unsaved (`take_unsaved`), with `start` 0 and no `sample_rate` or `file_seconds`; its "canceled" report leaves the file's three as they were (`docs/notes/lua/buffers.md`, States).
- **A take's settings** (3.7, `docs/notes/lua/samples.md`): a replace take unbinds its slot when the engine stops playing the old audio (`take_started`, once: at the send for a take with no delay, a threshold's included; for a synced take at the slot's first 0 s report while it records, `slot_reported`, or at its "recording" report), so through a synced take's arm wait the old audio keeps its settings; a canceled take's 0 s report unbinds a slot still bound (`lib/buffers.lua`). A playable recording's "ended" makes the slot's own record a copy of the file's it recorded over (`S.lib.samples.take_from_file`; a take dubbed keeps its own) and binds the slot when it holds audio (`loaded`; one that ends from 0 s is unbound, #975); the file's record is never changed by it. A playable recording's "canceled" marks the slot's peaks stale (`S.lib.waveforms.stale`): the engine may have set the slot's gain to 1 at the take's preparation, and the read sends a normalized slot's gain again. A recording started with `record_sync` on keeps the clock's tempo at its start (`60 / beat_seconds_now()`), and the take that ends takes it as its `tempo` once, before its bind (`M.synced_tempo(slot)`: at "ended" for a playable one, in `confirm_buffer_audio` for a replace take); a later tempo change counts for nothing.
- Every poll decoder floors its slot (`math.floor(value % NUM_BUFFERS) + 1` in `record_state_changed`, `buffer_load_failed` and `buffer_save_result`; `math.floor(value / BUFFER_STATE_STRIDE)` in `buffer_state_changed`). matron hands every poll value to Lua as a float (`lua_pushnumber`), so on Lua 5.3 an unfloored slot is `3.0`: the take becomes `take 3.0` on `scratch/buf3.0.wav` and `set_buffer_file_param` raises on `buf3.0_file` (#300). LuaJIT has one number type and hides it; the stubs deliver floats (`poll.fire`), and the `buffer slots` invariant (`lib/buffers.lua`) checks that a take is named for its own slot on a whole-numbered scratch path.

## Owns

`lib/record.lua` (`S.lib.record`; included after `lib/buffers.lua`, whose `BUFFER_SETTLE` it reads at include time):
- `record_buffer(slot)` (global), the take timers (`S.buffer_record_clocks`), `finish_buffer_take` (`S.finish_buffer_take`);
- `S.buffers[slot].records_sent` (counted here; the field lives in `lib/buffers.lua`'s table);
- `S.lib.record.take_length()`: the next recording's length at the tempo the clock runs from now on (`beat_seconds_now`), `{seconds, beats, capped}`: sync off `{seconds = min(record_duration, slot seconds)}` (no beats); sync on, with `fit = floor(slot seconds / beat seconds + 1e-9)`, `record_beats <= fit` gives those beats (`capped` false), `fit < 1` gives a slot's seconds with `beats` 0 (shown `<1b max`), else `fit` beats (`capped` true: 26 beats, 15.6 s, at 100 BPM on a 16 s slot); the seconds never pass the slot (a rounding error when a slot is a whole number of beats). A capped synced take is a beat or less shorter than the slot, so a loop recorded that way wraps on the beat (a deviation from the 3.3 design's "capped at `S.performance.buffer_seconds`");
- `S.lib.record.state(slot)`: nil when idle, else `{number, kind = "take" | "playable", phase = "armed" | "recording", started_at (nil while armed), seconds, loop, previous}` (`previous`: for a replace take, the state it replaced, as `apply_buffer_state` takes it; nil for a playable recording); `any_recording()`, `any_armed()`;
- `add_params()` (the `strata_record` group, registered after `"buffers"`), the `recordState` poll (start step `record_poll`, cleanup step `record`);
- `S.lib.record.slot_reported(slot, seconds)` (`buffer_state_changed` calls it for a recording slot; it marks a playable recording loaded once it has a length, names a dub into a slot with no file `take <n>` then, #400, and returns true when it changed the slot), `S.lib.record.cancel_timer(slot)`, `S.lib.record.end_take(slot)` (the slot's take ended or was superseded: its clock ends and its record goes; `lib/buffers.lua`'s settle of a replace take, `load_buffer` and `clear_buffer` call it), `S.lib.record.run_slot_clock(slot, role, seconds, action)`, `S.lib.record.clock_role(slot)` and `S.lib.record.stop_all()` (`lib/buffers.lua`'s cleanup step calls it);
- its tests: `test/unit/record_test.lua`, with #904's "a playable recording with no recordState settles after the fallback", "a dub that runs to its length with no report settles after the fallback", "an armed dub's fallback settles as an unsaved take", "a takeWriteFailed before a recording dub's fallback leaves the slot with no path" and #870's "a role's clock is not taken for another's" and "the take timer stopped as its wake is posted: no clock error, and nothing more happens".

`lib/buffers.lua` keeps the slots, loads, clears, saves, the polls' decoding, `settle_buffer_slot` and `confirm_buffer_audio`; it exposes `S.lib.buffers.display_changed()` and `S.lib.buffers.recording_started(slot, playable)` (`record_buffer` calls it after sending).
