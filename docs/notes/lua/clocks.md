## Must not break

- **A clock is never canceled while its resume may be posted** (#626, #870): norns delivers every resume matron has posted, and one whose coroutine was canceled meanwhile makes `clock.resume` raise (maiden prints `bad argument #1 to 'resume'`). `S.lib.clocks.stop(handle)` cancels a clock only while its wait's end is farther off than the margin; otherwise it retires it, and the clock ends at its own wake, its wait returning false and its body returning. The margin is `CLOCKS.sleep_margin` (0.1 s, the `learn_wake_margin` the zones editor, the macros and the matrix used before they moved onto the helper) before a sleep's wake and `CLOCKS.sync_tolerance` (1/32 beat, `lib/quantize.lua`'s `wake_tolerance`) before a sync's landing; a sync whose beat is below the one it synced at (a reset or a new clock source moved it) is retired too.
- **Every wait in a helper clock's body goes through its handle** and returns when the wait says false: `if not handle.sleep(seconds) then return end`, or `handle.sync(beat, offset)`. A body that waits with a bare `clock.sleep` or `clock.sync` is not told it was stopped and runs on; a `stop` while it waits so (its `waiting` nil and its coroutine, `clock.threads[id]`, suspended) retires it, its wait's end not known, and records it in `M.bare[id]`, which only `cancel` and the cleanup step clear (#954), so the `clocks` invariant names it after the test even when the clock ran on and ended. A stop from the body's own run is no bare wait: its coroutine is "running", or "normal" while a coroutine the body resumed makes the stop; nor is a stop of a handle whose body raised, which is ended (next line) and does nothing more.
- **A stop of a clock that has already gone neither cancels nor retires it** (#962): when `clock.threads[id]` is no live coroutine (its id canceled with a bare `clock.cancel` outside the helper, or dead), no wake is to come, so `stop` only marks the handle stopped; retiring it would keep it for ever and fail the invariant ("has gone but is kept"). `alive` answers false for it. It is checked before the bare-wait test (#954), which wants a suspended coroutine; a handle whose body raised is ended already (#956), so `stop` returns before either.
- **A body that raises frees its clock** (#956): `clock.resume` raises without canceling a body that raised, which would leave its coroutine in `clock.threads` and its scheduler slot (one of matron's hundred) claimed until cleanup. The helper runs the body in a `pcall` inside its clock; when it raises, the handle is ended, the helper `clock.cancel`s the clock from inside its own run (matron's `_clock_cancel` only clears the slot, which holds no scheduled wait while the body runs, and `clock.threads[id]` goes, while `clock.resume` still holds the coroutine) and raises the body's error again unchanged (`error(err, 0)`), so `clock.resume` reports it as before. A later `stop` or `cancel` of the handle does nothing. A body that raises before its first wait has no id and no slot; `clock.run`, and so `run`, raises its error as before, and its dead coroutine stays in `clock.threads` until cleanup (a table entry, no slot), as without the helper.
- **A sync's landing is worked out as matron's scheduler does** (`clock_scheduler_next_clock_beat`, `CLOCKS.sync_epsilon`): from the beat the sync was asked at, or, when the coroutine's last wait was a sync, from the landing matron stored for it (`clock_scheduler_schedule_sync` counts from the event's stored landing, never from the beat its tick posted the resume with, which is up to a tick later and, with an offset just under a period, a whole period off). The helper works that stored landing out from the resume's beat `woke` (`counted_from`, #953). Matron's tick posts a sync once the beat is past its stored landing, so the landing normally lies within a tick below `woke`; it is on the sync's grid (`k * beats + offset`) when it is the helper's own count or a reschedule's (a new clock source, crow's input division: the next landing after the new beat) of a sync whose coroutine had waited before, and 0 after a reset. So the helper counts from the grid point at most `CLOCKS.sync_tolerance` below `woke` when that is no later than its own landing (a resume a tick late; a beat moved back) or its landing was still ahead when the sync was asked (a reschedule forward past it); from 0 when the beat went back to within the tolerance of 0 (a reset); else from `math.min(landing, woke)` (a landing already past when asked fires at once). A landing not known (a sync given no positive beat) retires.
- **What the landing estimate guarantees**: it equals matron's stored landing after a resume a tick late, after a reset, and after a reschedule back or forward of a sync whose coroutine had waited before (the tests' sequences: both reschedule tests land once before the beat moves). It is later than matron's, by a period, only where the grid point the helper takes lies above the landing matron stored: (a) a tick of matron's that posts the resume at least a period less the tolerance after its landing (its tick thread stalled); (b) a forward jump of the beat with no reschedule (Link: `clock_link.cc` updates its reference beat through `clock_update_source_reference`, which reschedules nothing); (c) a reschedule of a coroutine's first wait when that is a sync: matron's new-slot branch of `clock_scheduler_schedule_sync` stores no `sync_beat_offset`, so `clock_scheduler_reschedule_sync_events` uses the slot's stale offset, and when (offset - stale) mod beats lies in (0, tick] the true grid point the helper takes is above matron's landing, on a reschedule back (below the helper's own landing) or forward (the stub stores the offset, #957, so no test shows it). In each the next landing is counted a period late, and a stop near the true one may cancel while its resume is posted. Everywhere else the estimate is matron's or earlier (a reschedule back to within the tolerance of 0, a first sync's reschedule onto a stale offset outside that window, a grid point the float division misses), and a stop retires where it could cancel, which holds a clock slot for at most a period.
- **`cancel(handle)` is for cleanup steps only**: it cancels at once, whatever the wait, which is safe only when norns ends every clock anyway. The cleanup-order test sees a step through its `clock.cancel`.
- **The helper's cleanup step is the last of all** (`CLEANUP_ORDER.clocks`, 190): a step before it can still retire a clock (the grids' own step, 170, ends their hold clocks).
- **`handle.id` is not known inside the body before its first wait** (`clock.run` resumes the coroutine before it returns). A module keeps the handle; where its tests read `S.<name>_clock`, it sets that to `handle.id` once `run` has returned, and to nil at a stop.
- `clock.cancel` is called only in `lib/clocks.lua` and the files `test/unit/structure_test.lua` lists, as often as listed: `BARE_CANCEL_FILES` (the nineteen that move onto the helper, each in the package that owns it, which removes its line) and `EDGE_CANCEL_FILES` (the nine that cancel only as the script starts or ends, and `lib/sequencer.lua` and `lib/quantize.lua`, which keep their own rule). The first list only shrinks.

## Machines

### clocks
- **State:** a handle's `waiting` (`"sleep"`, `"sync"` or nil while its body runs), `stopped`, `ended` (its body returned or raised; a canceled one's never does), the wait's `wake` (a sleep) or `sync_beat` and `landing` (a sync), `M.retired[id]` (the stopped handles that still wait for their wake) and `M.bare[id]` (the handles stopped while their body waited outside them, kept until `cancel` or cleanup); `S.lib.clocks.alive(handle)` names a running one.
- **Invariant:** `clocks`: no handle is in `M.bare`, and every retired handle is a stopped one under its own id whose coroutine is pending until its wake (it is forgotten when it ends).
- **Test:** `test/unit/clocks_test.lua`: "the invariant fails for a retired handle whose coroutine is dead and still kept".

| State | Event | Next | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| running | its body waits (`handle.sleep`, `handle.sync`) | waiting | the wait's end noted |
| running | `stop` (from its own body, or a coroutine the body resumed) | stopped | its next wait returns false at once |
| running | its body returns | ended | forgotten if it was retired |
| running | its body raises | ended | `clock.cancel` from inside its run (its slot and `clock.threads[id]` freed), forgotten if retired, the error raised again and reported as before |
| running, waiting outside its handle | `stop` (from outside its body) | retired | kept in `M.retired` and `M.bare`: the invariant fails |
| retired, waiting outside its handle | its bare wait ends and the body runs on | stopped | forgotten from `M.retired`, still in `M.bare`: the invariant fails until `cancel` or cleanup |
| stopped | its body waits | stopped | the wait returns false at once, without waiting |
| stopped | its body returns | ended | nothing kept |
| waiting | its wake | running | the wait returns true |
| waiting | `stop`, the end farther off than the margin | canceled | `clock.cancel` at once; its body never resumes |
| waiting | `stop`, the end within the margin, past, or the beat gone back | retired | kept in `M.retired` |
| running or waiting, its coroutine gone (a bare `clock.cancel` outside the helper) | `stop` | stopped | neither canceled nor retired: no wake is to come |
| waiting | `cancel` | canceled | `clock.cancel` at once; its body never resumes |
| retired | its wake | ended | the wait returns false, the body returns, the handle forgotten |
| retired | `cancel`, or cleanup step 190 | canceled | `clock.cancel`, the handle forgotten (from `M.bare` too) |
| stopped, retired, canceled or ended | `stop` | unchanged | nothing |

## Owns

`lib/clocks.lua` (`S.lib.clocks`, 3.9, design section 3, package 0b), included first after the constants:
- `run(fn) -> handle`: starts a clock (`clock.run`) whose body is `fn(handle)`; `handle.id` is the clock's id once `run` returns. A body that is not a function is an error.
- `handle.sleep(seconds) -> boolean` and `handle.sync(beat, offset) -> boolean`: wait as `clock.sleep` and `clock.sync` do, noting when the wait ends; false once the handle was stopped (at once, or at the wake of a retired clock), and the body returns.
- `stop(handle)`: nil-safe, never raises; a stopped or ended handle is left as it is, and one whose coroutine has gone is only marked stopped. Canceled at once while the wait's end is farther off than the margin, else retired. A clock stopped from its own body is neither: its next wait returns false. One stopped from outside while its body waits on a bare `clock.sleep` or `clock.sync` is retired and marked `bare` (a mistake the invariant names).
- `cancel(handle)`: nil-safe; `clock.cancel` at once, whatever the wait, and the handle forgotten (for a cleanup step).
- `alive(handle) -> boolean`: started, never stopped, its body not ended and its coroutine not dead.
- `retired`: the retired handles by id (tests read it); `bare`: the handles stopped while their body waited outside them, by id, until `cancel` or cleanup.
- The constants `S.constants.CLOCKS` (`sleep_margin`, `sync_tolerance`, `sync_epsilon`), the invariant `clocks` and the cleanup step `CLEANUP_ORDER.clocks` (190), which cancels every retired clock still waiting.
- Its tests: `test/unit/clocks_test.lua` (a stop as the wake is posted, driven with `context.clock.advance_until` and its `before_delivery`; a far stop; a reset's beat gone back; stops on nil, stopped, ended and running handles; cancel inside the margin; cleanup; the invariant; a repeated sync resumed a tick late, driven with `context.set_time` and `context.clock.advance`, which posts the beat at its step's time, after a beat moved back too; a sync rescheduled forward past its landing; a stop during a bare wait, still named once the clock ran on; stops from a coroutine the body resumed, from another clock's body and of a body that raised; a body that raised freeing its slot; a stop of a clock already gone; forward jumps of the beat with `context.clock.set_beat`; the margins pinned to the learns' former 0.1 s and quantize's) and, in `test/unit/structure_test.lua`, "clock.cancel is called only in lib/clocks.lua and the listed files, as often as listed".

## Moving a module onto it

The package that owns a module's file (#870; `lib/macros.lua`, `lib/morph.lua`, `lib/matrix.lua` and `lib/ui/zones.lua` in package 1q, which deletes their own retired lists) replaces the clock's `clock.run` and `clock.cancel` with `S.lib.clocks.run` and `stop`, makes every wait in its body `if not handle.sleep(...) then return end` (or `sync`), replaces a cancel in its cleanup step with `S.lib.clocks.cancel`, keeps `S.<name>_clock` as `handle.id` where a test reads it, adds the test "<the clock> stopped as its wake is posted: no clock error, and nothing more happens", and removes the file's line from `BARE_CANCEL_FILES`.

A retired sleep holds one of matron's hundred clock slots until it wakes, which is why a far wake is canceled: the cost of a retire is a slot for at most the margin (a sleep), or until the landing (a sync whose beat went back).
