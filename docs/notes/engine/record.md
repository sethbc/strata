## Must not break

- `recordBuffer` and `stopRecord` and every recording's state (`recordSynths`, `recordFrameBuses`, `stopReplies`, `areaState[\record][\takes]`) belong to the record area (`lib/engine/StrataRecord.sc`, `strataAreaRecord`, order 130). The record area's `stop` cancels every recording before any slot change or the input synth stops, then frees `\strataTapPreMaster` and the tap group; its `free` frees the head buses, the pre-master bus and the voice taps.
- **One record synth per slot, and it lives only in the tap group** (`areaState[\record][\tapGroup]`, added at the tail of `context.xg` in the record area's `start`, after the master): a new recording, load or clear frees the old one first, at once (`stopRecording`), including one still in its stop fade. Replace, never stack.
- **A playable recording never shrinks its slot and never zeroes it except through `afterSlotFade`.** A replace take fades, empties (`bufferFrames` 0, the voices fade) and zeroes as 3.2's did; a playable recording leaves a slot that holds audio untouched, and sets its length to `max(V, D)` only at its start (the arming reply). A playable recording into an empty slot zeroes it (fade first), because an empty slot may hold a canceled take. A slot holding audio at another sample rate is always a replace take: playing the old audio and the new at different pitches is the one thing a playable recording cannot do. An empty slot has no rate to clash with, and two guards keep it so (#374): every step that empties a slot sets `bufferSampleRates[i]` to the server's (`applyLoad` with the frames, as a clear and a replace take do), and `recordSlot` asks the rate only of a slot that holds audio (`bufferFrames[i] == 0` is playable). Since #946 the reset alone covers the last step of a load taking effect too: after its reads the slot stays at the server's rate with 0 frames until its peaks file is in place, the file's rate going to the peaks write (`writeSlotPeaks`' `rate`, which the file names) and set with the frames in the write's action; the frames test is kept as the second guard (the state table's row 4, walked with the step held open by the stopped peaks mover: a 44.1 kHz file's load, the recording judged playable on the empty slot; `"buffers: a slot being loaded is at the server's rate until its length is reported"` reads the rate there).
- **A take that is ending completes** (#890): from its record synth's free until its end (its frame count, a stopped replace take's, or its peaks and peaks file awaited; `takeEnding`), a bad load cancels neither kind of take, so the take ends in full as row 8 says and the slot holds its frames, and a load that takes effect, a clear or a new recording (the later command, which wins) reports it `ended` (state 2), never `canceled`, before its own reports, its slot change dropping the take's remaining steps (`stopRecording`; the state table's rows 14 to 17; a long good file's command cancels nothing, so the take completes during the check). **One predicate, `takeEnding`, says a take is ending** (#966), and two cases Lua cannot send today read it: a playable recording sent while a replace take is ending (the slot still reads 0 frames, the take's length not yet announced) waits for the take's end instead of finding the slot empty and zeroing the take: `recordSlot` counts it and queues it in the ending take (`take[\queued]`: its record number and its start, `beginRecording`), the take completes in full (its length, its peaks file, `ended`, its scratch take), and `endTake` then starts it, judged on the take's frames (row 15). A later load, clear or recording cancels the queued one with the take (`stopRecording`: the take `ended`, then the queued `canceled`), a newer playable recording takes its place (it is `canceled`), a bad load leaves it (row 14), and a `stopRecord` cancels it alone (`canceled`, then the slot's length; the take ends as it would have, row 17). An onset pass sent while a take is ending is refused as one on a recording slot is (`analyzeSlot`, `docs/notes/engine/onsets.md`). A take in its 5 ms stop fade is still recording: a bad load cancels it (rows 10 and 11).
- **Every recording sets its slot's gain to 1 at its preparation** (3.7, design §4.4): `prepareTake` calls `setSlotGainValue(index, 1)` first, for every kind, an overdub and a loop recording included, before its record synth can write, so a take is heard at its own level while it is made and Strata's own output recorded into a boosted slot never feeds back through the boost; Lua sends the gain again once the take's peaks are read. A take with no delay is prepared at its command (one armed by level too: its record synth is sent then, and writes a 10 ms pre-roll from before its crossing); a synced take only `bufferFadeWait + recordPrepMargin` before its start, so a normalized slot plays on at its gain through the wait, and one stopped or replaced before then never touches the gain (the final review's engine Minor 2; `"record: a recording with no delay sets the slot's gain to 1 at its command"`, `"record: a synced recording keeps the slot's gain through its wait and sets it to 1 at its preparation, and one stopped before then leaves it"`). A command too short to record (under one frame) returns before it.
- **A canceled take leaves its slot as the take left it**: untouched before its preparation (a synced replace take waiting for `delay - (bufferFadeWait + recordPrepMargin)`, a playable recording), empty after it. Every cancel by a stop, a load or a clear has its "canceled" report followed at once by the slot's `bufferState` (`bufferChanged`; `rejectLoad` then sends its `bufferLoadFailed`), so Lua learns which: `stopRecordSlot` never empties a slot itself (#311, #373). A cancel by a newer recording is followed by that take's own reports only (the state table's row 18), which Lua never sends: it stops a recording before it starts another. The state table below is the one Lua follows (`docs/notes/lua/record.md`).
- **A synced take's delay runs from the command's send** (#376, #565): `recordBuffer`'s ninth argument, `sent`, is Lua's wall clock modulo 1000 s at the send (`util.time() % 1000`; a float32 keeps about 61 us there, where an epoch time would keep only about 128 s). `recordSendStart` reads sclang's wall clock (`Date.getDate.rawSeconds`, sub-millisecond on 3.13 and 3.14) and its elapsed time together, and `recordSendWait` takes the wall clock less `sent`, wrapped into [0, 1000), as the time the command waited for sclang: the take starts at `elapsed - wait + delay` (never after `now + delay`, now the logical time sclang handles it at, so a take with no delay never waits for a timestamp), however long sclang was held and however many messages queued behind the first. matron and sclang read one system clock. A `sent` up to `recordSendLead` (1 ms) ahead counts as just sent (float32 rounding); a `sent` that is no send time (`recordSendKnown`: missing, negative or NaN, as the check's `h.command` sends, or past 1000 or infinite, never Lua's) runs the delay from when sclang handles the command (its logical time), and so does a wait over `recordSendWaitLimit` (10 s), which a working system never produces, posted as `strata: buffer <n> recording sent <s> s before sclang read it ...` (kept in `areaState[\record][\lastSendLine]`). The command is CroneEngine's own (`addCommand`): #376's OSCdef override, which stamped the message's OSC time, is gone, since sclang stamps only the first message that arrives while it is held.
- **Every delayed step runs only if `slotChanges` is unchanged**: the synced preparation, the zeroing, the start, the length update, the stop's frame count, the peaks and the report. A later load, clear or recording always wins; `"record: rapid record, load, clear and stop leave at most one record synth and drop every late step"`.
- **`slotHeadGuard` (the head guard)** is a no-op when the head bus reads -1 (no recording), moves a buffer grain's start out of the zone the head would reach during the grain's life (to the zone's edge, just behind the head: `docs/notes/engine/loops.md`), and never leaves `slotGrainPos`'s valid range. A start past D is never moved. Its zone follows the grain's life in output frames (`L = durFrames / |r|`, `|r|` floored at 0.001), not the frames it reads. Its `r` is the read rate in slot frames per output frame (`slotGrainReader`'s `frameRate`, `slotRate × BufRateScale.kr`), never `GrainBuf`'s rate argument: they differ by the server's `BufRateScale`, which a buffer keeps from the last file read into it through a clear and a recording, so after a 22.05 or 96 kHz file the zone would be the wrong size (#310). The head is sampled once per block (`Latch.ar` on a block-rate tick), never `K2A`'d: a wrap would otherwise sweep the zone across the whole slot during the block it wraps in.
- A recording's voice source reads the stage's tap (`voiceTaps[v]`, `docs/notes/engine/mix.md`), a pre-master one the tap synth's `masterSum` (the filtered dry bus and both returns times `masterSumGain`, 0.7, as the master sums them: 3.8), a post-master one norns' output bus, all after the master in the tap group; the record synths write no bus they read.
- **`stopReplies[index]` is set only while a stopped replace take's frame count is awaited**, and cleared by the reply, by the next slot change or where the take is forgotten (`stopRecording`, for a stop sent before the take armed). A stale entry makes `slotRecording` true with no recording, so a later failed load would cancel one that is not there (#261).
- `slotHeadBus(index)` reads -1 and 0 while idle: the engine sets it back when the record synth frees (`recordSynthFreed`), and a record synth writes -1 and 0 itself until it arms.
- `recordState` reports: armed only when a delay or threshold holds it, recording at the arming reply, ended and kept once the peaks are written and the length reported (or from `stopRecording` for a take that was ending, #890), canceled from `stopRecording` for any other; each carries its record number (`recordCounts`, counted at every `recordBuffer`), and Lua acts only on its newest recording's reports.
- **A scratch take that cannot be written is reported** (#312): `endTake` gives `writeSlot` a `done`, and a write that ends unwritten sends `takeWriteFailed` (`takeWriteFailedValue`: `record_number * numBuffers + slot index`, after `ended`, never holding it up). A written take reports nothing.
- **A playable recording's new length is reported after its peaks file** (#313): at the arming reply `takeArmed` sets `bufferFrames` at once and reports the length as the action of the write of the peaks file of that length (`docs/notes/engine/waveform.md`), guarded by `slotChanges[index] == take[\change]`, so a stop that keeps the take never drops it. Until that action runs the periodic `bufferState` poll still reports the old length: it repeats the seconds `bufferChanged` last sent (`announcedBufferState`, with the slot's load number, `docs/notes/engine/buffers.md`), never `bufferFrames`. Before the record synth is sent, a recording longer than the slot's audio puts the overview on the record length (`shrinkSlotPeaks`), and a cancel before it arms gives the old overview back (`\peaksBefore`).

## The record synth

Controls: `in, bufnumL, bufnumR, level=1, feedback=0, threshold=0, loop=0, frames, peakFrames, peakBuf, framesOut, headOut, gate=1, replyID`.

- Armed in its first block (threshold 0), or at the first block where `Amplitude.kr` of either channel (1 ms attack, 50 ms release) passes `threshold`; `armed` latches through a `SetResetFF`. While unarmed it writes nothing (`rec` 0) and the head bus reads -1.
- With a threshold the written signal is the source through a fixed 10 ms `DelayN` (`recordPrerollSeconds`): the take starts 10 ms before the crossing (the crossing lands at frame 480 at 48 kHz, up to a block and Amplitude's attack later). A `Select` keeps a take with no threshold sample-exact.
- `phase`, a `Phasor.ar` of rate `armed` over `[0, frames)`: stays at 0 until armed, then runs; in loop mode it wraps at `frames`.
- Writes `old × keep + new × rec`, with `keep = 1 - rec × (1 - feedback)` and `rec` a 5 ms linear ASR on `openedGate(gate) × armed × hold`: `hold` is `phase < frames - fade` (a one-shot's release starts per sample 5 ms before its end, so the take's fade ends on its last frame; loop keeps `hold` at 1). The result's first and last 5 ms differ from 3.2's take; everything between equals it to float rounding (`"record: a replace take without threshold or delay equals 3.2's except its first and last 5 ms"`). A one-shot shorter than its two fades is lengthened to 10 ms.
- The envelope frees the synth (`doneAction: 2`) when its release ends, at a stop or a one-shot's length.
- `framesOut`: the frames written before each block, a `Sweep.kr` from arming (design §1.4.4; `"record: the frames bus counts from arming, past a one-shot's last block"` is a smoke test, not proof: a one-shot releases per sample 5 ms before `frames`, so its head never wraps at a block start and the head-based count it replaced gave the same figure here) (never the head, which wraps when a stop's release runs past a one-shot's length), read with `/c_get` after the synth frees; sclang adds the last block, so the count includes the fade. A replace take's length is that count; a playable recording's stays `max(V, D)`.
- Gated off before it armed, it writes nothing (`rec` needs `armed`); `stopRecordSlot` frees it.
- `SendReply.kr(armed, '/strataRecord', 0, replyID)` on arming, which `takeArmed` matches against the take's node ID (another engine on the same server has its own).
- Peaks: one bin per `waveformPoints` of `peakFrames` (`head × points / peakFrames`), written in `peakBuf` every block while the head is in the bin: `min(1, old peak × feedback + new peak)`, with `old peak` latched as the head enters the bin (or in the synth's first block, for bin 0) and `new peak` the largest `|sample|` written since (both channels: the signal after the pre-roll, times the record level, so a threshold take's bins line up with its audio and nothing before arming counts). The bin it is in, partly written, is what a stop or a mid-take read shows. Nothing is written before arming. sclang reads the 64 values with `/b_getn` (`readSlotPeaks`, 1b).

## The recording path

`recordSlot` counts the command, cancels what was recording and picks the kind: replace (feedback 0 and loop 0, or a slot holding audio at another sample rate; the take's `feedback` and `loop` are then 0) or playable. A recording runs from frame 0 for `seconds`, at most `bufferSeconds`. Its start (`take[\startAt]`) is `delay` after the send (`recordSendStart`: the elapsed time less the wait `sent` gives, or the logical time when the wait is unknown), never more than `delay` after now. It reports armed (only with a delay or threshold) and either prepares at once or, for a synced take, `bufferFadeWait + recordPrepMargin` before its start through `SystemClock.sched` guarded by `slotChanges`.

`prepareTake`: a replace take reads 0 frames, its voices fade (`afterSlotFade`, `bufferChanged`), its buffers and its peaks (`clearSlotPeaks`: the peak buffer and sclang's copy, from which the file at arming is written) are zeroed after the fade, then a sync. A playable recording into an empty slot does the same zeroing (no fade needed for the frames, but the same path), and a playable recording into a slot that holds audio starts at once.

`startTake` checks the take is still the slot's, then, for a playable recording longer than the slot's audio (V > 0, D > V), first puts the overview on the record length (`shrinkSlotPeaks`, one `/b_getn`: the old bins in the first V / D of the bins, zeros after; the record synth's bins are over D from its first block, so this cannot wait for the arming) and keeps the overview as it was in `take[\peaksBefore]`, which `stopRecording` (and `recordSynthFreed`'s never-armed branch) puts back if the take never arms; a reply that finds the slot changed sends nothing. `sendTake` then sends the synth. With a delay still to run (its timestamp `delay` after the command, `take[\startAt]`, is still ahead) it creates the node paused and a second bundle, `/n_run 1` timestamped, starts it: the server starts the record synth on the block the timestamp names, and a cancel can still free the paused node first (a timestamped `/s_new` could not be canceled before the node exists). Past the timestamp (a preparation that outlasted the delay) it starts at once and posts `strata: buffer <n> recording started <ms> ms late` (also kept in `areaState[\record][\lastLateLine]`, which the check reads).

At the arming reply (`takeArmed`) the take reports recording; a playable one sets the slot's length to `max(V, D)` and, when that changed it, reports it once the peaks file of that length is in place (`writeRecordPeaks` with `bufferChanged` as its action, #313), and starts its peak refresh (every `peakRefreshSeconds`, 0.5 s: `readSlotPeaks`, then `writeSlotPeaks` with no action, so no `bufferState` report, unless the file already holds that overview, `peaksUnchanged`, or the take wrote one less than `peakWriteSeconds`, 2 s, ago, #523: a loop of silence or one whose bins have saturated writes nothing, a live loop every 2 s; `take[\peaksWrittenAt]`, the refresh's logical time of the last write, from the arming). A refresh's rename goes through the waveform area's peaks mover, never a fork of sclang, so it holds sclang under 1 ms instead of 4-6 ms (#210, `docs/notes/engine/waveform.md`).

`stopRecordSlot`: a recording not sent to the server yet (a synced take waiting for its preparation, or one still preparing) is canceled, keeps nothing, counts a slot change (which drops the waiting preparation) and reports the slot's length after "canceled": the slot is left as the take left it (a synced replace take that had not prepared it keeps its audio, #373). Any other (running, or paused until its timestamp: the server computes ahead of the clock by its latency, so one may have armed before sclang hears so), started or not as far as sclang knows, is gated off through `sendToSynth`: armed, it fades out over 5 ms and is kept. One not started is then checked after a sync: the server sends an arming reply before it answers a `/sync` that follows the gate, so one that has still not reported arming never armed before the gate, writes nothing gated off, and is canceled (`stopRecording`, then the slot's length). So a stop that crosses the arming reply never cuts a fade-in short. `"record: a stop as the recording arms fades it out and keeps what it wrote"` is a smoke test, not proof: on the check's server the arming reply, or the free, always won the race, so the code it replaced passed it too. A second stop during the fade is a no-op, and so is a `stopRecord` for a slot with no recording, armed or running. The synth stays in `recordSynths` until it frees, and its frame count is read in `recordSynthFreed`.

`stopRecording` (a load, clear or newer recording) frees the synth at once and sets the head bus back to -1 after the free, so a retake waiting for its delay or level never leaves the grains steering around the old head. It forgets the take and reports it canceled, or ended when the take was ending (its synth already freed: `takeEnding`, #890), since its audio was complete. A bad load (`rejectLoad`, and a long file's command in `loadSlot`) cancels only a take that is not ending (`slotRecording` and not `takeEnding`), so an ending take of either kind, a one-shot that ended on its own or a stopped replace take whose frame count is awaited, completes.

`endTake`, once the frame count is known (replace) or at once: `readSlotPeaks`, `writeSlotPeaks(index, frames, ...)`, then a replace take's `bufferChanged` (its length), the `ended` report, and the scratch take (`writeSlot(index, scratchPath(index), done)`; a write that ends unwritten sends `takeWriteFailed` with the take's record number, #312). A take stopped at F frames uses `ceil(64 × F / D)` of its peak bins. Every step checks the take is still the slot's and the slot unchanged. Lua relies on the order: the peaks file names the take's length when that length arrives, and the length comes before `ended` (#457).

## The state table

What a recording command, a stop, a load or a clear does to a slot, and what it reports, in order (#311). A slot has its audio (V frames at rate R; an empty slot has V = 0 and R = the server's rate), at most one load in flight (being checked: the slot still holds its old audio; or taking effect: the slot is emptied, the reads pending) and at most one take (replace or playable; waiting unprepared: a take sent with a delay of more than 0.21 s, until 0.21 s before its start, `bufferFadeWait + recordPrepMargin`; prepared, not started: sent and paused until its timestamp, or below its level; recording; ending: its record synth freed, by a one-shot's length or a stop's fade, and its frame count (a stopped replace take) or its peaks and peaks file still awaited, `takeEnding`: the take is in `takes[i]` and nothing is in `recordSynths[i]`), and, behind a replace take that is ending, at most one playable recording queued in it (`take[\queued]`, #966: counted at its command, no record synth yet, started by the take's end). A take in its stop fade (gate 0 sent, its synth still running) is recording, not ending.

## The phases

A take's eleven phases, as the engine's records hold them (`takes[i]` and its fields `started`, `sent`, `stopping`, `change`; `recordSynths[i]`; `stopReplies[i]`). None is kept as a name: each is read from those records, as the check's walk reads its phase (`walkPhase` in `record.scd`, its name in the last column), and sclang's records lag the server, so a sent take may have armed before `started` says so.

| # | phase | the records | entered by | left by | walk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | no take | `takes[i]` nil, `recordSynths[i]` nil | alloc, or any end below | `recordBuffer` | none |
| 2 | waiting unprepared | the take and its synth (not sent); a `SystemClock.sched` of `prepareTake` pending | `recordBuffer` with a delay of more than `bufferFadeWait + recordPrepMargin` (0.21 s) | the preparation (3), or a cancel (rows 6, 9, 11 to 13, 18) | waiting |
| 3 | preparing | as 2, `prepareTake` run: a replace take has emptied the slot (`bufferState` 0), the fade wait (`afterSlotFade`), the zeroing and a sync to come | `recordBuffer` with a shorter delay, or the preparation's time | `startTake` (4, or 5 and 6) | waiting |
| 4 | shrinking peaks | as 2, a playable recording longer than the slot's audio, `shrinkSlotPeaks`' `/b_getn` pending | `startTake` | the reply, then `sendTake` | waiting |
| 5 | sent and paused | `sent` true, `started` false; the node paused, its `/n_run 1` timestamped | `sendTake` with its start ahead | the arming reply (7) | sent |
| 6 | sent and unarmed | `sent` true, `started` false; the node running below its threshold | `sendTake` of a take with a threshold | the arming reply (7) | sent |
| 7 | recording | `started` true, `stopping` false (`recording` reported, `takeArmed`) | the arming reply | a stop (9), its one-shot's length (10), a cancel | recording |
| 8 | stopping, unconfirmed | `sent` and `stopping` true, `started` false: gated off, a sync pending; a replace take's `stopReplies[i]` set | `stopRecord` of a take sent and not started | the arming reply before the sync (9), else canceled (1) | sent |
| 9 | stopping | `started` and `stopping` true, the synth still in `recordSynths[i]` (its 5 ms fade); a replace take's `stopReplies[i]` set | `stopRecord` of a take recording | the synth's free (10), a cancel (rows 10 to 13, 18) | stopping |
| 10 | ending | the take in `takes[i]`, nothing in `recordSynths[i]` (`takeEnding`): a stopped replace take's frame count (`stopReplies[i]`) or the peaks and peaks file awaited; a playable recording sent meanwhile is queued in `take[\queued]` (#966) | `recordSynthFreed` | `endTake`'s report (11), or a later load, clear or recording (rows 15 and 16) | ending |
| 11 | ended | `takes[i]` nil, `ended` reported; the scratch take's write runs on, detached | `endTake` | (it is phase 1; a queued recording then starts: `beginRecording`, phase 2, 3 or 4) | none |

**What drives them** (`test/engine_check/record.scd`): the state table's walk reaches phases 1, 2 (a 0.3 s delay), 6 (a level not reached), 7, 9 and 10 and asserts each before its command, telling 2 from 3 by the slot's gain (a preparation sets it to 1) and 10's two kinds by `stopReplies`; phase 3 is crossed by every replace take it starts, phase 5 by its delayed row 1 entry, and `"record: delay starts the take within one block of its timestamp, also after a replace take's fade and zeroing"` drives 5's start. Phase 4 is driven by `"record: a recording longer than the slot's audio puts the old overview in its part of the new length"` and `"record: a recording that never arms gives the overview back"`, phase 8 by `"record: a stop as the recording arms fades it out and keeps what it wrote"` (a smoke test: the race is not forced) and `"record: a second stopRecord on an armed take changes nothing"`.

## The tap group

Every record synth and `\strataTapPreMaster` run in the tap group, the last group of `context.xg`, so every source is written before it is read: the input (`\strataInput`, head of `voiceGroup`), a voice's tap (its stage, in `processGroup`), the pre-master bus (`\strataTapPreMaster`, its first node) and norns' output bus (`\strataMaster`). `recordBuffer`'s `source` is 0 the input, 1 pre-master, 2 post-master, 3-9 voices 1-7. A record source outside 0-9 records the input.

## recordState

`recordStateValue(index, number, state)` gives one float: `((number * 4) + state) * numBuffers + index` (state 0 armed, 1 recording, 2 ended and kept, 3 canceled). `record_number` counts the slot's `recordBuffer` commands from 1. The poll is not periodic: one message per change of a recording's state.

## takeWriteFailed

`takeWriteFailedValue(index, number)` gives one float, `number * numBuffers + index`, sent on `takeWriteFailed` (`addPoll(\takeWriteFailed, func, false)`, not periodic; its function returns the last value, 0.0 before any report, which names record 0, never sent) when a kept take's scratch write ends unwritten (#312), after `ended` (seconds after it for a long take). Why a poll of its own: `recordState` uses all four of its states, and a report on `bufferSaveResult` has no room for the record number, which Lua needs for both orders that happen (the report reaching Lua before the take has settled, and after a newer take of the slot has started). Lua (`lib/buffers.lua`'s `take_write_failed`, `docs/notes/lua/buffers.md`) acts only on the slot's newest recording.
