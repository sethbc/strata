## Must not break

- norns' scsynth pool is 8192 KB. No grain SynthDef has a `PitchShift` or a delay line, and every `GrainBuf` uses `maxGrains` (128); never size a delay line or table from a control. No two grains of a grain synth start within `grainTriggerSeconds` (4 ms), so at most 125 sound at once, under `maxGrains - 1`, however fast retriggers come (#532).
- A voice never holds more than its live synths, one fading and one cutting set, however fast switches, stops and starts arrive (`freeCuttingSynths`, `fadeSupersededSynths`). A source switch frees at once any synth of that voice still in its kill fade (`freeCuttingSynths`), and so does a `voiceOn` that meets releasing synths built for another source (`gateVoice` hands them to `fadingSynths`, never leaves them releasing beside the new ones). A scene recall stops the voices it stops before setting their sources.
- The worst cases are 21 grain synths with 7 voice synths (spaced, burst, recalls) or with 14 (synth -> buffer -> synth, whose switch back cuts a voice synth per voice, #502), the cut ones still in their fade, with the seven mix stages (persistent) and, since 3.4, six engine LFOs (smooth and drunk, the largest shapes) on six voices and scatter on every voice and destination beside the offset node, all running through every case on both tiers (the check counts them at each probe): about 3.2-3.4 MB in use on standard (3.4 MB at synth -> buffer -> synth: 4800 KB free of 8192, the memory area alone on 3.13) and 3.3-4.1 MB on high with spectral freeze on every voice (4.1 MB at synth -> buffer -> synth: 4120-4140 KB free); 6.9 MB before 3.2. - The mixed worst cases (spaced, in a burst, for fast A -> B -> A -> C recalls and synth -> buffer -> synth), and the all-buffer worst case (21 `\strataGrainBuf`: every voice switched between three slots), leave at least **3 MB** of the pool free on the standard tier (twelve 256 KB probes). ## Real-time memory

Every UGen's delay lines and tables come from scsynth's real-time pool, allocated when a synth starts. norns never raises it: `Crone.sc` boots `Server.local` with `memSize` left at the `ServerOptions` default, **8192 KB** (its `memSize = 2**16` line is commented out). A UGen that cannot get its memory prints `<UGen>_Ctor: alloc failed` and outputs zeros, and a synth whose own node cannot be allocated fails with `/s_new out of real time memory`: either way that voice goes silent with nothing on screen. A failed `/s_new` is reported in maiden even inside a `voiceOn` that re-gates other synths first (`sendToSynth` closes its quiet messages with `/error -2`, #498), and the engine does not keep the synth that never started: it takes each new synth as playing, but one whose `/n_go` has not come within `startConfirmWait` (0.5 s) is forgotten at the voice's next `voiceOn`, which starts it again (#497; `docs/notes/engine/lifecycle.md`). Buffers (`b_alloc`: the slots and the rings) are not in this pool.

What a grain synth takes, at 48 kHz:
- `GrainBuf` grain tables: about 100 bytes per grain, `maxGrains` (128) per `GrainBuf`, two per synth: about 26 KB per synth (measured: 13.6 KB for one 128-grain `GrainBuf`, whatever its channel count). `maxGrains` is 128 (the UGen's default is 512): the largest overlap Lua can ask for in continuous mode is grain size 0.5 s × density 100 × master density 2 = 100 grains, and a `GrainBuf` runs at most `maxGrains - 1` (beyond that it drops grains and prints `Too many grains!`, on which `test/run_engine_check.sh` fails). In triggered mode a flood of retriggers (a MIDI flood: more than 127 in half a second) could ask for more, so `grainSignal` holds every grain trigger with a `Trig1` of `grainTriggerSeconds` (4 ms): a trigger sooner after the last start starts no grain, and at most 125 grains of 0.5 s overlap (the grains area's check sends 200 retriggers in 0.5 s and counts at most 125; before, GrainBuf reached 127 and printed `Too many grains!` 192 times). So two retriggers on one voice within 4 ms (a chord's notes arriving in separate blocks, not only a flood) give one grain; pattern steps (at least 50 ms apart) and continuous grains (at most 200 a second, 5 ms apart) never lose one.
- the rest of the graph: about 250 bytes per UGen (unit structs, inputs and outputs). `\strataGrainRing` has 144 UGens, `\strataGrainBuf` 192 and `\strataVoiceMix` 176 since 3.4 (its fast-modulation offsets, and the stage's step gate and mute, their lags and their five controls: 175 before #614's gate lag; the area's own defs: an engine LFO 15-27 by shape, scatter 182 with its gain lags, 147 before #610, the offset node 38); 138, 184 and 149 at 3.3.0 (`SynthDescLib.global.at(name).def.children.size`, after #535's per-block freeze flag and #305's complement highpass; 137, 184 and 146 before them; 85 and 103 before 3.2's variation draws, loops and freeze).
- 3.1's `\strataGrain` and `\strataGrainBuf` also each had a `PitchShift` (128 KB at its fixed 0.1 s window) and `\strataGrain` a position-spread `DelayC` (128 KB). 3.2 removed both: pitch is the grain rate and the spread is a backward position offset in the ring (`docs/notes/engine/rings.md`).

The second switch cuts the first one's fading set, the old ring grain synth and the old voice synth, beside the new voice synth, so for the 50 ms cut each voice holds two voice synths and three grain synths: 21 grain synths and 14 voice synths, a cutting set of 14 (a grain and a voice synth per voice; Karplus's two 0.1 s `Pluck` lines are about 64 KB by the UGen's sizing). - **Bursts**: switches that arrive inside one 50 ms cut (a chord of grid scene buttons recalls each scene at once and every recall switches each voice; PMAP-mapped `v<i>_source` params) would leave a synth in its kill fade per switch. Every step that makes a new fading set therefore first frees, at once (`freeCuttingSynths`: `/n_free` in a bundle with `/error -1`, since one may have just ended its fade on the server), the synths an earlier cut left in their fade (`cuttingSynths[voice]`, filled by `cutVoiceSynth`, emptied by `onFree`), then cuts the current fading set (`replaceFadingSynths`): a switch (`cutFadingSynths`), and a `voiceOn` that meets releasing synths built for another source (`gateVoice` hands them to `fadingSynths` through `fadeSupersededSynths`, before it starts anything; `releaseVoice` does the same with a superseded synth, which `gateVoice` no longer leaves). A synth freed this way stops without the rest of its 50 ms fade; that only happens to a synth already fading out under a newer one.
- **Recalls** (#46): scenes A = on/synth, B = off/input, C = on/buffer recalled A -> B -> A -> C at once (a grid chord, a fast scene sequencer). The voice holds its live synths, one fading set (at most one grain and one voice synth) and one cutting set, however switches, stops and starts interleave, and A -> B -> A -> C ends with the same synths as the worst case above. That bound counts one grain synth in the cutting set; synth -> buffer -> synth (above) shows a cutting set of a grain and a voice synth, the most a voice holds (#502). Lua also stops a voice a recall stops before setting its source (see `docs/notes/lua/scenes.md`).

The engine check boots its server with `memSize = 8192` (norns' value, set explicitly), runs the worst case, the burst, the A -> B -> A -> C recalls and synth -> buffer -> synth, and each time sends twelve 256 KB probes (`\strataCheckMemProbe`, a `DelayN` that passes 1 or reads 0 when its allocation failed, on a zeroed bus) right after the last command, inside the 50 ms kill fade, then measures what is left and asks (`/n_query`, errors off) which cutting synths still exist: every synth in a cutting set must still be there (the probes meet the real peak), and all probes must allocate, so the worst case leaves at least 3 MB free. **Holding the cut synths** (#209): on a loaded machine or a CI runner sclang can take longer than the 50 ms fade to get from the last command to the probes (48-54 ms measured on SuperCollider 3.14.1, locally and unloaded, from the start of `probeHeadroom` to the presence query: the old check used the whole window; 3.13 was not timed), so the cut synths could be gone before the probes met them. The commands that make each case's last cutting set therefore run with `voiceGroup` and `processGroup` paused (`holdVoices`, `/n_run 0`), so no fade advances however long sclang takes; `probeHeadroom` then, in one bundle, runs the groups again and starts one `\strataCheckCutHold` (`Pause.kr`) per cutting synth, which pauses it 10 ms later on the server's clock (after its first blocks, where a `GrainBuf` or `DelayN` takes its memory). The presence query waits until two fades (0.1 s) after the groups ran again, so a cut synth a hold missed is always gone by then and the check proves the hold every run; after that the holds are freed and the cut synths finish their fade. Only the check's own messages pause anything: the engine and its 50 ms fade are unchanged. Every scenario runs inside `guardedHold` (a `protect`): if it errors, the groups it paused run again and the holds and cut synths are freed and run, so a failed scenario never leaves the engine paused for the areas after it; a check proves it with a deliberate error (a clock in `processGroup` must advance after it). The check also bounds each scenario's held cutting set: `numVoices` synths for the cases whose last switch cuts one grain synth per voice (every one measures exactly seven), so a second cutting set kept past a switch (`freeCuttingSynths` not freeing) fails it at 21, which `2 * numVoices` would let pass; and exactly `2 * numVoices` for synth -> buffer -> synth, whose switch back cuts a grain and a voice synth per voice (#502), so that case must reach the state it claims. This is the scenarios' bound, not the engine's invariant (a legal cutting set is up to a grain and a voice synth per voice). Whoever hits it should check whether their scenario change legitimately cuts a voice synth too before loosening it. The all-buffer cases call `holdVoices` only for `step > 0`: the first switch's cut synths are gone before the second.

**Measuring without `/rtMemoryStatus`.** SuperCollider 3.13 (norns' and CI's version) has no `/rtMemoryStatus` (it is new in 3.14, and an unknown command posts a `FAILURE` line, which fails the check), so the area measures only with allocation probes, on every version. A failing `DelayN` probe prints `alloc failed`, so those are used only where all must fit (the floors). The figures come from a flood of small graph probes (`\strataCheckPoolProbe`: about 17 KB of controls, nothing that allocates in a UGen) sent in one bundle with `/error -1`: a probe whose graph does not fit is simply not created, quietly, so the number created times a probe's size is the free pool to within one probe. A small probe's size is calibrated on the running server against four `DelayN` probes (1 MB). A `DelayN`, `GrainBuf` or `Pluck` takes its memory when it first runs (the next block) while a graph takes its own at `/s_new`, so a flood waits a sync and 10 ms after everything sent before it. Two 64 KB `DelayN` probes (`\strataCheckReserveProbe`) held around each flood (and counted as free; 256 KB each until 2d, when beside sixteen probes the high worst case left too little room for them and the spectral writers' lazy allocations) keep the server from ever running with a full pool, so replies and anything an engine synth allocates meanwhile find room. Per grain synth: the small probes that fit with and without twenty of them (within 1 KB). On 3.14.1 the probes give the same figures as `/rtMemoryStatus` did (48.0 and 51.4 KB per synth, about 5.4 MB free at the worst case). The high run then replaces the default engine with a high one (`h.replaceEngine`: a `high` setting, a Pi 4 model, a large `MemAvailable`; one engine alone in the pool), asserts it runs tier high, runs the same cases with fourteen probes (3.5 MB), and brings the default engine back for the areas that follow. (Before this, the probes started after syncs and a status read, about 145 ms after the cut: the 50 ms fades had ended, so the figure, about 2400 KB, left the cutting synths out.) It also measures each grain SynthDef (under 88 KB each) and the idle engine's free pool. `test/run_engine_check.sh` also fails on any `alloc failed` in the server output. A new UGen with a delay line or table in a per-voice SynthDef multiplies by up to three grain (or two voice) synths per voice: rerun the check, and keep the headroom.

## The floors, and what 3.5 needs

3.3 lowered the high floor from 4 MB to 3.5 MB (Question 1 of `docs/design/3.3-design.md`, decided); the reasoning below is as of 3.2, the last bullet is redone for 3.3's mix stage (`docs/design/3.3-design.md` §0c.6), and 3.5's design re-measures on norns' own scsynth.

The floors replace 3.1's 1 MB rule, sized for 3.5's note streams (`docs/design/3.2-design.md` §2.7): a note stream is one grain synth, and under today's lifecycle rule (a switch keeps a full fading set) each note beyond the first adds 21 grain synths and about 14 voice synths across the seven voices.
- The design estimated about 35 KB per stream (24 KB of grain tables plus 11 KB of graph). Measured on scsynth 3.14.1 it was about 48-51 KB at 1b (26 KB of tables plus about 21-25 KB of graph), so each extra note cost about 21 × 50 + 250 ≈ 1.3 MB rather than 1.0 MB. With 2c's loops, 2b's draws and 2d's voice filter and sends it is 69-75 KB (about 72 KB on average, about 1.76 MB a note): see the last bullets.
- **Standard** (2 notes): 1.3 MB plus the 1 MB rule = 2.3 MB at 1b (1.76 + 1 = 2.76 MB with 3.2's whole grain path), inside the 3 MB floor; the measured worst case leaves about 4.9 MB free.
- **High**: both floors are kept. At 1b's grain path, under today's lifecycle rule, high fitted three 3.5 notes (2 × 1.3 + 1 = 3.6 MB, inside the 4 MB floor; with 3.2's whole grain path only two, see the 3.5 impact below); four (3 × 1.3 + 1 = 4.9 MB) need a lifecycle change (a switch that does not keep a full fading set of notes) or a smaller grain graph. The design (§2.7) records this; the 3.5 design re-measures on norns' own scsynth (the hardware check, #6) before it settles the note count.
- **The mix area (2d)** adds each grain synth's voice filter and sends (`docs/notes/engine/effects.md`): about 57 UGens, about 12-13 KB per synth (60.7/65.7 KB before 2b's draws), and the FDN reverb about 100 KB more than `FreeVerb2` on high (about 202 KB: each line's `DelayL` sized by its own length). With 2b's draws too, `\strataGrainRing` measures 69.1 KB and `\strataGrainBuf` 74.2-75.0 KB (the design budgeted about 10 KB for the filter, the sends and the draws together). The worst case leaves about 4.90-4.94 MB free on standard and about 4.24-4.26 MB (4240-4260 KB) on high with spectral freeze on every voice and the FDN; counting the spectral writers' late allocations (up to 112 KB), about 4.13-4.15 MB, about 30-50 KB above the 4 MB floor (4096 KB). That is enough to keep the three-type voice filter; its lowpass-only fallback would save about 4.2 KB per grain synth (about 88 KB) if a later package needs the room.
- **3.5 impact (redone for 3.3's mix stage, design §0c.6).** A grain stream is now about 53-67 KB (`\strataGrainRing` 53.1, `\strataGrainBuf` 59.0 measured after 0c's stage, before the guard and the scan hook, and 66.6 with them), about 59 KB on average with the rest of 3.3 and 3.4 (two ring streams of about 54.5 KB to one buffer stream of about 68.5 KB, as at the mixed worst case). The count: extra notes per voice fit when their streams (21 per extra note, about 1490 KB with today's lifecycle) fit in what the worst case leaves with 1 MB kept back, on high, with 3.3, 3.4 and one recording. Measured at 0c on 3.13 (no 3.3 guard, 3.4 or recording yet): high mixed 4350-4370 KB free as printed, about 4240-4260 KB counting the spectral writers' late allocations, and all-buffer 5010 KB (no spectral writer). With the rest of 3.3 (head guard and scan, 7 buffer streams at the mixed case, 21 at the all-buffer one), 3.4 (about 97 KB) and one recording (about 15 KB) in the design's estimates, that leaves about 4070 KB (mixed) and about 4720 KB (all-buffer). So **3 notes per voice at the mixed worst case (about 1.09 MB left) and 3 at the all-buffer worst case (about 1.74 MB left)**, against 2 and 2 without the stage; the design's estimate for the all-buffer case was 2 notes because it took that case as the tighter one, but it leaves more than the mixed case does (it has no voice synths). The floors guarantee a one-note worst case; this count, not the floor, is the budget a 3.5 worst case would have. Wave 3 and 3.5 re-measure.
- **Redone after #502.** The mixed worst case is now synth -> buffer -> synth: on high 4190 KB free as printed, about 4080 KB counting the spectral writers' late allocations, with all of 3.3 in (the guard and scan included). With 3.4 (about 97 KB) and one recording (about 30 KB, measured at 3.3.0: 4350 against 4320 KB at the spaced case) that leaves about 3950 KB, so two extra notes (about 2980 KB) leave about 970 KB: **3 notes per voice at the mixed worst case is now just under the 1024 KB kept back** (the all-buffer case, from 3.3.0's measured 4910 KB with one recording, less 3.4's 97 KB, leaves about 1.83 MB at 3 notes). 3.5's design re-measures before it settles the count; a lifecycle that does not keep a voice synth in the cutting set gives back the margin.
- **Redone with 3.4 measured (package 1a).** The high synth -> buffer -> synth case leaves 4120-4140 KB with 3.4 running, the memory area alone (what CI's shard measures; 3.3.0's 4190 came from full runs in one sclang, which read 100-120 KB less than the area alone, so the like-for-like loss is 150-170 KB, from 4290). Less the spectral writers' late allocations (112 KB) and one recording (30 KB) that is about 3980-4000 KB, and two extra notes at about 1568-1583 KB each leave about 810-860 KB at three notes per voice, under the 1024 KB kept back (the design's 780-820 KB). 3.5's design re-measures both the base and the note.
- If a floor ever fails, find which estimate was wrong before cutting anything.
