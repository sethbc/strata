## Must not break

- **Pattern steps and MIDI notes never create nodes.** They call `retrigger`. Velocity only scales the accent envelope's `levelScale` (sampled at trigger time), never a running signal. The engine check asserts that in every voice SynthDef and in `\strataVoiceMix` `velocity` feeds only `EnvGen` `levelScale` inputs (the grain SynthDefs have no `velocity`); the one allowance is `\strataKarplus`, where it may multiply a `T2A` of the `t_trig` control (a single-sample pluck impulse), and that product may reach only the pluck excitation (a `Decay` input, the `Pluck` trigger). Bent copies compiled in the check must fail it.
- **Silent bus**: an unrouted mod slot reads `silentBus`, never control bus 0 (norns allocates the input follower there), and `alloc` sets it to 0: a freed control bus keeps its last value on the server. The check builds an engine on control buses set to 1 and freed, and reads its silent bus (#504).
- The Lua `voices` table is the only source of values: the engine holds no per-voice defaults.
- The crossmod commands ignore a slot outside 1..`numModSlots`.
- Every voice command checks its index with `validVoice` (an integer in 0..`numVoices` - 1) and ignores any other, including `voiceOff` and `retrigger` (#262). `voiceOn` converts its index to an integer first (`msg[1].asInteger`), so 1.5 starts voice 1; `voiceOff`, `retrigger` and the others ignore 1.5. Matron sends integers; the check sends all ten voice commands (`setGrainParam` with a kept param, `envShape`, `freeze`, `history` and a stage param) -1, `numVoices`, 99 and 1.5, and asserts nothing raises, no node, tracked synth or kept value (`voiceControls`, `grainControls`, `grainSource`, `modSpeeds`) changes, and `voiceOn` 1.5 acts on voice 1 (#504).
- **The engine's own controls are no param name** (#458): `setVoiceParam` refuses `engineOwnedControls` (`out`, `gate`, `kill`, `t_trig`, `velocity`, a slot's `bufnumL`/`bufnumR`/`bufFrames`/`bufRateScale`/`recHeadBus`, a ring's `bufL`/`bufR`/`phaseBus`/`ringLevel`, `rateLevel`, `envBuf`) and `modSlotControls` (`modBus`, `modAmpAmt` and `modFreqAmt` of each slot, the crossmod commands' own), and `setGrainParam` refuses those and `voiceLevelGrainControls` (`pan`, `bend`: they come through `setVoiceParam`). Kept, such a value stayed in `voiceControls` or `grainControls` for good and reached every synth the engine started for the voice: `kill` 1 cut each grain synth 50 ms in. The Lua engine stub reads the same three methods and rejects the same names; a control the engine starts setting itself goes on one of the lists.
- `setVoiceControl` sends a voice's grain synth only the controls its SynthDef has, and nothing when it has none (#501): a buffer or input voice's synthesis params (`fold`, `brightness`, ...) and amplitude crossmod amounts reach its stage or nobody.
- **DC and subsonic blocking at the source** (#309): the FM voice's carrier and the Ring voice's ring product pass `subsonicBlock` (a 12 dB per octave `HPF` at `subsonicCutoff`, 15 Hz) before the voice bus. A new voice whose synthesis can put a component near 0 Hz blocks it the same way, in the voice SynthDef: later (a master blocker, the ring writer) the grains have already turned it into a tone at the grain rate, and the crossmod follower, the sends and the record taps have read it. The check measures the FM and Ring voices at ratio 1 (below 8 Hz under 0.15 of the bus's whole peak); it would not see the blocker moved later in its def, so it stays on the carrier (FM, before the amplitude wobble and pan) and on the ring product (Ring, before the dry carrier is mixed in).
- The Resonator's `RLPF` cutoff stays below `filterCeiling` × the sample rate, and its level goes through `resonatorGain` and its voice limiter.
- **The Pulse voice's `res` rises with resonance** (#314), as every other res in the instrument does: `\strataPulse` plays its `RLPF`'s rq as `1.1 - res.clip(0.1, 1)`, so res 0.1 (flat) to 1 (sharpest) is rq 1 down to 0.1, the reach it had when res was the rq itself. The def's default is 0.8 (rq 0.3, the old default 0.3). The 1.1 is Lua's `PULSE_RES.mirror` (`lib/constants.lua`), which mirrors the res of a scene or PSET saved before (`docs/notes/lua/scenes.md`, `docs/notes/lua/params.md`): change one and saved sounds move. The check measures the voice at 250 Hz with its cutoff at 1000 at res 0.1, 1, 0.1, 1 (6 s each) and asks res 1 to be at least 1 dB louder (+3.2 to +4.4 dB measured; -3.5 to -4.4 dB with res as the rq).
- `\strataMod` replaces bad values on its input and output (`zeroBadValues`), so a voice that blows up never spreads through crossmod.

## Cross-Modulation System

Each voice generates a modulation signal (envelope follower + slow LFO) that can modulate other voices:
- **Modulation Output**: Each voice has a `strataMod` synth that analyzes its audio output and creates a control signal (0-1 range)
- **Modulation Input**: Each voice synth can receive modulation from up to 3 different sources simultaneously
- **3 Modulation Slots**: Each voice has 3 independent modulation slots, each with its own source and depth controls
- **Routing**: Controlled via `setModSource(voice, slot, sourceVoice)` where slot is 1-3. The three crossmod commands ignore a slot outside 1..`numModSlots` (3, mirroring Lua's `NUM_MOD_SLOTS`; `validModSlot`), which would name a control no SynthDef has and stay in `voiceControls` for good
- **Depth Control**: Each slot has independent `modAmpAmt` and `modFreqAmt` controls
- **Signal Mixing**: The 3 modulation signals are mixed together (weighted sum) before applying to voice parameters
- **Signal Character**: The mod signal combines envelope following (70%) with slow LFO movement (30%) for organic evolution

## Voice commands and params

**Index Conversion**: Voice indices are 0-indexed in SuperCollider but 1-indexed in Lua. Always subtract 1 when calling engine commands from Lua (e.g., `engine.voiceOn(voice_idx - 1, ...)`). Mod sources are converted by `engine_mod_source()` (Lua 0=none / 1-7 → SC -1=none / 0-6).

**Starting a voice (`toggle_voice` → `S.lib.engine_sync.start_voice`)**: `voiceOn` only carries the initial freq/amp/pan and grain size/density/pitch, and param actions only reach the engine while a voice is active, so a start sends the rest itself, in the order the engine needs (#325). `setGrainParam` on a stopped voice stores the value in `grainControls[voice]` and reaches no synth (a releasing synth is in `releasingSynths`, not `synths`), and `voiceOn` merges its three grain arguments into those kept controls and, in one bundle, sets the stage and runs `gateVoice`: a new grain synth is created with `grainControlPairs(voice)`, and a releasing one gets `gate 1` and the same pairs in one message. So Lua sends `setGrainSource(voice, source - 1)` **first**, then all nine grain params (`setGrainParam`: size, density × `master_density`, pitch, spread, `envShape` and `grainMode` = option index − 1, `bufPos`, `bufRate`, `bufScan`) and the registered voice params' grain keys and syncs, then `voiceOn` (density = the stored density × `engine_param_value("master_density")`, the value just sent), and the first grain plays this start's values, never the last run's. `voiceOn` replaces `voiceControls[voice]` with a fresh table (freq, amp, pan, bend 1, no crossmod), so the voice-level values follow it: every `voices[i].params` entry (`setVoiceParam`), every crossmod slot (`setModSource`/`setModAmpAmt`/`setModFreqAmt`), `setModSpeed` and `bend`. For its first milliseconds a synth voice therefore plays its SynthDef defaults for the synth params `voiceOn` does not carry, and a buffer or input voice's first grain has no crossmod and bend 1: neutral values, not another run's. The engine holds no per-voice defaults of its own (there is no `getVoiceParams`); the Lua `voices` table is the only source of values. The `v<i>_source` param action is the one voice param that also reaches a stopped voice: it always sends `setGrainSource` (the engine only records it, so the next `voiceOn` builds the right synths), then, for a running voice, `sync_voice_values_to_engine` (not while `scene_recall_in_progress`: a scene recall syncs once at its end, see `docs/notes/lua/scenes.md`). A start's `setGrainSource` is then a no-op in the engine.

**Silent bus**: An unrouted mod slot must read a bus that is always zero. Control bus 0 is not safe on norns (CroneAudioContext allocates the input amplitude follower `amp_in_b` there before any engine loads), so the engine allocates `silentBus` in `alloc`, sets it to 0, uses its index for `sourceVoice = -1` and as the `voiceOn` default for `modBus1..3`, and frees it in `free`.

**Retrigger**: Pattern steps never create synth nodes. For a running voice the pattern clock calls `engine.retrigger(voice - 1, level / MAX_STEP_LEVEL)` (step level 1-15, so a step's brightness is its velocity). The engine sets `velocity` and `t_trig` in one message on the voice synth and, while the voice runs, on its stage (the voice-level gain on the buffer or input path, `docs/notes/engine/mix.md`), and `t_trig` alone on the grain synth (one grain in triggered mode). Every voice SynthDef and `\strataVoiceMix` has a plain `velocity=1` control and an accent envelope `EnvGen.kr(Env.perc(0.005, 0.4), t_trig, levelScale: velocity)` applied as `modAmp * (1 + accent)` inside the amplitude clamp. `levelScale` is sampled when the envelope (re)starts, so a new velocity never rescales a running accent tail; never multiply a running signal by `velocity`. Karplus also feeds `T2A.ar(t_trig) * velocity` (a single-sample impulse, so only that trigger's excitation is scaled) into its exciter and `Pluck` trig inputs so it re-plucks as hard as the velocity. In a grain synth the trigger only matters in triggered grain mode (one grain per trigger). For buffer and input sources there is no voice synth, so the accent lives in the voice's stage: `\strataVoiceMix` has the same `EnvGen.kr(Env.perc(0.005, 0.4), t_trig, levelScale: velocity)` accent, in the voice-level gain on its other path only (`voiceLevel`, `docs/notes/engine/mix.md`), so a synth voice is never accented twice. The grain SynthDefs have no accent and no `velocity`. Velocity is always per trigger (`levelScale`), never a bare multiplier on the output (`GrainBuf` grains still playing would jump); the engine check asserts `velocity` feeds only `EnvGen` `levelScale` inputs in every voice SynthDef and `\strataVoiceMix` (and that a bent copy of the stage fails). The sole allowance is `\strataKarplus`'s `T2A.ar(t_trig) * velocity` pluck impulse, checked strictly: the `T2A`'s input must be the `t_trig` control, and the product (the `MulAdd` sclang fuses `T2A * velocity + Impulse` into, or a `*` `BinaryOpUGen` whose users are `+` sums) may reach only a `Decay`'s input (the noise burst) and `Pluck`'s trigger. The check checks itself: check-only copies of the Karplus excitation compiled in the engine check must fail it when the impulse is a `T2A` of the gate, when the velocity-scaled impulse also scales the output, and when the excitation trigger is smeared into a level (a `Lag`) that scales it; a faithful copy must pass. A step on a stopped voice starts it (`S.lib.engine_sync.start_voice`, the start `toggle_voice` makes). K3 on MAIN keeps its start/freeze meaning: triggered grains fire from pattern steps and MIDI notes only (a deliberate deviation from the spec's "K3 on MAIN with the voice already on").

**Pitchbend (`bend`)**: every voice SynthDef has `bend=1` multiplying its frequency (`modFreq = freq * bend * (1 + ...)`; FM's `modFreqVal`), and both grain SynthDefs have `bend` in their rate (`pitchShift × (1 + rateLevel × (bend × (1 + rateMod) − 1))`; `rateLevel` is 0 for the synth source, whose voice synth already bends). It is transient: not a Lua param, not in `voices`, not captured by scenes. Lua sends it only as `setVoiceParam(voice, "bend", ratio)` (never `setGrainParam`): the engine's `setVoiceControl` keeps it in `voiceControls` and passes it to the grain synth for a buffer or input source, so a source switch carries the current bend to the new synths. `voiceOn` resets it to 1 in the engine and Lua then sends `midi_state.bend[i]` (a start after its `voiceOn`, `sync_voice_values_to_engine` for a running voice) (the wheel's current position, 1 at rest), so a restart never leaves a stale bend and a voice started while the wheel is held plays bent.

**Current Voice Parameters** (as of latest sync):
- **Resonator**: freq, rq, noise, mod1, mod2, amp, pan
- **FM**: freq, ratio, index, modFreq, amp, pan
- **Folder**: freq, fold, mod, amp, pan
- **Sub**: freq, drift, amp
- **Pulse**: freq, width, cutoff, res (0.1-1, rising with resonance: the filter's rq is 1.1 - res), amp, pan
- **Karplus**: freq, decay, damping, excite, amp, pan
- **Ring**: freq, ratio, mod, brightness, amp, pan

**Cross-Modulation Parameters** (per voice, 3 slots per voice):
- **Slot 1, 2, 3** - Each slot has:
  - **source**: Which voice modulates this slot (0=none, 1-7 in Lua, -1=none, 0-6 in SC)
  - **amp_amt**: Amplitude modulation depth (-2 to 2, bipolar)
  - **freq_amt**: Frequency modulation depth (-2 to 2, bipolar)
- **speed**: Modulation signal LFO speed (0.1 to 50 Hz, shared across all slots). The engine keeps each voice's last `setModSpeed` in `modSpeeds[voice]` (nil until one arrives) and passes it (`modSpeedPairs`) to every `\strataMod` it starts or re-gates, so a mod synth `onFree` restarts (re-gated just as its release ended) runs at the voice's speed, not the SynthDef default of 10

## Voice SynthDefs

**Modulation Signal Clamping (v2.0+, frequency floor in v2.1)**:
To prevent excessive amplitude spikes when multiple modulation sources are active, every voice SynthDef (including FM and Sub) clamps:
```supercollider
// Mix modulation signals with clamping to prevent excessive modulation
modSig = ((mod1Sig * modAmpAmt1) + (mod2Sig * modAmpAmt2) + (mod3Sig * modAmpAmt3)).clip(-2, 2);
modFreq = freq * (1 + ((mod1Sig * modFreqAmt1) + (mod2Sig * modFreqAmt2) + (mod3Sig * modFreqAmt3)).clip(-0.9, 2));
modAmp = (amp * (1 + modSig) * (1 + accent)).clip(0, 2);
```
This prevents the theoretical 4× amplitude spike that could occur with 3 sources all at maximum positive modulation. The frequency modulator is clamped to -0.9 (not -2) so the modulated frequency never reaches zero or goes negative (Karplus uses `modFreq.reciprocal` as a delay time, Resonator uses it as a filter cutoff).

**Signal safety**: the Resonator's `RLPF` cutoff (`modFreq` × a 1-4 noise factor) is capped at `SampleRate.ir * filterCeiling` (0.45): high freq, positive frequency crossmod (×3), pitchbend and the noise factor can push it past Nyquist, where RLPF's poles leave the unit circle (for cutoff × rq between SR/2 and SR, rq up to 1) and the resulting NaN killed the output until a restart. Below 0.45 × SR with rq ≤ 1 it is always stable. As a second line, `\strataFilter` (first in the effects chain) replaces any NaN, inf or denormal sample on `mainBus` with 0 (`CheckBadValues` per channel into a `Select`), so a voice that blows up can never poison the delay feedback or the reverb combs; normal samples pass unchanged. `\strataMod` guards the same way (`zeroBadValues`, the helper both use), on its summed input before `Amplitude.kr` (a NaN would poison the follower's state, so it follows again once the bad values stop) and on its output: a NaN or inf on a voice bus never reaches its mod bus, where it would spread through crossmod into every voice it modulates and, through their own mod synths, on, for good. The engine check feeds 50 ms of inf and NaN over a tone into a `\strataMod` on a private bus and finds its mod bus finite and following the tone again, and a Resonator it modulates at full depth free of bad values. The engine check runs sixteen Resonators far past Nyquist and injects 50 ms of inf and NaN on `mainBus` (delay feeding back, reverb on) and finds no bad value at the master output afterward.

**Subsonic components (#309)**: in the FM voice a sideband lands on 0 Hz when the ratio is near 1 (or 0.5), and in the Ring voice the difference tone of carrier and modulator does when the ratio is near 1. Both SynthDefs wander the ratio by 0.1 either way, so the component sits at 0 to 0.1 × freq Hz (0-8 Hz for the FM voice at 80 Hz, 0-30 Hz for the Ring voice at 300 Hz); it is no fixed offset (its mean over 14 s is 0.009 or less). On the voice bus it is granulated with everything else, and grains whose windows do not add up to a constant turn a slow component into a tone at the grain rate: at the engine's output, the Ring voice alone at ratio 1 with grains of 0.05 s at density 13 put 0.032 between 10 and 60 Hz (0.0005 at its default ratio). A survey of all seven voices on their buses, at their defaults and extremes, below 8 Hz against the whole peak over 14 s: FM at 80 Hz ratio 1.5 0.0004 / 0.32, ratio 1 0.153 / 0.33, ratio 0.5 0.093 / 0.33; Ring at 300 Hz ratio 1.618 0.0 / 0.20, ratio 1 0.089 / 0.20; the others stay under 0.025 (Pulse at any width, Folder, Karplus, the Resonator; the Sub at 20 Hz has its sub-octave at 10 Hz, which is the voice). With the 15 Hz highpass, FM at ratio 1 measures 0.007-0.018 of 0.36-0.54 and Ring 0.012 of 0.19. The highpass costs 0.005 dB at 80 Hz, 0.08 dB at 40 Hz, 0.26 dB at 30 Hz and 1.2 dB at 20 Hz (the freq param's lowest note), and takes 11 dB off 8 Hz, 23 dB off 4 Hz and 47 dB off 1 Hz. `LeakDC` was set aside (at its default it eats bass; low enough to spare it, it takes 3-7 dB off a component at 4-8 Hz), as was 20 Hz (5 dB more off 8 Hz, 3 dB off a 20 Hz note). The voice's peak can rise at ratio 1, because the offset hid part of the swing (FM 0.33 before, 0.36-0.54 after); the audible part is the same level. `subsonicCutoff` is the number to lower if a deep FM or Ring note sounds thin (12 Hz costs 0.5 dB at 20 Hz).

**Resonator level**: `DynKlank` driven by noise rings far hotter than the other voices: at its defaults (amp 0.3) it peaked about 23 before the master limiter (the others 0.05-0.5), and at reachable extremes (freq 20, noise 1, amp 1, accents) in the thousands, so whenever it played the master `Limiter` pulled every other voice down by about 28 dB. The SynthDef now scales it by `resonatorGain` (0.02: default peak about 0.5, so its level alone at the output is within about 2 dB of what the limiter used to leave) and puts a mono `Limiter.ar(..., voiceCeiling, voiceLimiterLookahead)` (level 1; lookahead `dur` 0.003 s: `Limiter` delays its output by 2 × dur, so its default 0.01 s put the Resonator about 20 ms behind the other voices and their accents, now 6 ms; under 6 KB of real-time memory per synth; the engine check asserts a lookahead of 2-5 ms) on `sig * resonatorGain * modAmp` before `Pan2`. At the defaults it only catches the hottest moments: sixteen default Resonators measured over 10 s peaked 19-84 before the change (median about 27, so about 0.55 after `resonatorGain`), the spread coming from the cutoff's slow 1-4 noise factor, and full-velocity accents add up to 2×. There the master limiter used to act on every sample. At the extremes, accents and full crossmod included, the voice peaks at most 1 (0.71 per channel when centered). A smaller gain that kept the limiter idle even then (about 0.007) would have made the Resonator alone about 8 dB quieter than it sounded before. The engine check measures it: 32 default Resonators have a median RMS of 0.07-0.22 and 32 with a gain 3× too high (amp 0.9, which scales the same product) a median above 0.22 (one Resonator's RMS over the meter's 1.5 s spreads 0.06-0.34 at the defaults and 0.2-0.47 at 3×, from the cutoff's and amplitude's 0.1-0.15 Hz noise, so a median of eight, as before #91, passed 0.2 about once in 100 runs; a median of 32 measures 0.127 ± 0.009 at the defaults and 0.327 ± 0.012 at 3×), the defaults peak at most 0.75 per channel, the centered extremes peak at most 0.75 (the limiter's 1 × `Pan2`'s 0.707), and one extreme panned hard right reaches 0.9-1.05 on its right channel. **Crossmod from the Resonator changed with it**: `\strataMod` follows `Amplitude.kr` of the voice bus (× 0.7, plus a 0-0.3 LFO), so before the fix the Resonator as a mod source put out about 3.8-16 (median 8.7; measured at its defaults), which drove every voice it modulated to the ±2 clip of `modSig` at almost any depth; now it puts out about 0.13-0.77 (median 0.33), like the other voices, so its depth controls work. Patches and scenes that used voice 1 as a mod source modulate far less than before: listen for that on hardware.
