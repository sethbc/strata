# Before 3.9's features: the state machines made explicit

Status: written on 2026-11-21, with the builder check's questions and its review's findings answered in the text; approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-22, built from 2026-11-23 and landed by 2026-11-24 (the docs pass, 6e, last), **released as 3.9.0** (2026-11-25), and marked "as built" where the build differs: the plan's sixteen rulings and the build's, each with what it costs if wrong, are in `docs/decisions/3.9.md`. It designs the first part of 3.9: every state machine Strata keeps without naming it is found, the ones whose history earns it are made explicit, and every finding of the search, 86 issues (#864-#949), is fixed or ruled on. The facts it rests on were gathered the same day from `main` at a1c8e8b4 (3.8.0) by ten read-only audits, one per area, and are kept under docs/design/3.9-state-machines-facts/ (ten area files and `probes.md`, the audits' probe tests, all run again after the audits). Its plan, of 2026-11-21, was removed at the 3.9.0 release (git history keeps it; `docs/decisions/3.9.md` keeps its rulings).

## Intent

Most of Strata's bugs since 3.2 have one shape: something was in a state its code never named, and an event met it there. The states are kept as a nil-able clock handle, three booleans that must agree, a counter that guards a late reply, or a rule in a notes file that says "X changes only through Y". A player meets the same thing from the other side: a key that does something else because a start is armed, a slot that looks empty because its file is missing, a loop that records every edit because overdub is on.

This work names those machines, writes their transitions down where a test runs them, and shows the player the states that change what a key does. It is done for three readers at once:

- **the player**, who should see the state a key will act on, and get out of it;
- **the developer**, who should find one place that says what a machine's states and events are;
- **the agent**, who works from AGENTS.md and the notes, and today meets these rules as prose of several hundred words a bullet.

## Decided (the maintainer, 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-22)

1. **Find every implicit state machine and make it explicit, in the way that serves the player, the developer and the agent together, and break nothing.**
2. **The findings are filed, this design and its plan are written, and no implementation starts until the maintainer says so.**
3. **Every agent this work dispatches for its audits, checks and reviews runs on the top model.** Building it follows `docs/workflow.md`.
4. **Every finding is an issue and every issue is fixed, whatever its size.** None is left out for being minor, and none is accepted in silence. Where a fix needs a ruling, the issue says so and this design proposes one.
5. **Three of this design's calls were put to the maintainer at the hand-over, and kept:** a direction change ends the pass where the new direction wraps (#874); clear on a frozen delay releases the freeze, then clears (#912); an LFO that is on keeps its engine node at depth 0 (#925).
6. **The other calls of this design's first group were put to the maintainer on 2026-11-22, each with its options, and all stand as written** (Calls made in this design): the freeze key schedules the value its press asked for (#864); a misstep wraps by the pass's own length (#920); a silent set that leaves the delay bypassed and frozen releases the freeze, so bypass wins (#883); fill skips a slot whose file is missing (#877); the undo holds a chain start's replaced patterns and a random sequence's `new` (#922); the undo holds a learn's rewired routing, with no key that cancels a learn (#927); the grid's step editor may cover any owner, which forgets its presses (#866).
7. **All seven waves are built, a batch landing a wave, stopping only for a real blocker,** by workers and reviewers on opus under a controller that briefs and lands (`docs/workflow.md`); the top-model word of 3 covered the audits and the reviews of this design and its plan. The build started at the maintainer's word on 2026-11-23.

## What the audits found

- About 110 machines in ten areas. Most are small and sound.
- Strata audited its state handling once before, ahead of 3.3 (#232-#258, `docs/decisions/3.3.md`). That left `S.register_invariant` and 34 invariants the harness runs after every unit test, States tables in some notes files, single exit functions such as `end_scene_transition`, and, from 3.5, one model with a seeded random walk (`test/notes_model.lua`, ruling 57).
- All of that checks **states**. Of the fixes the audits traced through the history, well over 200, about a dozen were for an illegal state. The rest were wrong **transitions** between legal states: a late report acting on newer state, an event met in a state its handler had not considered, a release that never arrived. An invariant run at a test's end cannot see those. The engine is the same: `warnInvariant` covers two conditions, and no bug in the history tripped either.
- Where a machine has a second record of a fact, the record is where its bugs were: the matrix's record of what it handed the engine (#591, #647), MIDI out's `held_owner` and due marks (#772, #788), the engine's `stopReplies` (#261), the remote's own `leaving` (#704). The machines with the cleanest histories keep no record and compare at the moment of use: an envelope route, scatter, MIDI out's route settle, the engine's voice type.
- Every finding was filed: 86 issues, #864-#949. Twenty were confirmed by a probe test or in the code before filing (#864-#883): defects in transitions, one rule written by hand in seven modules and missing from the rest, a model that no longer follows the engine, a behavior no design rules on, and states the player cannot see. The other sixty-six (#884-#949) are the smaller ones: machines with no invariant or table, a fact kept or derived in several places, a state shown nowhere, and a few faults read in the code and not yet run (their issues say so), whose fix starts with the test that would show them. Behaviors the notes already record as accepted were not filed (a debt keyed by a device's name, bend ranges not put back, a late copy's `[F]`).

## Goals

- Every machine with an invariant has a **Machines** entry at the top of its notes file: its states, its events, its invariant and the test that walks it.
- The machines with the worst histories have their transitions as a table a test executes, and the notes' copy is held to the test.
- Each area with sequences of events has a seeded random walk that runs every invariant after every event.
- A state that is derived in several places has one accessor, and a flag has one meaning.
- A state that changes what a key does is shown where the key is.
- #864-#949 are closed, but for #939, which waits for a device (below).

## Non-goals

- **A state-machine library, or a stored state value for a machine whose state can be derived.** See Approaches.
- **New features.** Where an issue's best answer would be one (a key that stops a fade in place, #891), this work makes the state visible and documented and says so in the issue; the feature is a later release's to design.
- **New engine reports to Lua.** Only a failed voice start might earn one (#939). It needs the real-time pool exhausted, so it is measured on a device first: the issue moves to the 4.0 milestone beside #6.
- **A rewrite of what the audits found sound**: the sequencer's clock code, the ports' hooks, the script's lifecycle and registries, the modal stack, grid and arc attachment, the send budget, the polls' numbering, the engine's area phases.

## Anti-goals

- A change to the default sound, to any param's id, default or spec, or to a scenes or PSET file's format. `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` stays 10.
- A transition table enforced on the device. An error raised in a performance is worse than an odd state the script tolerates; the invariants are never run on a device for that reason, and the tables follow them.
- A second record of any fact. A package that needs one has found the wrong fix.
- A longer AGENTS.md. It has 77 words left; this work adds no rule there and shortens the ones it corrects.
- An engine check over its time. The areas take 1768 s of the 1800 s a docs test allows; every engine package reports the time it adds, and the record area's new walk replaces the example checks it overlaps.

## Constraints

- Every package keeps the Lua suites green on Lua and LuaJIT, and the exact-message tests (3.3.2's verbatim sends, "3.4's messages", "a 3.6 pattern plays the same steps"). The one re-base this design allows is #925's (Calls); its package names every line it moves.
- Every name the tests reach through `local_value` and `find_upvalue` stays (`S.pattern_playing`, `S.scene_transition_active`, `S.morph_clock`, `S.grid_held`, ...). A new accessor reads the old field; it does not replace it.
- Lua's 200-local limit holds; none of the modules this work edits is near it (the audits measured each).
- Nothing under `lib/io/` has run on a device. Its packages change no message a device receives but #871's, and each is checked against the stubs alone: the clocks of 1h change when Lua ends a clock, never what is sent.
- Engine files add methods only, `lib/Engine_Strata.sc` is not edited, and a method that keeps a closure and is called from a function goes through `areaCall`.

## Approaches

Four ways to make a machine explicit were weighed against the audits' evidence.

1. **A stored state value and one transition function for each machine.** This is what "explicit state machine" usually means. It fits a machine with one owner and no derived state, and the gesture recorder already is one. As the general rule it fails here: a stored state is a second record of what the params, the nodes or the port already say, and second records are where the bugs were. An LFO's place, a matrix destination's holding and a slot's status are all re-derived at each step because they can change with no event (a silent PSET read, a source switch that re-resolves `v<i>_main`).
2. **One library every machine is written in.** No shape fits. A port's hooks must read answers in the middle of a transition (#704, #769 and #772 depend on it), the sequencer's state is beat arithmetic across a coroutine's yield, and an engine transition is a chain of closures bound by the `areaCall` rule.
3. **The tables enforced at run time.** See Anti-goals.
4. **Document the machines and change nothing.** The invariants and the States tables of 3.3 did exactly this for states, and the transition bugs kept coming.

**Chosen: explicit in four forms, each machine getting the ones its history earns.**

- **A name and one reader.** The state has one accessor that derives it (`S.lib.buffers.status` is the model), and a flag has one meaning. No field is added.
- **A table a test executes.** The transitions are rows of state, event and next state. A test runs every row from a fresh script, and a docs test holds the notes' copy to the test's rows.
- **A walk.** Seeded random sequences of the area's events, every invariant after each event, and an oracle where one exists.
- **Shown.** The page, the grid or the hint says so where the state changes what a key does.

## Design

### 1. The Machines entry

Each notes file whose module registers an invariant gets a `## Machines` section directly under "Must not break", one entry a machine:

```
### <name>
- **State:** the fields that hold it, and the accessor that names it.
- **Invariant:** `<registered name>` (or "none", with the reason).
- **Test:** `<test file>`: "<test name>".

| State | Event | Next | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
```

A field or flag that holds two facts (#948) is named in its machine's entry with a `- **Two facts:**` line saying both. A machine with no table yet has its first lines and one sentence saying why (a two-state flag needs none). A docs test loads the script, reads the names in `S.registry.invariants` (one module passes its name as a constant, so the source is not scanned) and requires each in an `**Invariant:**` line of a notes file. Names not yet entered are in a list in the test, `UNTABLED_INVARIANTS`; the test fails when a listed name has an entry, so the list only shrinks. Package 0c writes the convention and the list; each later package enters its own machines and removes their names. As built: a package that registers an invariant enters it in the same commit (build ruling B6), wave 2 emptied the list, and the docs pass removed it; "no registered invariant is untabled" keeps it from coming back. The table of invariants in `docs/notes/lua/state-and-voices.md` and the test that holds it (#470) go: the entries and this test replace them, so a new invariant is written down once, in its own module's notes.

`docs/notes/lua/scenes.md` is longer than one read of a file (over 27,000 tokens, #898). 0c makes it an index, as `docs/notes/lua/ui-core.md` is, and moves each section to `docs/notes/lua/scenes/<topic>.md`. A docs test requires every notes file to open with "Must not break", so each topic file takes the rules of its own topic and the index keeps the ones that span them. 0c also states the PSET read convention once, in `docs/notes/lua/params.md` (the clocks that start with `clock.sleep(0)` so the hook at a read's end can retract them, and the hooks' order).

### 2. The harness: tables and walks

Three helpers in `test/helpers.lua` (package 0a):

- **`h.transition_table(spec)`** runs a table. `spec.states` maps a state's name to a function that brings a fresh script into it; `spec.events` maps an event's name to a function that fires it; `spec.state_of` names the state the script is in; `spec.rows` are `{state, event, next, check}`; `spec.impossible` lists the pairs that cannot happen, each with its reason. For each row it loads the script, enters the state, asserts `state_of()` agrees, fires the event, asserts the next state, runs the row's `check` and every invariant. It fails when a state and event pair has neither a row nor an `impossible` entry, so a new state or event cannot be added without deciding every cell. A row may carry `known = <issue number>`: it asserts what the code does today where that is a fault with an open issue, and the helper prints the known rows, so a table can land before the fix of a fault it finds.
- **`h.notes_table(path, machine)`** reads the table under `### <machine>` in a notes file and returns its State, Event and Next columns. A test compares them with its spec's rows, so the notes cannot drift from the test. This is how the docs test of #459 holds the polls' encodings.
- **`h.walk(spec)`** runs seeded sequences: `spec.seeds`, `spec.steps`, `spec.setup`, weighted `spec.ops`, `spec.after(context, op)` returning problems (an oracle's comparison goes here), and `spec.finish(context)` for an end check. After every operation it runs every invariant. A failure names the seed, the step and the operations so far, so the sequence can be replayed as an ordinary test. `spec.reached` names counters the walk must have moved, as the notes walk asserts it reached each path. It is the loop of `test/unit/strata_test.lua:425-503`, lifted out; that test moves onto it.

### 3. Clocks

`lib/clocks.lua` (`S.lib.clocks`, package 0b) holds #626's rule once.

- `S.lib.clocks.run(fn)` starts a clock and returns its handle; `fn(handle)` runs in it. `handle.id` is the clock's id, for a module that keeps `S.<name>_clock` for its tests.
- `handle.sleep(seconds)` and `handle.sync(beat, offset)` wait as `clock.sleep` and `clock.sync` do, and note when the wait ends. They return false once the handle was stopped, and the function returns.
- `S.lib.clocks.cancel(handle)` cancels at once, whatever the wait. It is for a cleanup step, when norns ends every clock anyway and the cleanup-order test looks for the cancel.
- `S.lib.clocks.stop(handle)` ends it and never raises. Where the wait's end is farther off than a margin, the clock is canceled; otherwise it is retired and ends at its own wake. The margin is the one `lib/ui/zones.lua` and `lib/macros.lua` use for a sleep and `lib/quantize.lua`'s tolerance for a sync, with its rule for a beat that went backwards. A retired sleep holds one of matron's hundred clock slots until it wakes, which is why a far wake is canceled.
- A module keeps the handle. Where its tests read `S.<name>_clock`, it sets that field to `handle.id` once `run` has returned (the id is not known inside `fn` before its first wait) and to nil at a stop, as `lib/ui/zones.lua` does today. The helper keeps its retired handles itself, registers the invariant `clocks` (no retired handle's coroutine is dead and still kept) and a cleanup step, the last of all (the grids' own step ends hold clocks late), that cancels what is retired.
- A structure test counts `clock.cancel` outside `lib/clocks.lua`, file by file, against a list that only shrinks. `lib/sequencer.lua` and `lib/quantize.lua` keep their own code and their places in the list: their rules are ruled on and tested about twenty-five times, and the audits found them right.

Each module moves onto the helper in the package that owns its file (#870). The four other hand-written copies (`lib/macros.lua`, `lib/morph.lua`, `lib/matrix.lua`, `lib/ui/zones.lua`) move in a package of their own, 1q, which deletes their private retired lists. Nine files cancel a clock only as the script starts or ends, when no resume can go astray (`strata.lua`, `lib/ui/frame.lua`, `lib/lfos.lua`, `lib/engine_sync.lua`, `lib/playheads.lua`, `lib/waveforms.lua`, `lib/samples.lua`, `lib/side_files.lua`, `lib/factory.lua`): the structure test lists them apart, with that reason, and they do not move.

As built: the helper's cleanup step is 190, after every other (plan ruling 11); a package whose files hold a cancel moved every cancel in them, whatever clock its brief named (B10), and `lib/buffers.lua` kept its save clock's cancel until 4g moved that file onto the shared writer. 1s (#953, #954) worked the sync's landing out as matron's scheduler does and made a bare wait in a helper clock's body something the invariant sees; a stop of a clock that has already gone neither cancels nor retires it (#962), and a body that raises frees its clock (#956).

### 5. The tables

Each is a Machines entry and an `h.transition_table` test, in the package of its area. The product code does not change, but for the invariant lines named.

- **Buffer slot** (`lib/buffers.lua`): `status()` by event, over the sub-states the audit lists (three kinds of loading, three of armed, two of finishing, three of empty). The engine's table of a recording (`docs/notes/engine/record.md`) is its spine, with the missing rows for a take that is ending. Four invariant lines: finishing implies a replace take, `missing_path` implies no path and not loaded, `restoring` implies `confirm_since`, `recording` implies a take.
- **Voice kind** (`lib/voices.lua`, `lib/freeze.lua`, `lib/notes.lua`): kind before (synth, frozen synth, buffer, input, voice source) by event (source set, freeze set, start, stop), once by hand and once in a recall, asserting the freeze param, the reference, the extra notes and `scaleNote`.
- **Scene fade** (`lib/scenes.lua`): the audit's seven events from idle, fading before and after the midpoint, and landing.
- **Synced delay** (`lib/delay_sync.lua`): mode (free, division, synced with no length) by event (tempo move, modulation, feedback edit, sync toggle, recall, tempo to 0 and back), asserting the number of `setDelay` and the time each carries. It gets the invariant it lacks.
- **Gesture recorder** (`lib/gestures.lua`): seven events by four states.
- **A step's holds** (`lib/sequencer.lua`): the slice decision as the audit's truth table, and the invariant `step holds` (stopped means the six tables are empty; `step_region_to_end` implies `step_regions`; a slice's step is the last played).
- **Position and pass** (`lib/sequencer.lua`): direction by length change by direction change at each count of steps taken.
- **Port tellings** (`lib/io/ports.lua`): for each event (arrive, leave, claim, release, menu close), the order the hooks run in and what `device`, `owner` and `holds` answer inside each.
- **MIDI out's note record** (`lib/io/midi_out.lua`): live, held and departed by its end, a leaving device, a claim and an arrival; two invariant lines for the zone marks.
- **The ii lead** (`lib/io/ii.lua`): its invariant (not leading with owing; `leave_room` nil outside a leave; each learned line's `waiting` equal to its sends in the queue).

### 7. One name, one reader

- **A voice's start.** `S.register_grain_restart` hooks are called with a reason, `"start"` or `"source"`. The four hooks that read `not S.voices[v].active` to mean "this is a start" read the reason. The hooks at a source switch are contained as the others are.

- **Arc carries.** `arc_whole_steps(key, delta, ticks_per_step, context)` keeps the context beside the ticks and forgets them on a mismatch; the four page locals go.
- **The engine's freeze** (#887). `freezeKind(voice)` names what is frozen, from the frozen source and the mode: `\none`, `\holdSynth`, `\spectralSynth`, `\holdSlot`, `\copyPending` or `\copyHeld`. `freezeStale(voice)` says the voice no longer has the source it was frozen on. - **The patterns' play state.** `S.lib.sequencer.play_state()` returns `stopped`, `armed` or `playing` (built in section 8's package, which is its first reader).
- **Whether a routing plays** (#926). `S.lib.matrix.status(k)` returns `empty`, `incomplete`, `unknown`, `off`, `zero`, `summed` or `owned`; MOD and the envelopes read it.
- **Hooks contained alike** (#910: the grain-restart and source-change hooks in 4a, the lock-change hooks with the setter), **lock set in one place** (#917: a setter in `lib/engine_sync.lua` that a scene's apply calls and that runs the hooks contained), **one detach** for a grid's port and `S.arc_connected` read through an accessor (#932).

## Left to the builder

- Whether a table's test lives in its module's test file or a file of its own, and a walk's file name, so long as `lua test/run.lua <filter>` still finds a module's tests by its name.
- The states a table's test enters by driving the script and the ones it sets up through `S` (the buffer slot's deeper sub-states need the engine stub's reports in order).
- The mark for a missing slot, a held port, a failed target and a step lock, within the layout rules; and the blink's rate, which follows #593's.
- How the modulation model reads a fade (it sends outside `deferrable`, as #875 corrects the notes to say).
- Which of the record check's example checks the row walk replaces, so the area's time does not rise.
- Where a morph's apply clears a preset's name (a hook the presets register, or the apply itself).

## Cost if wrong

- **No stored state.** If a derived state proves too slow to compute, the accessor is the one place a cache goes. None of these is on a path that runs more than 48 times a second.
- **Tables in tests, not in the code.** A transition nobody wrote a row for is still found only when a walk reaches it. The table's missing-cell rule and the walks are the answer to that, and a table moved into the code later starts from the same rows.
- **The clock helper.** A module moved onto it whose stop was right to cancel at once now may leave a clock asleep until its wake. The cost is a clock slot for at most the margin.
- **The press time in the dispatcher.** An owner that wants a release after it was covered and uncovered no longer gets one. None does today; the walk of section 6 would show one that did.
- **Positions on the `still` check.** A positions key held while its pane changes page ends at the next 15 Hz tick, as today, but by the core's word and not the view's. If a layout needs a key to survive a view change, the page's closure is where that is said.
- **A direction change.** If the wrap is the wrong end for a pass, the conditions that count passes (`every`, `first`) count one pass at a different step; the old rule is one line in `take_position`.
- **The shown states.** A mark that crowds a row is found by the renders; each is one draw call to remove.

## Calls made in this design

Each can be reversed at the review. The first group changes what a player hears or what a key does; the maintainer has decided every one of them (Decided, 5 and 6).

**What plays, and what a key does**

- **A direction change** (#874) ends the pass where the new direction wraps, so no step repeats inside a pass.
- **A misstep** (#920) wraps by the pass's own length until the pass ends, as every other step of the pass does.
- **Clear on a frozen delay** (#912) releases the freeze first. The other answers are to refuse the clear, or to leave it and say the loop is empty and held.
- **An LFO's depth at 0** (#925): an LFO that is on keeps its engine node, so a sweep through 0 does not resync. It moves a few sends in the exact-message tests, which its package re-bases and names.
- **The freeze key** (#864) schedules a value, not a toggle.
- **Fill** (#877) skips a slot whose file is missing.
- **A silent set that leaves the delay bypassed and frozen** (#883) releases the freeze.
- **The undo** (#922, #927) also holds a chain start's replaced patterns, a random sequence's `new` and a learn's rewired routing.
- **The grid's step editor** (#866) may still cover another owner; the covered owner forgets its presses and nothing is refused.

**Left as they are, and written down**

- **A fade** (#891) gets no key that stops it in place here: the user page says what a hand edit and a cancel do. The key is a feature for a later design.
- **Edits to a voice in its release tail** (#918) still reach no engine, as the rule that a sender needs an active voice has it; the mute of a stopped voice is shown.
- **ALT after a name entry** (#947) stays forgotten until K1 is pressed again.
