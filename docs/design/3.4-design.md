## Questions for review

Every choice below could reasonably go the other way. Each has a recommendation, and the body is written to it.

1. **Replace norns' `lib/lfo.lua` with Strata's own LFOs?** The library's phase is readable (`lfo:get("phase_counter")`) and `change_period` keeps the phase continuous, so those are not the reasons. The reasons are: the new shapes (the library has six fixed ones, none of them smooth random, once-per-cycle sample-and-hold or drunk); one parameter set for Lua-run and engine-run LFOs (the library's period stops at 0.1 s, and an engine LFO needs rates up to 50 Hz and its own shapes); free mode that doesn't step with the tempo (the library advances every LFO by 1/ppqn of a beat per clock step, so a free LFO's step rate, and Strata's message rate, follow the tempo: 240 steps a second per LFO at 300 BPM); and the workarounds Strata carries (#103's offset wrapper, the depth rounding). The cost: the PSET ids change from `lfo_*` to `strata_lfo<n>_*`. **Recommended: yes**, with a one-time import of old PSETs' `lfo_*` lines (§2.9) and a scene migration (§9).
2. **Which LFOs run in the engine?** **Recommended: the destination decides.** An LFO on one of the five fast destinations (`v<i> pitch`, `pos`, `size`, `vfilt`, `amp`) always runs in the engine, at any rate; every other LFO runs in Lua, up to 10 Hz (§3). The alternative is a per-LFO "engine" switch, which would give two kinds of LFO on the same destination and a 10 Hz wall the user has to understand.
3. **The memory floor, one decision for 3.3 and 3.4. Decided 2026-11-08: high 3.5 MB, per-synth 88 KB (as 3.3's Q1).** The high tier's floor drops to 3.5 MB (3584 KB, fourteen 256 KB probes), standard keeps 3 MB, the per-synth check limit rises from 80 to 88 KB, and eight recordings at once must leave 3 MB on both tiers (3.3's rule). Measured: merged 3.2's high worst case leaves 4130-4150 KB free, late allocations counted; 3.3 adds about 30 KB (45 KB with one recording) and 3.4 about 92 KB, so high comes to about 3990-4030 KB, 405-445 KB above the new floor, and standard to about 4.78-4.82 MB. `\strataGrainBuf` reaches 79.8-80.6 KB with 3.3 and 3.4, inside the new limit. Since 2d, high fits two 3.5 notes, so the lower floor costs 3.5 nothing more (§3.6).
4. **Macros write absolute values.** Turning a macro sets each target to its own point between min and max, like Ableton's or Bitwig's macros, and the screen shows the values. The alternative, macros as a modulation source around the stored values, never changes a param, so the screen would not show what plays. **Recommended: absolute** (§4).
5. **Macro targets are stored outside the param system** (by param id, in a per-PSET side file and in scenes), because an option param's index would move whenever the target list grows. **Recommended: yes** (§4.3).
6. **Morph switches discrete keys with a small hysteresis around 0.5** (B from 0.52 up, back to A from 0.48 down), so a noisy CC or an arc resting on 0.5 can't flip sources and voices on and off. The roadmap says "at 0.5". **Recommended: hysteresis of 0.02** (§5.2).
7. **Morph starts and stops voices and switches sources at the crossing**, as a transition's end would. The alternative leaves voice states and sources alone and morphs sound values only. **Recommended: switch them**, with the hysteresis above.
8. **Morph applies at most 10 times a second**, coalescing faster moves, so a fast turn never sends more than a 2 s transition already does (§5.3). **Recommended: 10 Hz.**
9. **Scatter reaches only the five engine destinations** and leaves locked voices alone (§6). A Lua scatter on any param would cost up to 7 voices × N params × 48 messages a second. **Recommended: engine destinations only, locked voices excluded.**
10. **Gesture recorders record control params only, as absolute values**, and "quantized" means the loop's length rounds to whole bars (or beats) and each loop starts on one; single events are not snapped to a grid, which would collapse a fader move into steps. Recorders default to free time. **Recommended: as written** (§7).
11. **Grid step keys toggle on release instead of press**, so a held step can be edited (locks, probability) without first toggling it. A tap still toggles, about a tap's length later than today. **Recommended: yes** (§8.7).
12. **SEQ's ALT+K2 opens the step edit view** instead of clearing the pattern (clearing moves to a row of that view and stays on the 256's row 15). **Recommended: yes**; the alternative is a third hold gesture on K2 or K3, which collides with 3.1's long-press fallback (§8.6).
13. **Step notes are relative scale degrees, applied like a MIDI note** (a synth voice's `freq`, a buffer voice's rate, an input voice's grain pitch) and only while their step plays. **Recommended: yes**; 3.5 maps them onto its notes (§8.4).
14. **Step locks are temporary** (Elektron-style): a locked value plays for its step and the voice returns to its stored value at the next step without that lock. The stored param never changes. **Recommended: yes** (§8.3).
15. **Scenes are numbered 1-16**, and a bank is only a view of eight. The alternative labels them A1-A8 and B1-B8. **Recommended: numbers** (§9).
16. **The MOD page goes after LFO**: MAIN, VOICE, SEQ, FX, LFO, MOD, BUFFER, SCENES, SETUP. BUFFER, SCENES and SETUP move one E1 step further. **Recommended: after LFO**, beside the other modulation page.
17. **Lua LFOs step on one free-running 48 Hz clock** instead of 48 steps per beat, with at most 12 engine messages per step (576 a second for all six), tempo or not (§2.5). A 10 Hz LFO then gets 4.8 steps per cycle, where today it gets 9.6 at 120 BPM. **Recommended: yes**: the fast destinations no longer depend on it.
18. **Engine LFOs top out at 50 Hz** (15 control blocks per cycle). **Recommended: 50 Hz.**
19. **Pattern chaining plays the patterns of scenes `start .. start + length - 1`, one link every N bars**, patterns only, never the sound (§8.8). The alternative is an explicit chain list with its own editor. **Recommended: scene ranges.**
20. **Beat-quantized actions** cover scene recalls from keys, grid and arc, freeze, bookmark recalls and pattern start, default off (§8.9). **Recommended: as written.**
21. **The 256's page 1 rows 9-13** become the gesture recorders (row 9, columns 1-4) and macro faders 1-4 (rows 10-13); the morph fader is on page 3. Row 8 columns 9-12, the first choice, clash with 3.3's page-5 key (row 8 column 12). The roadmap had row 9 as "selected voice level", which 3.1 never built. **Recommended: as written** (§11).
22. **Euclidean fill is destructive**: it writes the voice's steps once, and the steps can then be edited by hand. The alternative, a live generator, would fight hand edits. **Recommended: destructive fill** (§8.5).
23. **The overall message rate.** Each source is bounded, but they add up: morph is about 270 values × 10 Hz = 2700 messages a second, and a gesture recorder looping the morph keeps that up indefinitely; Lua's LFOs add up to 576, four recorders looping macros about 960 (4 × 8 targets × 30 moves a second) and the sequencer up to 350. Every message passes through sclang, and heavy sclang allocation already trips sclang 3.13's garbage-collector counter on norns (#131). **Recommended: (a) at most 120 values per morph apply (1200 a second), the plan's entries taken round robin; (b) one budget for every deferrable sender (Lua LFOs, morph, gesture playback, LFOs on macros) of 1500 messages a second, counted per 100 ms window, a deferred send keeping only its newest value and going first in the next window, while hand edits, steps, notes, locks and recalls are never deferred (§0.1, §2.5, §5.3); (c) a one-hour soak test on a CM3 in 3b with every source running, watching sclang's CPU, xruns and #131.**
24. **Step notes on input voices.** MIDI notes don't change an input voice's pitch today (`lib/midi_in.lua:121-136`), so applying a step note to an input voice's grain pitch is new behavior, and it meets a `pitch` lock in the same step. **Recommended: input voices take step notes on their grain pitch, multiplied onto the step's `pitch` lock when it has one (the lock sets the base, the note transposes it); MIDI notes on input voices stay as they are until 3.5.** Related: the roadmap's quantized "position jumps" are covered only by quantizing bookmark recalls; **recommended: also quantize 3.3's position-page jumps** (§8.9).
25. **Bookmark locks and loop regions.** 3.3's `S.lib.bookmarks.recall(v, n)` resets the voice's loop params when the bookmark lies outside the loop: a lasting change, against Question 14's temporary locks. Options: (a) a step's bookmark lock does nothing when the bookmark lies outside the voice's current loop (checked from the bookmark and loop params before the call); (b) let it reset the loop; (c) ask 3.3 for a recall that never touches the loop. **Recommended: (a)**: the lock stays temporary and 3.3's interface stays exactly `recall` and `is_set` (§8.3).

## Intent

3.4 makes Strata's sound move on its own and its sequencer deep enough to carry a piece:
- **Six LFOs** of Strata's own, with smooth random, sample-and-hold and drunk shapes, and LFOs that modulate other LFOs' rate and depth.
- **Fast modulation in the engine** for grain pitch, position, size, the voice filter's cutoff and level, so LFOs above 10 Hz cost no messages.
- **Scatter**: slow random movement on every voice with one switch.
- **Four macros**, each driving up to eight targets with their own ranges.
- **Scene morph** between two scenes, writing params so the result can be saved.
- **Four gesture recorders** that loop encoder, arc and grid fader moves.
- **A deeper step sequencer**: per-voice length and division, probability, ratchets, step locks, Euclidean fill, notes per step, chaining and beat-quantized actions.
- **16 scenes in two banks, with names.**
- **A new MOD page** that holds the macros, the morph, the recorders, scatter and an overview of the LFOs.

Everything defaults to 3.3's sound. A scene saved in 3.3 recalls the same voices, values and patterns.

## Goals

0. A restructure that changes no behavior: the Lua hooks later packages need (registries, a base-value override layer, a "param touched" hook, PSET hooks, a modal screen owner), the scene format's registries, and the engine area's four commands as stubs.
1. Engine-side fast modulation: five destinations per voice on one five-channel control bus each, read by every grain synth, written by engine LFOs and scatter, adding offsets to the values Lua sends. At most about 90 KB of the real-time pool at the worst case, no `LocalBuf`, no delay line or table.
2. Six LFOs (`NUM_LFOS` 6) of Strata's own: eight shapes, free or clocked rates, LFO-to-LFO rate and depth modulation without loops, run in Lua up to 10 Hz or in the engine up to 50 Hz by destination.
3. Lua's LFO traffic is bounded: at most 576 engine messages a second for all six, whatever the tempo, and none for the engine-run ones beyond their setting changes.
4. Four macros (`NUM_MACROS` 4), each with up to eight targets (`MACRO_TARGETS` 8) and a min and max per target, edited on MOD; their values are params (CC, LFOs, PSETs).
5. A morph param between two chosen scenes, writing params, never recalling.
6. Scatter: engine-side slow random modulation of chosen destinations on every unlocked voice, one switch.
7. Four gesture recorders (`NUM_GESTURES` 4) built on norns' `lib/pattern_time`, free or bar-quantized, stored per PSET; scenes store only whether each runs.
8. The sequencer additions of the roadmap, each step still a `retrigger` that creates no nodes.
9. 16 scenes (`NUM_SCENES` 16) in two banks of eight (`SCENE_BANK_SIZE` 8), with names edited through norns' `textentry`; older files load into slots 1-8.
10. The MOD page, and the SEQ, LFO and SCENES pages, grid layouts and arc rings for all of the above, within the 128×64 rules.

## Non-goals

- Polyphony and note allocation (3.5). Step notes are stored as lists so 3.5 can play them as chords; 3.4 plays the first note.
- MIDI out of LFOs and macros, crow, transport start/stop (3.6).
- LFOs on LFO params through the destination list (LFO-to-LFO goes through the dedicated rate and depth inputs only, §2.4).
- An LFO, macro or morph on the morph position. Morph can be moved by hand, the grid, the arc, a MIDI CC and a gesture recorder.
- Elektron trig conditions (fill, A:B ratios), step slides, per-step micro-timing.
- Modulating discrete params (sources, envelope shapes, filter types) from LFOs, macros or scatter.

## Anti-goals

- Breaking a CM3. Everything here works on the standard tier; the engine part is control-rate only.
- Old scenes and PSETs that don't load or sound different. Every new scene field gets a default that reproduces 3.3: LFOs 4-6 off, no macro targets, scatter off, recorders stopped, every voice at 16 steps of 1/16, every step at 100% with one hit and no locks or notes, names empty. Old PSETs' LFO settings are imported.
- Messages that grow with the number of modulators. The fast destinations cost none per step; Lua's LFOs have a hard per-step cap.
- Creating nodes from the sequencer. A step, a ratchet, a lock, a note, a chain link and a probability draw are messages to existing nodes, or nothing.
- Hiding state. Engine-run LFOs, scatter and running recorders show on MOD and LFO, and on MAIN for the selected voice.
- Feedback loops in modulation: no LFO modulates itself or a lower-numbered LFO, no macro targets a macro or the morph.

## Constraints

**Platform:**
- **scsynth's real-time pool is 8192 KB** (roadmap, Constraints). Control buses are not in it: scsynth allocates them at boot (`numControlBusChannels`, 16384 by default; norns' `Crone.sc` leaves the server options at their defaults, `sc/core/Crone.sc:61`). A synth's graph is in it: about 250 bytes per UGen (`docs/notes/engine/memory.md`).
- **Control buses sum within a block.** scsynth's `Out.kr` copies into a control bus for the first writer of a block and adds for every later writer in the same block (the bus's "touched" stamp); a bus nobody writes in a block keeps its last value. So several writers on one channel add, and a channel whose last writer is gone must be set to 0 by hand (`/c_set`).
- **matron polls carry one float**, and every `engine.*` command passes through sclang to scsynth. Each costs sclang more than a control-rate UGen costs scsynth, which is the case for moving fast modulation into the engine.
- **norns' clock**: `clock.sync(beats, offset)` resumes at the next multiple of `beats` plus `offset` (`lua/core/clock.lua:53-54`); `clock.get_beats()` is the transport's beat position. Clock coroutines are limited to 100 slots (the test stub mirrors it).
- **norns' `lib/pattern_time`** records `event` and `time` arrays with `util.time()` deltas and plays them with one metro per pattern (`lua/lib/pattern_time.lua:13-30`, `:113-137`). norns gives a script 30 metros (`lua/core/metro.lua:13`); Strata uses two today (drift, mutation).
- **norns' `lib/er`** gives Euclidean patterns, `er.gen(k, n, w)` (`lua/lib/er.lua`).
- **norns' `lib/textentry`** replaces `key`, `enc` and `redraw` while open and hands back the text, or nil (`lua/lib/textentry.lua:41-90`), as `fileselect` does.
- **PSET hooks**: `params.action_write(filename, name, number)`, `action_read(filename, silent, number)` and `action_delete(filename, name, number)` run after norns writes, reads or deletes a PSET (`lua/core/paramset.lua:518-519`, `:572-573`, `:592-593`). A PSET is `"<id>": <value>` lines, and `params:read` skips ids it doesn't know.
- **norns' LFO library's ceiling** is a 0.1 s period (10 Hz), and it calls its action on every step at its `ppqn`, in free mode too (a free LFO's phase advances by `1/ppqn` of a beat per step, converted through the beat's length), so its step rate follows the tempo. Strata sets 48 steps per beat (`lib/lfos.lua:43`) and skips sends under 1/1000 of the range (`lib/lfos.lua:46`). Its phase is readable (`get("phase_counter")`) and `change_period` keeps it continuous.

**Project:**
- Everything in CLAUDE.md, in particular: Lua as the only source of values, `send_param_to_engine` and `S.engine_senders`, LFOs never `params:set` their destination, replace-never-stack and `sendToSynth`, `openedGate`, the velocity invariant (steps and notes never create nodes), no `LocalBuf`, the command table, the registries, one owner per file per wave, the locals probe (30 free locals per constructor), `request_redraw()`, arc rings through `arc_sweep_param` and `param_fraction`, grid handlers on the vport only.
- The real-time memory floors, as decided on 2026-11-08 for 3.3 and 3.4 (Question 3): 3 MB free on standard and 3.5 MB (3584 KB) on high at the worst case, at most 88 KB per grain synth, and 3 MB left on both tiers with eight recordings at once. Measured (2d final, ef09396): the merged 3.2 high worst case leaves 4130-4150 KB free counting late allocations; `\strataGrainRing` is 69.1 KB, `\strataGrainBuf` 74.2-75.0 KB.

**Dependency on 3.3** (designed in parallel; the names below are fixed when the 3.3 design is approved):
- **Position bookmarks.** A step lock can recall one of a voice's four bookmarks. 3.3 adds exactly `S.lib.bookmarks.recall(voice_idx, n)` (n 1..`NUM_BOOKMARKS`, 4) and `S.lib.bookmarks.is_set(voice_idx, n)` (a valid n and a voice whose slot holds audio), on top of its `S.lib.positions` (`lib/positions.lua`). A recall outside the voice's loop resets the loop params (Question 25). Nothing else here depends on bookmarks.
- **The scene format.** 3.3's `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` is 4 (its bookmarks), so 3.4's is 5, and 3.4's migrations run after 3.3's.
- **Engine areas.** 3.3 adds record (130), waveform (140) and positions (150), so 3.4's modulation area is 160.
- **Grid.** 3.3 adds the 256's page 5 on row 8 column 12, the 128h's page 5 through its sources key and a bookmark strip on its page 4 (row 5 columns 13-16, row 6 columns 13-15), and five pages on the 128v. 3.4's keys (§11) avoid all of them.
- **The memory floor** was decided once for both releases (Question 3).
- **Order of work.** 3.4's Wave 0 starts once 3.3's last package that edits `lib/scenes.lua`, `lib/engine/StrataGrainDefs.sc` or a grid layout has merged.

## Approaches

### The LFOs

- **A. Keep norns' LFO library and add an engine LFO kind beside it (rejected).** Two LFO systems with two parameter sets, and the library still lacks the new shapes and LFO-to-LFO modulation.
- **B. Keep the library and work around it (rejected).** Run every LFO with the `up` shape, so its raw value is its phase, and compute the shapes in Strata; modulate rates by calling `set("period")` on every step (`change_period` keeps the phase continuous, and `get("phase_counter")` reads it). It keeps the PSET ids, but the shapes and engine rates live beside the library's own params, and free LFOs still step with the tempo.
- **C. Strata's own LFOs (chosen; Question 1).** One Lua clock steps all six at a fixed 48 Hz, so free LFOs no longer step with the tempo; clocked LFOs take their phase from `clock.get_beats()`. The new shapes are Strata's, the offset needs no wrapper, and the same parameter set drives a Lua-run and an engine-run LFO. The cost is new PSET ids, handled by an import (§2.9).

### Fast modulation in the engine

- **A. Per-voice control buses that every grain synth reads (chosen).** Each voice has five control channels, one per destination. Grain synths read them with one `In.kr` and add the offsets to their own controls. Engine LFOs and scatter write them; several writers on a channel add up. Lua keeps sending the base values to the grain synth's controls exactly as now, so Lua stays the owner: with nothing written, the bus is 0 and the grain synth plays Lua's value. About 10 control-rate UGens per grain synth.
- **B. Map the grain controls to buses (`/n_map`) (rejected).** No UGens in the grain synths, but the base value would then have to live on the bus, written by a summing synth per modulated destination, and every grain synth created by a switch or a restart would need its maps. Lua's `setGrainParam` on a mapped control would no longer reach it, so Lua would stop owning the value.
- **C. Faster Lua (rejected).** Five destinations on seven voices at 200 Hz would be 7000 messages a second through sclang.

### Engine LFOs modulated by other LFOs

- **Lua computes every LFO-to-LFO effect (chosen).** Lua runs the phase and value of all six LFOs, engine-run or not. When an LFO modulates an engine-run LFO, Lua sends the target's new rate or depth (deduped, inside the message cap). Rate and depth modulation is slow by nature, so Lua's 48 Hz is enough, and the engine needs no LFO-to-LFO routing.
- **Engine-side routing (rejected).** Each engine LFO would also write its raw value to a bus and read its modulators' buses: a second command and graph per LFO, and the Lua-run case would still need Lua's version.

### Macros

- **Absolute writes (chosen; Question 4).** A macro at value v sets each target to `min + v × (max − min)` of its normalized range. The screen shows the values, scenes and PSETs capture them, and gesture recorders can loop the macro.
- **A modulation source (rejected).** The macro would add offsets around the targets' stored values without writing them, like an LFO. Nothing on screen would show the result.

### Morph

- **Write the interpolated params (chosen; the roadmap).** The result is the params, so it can be saved as a scene, and it works for every field a transition can fade.
- **Recall A or B at 0.5 (rejected).** No interpolation.
- **Crossfade two running sound states in the engine (rejected).** Two scenes can use different sources and voice types; the engine would need two of everything.

### Gesture recorders

- **`lib/pattern_time` for recording and free playback, a clock coroutine for quantized playback (chosen).** pattern_time records and overdubs as the roadmap asks, and plays free loops with its own metro. A quantized loop is converted to beats when its recording stops and played by a clock coroutine that starts each loop on its bar, so it stays locked to Link or MIDI clock where a metro would drift.
- **pattern_time only (rejected).** Its metro can't follow the clock.
- **A recorder of Strata's own (rejected).** It would redo what pattern_time does, against the roadmap.

### The sequencer clock

- **One coroutine per voice (chosen).** Each voice wakes only at its own steps (`clock.sync(division, offset)`), so a voice at 1/2 notes costs nothing between them, and ratchets are sub-syncs inside its step. Seven coroutines and the transport's are well inside the 100 slots.
- **One master clock at a common resolution (rejected).** Triplets, ratchets of 3 and 4, and 1/32 steps need 96 ticks per beat: 192 wake-ups a second at 120 BPM, mostly doing nothing.

### Pattern chaining

- **Chain the patterns stored in scenes (chosen; Question 19).** Scenes already store patterns (roadmap: "per-scene patterns, already stored"). A chain plays scene `start`'s patterns, then the next scene's, each for N bars, without touching the sound.
- **Separate pattern banks (rejected).** A second store for patterns, beside the scenes that already hold them.

### Scene banks

- **Numbers 1-16, banks as views (chosen; Question 15).** Every param that names a scene (`morph_a`, `scene_sequence_length`, the chain) is a plain number, and the header shows `S12`.
- **A1-B8 (rejected).** Two-part labels in every param and the header, for no gain.

## Design

### 0. Restructure first (Wave 0)

**Rule:** after Wave 0, a 3.4 feature lives in files only its package edits, apart from one-line registrations. Wave 0 changes no behavior: every test passes unchanged, and every SynthDef but the two grain defs compiles to the same bytes (those two gain one control, §0.3).

#### 0.1 Lua hooks (package 0a)

New registries in `lib/state.lua`, each with a register function that checks its argument and refuses a duplicate name, read at init (never at include time), like 3.2's:

| Registry | Register function | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `S.registry.scene_blocks` | `S.register_scene_block({key, default = fn(), capture = fn(), validate = fn(value) -> ok, apply = fn(value), discrete = true})` | `lib/scenes.lua`: a top-level scene block (macro targets, step data) |
| `S.registry.scene_migrations` | `S.register_scene_migration({name, before_version, migrate = fn(scene)})` | `lib/scenes.lua`: runs `migrate` on every loaded scene older than `before_version`, by `before_version` then registration |
| `S.registry.pset_hooks` | `S.register_pset_hook({name, write = fn(path_base, number), read = fn(path_base, number, pset_path), delete = fn(path_base, number)})` | `lib/params_setup.lua`, which installs `params.action_write`, `action_read` and `action_delete` once and calls every hook; `path_base` is the PSET's path without `.pset` |
| `S.registry.param_touch` | `S.register_param_touch(fn(param_id, source))` | `lib/ui/detent.lua` (`enc_param_delta`, `delta_with_detent`), `lib/arc/core.lua` (`arc_sweep_param`), `lib/grid/faders.lua` (`set_param_from_column`): after each user edit, `source` is `"enc"`, `"arc"` or `"grid"` |
| `S.registry.lfo_destinations` (extended) | `S.register_lfo_destination(label, suffix)` as now, plus `S.register_lfo_global_destination(label, param_id)` | `lib/lfos.lua`, from 1b on (0a adds only the registry and its register function): a global entry appends one destination, in registration order with the per-voice ones |
| `S.registry.lock_change` | `S.register_lock_change(fn(voice_idx, locked))` | run by `toggle_lock` (`lib/engine_sync.lua`, 0a) and by a scene recall's lock states (`lib/scenes.lua`, 0b) |

**The base override and extra offsets** (`lib/engine_sync.lua`), for step locks and LFOs on macros:
- `S.lib.engine_sync.set_base_override(param_id, value)` and `clear_base_override(param_id)`: while an override is set, `with_lfo_offsets` and `apply_lfo` start from it instead of `params:get(param_id)`, and setting or clearing it sends at once. Only a hand edit of that param clears it (the `param_touch` hook, below): drift (`lib/mutation.lua:176`, `:182` set `grain_pitch` and `buf_pos` ten times a second), morph, macros and gesture playback all run param actions, and none of them may end a lock. While an override is set, a param action's send is held under it: the stored value changes, and the engine keeps the lock until the step ends.
- **Deferrable sends** (Question 23): `send_engine_value(id, value)` counts every send. While `S.deferrable_write` is set (Lua LFOs, morph, gesture playback and LFOs on macros set it around their writes, as the drift sets `S.drift_writing`), a send beyond the budget (`ENGINE_BUDGET.messages_per_window`, 150 per 100 ms window) is kept as the id's pending value and goes first in the next window. Other sends always go at once and count against the window.
- `S.lib.engine_sync.set_extra_offset(source_key, param_id, offset)` (nil clears): offsets in the param's units, summed into `with_lfo_offsets` with the LFOs' and clamped with them.

### 2. The LFOs

#### 2.1 Parameters

`lib/lfos.lua` is rewritten (package 1b): norns' `lib/lfo.lua` is no longer used. Each LFO n (1-6) has a group `strata_lfo_<n>` ("LFO n", 13 params; groups can't nest, so the six groups replace today's `strata_lfos` group and the library's `lfo_group_<n>`):

| Param | Kind, range | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `strata_lfo<n>` | binary toggle | 0 (off) | on/off |

| `strata_lfo<n>_shape` | option: sine, tri, square, up, down, s&h, smooth, drunk | 1 (sine) | §2.2 |
| `strata_lfo<n>_mode` | option: free, clocked | 1 (free) | |
| `strata_lfo<n>_rate` | control 0.003-50 Hz, exp | 0.25 Hz (a 4 s period, today's default) | free mode; Lua-run LFOs play at most 10 Hz (§2.5). The minimum takes the library's longest free period (300 s, `lfo_free_<n>`) exactly |
| `strata_lfo<n>_division` | option: the library's 22 clocked rates, `1/16` ... `1024` | 13 (`4`) | clocked mode; a rate r is a period of 4r beats, as the library's |
| `strata_lfo<n>_depth` | control 0-100 %, step 1 | 25 % | 3.1's default |

| `strata_lfo<n>_depth_mod` | option: none, L1 ... L(n−1) | 1 (none) | §2.4 |
| `strata_lfo<n>_depth_mod_amt` | control -100..100 % | 0 | §2.4 |

LFO 1's mod options are only `none`. The library's min, max, baseline and reset params are gone: Strata's LFOs always run from -1 to 1 around the stored value, which is what `docs/user/params.md` told users to keep.

#### 2.2 Shapes

Every shape is a function of the phase φ (0-1) giving u in 0..1; the LFO's output is `m = (2u − 1) × depth + 2 × offset` in half-range units (−1..1 is ± half the destination's range), so depth 100 % sweeps the whole range, as today.

| Shape | u(φ) |
|---|---|
| sine | (sin 2πφ + 1) / 2 |
| tri | 2φ for φ < ½, 2 − 2φ after |
| square | 1 for φ < ½, else 0 |
| up / down | φ / 1 − φ |
| s&h | a new uniform value at each cycle start, held |
| smooth | linear from the previous s&h value to the next over the cycle |
| drunk | a random walk: at each cycle start, the last value plus a uniform step of ±0.2, reflected at 0 and 1; linear between, like smooth |

The library's `random` changed value twice per cycle; scenes and PSETs that used it become s&h at twice the rate (§2.9).

#### 2.3 Destinations

The destination list keeps its order: the 68 built-in entries (`lib/lfos.lua:55-87`), then the registered ones in registration order. 3.2 registers `v<i> hist`, `vfilt`, `dsend` and `rsend`. 3.4 appends:
- `v<i> amp` (registered by `lib/lfos.lua`, suffix `amp`): the voice's level, run in the engine (§3.3);
- `macro 1` ... `macro 4` (global entries, registered by `lib/macros.lua`, **(new)**): an LFO on a macro (§4.4).

**Five destinations run in the engine** (Question 2): `v<i> pitch` (`grain_pitch`), `v<i> pos` (`buf_pos`), `v<i> size` (`grain_size`), `v<i> vfilt` (3.2's `filter_freq`) and `v<i> amp`. The LFO page marks them with `~`. Every other destination, `v<i> main` included (it can resolve to any param), runs in Lua as now. `S.lib.lfos.FAST_DESTINATIONS` maps each param suffix to its engine destination index (0 pitch, 1 position, 2 size, 3 cutoff, 4 amp).

The same math applies on both sides, so an old scene's LFO on `v3 pitch` sounds as it did, only smoother: the engine adds `m` × half the range to the stored value and clamps, which is what Lua's `with_lfo_offsets` does. Two exceptions, both on destinations 3.2 added and no released scene uses: the voice filter cutoff moves in its exponential warp (±half its ten octaves at 100 %), and amp moves the level by ±depth (§3.3).

#### 2.4 LFO-to-LFO modulation

Each LFO has a rate input and a depth input, each reading one lower-numbered LFO. LFO n's sources are 1..n−1, so the graph is acyclic (no LFO modulates itself or a lower-numbered one, Anti-goals) and Lua evaluates the six in index order on every step. The source's value is its bipolar shape value s = 2u − 1, before its own depth and destination, so a modulator needs no destination (`none` is the usual choice). A source that is off contributes 0.
- **Rate:** `rate' = rate × 4 ^ (amount × s)`, so ±2 octaves at 100 %, clamped to 0.003 Hz and the run's ceiling. In clocked mode the division's rate is multiplied the same way.
- **Depth:** `depth' = clamp(depth × (1 + amount × s), 0, 100 %)`, so depth 0 stays 0.

For an engine-run target, Lua sends `setFastLfo` with the new rate when it moves by more than 0.5 % (`LFO.engine_rate_threshold`) and with the new depth when it moves by more than 1/1000. These sends count against the per-step cap.

#### 2.5 The Lua clock and the message budget

- One clock coroutine (`S.lfo_clock`) wakes every 1/48 s (`clock.sleep`), in free time, and steps every running LFO in index order. The phase of a free LFO advances by `rate' × elapsed` (elapsed from `util.time()`, so a late wake-up doesn't slow it). A clocked LFO's phase is `(clock.get_beats() / period_beats + phase) mod 1`, exact against the clock (Link and MIDI clock included) with no drift. The phase is known exactly, so the LFO page draws its dot from it.
- A Lua-run LFO above 10 Hz plays at 10 Hz; the LFO page shows its rate followed by `max`.
- **Sends:** each Lua-run LFO's destination is sent through `send_engine_value` as now, skipped under 1/1000 of the range (`LFO.send_threshold`). At most 12 sends go out per step (`LFO.sends_per_step`): the destinations due are queued in a fixed round-robin order, so none starves and a skipped one goes out on the next step with its newest value. So Lua's LFOs never send more than 576 messages a second together (today: 3 × 96 = 288 at 120 BPM, 720 at 300 BPM), and an LFO on a macro (up to eight targets) can't flood sclang. Their sends are also deferrable under the overall budget (§0.1, Question 23).
- Engine-run LFOs send nothing per step, only their setting changes (§3.4).

#### 2.6 Engine-run LFOs, from Lua

- An LFO runs in the engine while it is on and its destination is one of the five. Lua sends `setFastLfo(n − 1, voice − 1, destination, shape − 1, rate', depth' / 100, 2 × offset / 100)` when it starts and whenever a setting or a modulated value changes, and `stopFastLfo(n − 1)` when it stops or its destination stops being an engine one.
- **Phase.** After a start or a shape change Lua sends `syncFastLfo(n − 1, φ)` with its own phase. A clocked LFO is also resynced once per bar (every 4 beats, from Lua's clock), so it follows the transport; a resync moves the phase by the drift since the last one plus the message's latency (under a millisecond on norns). Free LFOs are not resynced, since a resync is an audible jump at high rates: Lua's and the engine's clocks drift by tens of parts per million (tens to hundreds of milliseconds an hour), and more under rate modulation, where Lua integrates a rate it sends only in 0.5 % steps. So for a free engine-run LFO the LFO page's dot is an estimate, drawn hollow (a 3×3 outline instead of the filled dot); the sound never depends on it.
- **Random shapes** draw their own values in the engine; the page's wave and dot show Lua's draws, statistically the same, not the same values.
- A destination change between two engine destinations is one `setFastLfo` (the engine moves the node's bus and zeroes the old channel, §3.4).

#### 2.7 The rules that stay

- LFOs modulate around the stored value and never `params:set` their destination.
- A destination change, turning an LFO off and depth 0 restore the destination: Lua-run, `restore_lfo_destination` as now; engine-run, `stopFastLfo`, whose release ends at 0.
- Every destination is a control param and every label fits the LFO page's right column (49 px; `v7 amp~` is the longest new one).
- Cleanup (step 120) stops the clock, sends `stopFastLfo` for every engine-run LFO and restores every Lua-run destination.

#### 2.8 What the scenes store

Per LFO, in recall order (`S.lib.lfos.SCENE_FIELDS`): shape, mode, division, rate, depth, offset, phase, the four mod params, destination, then the state last, as now. Rate, depth and offset crossfade (today's free period, depth and offset did); the rest switch at a transition's end. The phase and the four mod params are new scene fields (scenes store no LFO phase today, `lib/scenes.lua:88-97`), migrating to 0 and none; LFOs 4-6 migrate to off.

#### 2.9 Old PSETs and scenes

- **PSETs.** A PSET hook (§0.1) reads the PSET file after norns has read it. If it holds `lfo_shape_1` and no `strata_lfo1_shape`, it converts each LFO n = 1..3: `lfo_<n>` (1 off, 2 on) to `strata_lfo<n>`; `lfo_shape_<n>` (sine, tri, square, random, up, down) to sine, tri, square, s&h (rate doubled), up, down; `lfo_mode_<n>` (1 clocked, 2 free) to free or clocked; `lfo_clocked_<n>` to the same division index; `lfo_free_<n>` (a period in seconds, 0.1-300) to `rate = 1 / period` (within 0.003-10 Hz, so no import clamps); `lfo_depth_<n>`, `lfo_offset_<n>` and `lfo_phase_<n>` to the same percentages. `strata_lfo<n>_dest` already loaded. It prints `imported 3.3 LFO settings`. The PSET is not rewritten; the next save writes the new ids.
- **Scenes.** A registered migration (`before_version` 5) converts the `lfo_*` globals the same way (no phase: scenes never stored one) and drops them.

### 3. Fast modulation in the engine

#### 3.1 What it does

Five destinations per voice get an engine-side offset: grain pitch (the grain rate, `pitchShift`), position (`bufPos`, buffer voices only, as `buf_pos` today), grain size (`grainSize`), the voice filter's cutoff (`filterFreq`, audible while its mix is above 0) and amp (the grain synth's output level). The offsets come from engine LFOs (§2.6) and scatter (§6). Lua keeps sending every base value to the grain synth's controls as now; the engine adds the offsets inside the grain synth. With no LFO and no scatter the buses read 0 and the grain synth plays exactly what Lua sent.

#### 3.2 Buses and groups

In the modulation area's `allocState`, in `areaState[\modulation]`:
- `fastModBus`: one control bus of 35 channels (`numVoices × numFastDestinations`). Voice v's destination d is channel `index + 5v + d`. Contiguous, so scatter writes all 35 with one `Out.kr`.
- `silentFastModBus`: 5 channels, always 0. It is the SynthDef default of `fastModIn`, never control bus 0 (norns' input follower), and never `silentBus` (one channel).
- `fastModGroup`: a group at the head of `context.xg`, before `voiceGroup`, so offsets are written before any grain synth reads them in the same block. `docs/notes/engine/structure.md`'s node order and the structure check gain it.
- `grainControls[v][\fastModIn]` = voice v's first channel, set for every voice at alloc, so every grain synth, replacements and restarts included, reads its voice's bus.

No real-time memory: control buses are allocated at server boot.

#### 3.3 In the grain synths

With `m = this.fastModRead(fastModIn)` (one `In.kr` of 5 channels, read once per SynthDef function): position is applied in `\strataGrainBuf`'s function itself, to `bufPos` before `slotGrainReader` (`StrataGrainDefs.sc:150`) and 3.3's `slotScanPhase` and `slotHeadGuard` see it, because `bufPos` goes straight to the reader and is not in the controls Event (`:136-148`). `m` then goes into the Event, and `this.fastMod(controls)` (`StrataModulation.sc`) replaces the other four controls before `grainRate`, `grainDur`, the readers and `voiceFilter` see them. `\strataGrainRing` reads the same five channels and ignores position.

| Destination | Applied as | Same as |
|---|---|---|
| 0 pitch | `pitchShift' = (pitchShift + 1.875 × m0).clip(0.25, 4)` | a Lua LFO on `grain_pitch` (0.25-4, linear) |
| 1 position | `bufPos' = (bufPos + 0.5 × m1).clip(0, 1)` (`\strataGrainBuf` only) | a Lua LFO on `buf_pos` |
| 2 size | `grainSize' = (grainSize + 0.245 × m2).clip(0.01, 0.5)` | a Lua LFO on `grain_size` |
| 3 cutoff | `filterFreq' = (filterFreq × 31.62 ^ m3).clip(20, 20000)` | ± half of 20-20000 Hz's ten octaves |
| 4 amp | `(1 + modSig + m4)` in place of `(1 + modSig)` (`StrataGrainDefs.sc:217`, inside the existing `.clip(0, 2)`; `modSig` is computed at `:188`) | the amplitude crossmod's law: depth 100 % moves the level from silence to double |

- The half ranges are the Lua specs' (`lib/params_setup.lua:734`, `:764`, `:686`; 3.2's `filter_freq`). The engine check compares them with the Lua specs, as it compares the FX defaults today.
- Amp works for every source: for the synth source it scales the grain synth's output, after the voice synth. It is not gated by `modLevel`, which only gates crossmod.
- The rules that stay: the ring reader still clips the rate to ±4 and `grainDur` still caps at 0.5 s, so the lookback bound of `docs/notes/engine/rings.md` holds whatever the offset; `velocity` still feeds only the accent's `levelScale`, so the velocity check passes unchanged.

#### 3.4 Engine LFOs, scatter and the commands

**SynthDefs** (in `StrataModulation.sc`, built by one function):
- `\strataFastLfoSine`, `...Tri`, `...Square`, `...Up`, `...Down`, `...Sah`, `...Smooth`, `...Drunk`: one SynthDef per shape (about 10 control-rate UGens each, against about 35 for one def selecting among eight). Controls: `out` (one channel), `rate`, `depth`, `offset`, `t_sync`, `syncPhase`, `gate`. A `Phasor.kr` over 0-1 at `rate × ControlDur`, reset by `t_sync` to `syncPhase`; the shape of §2.2 from the phase (s&h, smooth and drunk latch a `WhiteNoise.kr` draw, or a `Dbrown` step through `Demand.kr`, on the phase's wrap); `depth` lagged 20 ms; an ASR envelope through `openedGate(gate)` with 20 ms attack and release, `doneAction: 2`; `Out.kr(out, ((2u − 1) × depth + offset) × env)`.
- `\strataScatter`: 35 `LFDNoise3.kr` at `rate × (0.8 … 1.2)` (a fixed spread per channel, drawn at build, so voices and destinations never move together), each times a gain from a 35-element control (`gains`: depth × the destination's and the voice's enable bits), through `openedGate(gate)` with 0.5 s attack and release, into `Out.kr(fastModBus index, 35 channels)`.

**State and lifecycle:**
- At most one node per LFO (`areaState[\modulation][\lfos][n]`) and one scatter node, all in `fastModGroup`. A shape change replaces the LFO's node with `Synth.replace` (the old node goes in the same server step), as 3.2's spectral writer swap does; nothing else creates a node.
- They can end on their own (their release), so every message to them goes through `sendToSynth`. When an LFO's node ends (`onFree`) the engine sets its channel to 0 (`/c_set`) unless another node of the same LFO now writes it; other writers on that channel write it again in the next block, so the sum stays right. Scatter's end zeroes all 35 channels the same way, and every remaining LFO writes its own again in the next block.
- `stop` (the area's) frees every node; `free` frees the buses.

**Commands** (rows for CLAUDE.md's table, added by 0c):

| Lua Command | SC Signature | Purpose |
|-------------|--------------|---------|
| `engine.setFastLfo(lfo, voice, destination, shape, rate, depth, offset)` | `"iiiifff"` | Engine LFO 0-5 on voice 0-6's fast destination 0-4 (pitch, position, size, cutoff, amp): shape 0-7 (sine, tri, square, up, down, s&h, smooth, drunk), rate 0.003-50 Hz, depth 0-1, offset -2..2 (half ranges). Creates the LFO's node if it has none, else sets it; a new shape replaces the node, a new voice or destination moves it and zeroes the old channel. Never more than one node per LFO |
| `engine.stopFastLfo(lfo)` | `"i"` | Release LFO 0-5's node (20 ms) and zero its channel once it ends. No-op without one |
| `engine.syncFastLfo(lfo, phase)` | `"if"` | Set LFO 0-5's phase (0-1) now. No-op without a node |
| `engine.setScatter(depth, rate, destinations, voices)` | `"ffii"` | Scatter: depth 0-1 (0 releases the node over 0.5 s), rate 0.01-1 Hz, destinations a 5-bit mask (bit d = destination d), voices a 7-bit mask (bit v = voice v). Creates the node when depth rises above 0 |

Out-of-range indices are ignored with a console line, as the crossmod commands ignore an unknown slot; the Lua engine stub rejects them.

#### 3.5 How Lua stays the owner

- The grain synth's own controls hold Lua's values, set by `setGrainParam` and the voice sync exactly as now. The buses hold only offsets, which the engine never folds into those controls. Restarting a voice, switching its source or recalling a scene sends the base values as always; the offsets carry on from the bus.
- The engine keeps no base values of its own, so there is nothing for Lua to resync after a switch: a replacement grain synth reads the same bus.
- What Lua shows (the screen, the arc, the grid faders) is the stored value, as with Lua LFOs.

### 4. Macros

#### 4.1 Model

- Four macros (`NUM_MACROS` 4), each a param `macro_<m>` (control 0-1, default 0, shown in percent) in a group `strata_macros` ("macros", 4 params) after the LFO groups.
- Each macro has up to eight targets (`MACRO_TARGETS` 8): `S.macros[m].targets[k] = {id = <param id or "">, min = 0, max = 1}`, min and max being fractions of the target's normalized range (its controlspec's warp); min above max inverts.
- **Applying** (`S.lib.macros.apply(m)`): the param's action schedules it for the next clock tick (`clock.run` with `clock.sleep(0)`, coalescing repeated moves of one macro into one apply). norns' `params:read` runs every param's action before it calls `action_read`, so an immediate apply during a PSET load would use the old targets; the PSET hook (§4.3) cancels every pending apply, then loads the PSET's targets, and the targets' own values come from the PSET itself. Then, for each target with an id, `params:set(id, spec:map(min + v × (max − min)))`. So the targets' actions send to the engine, the screen shows the values and scenes capture them. A macro with no target does nothing.
- **Silent sets.** A scene recall, a transition and morph set `macro_<m>` without applying it (`S.lib.macros.set_silently(m, v)`, `params:set(id, v, true)`), because the scene already holds each target's value. Only a hand move (encoder, arc, grid fader), a MIDI CC, a PSET load and a gesture recorder apply.
- **Messages:** one per target and move: an encoder at 30 detents a second on a full macro is 240 a second, the rate of turning eight knobs.

#### 4.2 What a macro can target

`S.lib.macros.target_ids()`: every LFO destination (§2.3) except the macros themselves, plus each LFO's `strata_lfo<n>_rate` and `strata_lfo<n>_depth`. Never another macro, the morph params, a discrete param or a non-control param, so a macro can't start a loop or flip a source. `v<i> main` resolves when the macro applies, so it follows the voice's source.

#### 4.3 Storage

- **Values** are params: PSETs store them, MIDI CCs map to them through PARAMS > MAP, and scenes store them as continuous globals set silently.
- **Targets** are not params (Question 5): they are stored by param id.
  - In scenes as a block (`data.macros`, registered by `lib/macros.lua`: discrete, applied at a transition's end before the macro values).
  - Per PSET in `<pset path base>.macros.data` (a PSET hook writes, reads and deletes it).
  - Live in `data/strata/macros.data`, saved at cleanup and loaded at init like `scenes.data` (with its `pcall` and move-aside rules), so the macros survive a restart without a PSET.
- An unknown id (a param a later or earlier release doesn't have) loads as an empty target with a console line, never a failed file.

#### 4.4 What reaches a macro

- **MIDI CC** through PARAMS > MAP (it is a control param).
- **LFOs** (`macro <m>` destinations, Lua-run). An LFO never `params:set`s its destination, so it can't move the macro param. Instead it moves the macro's targets around their stored values: each step it computes the macro at `v + offset` and, per target, the difference from the target at `v`, which it sets as that target's extra offset (`set_extra_offset("macro<m>", id, ...)`, §0.1). The targets' engine values move; their params don't. This counts up to eight sends per step against the cap (§2.5).
- **Gesture recorders** record and replay macro moves (§7).
- **Morph and scenes** set macro values silently (above).

#### 4.5 Learn

On MOD's target view, ALT+K3 arms learn for the cursor's target row: the next control param touched by hand (the `param_touch` registry, §0.1) that is a valid target becomes the row's id, with min 0 and max 1, and the flash shows `M1 > v3 pitch`. A touched param that isn't a target flashes `not a macro target`. K2, a page change or 10 s (`MACROS.learn_seconds`) cancels.

### 5. Scene morph

#### 5.1 Params

| Param | Kind, range | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `morph` | control 0-1 (percent) | 0 |
| `morph_a` | number 1..`NUM_SCENES` | 1 |
| `morph_b` | number 1..`NUM_SCENES` | 2 |

In a group `strata_morph` (3 params) after the macros. They are params, so PSETs store them and CCs reach them; scenes don't store them (a scene holding the morph between two scenes would be circular). Morph is not an LFO destination or a macro target (Non-goals).

#### 5.2 Interpolation

`S.lib.morph` (`lib/morph.lua`, **(new)**) builds a plan whenever A, B or either slot's contents change (a save, a load, a migration): `scene_entries` of both (§0.2), validated, paired by id.
- **Continuous entries** (exactly the ones a transition fades: voice, grain and crossmod values, the registered extras that crossfade, the continuous globals, LFO rate, depth and offset) are set to `A + morph × (B − A)` in raw values, as a transition's steps are. Number params round to whole numbers, as transitions round the LFO depths. Entries equal in A and B are left out of the plan.
- **Discrete entries** (sources, mod sources, envelope shapes and grain modes, voice active and lock states, tempo, filter type, mutation and LFO settings, the registered discrete keys and fields, patterns, step data, macro targets, the recorders' run states) take A's value below 0.5 and B's above, with hysteresis (Question 6): from A they switch to B when morph reaches 0.52, from B back to A when it falls to 0.48 (`MORPH.hysteresis` 0.02). The first apply after a plan change takes A below 0.5 and B at or above it. A switch applies the discrete entries in recall order through `apply_entries`, so voices that stop stop first, sources come before their params, discrete extras after their source and voices that start start last (Question 7); one full voice sync per running voice whose source changed, as a recall does.
- Macro values are continuous and set silently.

#### 5.3 Applying

- The `morph` action records the position; a clock (`S.morph_clock`) applies the latest position at most 10 times a second (`MORPH.apply_hz`), so a fast turn is coalesced, and once more when the position stops moving (Question 8). A continuous entry is skipped when it moved by less than 1/1000 of its range since the last apply.
- At most 120 values per apply (`MORPH.max_values_per_apply`, Question 23): with more in the plan, each apply takes the next 120 round robin, so every value is reached within a few applies and a still morph converges. So morph sends at most 1200 messages a second, against about 2700 for every field of seven running voices at 10 Hz, and its sends are deferrable (§0.1).
- Moving the morph cancels a running transition (`cancel_scene_transition`) and the scene sequencer's wait moves on, as after a manual recall. A recall doesn't move the morph param; the next morph move re-applies from the recalled state.
- `S.current_scene` becomes 0 when the morph applies (the state is no longer a stored scene), so the header shows no scene; saving to a slot makes it current again, as now.
- An empty or invalid endpoint makes the morph do nothing and flashes `S5 empty` once.
- Morph never runs `recall_scene` or `recall_scene_instant`, never clears the randomize undo by itself (each param it sets goes through the actions, which do) and never starts a transition.

#### 5.4 How morph differs from a transition

| | Transition | Morph |
|---|---|---|
| driven by | time (`scene_transition_time`) | the `morph` param, any speed, any direction |
| ends | in `recall_scene_instant`: exact values, discrete keys at the end, `S.current_scene` set | never: discrete keys at 0.5 (with hysteresis), `S.current_scene` 0 |
| steps | 20 | at most 10 a second, only fields that differ |
| result | a recalled scene | params, which can be saved as a new scene |

#### 5.5 XY morph (stretch goal)

Four corners (`morph_a`, `morph_b`, and new `morph_c`, `morph_d`) and two params `morph_x`, `morph_y` (0-1). Continuous entries take the bilinear blend of the four corners; discrete entries take the corner with the largest weight (ties go to A, then B, C, D), with the same hysteresis on the weights. On the 256, page 3's rows 11-16 × columns 11-16 are a 6×6 pad (§11). It is built only after everything else (package 3a).

### 6. Scatter

- **Params** (group `strata_scatter`, 8 params, after morph):

  | Param | Range | Default |
  |---|---|---|
  | `scatter` | binary toggle | 0 (off) |
  | `scatter_depth` | 0-100 % | 10 % |
  | `scatter_rate` | 0.01-1 Hz, exp | 0.1 Hz |
  | `scatter_pitch`, `scatter_pos`, `scatter_size`, `scatter_cutoff`, `scatter_amp` | binary toggles | pitch and pos on, the rest off |

- **What it does:** every unlocked voice gets slow smooth random modulation on each enabled destination, independent per voice and destination, at depth × half the destination's range (§3.3). It is one engine node (`\strataScatter`), no messages per step (Question 9).
- **Sends:** `setScatter(depth / 100 (0 while off), rate, destination mask, voice mask)` from each param's sender and whenever a lock changes (through `S.register_lock_change`, §12). The voice mask is the unlocked voices: a locked voice is left alone by scatter, as by drift and mutation.
- **Scenes** store all eight params (depth and rate crossfade; the toggles and on/off switch at the end). MOD shows them (§10).

### 7. Gesture recorders

#### 7.1 What they record

- Four recorders (`NUM_GESTURES` 4), `S.lib.gestures` (`lib/gestures.lua`, **(new)**), each a `lib/pattern_time` pattern (`S.gestures[k].pattern`) plus Strata's state: `state` (`empty`, `recording`, `playing`, `stopped`), `quantize` and, for a quantized loop, its length in beats.
- **Events** are `{id, value}`: the param's value after a hand edit, so playback is absolute and repeatable (Question 10). They come from the `param_touch` registry (§0.1): encoder edits (`enc_param_delta`), arc rings (`arc_sweep_param`, `delta_with_detent`) and grid faders (`set_param_from_column`), for **control params only** (`params.tCONTROL`): macros and morph included, sources, options and binary params never.
- Not recorded: a recorder's own or another recorder's playback (it writes with `S.gesture_playing` set, which the touch hook skips), LFOs, scatter, mutations, drift, scene recalls and transitions, morph's writes (only the morph param itself, if touched by hand).
- One recorder records at a time: starting one stops another's recording, which then plays.
- At most 1024 events per recorder (`GESTURES.max_events`); the recording stops at the cap and flashes `G2 full`. A recording also stops at 10 minutes.

#### 7.2 Keys

`S.lib.gestures.press(k)` (MOD's K3, the grid keys, the arc button on a gesture row):
- empty → recording (it records from the first event, so a silent lead-in is not part of the loop; the loop's length runs from the first event to the stop);
- recording → playing (a free loop at once; a quantized loop from the next bar or beat);
- playing → stopped; stopped → playing.

`S.lib.gestures.clear(k)` (ALT+K2 on MOD, a 0.5 s grid hold) stops and empties it. `S.lib.gestures.overdub(k)` (ALT+K3 on MOD) toggles overdub while playing. A free loop overdubs through pattern_time's own `set_overdub`, which relies on its metro's `step` and `prev_time`. A quantized loop is played by Strata's coroutine, which pattern_time's `overdub_event` knows nothing about, so Strata overdubs it itself: an event touched while overdubbing is inserted into the loop's beat list at `(clock.get_beats() − loop_start) mod length`, kept sorted, under the same 1024-event cap.

#### 7.3 Quantization

- `gesture_<k>_quantize` (option: free, beat, bar; default free) per recorder, in a group `strata_gestures` (8 params) after scatter: the four `gesture_<k>_quantize`, then the four hidden `gesture_<k>_play` (§7.4). Hidden params still count in a group, so the stub's group check sees eight.
- **Free:** pattern_time plays it with its metro, as recorded.
- **Beat or bar:** when the recording stops, the loop's length in beats (seconds over `clock.get_beat_sec()` at the stop) rounds to the nearest whole beat or bar (at least one), and each event's time is scaled by the same factor and stored in beats. A clock coroutine (`S.gestures[k].clock`) plays it: `clock.sync(length_beats)` at each loop's start, so every loop starts on its bar, then each event after its offset in beats (`clock.sleep(offset × clock.get_beat_sec())`, re-read at every event, so it follows tempo changes). It stays locked to Link and MIDI clock. Changing the quantize of a recorded loop converts it the same way (free → beat or bar), or back to seconds at the current tempo.
- Playback conflicts: a hand edit of a param the recorder plays wins until the recorder's next event for it.

#### 7.4 Storage

- **Per PSET:** a PSET hook writes `<pset path base>.gestures.data` (`{version = 1, recorders = {...}}`: each recorder's events, times, quantize and length; `tab.save`), reads it after a PSET load (stopping every recorder first) and deletes it with the PSET.
- **Live:** `data/strata/gestures.data`, saved at cleanup and loaded at init (stopped), like the macros.
- **Scenes** store only whether each recorder plays: four binary params `gesture_<k>_play`, hidden from the PARAMS menu, kept in step with each recorder's state and registered as discrete scene fields (`S.register_scene_field`). A recall that sets 1 on an empty recorder does nothing; setting 0 stops it.
- Cleanup stops every recorder's metro and clock (a new cleanup step, §12).

### 8. The step sequencer

#### 8.1 Data

- **Levels stay `S.patterns[voice][step]`** (0-15), so every grid layout, the SEQ page and `step_brightness` keep working.
- **Step attributes:** `S.pattern_steps[voice][step]`, nil for a plain step, or a table with any of:

  | Key | Range | Default (nil) | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `prob` | 0-100 (%) | 100 | the chance the step plays, drawn once per step |
  | `ratchet` | 1-4 | 1 | hits within the step |
  | `notes` | a list of whole numbers -14..14 | none | scale degrees from the voice's pitch (§8.4); 3.4 plays the first |
  | `pos` | 0-1 | none | locks `buf_pos` |
  | `pitch` | 0.25-4 | none | locks `grain_pitch` |
  | `size` | 0.01-0.5 | none | locks `grain_size` |
  | `bookmark` | 1..`NUM_BOOKMARKS` | none | recalls a 3.3 bookmark |

  The lock ranges are the params' specs; the arc and grid draw them through `param_value_fraction` of the matching param.
- **Per voice** (a registered voice-param record, `seq`, with no grain keys, discrete scene keys and no VOICE rows): `v<i>_seq_length` (number 1-16, default 16) and `v<i>_seq_division` (option: `1/32`, `1/16t`, `1/16`, `1/8t`, `1/8`, `1/4`, `1/2`, `1 bar`; that is 1/8, 1/6, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1, 2 and 4 beats; default `1/16`).
- Scenes store the step attributes as a block (`data.steps`, registered by `lib/sequencer.lua`: `steps[voice] = {[step] = {...}}`, sparse), switched at a transition's end with the patterns.

#### 8.2 The clock

- One coroutine per voice (`S.pattern_clocks[voice]`) plus the transport's (`S.pattern_clock`, which keeps counting 16ths for the 8×8's half blink and the chain's bars). Start: every voice plays its step 1 at the start beat `b0` (now, or the next beat or bar under quantized actions, §8.9), then `clock.sync(division, b0 mod division)`, so its steps fall on `b0 + k × division` whatever the division.
- Each voice wraps at its own `v<i>_seq_length`; a length change takes effect at the voice's next wrap if the position is past the new length, else at once.
- **Per-voice playheads:** `S.lib.sequencer.sounding_step(voice)` is the voice's sounding step (nil while stopped and before its first step); with no argument it is the transport's, as today. `step_brightness(voice, step)` uses the voice's own, so every layout's playheads follow each voice's length and division without layout changes.
- Stop cancels every coroutine; cleanup (step 90) does the same.

#### 8.3 A step, and why it creates no nodes

When voice v's coroutine reaches step s with level L > 0:
1. **Probability:** one `math.random()` against `prob`; a miss plays nothing and changes nothing.
2. **Locks:** for each of `pos`, `pitch`, `size`, `set_base_override(v<i>_<param>, value)` (§0.1), which sends it through the param's sender (`setGrainParam`) with the LFO offsets on top. Each lock the previous played step of this voice had and this one lacks is cleared (`clear_base_override`, which sends the stored value back). Only a hand edit of the param clears its override early (Question 14); drift, morph, macros and gesture playback change the stored value under it.
3. **Bookmark:** `S.lib.bookmarks.recall(v, n)` (3.3), if `is_set(v, n)` and the bookmark lies inside the voice's current loop (`v<i>_loop_start`, `v<i>_loop_length`); outside it the lock does nothing, because 3.3's recall would reset the loop for good (Question 25).
4. **Note** (§8.4): a base override on the voice's pitch param.
5. **Hits:** `engine.retrigger(v − 1, L / MAX_STEP_LEVEL)` at the step's start, then, for a ratchet r, r − 1 more at `division / r` intervals (`clock.sync(division / r, b0 mod (division / r))` inside the step), each with the same velocity. Ratchets that would fall closer than 20 ms (`SEQ.min_ratchet_seconds`) are dropped from the end.
6. A stopped voice is started with `toggle_voice` instead of the first hit, as today; the overrides apply after its sync.

Every message here is `retrigger`, `setGrainParam` or `setVoiceParam` to nodes that exist. The only node-creating call is `toggle_voice` for a stopped voice, exactly as in 3.1. Worst case: seven voices at `1/32` with four hits at 300 BPM would be 160 retriggers a second per voice; the 20 ms rule caps it at 50, so 350 a second for all seven, plus at most four lock sends per step.

#### 8.4 Notes per step

- A note is a scale degree relative to the voice's own pitch: degree 0 is the stored pitch, degree k the k-th tone above it in the global scale (3.2's `scale` and `root`; `S.lib.variation.scale_mask()`, chromatic while the scale is off), so ±14 is two octaves of a heptatonic scale (Question 13).
- Applied as a MIDI note would be (`docs/notes/lua/midi.md`, `lib/midi_in.lua:121-136`), but as a base override for this step only: a synth voice's `v<i>_freq` (`setVoiceParam`) and a buffer voice's `v<i>_buf_rate`, each times `2 ^ (semitones / 12)` of its stored value and clamped to its range. An input voice, which MIDI notes leave alone, takes the note on its `v<i>_grain_pitch`, multiplied onto the step's `pitch` lock when it has one (Question 24). The next step without a note clears it.
- 3.4 plays the first note of the list. 3.5 plays the whole list through its note allocation; the data needs no migration.

#### 8.5 Euclidean fill

The SEQ edit view's `euclid` row holds hits k (0..length) and rotation w (0..length − 1) for the selected voice (`S.seq_euclid[voice]`, not stored). Changing either writes the voice's first `length` steps from `er.gen(k, length, w)`: hits at level 15, the rest at 0 (Question 22). Step attributes are kept. While a scene recall is in progress nothing fills.

#### 8.6 The SEQ page

**Pattern view** (3.1's, with these changes):
- Each voice's steps past its length are drawn as 1×1 dots at level 1 instead of cells; each row's playing column is its own voice's sounding step.
- ALT+K2 opens the edit view (Question 12); clearing moves to the edit view's `clear` row (the 256's row 15 still clears).
- Hints: `K2:play K3:step E3:level`, ALT `K2:edit K3:rand E3:bpm`.

**Edit view** (a field list through `S.lib.ui_field_list`, 5 visible rows 7 px apart from y 24, as VOICE):
- y 16: `v3 step 5`, and a 16-cell strip of the voice's steps (3×3 px cells, 4 px apart from x 60), the cursor's step outlined, past-length steps as dots.
- Section `voice`: `length` (`v<i>_seq_length`), `division`, `euclid` (`5/12 +2`: E3 hits, ALT+E3 rotation), `clear` (K3 clears the pattern).
- Section `step <n>`: `level`, `prob` (`80%`), `ratchet` (`x2`), `note` (`+3`, `off`), `pos` (`0.42`, `off`), `pitch` (`1.50`, `off`), `size` (`0.12 s`, `off`), `bookmark` (`2`, `off`; only when 3.3's bookmarks exist).
- Section `chain` (global, §8.8): `chain` (off/on), `from`, `scenes`, `bars`; and `quantize` (§8.9).
- E2 row; E3 value (a lock or note from `off` starts at the stored value or 0 and steps from there; whole steps for whole-number rows); ALT+E2 moves the step; ALT+E3 fine on control rows. K2 back to the pattern view; K3 the step on/off (on the `clear` row, clears). ALT+K2 resets the row (a lock or note to `off`, a voice param to its default); ALT+K3 randomizes the pattern, as in the pattern view.
- Hints: `K2:back K3:step E3:<label>`, ALT `K2:reset K3:rand E2:step`.
- `layout_states`: the edit view with every row at its widest value on voices 1 and 7, step 16, with and without bookmarks.

#### 8.7 Editing a held step from the grid

- `S.lib.grid_core.step_key` toggles a step on release instead of press, unless the press was consumed: by the level gesture (a second key on the row, as now) or by an edit while held (Question 11).
- A step key held for 0.3 s (`GRID_HOLD.step_edit_seconds`, through `on_hold`) opens the **step editor**: SEQ's edit view for that voice and step, pushed as a modal owner (§0.1) so it shows on any page and takes E2/E3 and the arc. Any edit consumes the key. Releasing the key closes it and the previous page returns; nothing toggles. A second step key held meanwhile still sets the first's level.
- Arc rings while it is open: 1 `pos`, 2 `pitch`, 3 `size`, 4 `prob` (each from `off` at the stored value; a turn below the range's start back to `off`), drawn through `param_value_fraction`; with ALT: 1 `ratchet`, 2 `note`, 3 `bookmark`, 4 `level` (whole steps, 20 ticks each).
- Interface (SEQ's package provides, the grid's uses): `S.lib.pages_seq.open_step_editor(voice, step, port)` and `close_step_editor(port)`.

#### 8.8 Pattern chaining

Params (group `strata_seq`, 6 params): `seq_chain` (binary, 0), `seq_chain_from` (1-16, 1), `seq_chain_scenes` (1-16, 2), `seq_chain_bars` (1-16, 4), `quantize_actions` (§8.9), `seq_division_reset` (trigger: every voice back to step 1 at the next bar). While the chain is on and the patterns play, every `bars` bars (counted by the transport, on the bar) the sequencer loads the next scene's patterns-only block (`S.patterns`, `S.pattern_steps`, each voice's length and division) from the range `from .. from + scenes − 1`, wrapping, skipping empty scenes. Nothing else of the scene is applied: no params, sources or voice states. PSETs store the chain; scenes don't (they are its contents).

#### 8.9 Beat-quantized actions

- `quantize_actions` (option: off, beat, bar; default off). `S.lib.quantize.schedule(key, fn)` (`lib/quantize.lua`, **(new)**) runs `fn` at once while off, otherwise at the next beat or bar (`clock.sync(1)` or `clock.sync(4)`); a new action with the same key replaces a waiting one, and the header flashes `S3 at bar`.
- Quantized: scene recalls from SCENES' keys, the grid's scene keys and the arc button (key `scene`); freeze toggles from any control (key `freeze<v>`, by a one-line wrap in `S.lib.freeze.toggle`); bookmark recalls and position jumps from keys, BUFFER and the grid (3.3's calls in `lib/positions.lua` and `lib/pages/buffer.lua`, one-line wraps by 1f, keys `bookmark<v>` and `position<v>`; a step's bookmark lock is already on the grid); pattern start (key `patterns`). Not quantized: the scene sequencer (time-based), steps, LFOs, morph.
- Cleanup cancels every waiting action (a new step, §12).

### 9. Scenes: 16 in two banks, with names

#### 9.1 Slots

- `NUM_SCENES` 16, `SCENE_BANK_SIZE` 8: bank A is 1-8, bank B 9-16. A bank is only a view (Question 15).
- `scene_sequence_length` becomes 1-16 (default 4); `morph_a`, `morph_b` and the chain take 1-16.
- The header shows `S1`-`S16` (`S16` is 3 px wider than `S8`; the layout test checks it against `REC`).

#### 9.2 File format and migration

- `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` 5 (3.3's is 4). Files of any earlier version load: slots 9-16 are filled empty, and the migrations below run in order after 3.3's.
- **Migration defaults** (each reproduces 3.3):

  | Field | Default |
  |---|---|
  | slot name | `""` (and an old default `scene <n>` becomes `""`) |
  | LFOs 1-3 | converted from the library's fields (§2.9) |
  | LFOs 4-6 | every param's default, off |
  | `macro_1`..`macro_4` | 0 |
  | `data.macros` | four empty target lists |
  | `scatter`, its depth, rate and toggles | the param defaults (off) |
  | `gesture_1_play`..`gesture_4_play` | 0 |
  | `voices[i].extras.seq_length`, `seq_division` | 16, `1/16` (through the registered record's `scene_defaults`) |
  | `data.steps` | empty (every step plain) |

- **`validate_scene` covers every new field:**
  - `name`: a string of at most `SCENE_NAME_MAX` (12) printable ASCII characters;
  - each LFO's 13 values: the binary and option params integers in range (a mod source within 1..n), the controls numbers in range;
  - `macro_<m>`: numbers 0-1; `data.macros`: `NUM_MACROS` tables of at most `MACRO_TARGETS` `{id = string, min, max}` with min and max numbers 0-1 (an unknown id is emptied with a console line, not rejected, §4.3);
  - the scatter fields: binaries 0/1, depth 0-100, rate in range;
  - `gesture_<k>_play`: 0/1;
  - `seq_length`: an integer 1-16; `seq_division`: an integer 1-8;
  - `data.steps`: `NUM_VOICES` tables keyed by whole steps 1..`PATTERN_LENGTH`, each `prob` an integer 0-100, `ratchet` 1-4, `notes` a list of at most four integers -14..14, `pos`, `pitch`, `size` numbers in their param's range, `bookmark` an integer 1..`NUM_BOOKMARKS`; anything else rejects the scene.
- Patterns stay 7 × 16 integers 0-15, as now.

#### 9.3 Names

- On SCENES, ALT+K2 on a slot opens norns' `textentry` (`textentry.enter(callback, name, "scene <n> name")`) through a modal owner (§0.1) that draws `textentry.redraw()` while it is open, as the file selector does on BUFFER (`strata.lua:226`). The callback stores the name, cut to 12 characters, or keeps the old one on cancel. Naming an empty slot is allowed; the name stays with the slot.
- A capture keeps the slot's name. Names show on SCENES, on MOD's morph rows and in flashes (`S3 dusk`).

## Cost if wrong

- **Own LFOs (Question 1).** If keeping norns' library matters more (for example another script's PSET habits), the library can come back for the Lua-run LFOs, with the new shapes and LFO-to-LFO computed around it (Approach B). The engine-run part doesn't change.
- **LFOs on pitch, position and size move to the engine.** They become smoother; old scenes keep their depths and rates. If an old patch relied on the stepping (a 10 Hz LFO stepping at 48 per beat), it sounds cleaner, not louder. The destination rule is one table (`FAST_DESTINATIONS`).
- **High-tier memory (Question 3, decided).** With the 3.5 MB high floor, 3.5's high tier plays two notes, as 2d's measurement already decided. If 3.5 needs more room, the engine LFOs could share one node; 3.4 removes nothing of 3.2.
- **The message budget (Question 23).** If 1500 a second proves too low (LFOs that step under a morph) or too high (#131 on a CM3), it is one constant (`ENGINE_BUDGET`), set from the 3b soak test.
- **Bookmark locks inside the loop only (Question 25).** A lock on a bookmark outside the loop is silent; if performers want the jump, 3.3's recall would need a variant that keeps the loop.
- **48 Hz Lua steps (Question 17).** A 10 Hz Lua LFO is steppier than 3.3's at 120 BPM. `LFO.step_hz` is one constant; raising it raises the cap's message rate with it.
- **Absolute macros (Question 4).** A macro move overwrites a hand edit of its target. Relative macros would be a change to `apply` and the LFO path only; the data stays.
- **Morph hysteresis and voice switching (Questions 6-7).** A performer who wants a hard 0.5 or morphs that never start voices: one constant, or a filter on the discrete entries.
- **Morph at 10 Hz (Question 8).** A fast morph sweep sounds stepped at 100 ms; raising `MORPH.apply_hz` costs messages linearly.
- **Scatter on five destinations (Question 9).** Scatter on other params means a Lua scatter with its own message budget, a separate feature.
- **Gestures record absolute control values (Question 10).** A loop recorded on a param and replayed after a scene recall sets that param back to the recorded values; that is the point of absolute playback. Relative recording would be a new event kind.
- **Step keys toggle on release (Question 11).** If live step entry feels late, step keys can toggle on press again and the editor open only on steps that are already on.
- **Temporary locks and relative notes (Questions 13-14).** If permanent p-locks are wanted, the step writes the param instead of an override; the data is the same.
- **Chaining through scenes (Question 19).** If performers want chains independent of scenes, a chain list is a new param set and the loader is unchanged.
- **Scene numbers 1-16 (Question 15).** A1-B8 labels are a display change only.
- **MOD after LFO (Question 16).** One line of `UI_PAGES`.
- **Two engine LFO models (Lua's draws differ from the engine's for random shapes).** If the dot's mismatch bothers anyone, random shapes' Lua values can be fed from a seeded generator shared with the engine; nobody should hear the difference.

## Calls made in this design

- Strata's own six LFOs, one 48 Hz Lua clock, exact phases (free from `util.time()`, clocked from `clock.get_beats()`), eight shapes, LFO-to-LFO rate (±2 octaves) and depth (×(1 ± amount)) inputs from lower-numbered LFOs only, evaluated in Lua for every target.
- The destination decides where an LFO runs: `v<i> pitch`, `pos`, `size`, `vfilt` and the new `v<i> amp` in the engine, everything else in Lua; `v<i> main` always in Lua.
- The same offset math in both places (± half the range, clamped), except the voice cutoff (exponential) and amp (the crossmod amplitude law), both new to 3.2 or 3.4.
- At most 12 Lua LFO sends per step, round robin; engine LFOs send only setting changes, rates deduped at 0.5 % and depths at 1/1000; clocked engine LFOs resynced once a bar.
- One 35-channel control bus for the fast destinations, read by one `In.kr` in every grain synth; engine LFO and scatter nodes in a `fastModGroup` at the head of the engine group; one SynthDef per LFO shape, replaced with `Synth.replace` on a shape change; channels zeroed when their last writer ends.
- Four commands: `setFastLfo`, `stopFastLfo`, `syncFastLfo`, `setScatter`; the area `modulation` at order 160.
- Macros: absolute, normalized-range min and max, silent sets from scenes and morph, targets stored by id outside the params, learn through the touch hook, LFOs on macros as extra offsets on the targets, no macro on macros or morph.
- Morph writes params at most 10 times a second, only the differing fields, discrete keys at 0.5 with 0.02 of hysteresis, in recall order through the same functions a recall uses; `S.current_scene` 0 while morphed; never a recall.
- Scatter: one engine node, smooth random per voice and destination, unlocked voices only, depth 10 % and 0.1 Hz by default with pitch and position on.
- Gesture recorders: control params only, absolute values, from hand edits only; free loops through pattern_time's metro, quantized loops converted to beats and clocked per bar; per-PSET and live side files; scenes store only whether each plays.
- Sequencer: levels stay in `S.patterns`; sparse step attributes; per-voice length and division as registered voice params; one coroutine per voice; temporary locks as base overrides; relative scale-degree notes applied like MIDI notes; ratchets at least 20 ms apart; Euclidean fill destructive; chaining through scene ranges every N bars; quantized actions through one scheduler.
- Scenes: 16 numbered slots in banks of eight, 12-character names through `textentry`, old default names dropped.
- The MOD page after LFO; the LFO page shows a window of three LFOs; the 256's page 1 rows 9-13 hold the gesture recorders and the macros (row 8 stays clear for 3.3's page-5 key); step keys toggle on release and a 0.3 s hold opens the step editor on any page.
