## Questions for review

**All decided 2026-11-08, each as recommended** (the body is written to the recommendations); Question 40, added by the joint review, was decided 2026-11-09 as recommended.

Every choice below could reasonably go the other way. Each has a recommendation, and the body is written to it.

1. **Replace norns' `lib/lfo.lua` with Strata's own LFOs?** The library's phase is readable (`lfo:get("phase_counter")`) and `change_period` keeps the phase continuous, so those are not the reasons. The reasons are: the new shapes (the library has six fixed ones, none of them smooth random, once-per-cycle sample-and-hold or drunk); one parameter set for Lua-run and engine-run LFOs (the library's period stops at 0.1 s, and an engine LFO needs rates up to 50 Hz and its own shapes); free mode that doesn't step with the tempo (the library advances every LFO by 1/ppqn of a beat per clock step, so a free LFO's step rate, and Strata's message rate, follow the tempo: 240 steps a second per LFO at 300 BPM); and the workarounds Strata carries (#103's offset wrapper, the depth rounding). The cost: the PSET ids change from `lfo_*` to `strata_lfo<n>_*`. **Recommended: yes**, with a one-time import of old PSETs' `lfo_*` lines (§2.9) and a scene migration (§9).
2. **Which LFOs run in the engine?** **Recommended: the destination decides.** An LFO on one of the five fast destinations (`v<i> pitch`, `pos`, `size`, `vfilt`, `amp`) always runs in the engine, at any rate; every other LFO runs in Lua, up to 10 Hz (§3). The alternative is a per-LFO "engine" switch, which would give two kinds of LFO on the same destination and a 10 Hz wall the user has to understand.
3. **The memory floor, one decision for 3.3 and 3.4. Decided 2026-11-08: high 3.5 MB, per-synth 88 KB (as 3.3's Q1).** The high tier's floor drops to 3.5 MB (3584 KB, fourteen 256 KB probes), standard keeps 3 MB, the per-synth check limit rises from 80 to 88 KB, and eight recordings at once must leave 3 MB on both tiers (3.3's rule). Measured: merged 3.2's high worst case leaves 4130-4150 KB free, late allocations counted. *Re-planned 2026-11-11 from 3.3.0's measured figures (#229; the arithmetic is in §3.6):* 3.3.0 leaves 4320 KB free on high at the mixed worst case with one recording, 4190 at its tightest case, synth → buffer → synth (#502, which the earlier estimate did not name), and 4910 at the all-buffer worst case with one recording, as printed; the spectral writers' late allocations (up to 112 KB) take the two mixed cases to about 4210 and 4080. 3.4 takes about 97 KB with the stage (estimated). So high comes to about 4220 KB free at the mixed worst case with one recording (about 640 above the floor, about 530 counting the late allocations), about 4090 at synth → buffer → synth (about 510 above it, about 400 counting them) and about 4810 at the all-buffer worst case with one recording (about 1230 above it), and standard to about 4890 at the mixed one, 4750 at synth → buffer → synth and 4940 at the all-buffer one (§3.6). With 3.3 and 3.4, `\strataGrainBuf` is about 68.9 KB (67.4 measured), `\strataGrainRing` about 59.6 KB (58.1 measured; both under the 88 KB limit) and the stage about 36.75 KB with 3.4's offsets (34.5 measured; 3.3's check allows 40 KB), so `vfilt` and `amp` stay in the stage as designed. With the stage, three notes per voice on high leave about 970 KB at the mixed worst case, now synth → buffer → synth (the estimate said about 1.07 MB), just under the 1 MB kept back (3.3's §0c.6), and about 1.83 MB at the all-buffer one (the estimate fitted two notes there); 3.5's design settles the count from what its worst case leaves, not from the floor (§3.6), so the lower floor costs 3.5 nothing more.
4. **Macros write absolute values.** Turning a macro sets each target to its own point between min and max, like Ableton's or Bitwig's macros, and the screen shows the values. The alternative, macros as a modulation source around the stored values, never changes a param, so the screen would not show what plays. **Recommended: absolute** (§4).
5. **Macro targets are stored outside the param system** (by param id, in a per-PSET side file and in scenes), because an option param's index would move whenever the target list grows. **Recommended: yes** (§4.3).
6. **Morph switches discrete keys with a small hysteresis around 0.5** (B from 0.52 up, back to A from 0.48 down), so a noisy CC or an arc resting on 0.5 can't flip sources and voices on and off. The roadmap says "at 0.5". **Recommended: hysteresis of 0.02** (§5.2).
7. **Morph starts and stops voices and switches sources at the crossing**, as a transition's end would. The alternative leaves voice states and sources alone and morphs sound values only. **Recommended: switch them**, with the hysteresis above.
8. **Morph applies at most 10 times a second**, coalescing faster moves, so a fast turn never sends more than a 2 s transition already does (§5.3). **Recommended: 10 Hz.**
9. **Scatter reaches only the five engine destinations** and leaves locked voices alone (§6). A Lua scatter on any param would cost up to 7 voices × N params × 48 messages a second. **Recommended: engine destinations only, locked voices excluded.**
10. **Gesture recorders record control params only, as absolute values**, and "quantized" means the loop's length rounds to whole bars (or beats) and each loop starts on one; single events are not snapped to a grid, which would collapse a fader move into steps. Recorders default to free time. **Recommended: as written** (§7).
11. **Grid step keys toggle on release instead of press**, so a held step can be edited (locks, probability) without first toggling it. A tap still toggles, about a tap's length later than today. **Recommended: yes** (§8.7).
12. **SEQ's ALT+K2 held opens the step edit view** instead of clearing the pattern (clearing moves to a row of that view and stays on the 256's row 15). **Recommended: yes**. Changed 2026-11-11 by #557 (page randomize, shipped before 3.4): on SEQ, ALT+K2 now randomizes the pattern (on release, with its undo on the same key) and ALT+K3 clears it, so the edit view opens on ALT+K2 held (past `ALT_HOLD_SECONDS`, 0.5 s), SEQ's hold action. The first reading rejected a hold gesture because a hold on plain K2 or K3 collides with 3.1's long-press fallback (§8.6); a hold under ALT does not.
13. **Step notes are relative scale degrees, applied like a MIDI note** (a synth voice's `freq`, a buffer voice's rate, an input voice's grain pitch) and only while their step plays. **Recommended: yes**; 3.5 maps them onto its notes (§8.4).
14. **Step locks are temporary** (Elektron-style): a locked value plays for its step and the voice returns to its stored value at the next step without that lock. The stored param never changes. **Recommended: yes** (§8.3).
15. **Scenes are numbered 1-16**, and a bank is only a view of eight. The alternative labels them A1-A8 and B1-B8. **Recommended: numbers** (§9).
16. **The MOD page goes after LFO**: MAIN, VOICE, SEQ, FX, LFO, MOD, BUFFER, SCENES, SETUP. BUFFER, SCENES and SETUP move one E1 step further. **Recommended: after LFO**, beside the other modulation page. A ninth page also moves the header's page dots on every page 4 px left (`HEADER.dots_x` 40 → 36, §6) so the ninth dot clears SEQ's play mark at x 73; the titles, `[M]` and the scene stay where they are.
17. **Lua LFOs step on one free-running 48 Hz clock** instead of 48 steps per beat, with at most 12 engine messages per step (576 a second for all six), tempo or not (§2.5). A 10 Hz LFO then gets 4.8 steps per cycle, where today it gets 9.6 at 120 BPM. **Recommended: yes**: the fast destinations no longer depend on it.
18. **Engine LFOs top out at 50 Hz** (15 control blocks per cycle). **Recommended: 50 Hz.**
19. **Pattern chaining plays the patterns of scenes `start .. start + length - 1`, one link every N bars**, patterns only, never the sound (§8.8). The alternative is an explicit chain list with its own editor. **Recommended: scene ranges.**
20. **Beat-quantized actions** cover scene recalls from keys, grid and arc, freeze, bookmark recalls and pattern start, default off (§8.9). **Recommended: as written.**
21. **The 256's page 1 rows 9-13** become the gesture recorders (row 9, columns 1-4) and macro faders 1-4 (rows 10-13; amended 2026-11-08: the shown bank's four macros, with the bank keys on row 9 columns 13-16, cross-release decision 6); the morph fader is on page 3. Row 8 columns 9-12, the first choice, clash with 3.3's page-5 key (row 8 column 12). The roadmap had row 9 as "selected voice level", which 3.1 never built. **Recommended: as written** (§11).
22. **Euclidean fill is destructive**: it writes the voice's steps once, and the steps can then be edited by hand. The alternative, a live generator, would fight hand edits. **Recommended: destructive fill** (§8.5).
23. **The overall message rate.** Each source is bounded, but they add up: morph is about 270 values × 10 Hz = 2700 messages a second, and a gesture recorder looping the morph keeps that up indefinitely; Lua's LFOs add up to 576, four recorders looping macros about 960 (4 × 8 targets × 30 moves a second) and the sequencer up to 350. Every message passes through sclang, and heavy sclang allocation already trips sclang 3.13's garbage-collector counter on norns (#131). **Recommended: (a) at most 120 values per morph apply (1200 a second), the plan's entries taken round robin; (b) one budget for every deferrable sender (Lua LFOs, morph, gesture playback, LFOs on macros) of 1500 messages a second, counted per 100 ms window, a deferred send keeping only its newest value and going first in the next window, while hand edits, steps, notes, locks and recalls are never deferred (§0.1, §2.5, §5.3); (c) a one-hour soak test on a CM3 in 3b with every source running, watching sclang's CPU, xruns and #131.** Amended 2026-11-08 for 16 macros (cross-release decision 6, §4.7): the budget stays 1500 a second, macro fan-out becomes deferrable whatever moves the macro, and the macros' worst-case demand rises from 960 to 7680 a second (3840 from recorders, 3840 from hand moves and CCs), all of it inside the budget.
24. **Step notes on input voices.** MIDI notes don't change an input voice's pitch today (`lib/midi_in.lua:121-136`), so applying a step note to an input voice's grain pitch is new behavior, and it meets a `pitch` lock in the same step. **Recommended: input voices take step notes on their grain pitch, multiplied onto the step's `pitch` lock when it has one (the lock sets the base, the note transposes it); MIDI notes on input voices stay as they are until 3.5.** Related: the roadmap's quantized "position jumps" are covered only by quantizing bookmark recalls; **recommended: also quantize 3.3's position-page jumps** (§8.9).
25. **Bookmark locks and loop regions.** 3.3's `S.lib.bookmarks.recall(v, n)` resets the voice's loop params when the bookmark lies outside the loop: a lasting change, against Question 14's temporary locks. Options: (a) a step's bookmark lock does nothing when the bookmark lies outside the voice's current loop (checked from the bookmark and loop params before the call); (b) let it reset the loop; (c) ask 3.3 for a recall that never touches the loop. **Recommended: (a)**: the lock stays temporary and 3.3's interface stays exactly `recall` and `is_set` (§8.3).

### Decided 2026-11-08 (cross-release)

The maintainer took these from what 3.5 (polyphony and MPE) and 3.6 (integration) need, so neither has to redesign 3.4. The body is written to them; `docs/design/3.1-3.6-roadmap.md` ("Cross-release decisions (2026-11-08)") records them for every release, numbered 2-7 there after 3.3's per-voice mix stage (its 1); "cross-release decision n" in this document uses the numbers below.

1. **The fast-modulation bus is indexed by (voice, note slot, destination)**, with **one note slot per voice in 3.4** (`FAST_MOD_NOTE_SLOTS` 1, engine `fastModNoteSlots`). With one slot the channels sit exactly where the approved layout put them; 3.5 raises the slot count for per-note MPE pressure and slide without changing the layout's rule, the grain synths' read or the commands (§3.2). Because 3.3's Wave 0 (package 0c, being written into the 3.3 design in parallel) moves the voice filter and the sends out of every grain synth into one per-voice stage after the grains, the `vfilt` (cutoff, 3) and `amp` (4) destinations are applied by **3.3 0c's per-voice mix stage** (`\strataVoiceMix`: its `filterFreq` control and `voiceLevel`), not by the grain synths (§3.3).
2. **Steps store a list of notes** (`notes`, up to `SEQ.max_step_notes` 8 scale degrees), in the pattern data, the scenes and the chain alike. 3.4's UI edits one note; 3.5 adds chord editing and plays every note through its note allocation with no format change. Until 3.5, a step with several notes plays its first note on Strata's voices, and the step outputs receive the whole list (§8.4, §8.10).
3. **A step output registry**: the sequencer fires every hit through the outputs registered with `S.register_step_output(...)`. 3.4 registers only Strata's own voices (`voices`); 3.6 adds MIDI out, Just Friends and W/ over ii, and nb, one registration each (§8.10).
4. **A modulation source registry**: LFOs, macros and gesture recorders register with `S.register_mod_source(...)`, one interface (a key, a name, a value and its range), and MOD lists the registered sources generically. 3.5 registers MPE pressure and slide, 3.6 crow CV and MIDI CC (§10.1).
5. **Transport following lands in 3.4**: the patterns follow norns' clock transport (`clock.transport.start` and `stop`), so Link and MIDI clock start and stop Strata. It is a SETUP option, **on by default** (`seq_follow_transport`). 3.6 then only adds MIDI out (§8.11).
6. **16 macros** (in place of four): four banks of four (`NUM_MACROS` 16, `MACRO_BANK_SIZE` 4), one shown bank shared by MOD, the arc and the grids, params `macro_1`..`macro_16`, 16 values and target lists in scenes, and every macro fan-out deferrable under the 1500-a-second budget. 3.6's OXI E16 maps its 16 encoders one to one (§4.6, §4.7, §10, §11).

What changes elsewhere: Question 21's rows 10-13 show a bank, Question 23's figures are revised (above), the Non-goals no longer list transport start and stop, §14's packages own the new pieces, and "Interfaces for 3.5 and 3.6" (§14) lists the contracts.

### Questions 26-30 (decided 2026-11-08)

The maintainer answered these on the pull request: 26-29 as recommended, 30 against the recommendation.

26. **Decided 2026-11-08: follow only (as recommended).** **Strata follows the transport but never leads it.** Strata's play key, SEQ and the grid start and stop the patterns locally; they never call `clock.internal.start()`, `clock.link.start()` or their stops. The alternative, a play key that also starts the transport, would restart other Link peers or reset norns' beat from Strata, which belongs with 3.6's MIDI out if anywhere. **Recommended: follow only in 3.4** (§8.11).
27. **Decided 2026-11-08: restart (as recommended).** **A transport start while the patterns play restarts them from step 1** on the new beat 0 (and the chain from `seq_chain_from`), as a DAW's start from the top would. The alternative ignores a start while playing. **Recommended: restart** (§8.11).
28. **Decided 2026-11-08: one shared bank (as recommended).** **One shown macro bank for every surface.** MOD, the arc's MOD rings, the 256's rows 10-13 and the 128h and 128v macro pages all show `S.lib.macros.bank()`, and any of them changing it changes it for all. The alternative, a bank per surface (a grid on bank 3 while the arc shows bank 1), lets two hands play eight macros but makes the screen disagree with what is under them. **Recommended: shared** (§4.6).
29. **Decided 2026-11-08: always deferrable (as recommended).** **Macro fan-out is always deferrable.** Every target send of a macro apply counts as deferrable, whether a hand, a CC, a recorder or an LFO moved the macro; the macro param itself and every target's stored value change at once, and only the engine sends wait (at most about 400 ms at the worst case, the matrix's sends counted, §4.7). The alternative keeps hand-moved and CC-moved macros undeferred, which lets an E16 sweeping all 16 send up to 3840 messages a second past the budget. **Recommended: always deferrable** (§4.7).
30. **Decided 2026-11-08: a general modulation matrix in 3.4 (against the recommendation).** The question was whether 3.4 should have a matrix (any registered source to any destination with a depth) or leave routing to each source. The recommendation was no matrix; the maintainer chose one. It is designed in §10.2, and its choices are Questions 31-39.

### Questions 31-39: the modulation matrix (decided 2026-11-08)

The maintainer decided each as recommended on the pull request; §10.2 is written to them.

31. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **16 routings** (`MATRIX_SLOTS` 16). Lua's per-step cap bounds the matrix's messages whatever the count (Question 36), so the count trades refresh rate and screen rows: with 16 routings and six LFOs on 22 different destinations, every destination is still sent at least every second step (24 Hz); 32 would halve that and double MOD's section. 16 also matches the E16's encoders (3.6). **Recommended: 16.**
32. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **Routings are a side table, depths and on/off are params.** A routing's source and destination are stored by key and param id outside the param system, as macro targets are (§4.3), because the source registry and the destination list grow between releases and an option param's index would move. Its depth (`matrix_<k>_depth`) and on/off (`matrix_<k>`) are params, so CCs, PSETs, scenes, macros and morph reach them. **Recommended: as written.**
33. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **Destinations are the LFO destination list without the macros.** Every entry there is a control param with a sender, vetted by its owner and extended through `register_lfo_destination`; macros are left out because a macro is also a source. LFO params and matrix depths are not destinations, so no source's output can depend on a routing and the matrix can't form a loop. The alternative, any control param, would reach params whose senders were never written for modulation. **Recommended: as written.**
34. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **LFO destinations and macro targets stay as they are; the matrix adds routings beside them, and every layer sums into one offset per destination.** One model (an LFO's destination and a macro's targets becoming matrix routings) would change every LFO and macro PSET id, scene field and editor, mix macros' absolute writes with the matrix's offsets, and lose the rule that an LFO on a fast destination runs in the engine at any rate. Nothing migrates. **Recommended: two layers, one sum.**
35. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **Matrix offsets on the five fast destinations go to the engine's offset bus**, through one Lua-written node (`\strataFastOffsets`) and a new command, `setFastOffset(voice, noteSlot, destination, offset)`, lagged 50 ms (amended 2026-11-09, §10.2). They sum on the bus with engine LFOs and scatter, and the grain synth (or the mix stage) clips once. The alternative sends base + offset through `setGrainParam`, which mixes the stored value with modulation in one message, clamps twice (Lua's, then the engine's), and has no per-note path for 3.5. **Recommended: the offset node.**
36. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **The matrix shares the Lua LFOs' cap of 12 sends per step** (576 a second for both), so adding routings never raises Lua's modulation traffic. The alternative, its own cap, would add up to 768 a second. **Recommended: shared.**
37. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **A unipolar source (a macro, a recorder's phase) adds 0 to depth; a bipolar one (an LFO) adds −depth to +depth.** A macro at 0 then leaves its destinations alone, and a routing's depth is the most it moves them. The alternative centers every source (−depth at 0). **Recommended: as written.**
38. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **Matrix depths are macro targets but not LFO destinations.** A macro can fade a routing in, and morph and scenes crossfade depths; an LFO on a depth would be modulated modulation, which the LFOs' own rate and depth inputs already cover. **Recommended: as written.**
39. **Decided 2026-11-08 (as recommended).** **MOD's arc rings follow the cursor's section**: with the cursor in the matrix section, rings 1-4 are the depths of the four routings around it (R1-R4, ..., R13-R16); anywhere else they stay the shown macro bank. No grid keys for the matrix (the 256's free cells are taken by 3.3 and 3.4). **Recommended: as written.**

### Question 40 (decided 2026-11-09, joint review with 3.3's mix stage)

40. **Decided 2026-11-09 (as recommended).** **How the amp offset reaches `\strataVoiceMix`'s level.** 3.3's §0c.7 has 3.4 fill the hook `voiceMixMod(controls)`, but the hook runs before the stage computes `modSig`, and the stage's synth path takes no level at all (`sig = fs + other`). So the hook alone can replace `filterFreq` but can't put the amp offset inside `voiceLevel` or on the synth path. **Recommended: 3.4's 0c also changes the stage's two level lines in `StrataMix.sc`, neutrally** (`other = fo × voiceLevel(amp, modSig + controls[\ampOffset], accent)` and `sig = fs × voiceLevel(1, controls[\ampOffset], 0) + other`; at the neutral `\ampOffset` of 0 both fold to 3.3's graph, no UGen added). The alternative is that 3.3's 0c builds those two lines in, with the same neutral key, so 3.4's edit of `StrataMix.sc` is only the moved hook; it costs one more change to 3.3's 0c. The body is written to the recommendation (§0.3, §3.3).

### Question 41 (proposed 2026-11-11, awaiting review): step gates

Asked for on 2026-11-11: today a step starts a stopped voice or accents a running one and never silences one, so on a drone the sequencer is only a pattern of accents. The request is a gate: a sequenced voice that is heard while its step's gate is open and not otherwise. §8.12 is written to the recommendations below; each could go the other way.

41. **Gates.**
    - **(a) Where the gate lives. Recommended: in the per-voice mix stage,** as a level the stage multiplies its whole output by, opened by the `retrigger` the step already sends. No node is created, no message is added per hit, every source kind is gated alike, and the sends come after it, so delay and reverb tails ring on. The alternatives: `voiceOff` and `voiceOn` per step (rejected: steps must never create nodes, and the release tails would stack), or the amp channel of the fast-modulation bus at −1 (rejected: it sums with LFOs, scatter and routings on amp, so a closed gate would leak, and its 50 ms lag would be the only envelope).
    - **(b) Per voice, off by default. Recommended: a per-voice switch,** `v<i>_seq_gate`, so drones and gated voices play side by side and every old scene sounds as it did.
    - **(c) What a gated voice does when nothing plays it. Recommended: it is silent.** With the patterns stopped, or between steps, a gated voice makes no sound; it still shows as running. A MIDI note also opens the gate (it sends the same `retrigger`). The alternative, a gate that opens whenever the patterns stop, would bring every gated drone back at full level at the stop.
    - **(d) The gate's length. Recommended: in steps, per voice, with a per-step override:** `v<i>_seq_gate_length` (0.05-16 steps, default 0.5) and a step attribute `gate` in the same unit. A gate longer than the gap to the next hit is simply reopened by it, so long gates tie. A ratchet's hits each get the length divided by the hit count.
    - **(e) The gate's shape. Recommended: a fixed 5 ms attack and a per-voice release,** `v<i>_seq_gate_release` (0.005-4 s, default 0.1). An attack param is left out until someone misses it.

### Questions 42-44 (proposed 2026-11-11, awaiting review): swing, mute and solo, the input level

Asked for on 2026-11-11, each because it rides on work 3.4 does anyway. §8.13, §8.14 and §10.3 are written to the recommendations.

42. **Swing. Recommended: per voice,** `v<i>_seq_swing` (50-75 %, default 50), delaying every second step of that voice's own division. A voice at 1/8 and one at 1/16 then each swing their own grid; one global swing would have to pick a grid. Micro-timing per step stays a non-goal.
43. **Mute and solo. Recommended: per-voice params, in the mix stage, beside the gate.** Today the only way to silence a voice is to stop it, and a step starts it again. `v<i>_mute` silences a voice without stopping it (its pattern, grains and crossmod carry on); `v<i>_solo` silences every voice that is not soloed. Scenes store mutes (an arrangement is made of them) and never solos (a solo is a moment's listening aid). The alternative, mute as a level of 0, would lose the voice's level and fight scenes and LFOs on amp.
44. **The input level as a modulation source. Recommended: one source, `input`, from norns' own input level polls,** so the matrix can make any destination follow the live input, and duck a voice under it with a negative depth. Followers of the seven voices' own levels would need seven new engine polls and are left to a later release.

## Intent

3.4 makes Strata's sound move on its own and its sequencer deep enough to carry a piece:
- **Six LFOs** of Strata's own, with smooth random, sample-and-hold and drunk shapes, and LFOs that modulate other LFOs' rate and depth.
- **Fast modulation in the engine** for grain pitch, position, size, the voice filter's cutoff and level, so LFOs above 10 Hz cost no messages.
- **Scatter**: slow random movement on every voice with one switch.
- **16 macros in four banks of four**, each driving up to eight targets with their own ranges.
- **Scene morph** between two scenes, writing params so the result can be saved.
- **Four gesture recorders** that loop encoder, arc and grid fader moves.
- **A deeper step sequencer**: per-voice length and division, probability, ratchets, step locks, Euclidean fill, notes per step (stored as chords), chaining, beat-quantized actions, and patterns that follow norns' clock transport (Link and MIDI start and stop).
- **16 scenes in two banks, with names.**
- **A new MOD page** that holds the macros, the morph, the recorders, scatter and an overview of the LFOs.
- **A modulation matrix**: 16 routings from any registered source to any LFO destination, each with a bipolar depth, summed with the LFOs around the stored values (Question 30).
- **The hooks 3.5 and 3.6 build on**: a fast-modulation bus addressed per note slot, chord-ready steps, a step output registry and a modulation source registry (cross-release decisions, 2026-11-08).

Everything defaults to 3.3's sound. A scene saved in 3.3 recalls the same voices, values and patterns.

## Goals

0. A restructure that changes no behavior: the Lua hooks later packages need (registries, a base-value override layer, a "param touched" hook, PSET hooks, a modal screen owner), the scene format's registries, and the engine area's five commands as stubs.
1. Engine-side fast modulation: five destinations per voice and note slot (one slot per voice in 3.4) on one five-channel control block each, read by every grain synth (and, for the cutoff and amp, by 3.3's per-voice mix stage), written by engine LFOs and scatter, adding offsets to the values Lua sends. About 97 KB of the real-time pool at the worst case (estimated, §3.6), no `LocalBuf`, no delay line or table.
2. Six LFOs (`NUM_LFOS` 6) of Strata's own: eight shapes, free or clocked rates, LFO-to-LFO rate and depth modulation without loops, run in Lua up to 10 Hz or in the engine up to 50 Hz by destination.
3. Lua's LFO traffic is bounded: at most 576 engine messages a second for all six, whatever the tempo, and none for the engine-run ones beyond their setting changes.
4. 16 macros (`NUM_MACROS` 16) in four banks of four (`MACRO_BANK_SIZE` 4), each with up to eight targets (`MACRO_TARGETS` 8) and a min and max per target, edited on MOD; their values are params (CC, LFOs, PSETs).
5. A morph param between two chosen scenes, writing params, never recalling.
6. Scatter: engine-side slow random modulation of chosen destinations on every unlocked voice, one switch.
7. Four gesture recorders (`NUM_GESTURES` 4) built on norns' `lib/pattern_time`, free or bar-quantized, stored per PSET; scenes store only whether each runs.
8. The sequencer additions of the roadmap, each step still a `retrigger` that creates no nodes, fired through registered step outputs, and patterns that follow norns' clock transport (on by default).
9. 16 scenes (`NUM_SCENES` 16) in two banks of eight (`SCENE_BANK_SIZE` 8), with names edited through norns' `textentry`; older files load into slots 1-8.
10. The MOD page, and the SEQ, LFO and SCENES pages, grid layouts and arc rings for all of the above, within the 128×64 rules.
11. A modulation source registry that every 3.4 modulator joins and MOD reads, so 3.5's and 3.6's sources need no MOD change.
12. A modulation matrix (`MATRIX_SLOTS` 16) from any registered source to any LFO destination except the macros, adding offsets in Lua or on the engine's offset bus, with no rise in Lua's message cap and one clamp per destination.

## Non-goals

- Polyphony, note allocation and chord editing (3.5). Step notes are stored as lists so 3.5 can play them as chords; 3.4 plays the first note on its voices.
- Per-note modulation (3.5). The fast-modulation bus has room for it (one note slot per voice in 3.4); nothing writes per note yet.
- MIDI out (notes, and LFOs and macros as CCs), crow, ii and nb (3.6). They plug into the step outputs (§8.10) and the modulation sources (§10.1). Transport following is in 3.4 (§8.11); Strata leading the transport is not (Question 26).
- Matrix routings per note (3.5): 3.4's routings act on the whole voice (note slot 0). Matrix routings to LFO params or matrix depths (Question 38), and LFOs on matrix depths.
- LFOs on LFO params through the destination list (LFO-to-LFO goes through the dedicated rate and depth inputs only, §2.4).
- An LFO, macro or morph on the morph position. Morph can be moved by hand, the grid, the arc, a MIDI CC and a gesture recorder.
- Elektron trig conditions (fill, A:B ratios), step slides, per-step micro-timing.
- Modulating discrete params (sources, envelope shapes, filter types) from LFOs, macros or scatter.

## Anti-goals

- Breaking a CM3. Everything here works on the standard tier; the engine part is control-rate only.
- Old scenes and PSETs that don't load or sound different. Every new scene field gets a default that reproduces 3.3: LFOs 4-6 off, no macro targets, scatter off, recorders stopped, every voice at 16 steps of 1/16, every step at 100% with one hit and no locks or notes, names empty. Old PSETs' LFO settings are imported.
- Messages that grow with the number of modulators. The fast destinations cost none per step; Lua's LFOs have a hard per-step cap.
- Creating nodes from the sequencer. A step, a ratchet, a lock, a note, a chain link and a probability draw are messages to existing nodes, or nothing.
- Hiding state. Engine-run LFOs, scatter and running recorders show on MOD and LFO, and on MAIN for the selected voice.
- Feedback loops in modulation: no LFO modulates itself or a lower-numbered LFO, no macro targets a macro or the morph, no matrix destination feeds a source (macros, LFO params and matrix depths are not destinations, §10.2), and an LFO on a macro skips the macro's LFO-param and matrix-depth targets (§4.4).

## Constraints

**Platform:**
- **scsynth's real-time pool is 8192 KB** (roadmap, Constraints). Control buses are not in it: scsynth allocates them at boot (`numControlBusChannels`, 16384 by default; norns' `Crone.sc` leaves the server options at their defaults, `sc/core/Crone.sc:61`). A synth's graph is in it: about 250 bytes per UGen (`docs/notes/engine/memory.md`).
- **Control buses sum within a block.** scsynth's `Out.kr` copies into a control bus for the first writer of a block and adds for every later writer in the same block (the bus's "touched" stamp); a bus nobody writes in a block keeps its last value. So several writers on one channel add, and a channel whose last writer is gone must be set to 0 by hand (`/c_set`).
- **matron polls carry one float**, and every `engine.*` command passes through sclang to scsynth. Each costs sclang more than a control-rate UGen costs scsynth, which is the case for moving fast modulation into the engine.
- **norns' clock**: `clock.sync(beats, offset)` resumes at the next multiple of `beats` plus `offset` (`lua/core/clock.lua:53-54`); `clock.get_beats()` is the transport's beat position. Clock coroutines are limited to 100 slots (the test stub mirrors it).
- **norns' `lib/pattern_time`** records `event` and `time` arrays with `util.time()` deltas and plays them with one metro per pattern (`lua/lib/pattern_time.lua:13-30`, `:113-137`). norns gives a script 30 metros (`lua/core/metro.lua:13`); Strata uses two today (drift, mutation).
- **norns' `lib/er`** gives Euclidean patterns, `er.gen(k, n, w)` (`lua/lib/er.lua`).
- **norns' `lib/textentry`** replaces `key`, `enc` and `redraw` while open and hands back the text, or nil (`lua/lib/textentry.lua:41-90`), as `fileselect` does.
- **PSET hooks**: `params.action_write(filename, name, number)`, `action_read(filename, silent, number)` and `action_delete(filename, name, number)` run after norns writes, reads or deletes a PSET (`lua/core/paramset.lua:518-519`, `:572-573`, `:592-593`). A PSET is `"<id>": <value>` lines, and `params:read` skips ids it doesn't know.
- **norns' LFO library's ceiling** is a 0.1 s period (10 Hz), and it calls its action on every step at its `ppqn`, in free mode too (a free LFO's phase advances by `1/ppqn` of a beat per step, converted through the beat's length), so its step rate follows the tempo. Strata sets 48 steps per beat (`lib/lfos.lua:43`) and skips sends under 1/1000 of the range (`lib/lfos.lua:46`). Its phase is readable (`get("phase_counter")`) and `change_period` keeps it continuous.

**Project:**
- Everything in AGENTS.md, in particular: Lua as the only source of values, `send_param_to_engine` and `S.engine_senders`, LFOs never `params:set` their destination, replace-never-stack and `sendToSynth`, `openedGate`, the velocity invariant (steps and notes never create nodes), no `LocalBuf`, the command table, the registries, one owner per file per wave, the locals probe (30 free locals per constructor), `request_redraw()`, arc rings through `arc_sweep_param` and `param_fraction`, grid handlers on the vport only.
- The real-time memory floors, as decided on 2026-11-08 for 3.3 and 3.4 (Question 3): 3 MB free on standard and 3.5 MB (3584 KB) on high at the worst case, at most 88 KB per grain synth, and 3 MB left on both tiers with eight recordings at once. Measured at 3.3.0 (re-planned 2026-11-11, #229; `docs/notes/engine/memory.md`): the high mixed worst case with one recording leaves 4320 KB free as printed and the tightest, synth → buffer → synth, 4190 (about 4210 and 4080 counting the spectral writers' late allocations); `\strataGrainRing` is 58.1 KB, `\strataGrainBuf` 67.4 KB and the stage 34.5 KB.

**Dependency on 3.3** (designed in parallel; the names below are fixed when the 3.3 design is approved):
- **Position bookmarks.** A step lock can recall one of a voice's four bookmarks. 3.3 adds exactly `S.lib.bookmarks.recall(voice_idx, n)` (n 1..`NUM_BOOKMARKS`, 4) and `S.lib.bookmarks.is_set(voice_idx, n)` (a valid n and a voice whose slot holds audio), on top of its `S.lib.positions` (`lib/positions.lua`). A recall outside the voice's loop resets the loop params (Question 25). Nothing else here depends on bookmarks.
- **The scene format.** 3.3's `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` is 4 (its bookmarks), so 3.4's is 5, and 3.4's migrations run after 3.3's.
- **Engine areas.** 3.3 adds record (130), waveform (140) and positions (150), so 3.4's modulation area is 160.
- **Grid.** 3.3 adds the 256's page 5 on row 8 column 12, the 128h's page 5 through its sources key and a bookmark strip on its page 4 (row 5 columns 13-16, row 6 columns 13-15), and five pages on the 128v. 3.4's keys (§11) avoid all of them.
- **The memory floor** was decided once for both releases (Question 3).
- **The per-voice mix stage** (3.3's package 0c, `docs/design/3.3-design.md` §0c). The voice filter, the voice-level gain, the sends and the mirror move out of every grain synth into one persistent `\strataVoiceMix` per voice (`lib/engine/StrataMix.sc`), and `grainBody` becomes `grainSignal` (grains, rate, pan and the synth's own fades) and one `Out`. 3.4 consumes §0c.7 of that design: the stage's `filterFreq` control; `voiceLevel(amp, modSig, accent)`, the one place the level is computed; the hook `voiceMixMod(controls) -> controls`, called before the filter banks and `voiceLevel` (3.4's 0c moves it to `StrataModulation.sc`, §0.3); and `areaState[\mix][\stageControls][v]`, the stage's start controls, to which the modulation area adds `\fastModIn`. §3.3 says how each offset lands.
- **Order of work.** 3.4's Wave 0 starts once 3.3's last package that edits `lib/scenes.lua`, `lib/engine/StrataGrainDefs.sc`, `lib/engine/StrataMix.sc` or a grid layout has merged.

## Approaches

### The LFOs

- **A. Keep norns' LFO library and add an engine LFO kind beside it (rejected).** Two LFO systems with two parameter sets, and the library still lacks the new shapes and LFO-to-LFO modulation.
- **B. Keep the library and work around it (rejected).** Run every LFO with the `up` shape, so its raw value is its phase, and compute the shapes in Strata; modulate rates by calling `set("period")` on every step (`change_period` keeps the phase continuous, and `get("phase_counter")` reads it). It keeps the PSET ids, but the shapes and engine rates live beside the library's own params, and free LFOs still step with the tempo.
- **C. Strata's own LFOs (chosen; Question 1).** One Lua clock steps all six at a fixed 48 Hz, so free LFOs no longer step with the tempo; clocked LFOs take their phase from `clock.get_beats()`. The new shapes are Strata's, the offset needs no wrapper, and the same parameter set drives a Lua-run and an engine-run LFO. The cost is new PSET ids, handled by an import (§2.9).

### Fast modulation in the engine

- **A. Per-voice control buses that every grain synth reads (chosen).** Each voice has five control channels per note slot, one per destination (one note slot per voice in 3.4, cross-release decision 1). Grain synths read them with one `In.kr` and add the offsets to their own controls (cutoff and amp in 3.3's per-voice mix stage instead, §3.3). Engine LFOs and scatter write them; several writers on a channel add up. Lua keeps sending the base values to the grain synth's controls exactly as now, so Lua stays the owner: with nothing written, the bus is 0 and the grain synth plays Lua's value. About 6 control-rate UGens per grain synth.
- **B. Map the grain controls to buses (`/n_map`) (rejected).** No UGens in the grain synths, but the base value would then have to live on the bus, written by a summing synth per modulated destination, and every grain synth created by a switch or a restart would need its maps. Lua's `setGrainParam` on a mapped control would no longer reach it, so Lua would stop owning the value.
- **C. Faster Lua (rejected).** Five destinations on seven voices at 200 Hz would be 7000 messages a second through sclang.

### Engine LFOs modulated by other LFOs

- **Lua computes every LFO-to-LFO effect (chosen).** Lua runs the phase and value of all six LFOs, engine-run or not. When an LFO modulates an engine-run LFO, Lua sends the target's new rate or depth (deduped, inside the message cap). Rate and depth modulation is slow by nature, so Lua's 48 Hz is enough, and the engine needs no LFO-to-LFO routing.
- **Engine-side routing (rejected).** Each engine LFO would also write its raw value to a bus and read its modulators' buses: a second command and graph per LFO, and the Lua-run case would still need Lua's version.

### Macros

- **Absolute writes (chosen; Question 4).** A macro at value v sets each target to `min + v × (max − min)` of its normalized range. The screen shows the values, scenes and PSETs capture them, and gesture recorders can loop the macro.
- **A modulation source (rejected).** The macro would add offsets around the targets' stored values without writing them, like an LFO. Nothing on screen would show the result.

### Morph

- **Write the interpolated params (chosen; the roadmap).** The result is the params, so it can be saved as a scene, and it works for every field a transition can fade.
- **Recall A or B at 0.5 (rejected).** No interpolation.
- **Crossfade two running sound states in the engine (rejected).** Two scenes can use different sources and voice types; the engine would need two of everything.

### Gesture recorders

- **`lib/pattern_time` for recording and free playback, a clock coroutine for quantized playback (chosen).** pattern_time records and overdubs as the roadmap asks, and plays free loops with its own metro. A quantized loop is converted to beats when its recording stops and played by a clock coroutine that starts each loop on its bar, so it stays locked to Link or MIDI clock where a metro would drift.
- **pattern_time only (rejected).** Its metro can't follow the clock.
- **A recorder of Strata's own (rejected).** It would redo what pattern_time does, against the roadmap.

### The sequencer clock

- **One coroutine per voice (chosen).** Each voice wakes only at its own steps (`clock.sync(division, offset)`), so a voice at 1/2 notes costs nothing between them, and ratchets are sub-syncs inside its step. Seven coroutines and the transport's are well inside the 100 slots.
- **One master clock at a common resolution (rejected).** Triplets, ratchets of 3 and 4, and 1/32 steps need 96 ticks per beat: 192 wake-ups a second at 120 BPM, mostly doing nothing.

### Pattern chaining

- **Chain the patterns stored in scenes (chosen; Question 19).** Scenes already store patterns (roadmap: "per-scene patterns, already stored"). A chain plays scene `start`'s patterns, then the next scene's, each for N bars, without touching the sound.
- **Separate pattern banks (rejected).** A second store for patterns, beside the scenes that already hold them.

### Scene banks

- **Numbers 1-16, banks as views (chosen; Question 15).** Every param that names a scene (`morph_a`, `scene_sequence_length`, the chain) is a plain number, and the header shows `S12`.
- **A1-B8 (rejected).** Two-part labels in every param and the header, for no gain.

**The base override and extra offsets** (`lib/engine_sync.lua`), for step locks and LFOs on macros:
- `S.lib.engine_sync.set_base_override(param_id, value)` and `clear_base_override(param_id)`: while an override is set, `with_lfo_offsets` and `apply_lfo` start from it instead of `params:get(param_id)`, and setting or clearing it sends at once. Only a hand edit of that param clears it (the `param_touch` hook, below): drift (`lib/mutation.lua:176`, `:182` set `grain_pitch` and `buf_pos` ten times a second), morph, macros and gesture playback all run param actions, and none of them may end a lock. While an override is set, a param action's send is held under it: the stored value changes, and the engine keeps the lock until the step ends.
- **Deferrable sends** (Question 23): `send_engine_value(id, value)` counts every send. While `S.deferrable_write` is set (Lua LFOs, morph, gesture playback, LFOs on macros and every macro apply set it around their writes, as the drift sets `S.drift_writing`; Question 29), a send beyond the budget (`ENGINE_BUDGET.messages_per_window`, 150 per 100 ms window) is kept as the id's pending value and goes first in the next window. Other sends always go at once and count against the window.
- `S.lib.engine_sync.set_extra_offset(source_key, param_id, offset)` (nil clears): offsets in the param's units, summed into `with_lfo_offsets` with the LFOs' and clamped with them. Lua destinations only: a fast destination's extras join the `setFastOffset` sum (§4.4, §10.2), so one clip applies.
- `S.lib.engine_sync.engine_base_value(param_id)`: the override or the stored value.

**A modal screen owner** (`lib/ui/modal.lua`, **(new)**, `S.lib.ui_modal`): `push(owner)` and `pop(owner)`, where `owner = {draw, hints, enc, key, arc_delta, arc_redraw}`. While one is pushed, `strata.lua`'s `enc`, `key` and `redraw` and `lib/arc/core.lua`'s dispatch go to it, after the file selector's check (`strata.lua:226`) and before the page. The step editor (§8.7) and the scene name entry (§9.3) use it. Page changes (E1) are ignored while it is up.

**Constants** (`lib/constants.lua`): `START_ORDER` and `CLEANUP_ORDER` entries for the new modules (§12 lists them), so feature packages add no order lines of their own.

**The PARAMS menu records** (`lib/params_setup.lua`, its local `register_params`, `:80`): 0a adds, in menu order, one record per new group (the six LFO groups, macros, morph, scatter, gestures, seq), each calling its module's `add_params` when that module is loaded and adding nothing otherwise. So no Wave 1 package edits `lib/params_setup.lua`.

**The quantized-action scheduler** (`lib/quantize.lua`, **(new)**, `S.lib.quantize.schedule(key, fn)`, §8.9) comes in Wave 0, running every action at once until 1f adds its param, so 1c, 1d and 1f's callers can use it in the same wave.

#### 0.2 The scene format (package 0b)

In `lib/scenes.lua`, with `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` and `NUM_SCENES` unchanged:
- **Flattening.** `S.lib.scenes.scene_entries(scene_data)` returns the ordered list a recall applies, `{id or key, value, kind}` (kind `continuous`, `discrete` or `block`), and `S.lib.scenes.apply_entries(entries, opts)` applies a list in recall order (voices stopped first, sources, params, discrete extras, blocks, voices started; `opts.silent_macros`). `recall_scene_instant` and `scene_transition_targets` are rewritten on top of them with the same order and the same results (a test compares both against today's for every voice state and source). Morph (§5) uses the same two functions.
- **Scene blocks and migrations** from the registries above. A block's optional `through_zero(a_value, b_value)` returns `{{id, switch = fn()}, ...}`: each entry's depth `id` then ramps through 0 and its `switch` runs once at the crossing (§10.2, `lib/matrix.lua`), in a transition and in a morph; an instant recall ignores it.
- **Slot names** are written in `scenes.data` as now (`S.scenes[i].name`), but a name equal to the old default `scene <i>` loads as `""` (§9.3).
- **The LFO fields leave `S.SCENE_GLOBAL_PARAMS`**: the list at `lib/scenes.lua:88-97` moves, unchanged in ids, order and defaults, into `S.lib.lfos.SCENE_FIELDS` (in `lib/lfos.lua`), which `lib/scenes.lua` appends to the globals at the same point. So 1b can change the LFO fields without editing `lib/scenes.lua`.
- Every loop over scenes reads `NUM_SCENES` (still 8 here), and no code assumes a slot number below 10 (two-digit labels).
- A recall's lock states run the `lock_change` hooks (§0.1), as `toggle_lock` does.

#### 0.3 The engine skeleton (package 0c)

- **A new area, `lib/engine/StrataModulation.sc`** (**(new)**; method `strataAreaModulation`, area name `modulation`, `order` 160, after 3.2's mix at 120 and 3.3's record, waveform and positions at 130-150). In Wave 0 it allocates the buses (§3.2) with their addressing methods (`fastModNoteSlots`, 1; `fastModChannel(voice, noteSlot, destination)`; `fastModBlock(voice, noteSlot)`), registers the five commands of §3.4 and §10.2 with their argument checks (`setFastOffset` also ignores a non-finite offset with a console line, §10.2), records what they receive in `areaState[\modulation]`, and creates no node.
- **The grain SynthDefs** (`lib/engine/StrataGrainDefs.sc`) gain one control, `fastModIn` (the voice's bus, given through `grainControls` as 3.2's mix area gives its buses; default `silentFastModBus`), and both SynthDef functions read it through `this.fastModRead(fastModIn)` (the modulation area's; neutral in Wave 0: five zeros, no UGen) before they build their controls Event and reader (`StrataGrainDefs.sc:93`, `:136-150`), so position can be applied before `slotGrainReader` (§3.3). `grainSignal` (3.3 0c's, what is left of `grainBody`, `:178`) applies pitch and size through `this.fastMod(controls)`, neutral in Wave 0; the amp channel stays in the Event, read and unused until 3.5.
- **The per-voice mix stage** (`\strataVoiceMix`, `lib/engine/StrataMix.sc`). **The hook moves to the modulation area:** 0c adds `voiceMixMod(controls) -> controls` to `StrataModulation.sc` and deletes 3.3's neutral one from `StrataMix.sc` in the same commit, so the method is never defined twice. 1a follows 0c and never edits `StrataMix.sc`: it fills `voiceMixMod` where it now lives. The hook declares the stage's `fastModIn` control as a named control (`\fastModIn.kr(silentFastModBus.index)`, as `voiceOutput` declares `fxMixIn`), adds `\ampOffset` (0) to the Event and otherwise returns it unchanged: neutral in Wave 0, no UGen. The modulation area's `allocState` puts `\fastModIn` = `fastModBlock(v, 0)` into `areaState[\mix][\stageControls][v]` for every voice. **The stage's two level lines** (Question 40), the only other change to `StrataMix.sc`, become `other = fo × voiceLevel(amp, modSig + controls[\ampOffset], accent)` and `sig = fs × voiceLevel(1, controls[\ampOffset], 0) + other`; with `\ampOffset` 0 both fold to 3.3's graph.
- **The check area** `test/engine_check/modulation.scd` (**(new)**): the commands exist with their signatures and ranges, create no node, and leave the buses at 0. ### 2. The LFOs

#### 2.1 Parameters

`lib/lfos.lua` is rewritten (package 1b): norns' `lib/lfo.lua` is no longer used. Each LFO n (1-6) has a group `strata_lfo_<n>` ("LFO n", 13 params; groups can't nest, so the six groups replace today's `strata_lfos` group and the library's `lfo_group_<n>`):

| Param | Kind, range | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `strata_lfo<n>` | binary toggle | 0 (off) | on/off |
| `strata_lfo<n>_dest` | option (the destination list, §2.3) | 1 (none) | the id stays today's, so stored indices keep their meaning |
| `strata_lfo<n>_shape` | option: sine, tri, square, up, down, s&h, smooth, drunk | 1 (sine) | §2.2 |
| `strata_lfo<n>_mode` | option: free, clocked | 1 (free) | |
| `strata_lfo<n>_rate` | control 0.003-50 Hz, exp | 0.25 Hz (a 4 s period, today's default) | free mode; Lua-run LFOs play at most 10 Hz (§2.5). The minimum takes the library's longest free period (300 s, `lfo_free_<n>`) exactly |
| `strata_lfo<n>_division` | option: the library's 22 clocked rates, `1/16` ... `1024` | 13 (`4`) | clocked mode; a rate r is a period of 4r beats, as the library's |
| `strata_lfo<n>_depth` | control 0-100 %, step 1 | 25 % | 3.1's default |
| `strata_lfo<n>_offset` | control -100..100 %, step 1 | 0 | moves the destination by offset % of its range, as the library's (`docs/notes/lua/pages/lfo.md`) |

| `strata_lfo<n>_rate_mod_amt` | control -100..100 % | 0 | ±2 octaves at 100 % |
| `strata_lfo<n>_depth_mod` | option: none, L1 ... L(n−1) | 1 (none) | §2.4 |
| `strata_lfo<n>_depth_mod_amt` | control -100..100 % | 0 | §2.4 |

LFO 1's mod options are only `none`. The library's min, max, baseline and reset params are gone: Strata's LFOs always run from -1 to 1 around the stored value, which is what `docs/user/params.md` told users to keep.

#### 2.2 Shapes

Every shape is a function of the phase φ (0-1) giving u in 0..1; the LFO's output is `m = (2u − 1) × depth + 2 × offset` in half-range units (−1..1 is ± half the destination's range), so depth 100 % sweeps the whole range, as today.

| Shape | u(φ) |
|---|---|
| sine | (sin 2πφ + 1) / 2 |
| tri | 2φ for φ < ½, 2 − 2φ after |
| square | 1 for φ < ½, else 0 |
| up / down | φ / 1 − φ |
| s&h | a new uniform value at each cycle start, held |
| smooth | linear from the previous s&h value to the next over the cycle |
| drunk | a random walk: at each cycle start, the last value plus a uniform step of ±0.2, reflected at 0 and 1; linear between, like smooth |

The library's `random` changed value twice per cycle; scenes and PSETs that used it become s&h at twice the rate (§2.9).

#### 2.3 Destinations

The destination list keeps its order: the 68 built-in entries (`lib/lfos.lua:55-87`), then the registered ones in registration order. 3.2 registers `v<i> hist`, `vfilt`, `dsend` and `rsend`. 3.4 appends:
- `v<i> amp` (registered by `lib/lfos.lua`, suffix `amp`): the voice's level, run in the engine (§3.3);
- `macro 1` ... `macro 16` (global entries, registered by `lib/macros.lua`, **(new)**): an LFO on a macro (§4.4). `macro 16` is 8 characters, inside the right column.

**Five destinations run in the engine** (Question 2): `v<i> pitch` (`grain_pitch`), `v<i> pos` (`buf_pos`), `v<i> size` (`grain_size`), `v<i> vfilt` (3.2's `filter_freq`) and `v<i> amp`. The LFO page marks them with `~`. Every other destination, `v<i> main` included (it can resolve to any param), runs in Lua as now. `S.lib.lfos.FAST_DESTINATIONS` maps each param suffix to its engine destination index (0 pitch, 1 position, 2 size, 3 cutoff, 4 amp).

The same math applies on both sides, so an old scene's LFO on `v3 pitch` sounds as it did, only smoother: the engine adds `m` × half the range to the stored value and clamps, which is what Lua's `with_lfo_offsets` does. Two exceptions, both on destinations 3.2 added and no released scene uses: the voice filter cutoff moves in its exponential warp (±half its ten octaves at 100 %), and amp moves the level by ±depth (§3.3).

#### 2.4 LFO-to-LFO modulation

Each LFO has a rate input and a depth input, each reading one lower-numbered LFO. LFO n's sources are 1..n−1, so the graph is acyclic (no LFO modulates itself or a lower-numbered one, Anti-goals) and Lua evaluates the six in index order on every step. The source's value is its bipolar shape value s = 2u − 1, before its own depth and destination, so a modulator needs no destination (`none` is the usual choice). A source that is off contributes 0.
- **Rate:** `rate' = rate × 4 ^ (amount × s)`, so ±2 octaves at 100 %, clamped to 0.003 Hz and the run's ceiling. In clocked mode the division's rate is multiplied the same way.
- **Depth:** `depth' = clamp(depth × (1 + amount × s), 0, 100 %)`, so depth 0 stays 0.

For an engine-run target, Lua sends `setFastLfo` with the new rate when it moves by more than 0.5 % (`LFO.engine_rate_threshold`) and with the new depth when it moves by more than 1/1000. These sends count against the per-step cap.

#### 2.5 The Lua clock and the message budget

- One clock coroutine (`S.lfo_clock`) wakes every 1/48 s (`clock.sleep`), in free time, and steps every running LFO in index order. The phase of a free LFO advances by `rate' × elapsed` (elapsed from `util.time()`, so a late wake-up doesn't slow it). A clocked LFO's phase is `(clock.get_beats() / period_beats + phase) mod 1`, exact against the clock (Link and MIDI clock included) with no drift. The phase is known exactly, so the LFO page draws its dot from it.
- A Lua-run LFO above 10 Hz plays at 10 Hz; the LFO page shows its rate followed by `max`.
- **Sends:** each Lua-run LFO's destination is sent through `send_engine_value` as now, skipped under 1/1000 of the range (`LFO.send_threshold`). At most 12 sends go out per step (`LFO.sends_per_step`): the destinations due are queued in a fixed round-robin order, so none starves and a skipped one goes out on the next step with its newest value. So Lua's LFOs never send more than 576 messages a second together (today: 3 × 96 = 288 at 120 BPM, 720 at 300 BPM), the matrix's sends included (it queues its destinations in the same round robin, §10.2), and an LFO on a macro (up to eight targets) can't flood sclang. Their sends are also deferrable under the overall budget (§0.1, Question 23).
- Engine-run LFOs send nothing per step, only their setting changes (§3.4).

#### 2.6 Engine-run LFOs, from Lua

- An LFO runs in the engine while it is on and its destination is one of the five. Lua sends `setFastLfo(n − 1, voice − 1, destination, shape − 1, rate', depth' / 100, 2 × offset / 100)` when it starts and whenever a setting or a modulated value changes, and `stopFastLfo(n − 1)` when it stops or its destination stops being an engine one.
- **Phase.** After a start or a shape change Lua sends `syncFastLfo(n − 1, φ)` with its own phase. A clocked LFO is also resynced once per bar (every 4 beats, from Lua's clock), and at once on a transport start (§8.11), so it follows the transport; a resync moves the phase by the drift since the last one plus the message's latency (under a millisecond on norns). Free LFOs are not resynced, since a resync is an audible jump at high rates: Lua's and the engine's clocks drift by tens of parts per million (tens to hundreds of milliseconds an hour), and more under rate modulation, where Lua integrates a rate it sends only in 0.5 % steps. So for a free engine-run LFO the LFO page's dot is an estimate, drawn hollow (a 3×3 outline instead of the filled dot); the sound never depends on it.
- **Random shapes** draw their own values in the engine; the page's wave and dot show Lua's draws, statistically the same, not the same values.
- A destination change between two engine destinations is one `setFastLfo` (the engine moves the node's bus and zeroes the old channel, §3.4).

#### 2.7 The rules that stay

- LFOs modulate around the stored value and never `params:set` their destination.
- A destination change, turning an LFO off and depth 0 restore the destination: Lua-run, `restore_lfo_destination` as now; engine-run, `stopFastLfo`, whose release ends at 0.
- Every destination is a control param and every label fits the LFO page's right column (49 px; `macro 16` and `v7 amp~` are the longest new ones).
- Cleanup (step 120) stops the clock, sends `stopFastLfo` for every engine-run LFO and restores every Lua-run destination.

#### 2.8 What the scenes store

Per LFO, in recall order (`S.lib.lfos.SCENE_FIELDS`): shape, mode, division, rate, depth, offset, phase, the four mod params, destination, then the state last, as now. Rate, depth and offset crossfade (today's free period, depth and offset did); the rest switch at a transition's end. The phase and the four mod params are new scene fields (scenes store no LFO phase today, `lib/scenes.lua:88-97`), migrating to 0 and none; LFOs 4-6 migrate to off.

#### 2.9 Old PSETs and scenes

- **PSETs.** A PSET hook (§0.1) reads the PSET file after norns has read it. If it holds `lfo_shape_1` and no `strata_lfo1_shape`, it converts each LFO n = 1..3: `lfo_<n>` (1 off, 2 on) to `strata_lfo<n>`; `lfo_shape_<n>` (sine, tri, square, random, up, down) to sine, tri, square, s&h (rate doubled), up, down; `lfo_mode_<n>` (1 clocked, 2 free) to free or clocked; `lfo_clocked_<n>` to the same division index; `lfo_free_<n>` (a period in seconds, 0.1-300) to `rate = 1 / period` (within 0.003-10 Hz, so no import clamps); `lfo_depth_<n>`, `lfo_offset_<n>` and `lfo_phase_<n>` to the same percentages. `strata_lfo<n>_dest` already loaded. It prints `imported 3.3 LFO settings`. The PSET is not rewritten; the next save writes the new ids.
- **Scenes.** A registered migration (`before_version` 5) converts the `lfo_*` globals the same way (no phase: scenes never stored one) and drops them.

### 3. Fast modulation in the engine

#### 3.1 What it does

Five destinations per voice get an engine-side offset: grain pitch (the grain rate, `pitchShift`), position (`bufPos`, buffer voices only, as `buf_pos` today), grain size (`grainSize`), the voice filter's cutoff (the per-voice mix stage's `filterFreq`, audible while the filter's mix is above 0) and amp (the voice's level in the stage, `voiceLevel`). The offsets come from engine LFOs (§2.6) and scatter (§6). Lua keeps sending every base value as now, to the grain synth's controls and, through 3.3 0c's handlers, to the stage's; the engine adds the offsets inside the grain synth (pitch, position, size) and the stage (cutoff, amp). With no LFO and no scatter the buses read 0 and both play exactly what Lua sent.

#### 3.2 Buses and groups

In the modulation area's `allocState`, in `areaState[\modulation]`:
- `fastModBus`: one control bus of `numVoices × fastModNoteSlots × numFastDestinations` channels, 35 in 3.4. It is indexed by (voice, note slot, destination) (cross-release decision 1): voice v's note slot n, destination d, is channel `index + (v × fastModNoteSlots + n) × numFastDestinations + d`, computed only by `fastModChannel(v, n, d)`; `fastModBlock(v, n)` is the block's first channel (d = 0). Each block's five channels are contiguous, so a grain synth reads its block with one `In.kr`. 3.4 has one note slot per voice (`fastModNoteSlots` 1, n = 0 everywhere), so the channels are exactly `index + 5v + d`, and the whole bus is contiguous, so scatter writes all 35 with one `Out.kr`.
- **What 3.5 changes** (and what it must not): it raises `fastModNoteSlots` to its tier's note limit (for example 6 on high: 210 channels of the server's 16384, still no real-time memory), points each note's grain synth at `fastModBlock(v, its slot)`, and adds per-note writers (MPE pressure and slide). The formula, the one `In.kr` per grain synth, the five commands' meaning (an engine LFO, scatter and a matrix offset act on what they address; `setFastOffset` already takes a note slot) and Lua's ownership stay. How a voice-wide writer reaches every note slot of its voice (one write per slot, or a shared voice block that note synths also read) is 3.5's to decide; 3.4 only guarantees that every writer goes through `fastModChannel`, so that choice touches one method. The mix stage reads note slot 0's cutoff and amp channels: the filter and the voice-level gain are per voice, so a per-note cutoff is not something the bus can give it (3.5 decides what MPE on cutoff means). Per-note amp is the grain synths' own: from 3.5 each reads its note's amp channel as a per-synth gain before the voice filter.
- `silentFastModBus`: 5 channels, always 0. It is the SynthDef default of `fastModIn` (grain synths and the stage), never control bus 0 (norns' input follower), and never `silentBus` (one channel).
- `fastModGroup`: a group at the head of `context.xg`, before `voiceGroup`, so offsets are written before any grain synth reads them in the same block. It holds the engine LFOs, scatter and the matrix's offset node (`\strataFastOffsets`, §10.2), which Lua writes. `docs/notes/engine/structure.md`'s node order and the structure check gain it.
- `grainControls[v][\fastModIn]` = `fastModBlock(v, 0)`, set for every voice at alloc, so every grain synth, replacements and restarts included, reads its voice's block. The modulation area's `allocState` also puts `\fastModIn` = `fastModBlock(v, 0)` into `areaState[\mix][\stageControls][v]` (the mix area, order 120, has made it by then), and the stage starts with it.

No real-time memory: control buses are allocated at server boot.

#### 3.3 In the grain synths

With `m = this.fastModRead(fastModIn)` (one `In.kr` of the block's 5 channels, read once per SynthDef function), the grain synths use four of the channels: pitch, position, size and amp. The cutoff, channel 3, lies between size and amp in the block, so one `In.kr` of all five costs less than two reads, and that output goes unused. Position is applied in `\strataGrainBuf`'s function itself, to `bufPos` before `slotGrainReader` (`StrataGrainDefs.sc:150`) and 3.3's `slotScanPhase` and `slotHeadGuard` see it, because `bufPos` goes straight to the reader and is not in the controls Event (`:136-148`). `m` then goes into the Event, and `this.fastMod(controls)` (`StrataModulation.sc`), called inside `grainSignal`, replaces pitch and size before `grainRate`, `grainDur` and the readers see them. `\strataGrainRing` reads the same block and ignores position. Both ignore the cutoff channel, since after 3.3 0c the grain synths have no filter. The amp channel stays in the Event, read and unused until 3.5 (maintainer's decision): after 3.3 0c a grain synth has no level of its own, and 3.5's per-note amp becomes a per-synth gain before the voice filter.

**Cutoff and amp, in the per-voice mix stage.** `voiceMixMod(controls)` (`StrataModulation.sc`) reads the stage's `fastModIn` block with one `In.kr` of 5 channels, replaces the Event's `filterFreq` by `(filterFreq × 31.62 ^ m3).clip(20, 20000)` before both filter banks, and sets `\ampOffset` to m4, which the stage's two level lines (§0.3, Question 40) pass through `voiceLevel`. There is one stage per voice, so cutoff and amp cost seven readers, not one per grain synth.

| Destination | Applied as | Same as |
|---|---|---|
| 0 pitch | `pitchShift' = (pitchShift + 1.875 × m0).clip(0.25, 4)` | a Lua LFO on `grain_pitch` (0.25-4, linear) |
| 1 position | `bufPos' = (bufPos + 0.5 × m1).clip(0, 1)` (`\strataGrainBuf` only) | a Lua LFO on `buf_pos` |
| 2 size | `grainSize' = (grainSize + 0.245 × m2).clip(0.01, 0.5)` | a Lua LFO on `grain_size` |
| 3 cutoff | in the per-voice mix stage (`voiceMixMod`, before both filter banks): `filterFreq' = (filterFreq × 31.62 ^ m3).clip(20, 20000)` | ± half of 20-20000 Hz's ten octaves |
| 4 amp | in the per-voice mix stage, through `voiceLevel`: `(amp × (1 + modSig + m4) × (1 + accent)).clip(0, 2)` on the other path, `(1 + m4).clip(0, 2)` on the synth path | the amplitude crossmod's law: depth 100 % moves the level from silence to double |

- The half ranges are the Lua specs' (`lib/params_setup.lua:734`, `:764`, `:686`; 3.2's `filter_freq`). The engine check compares them with the Lua specs, as it compares the FX defaults today.
- Amp works for every source: the other path's level (buffer and input sources) and, on the synth path, a gain of its own after the filter, since the voice synth has already applied the stored amp, crossmod and accent there. It does not depend on crossmod.
- The rules that stay: the ring reader still clips the rate to ±4 and `grainDur` still caps at 0.5 s, so the lookback bound of `docs/notes/engine/rings.md` holds whatever the offset; `velocity` still feeds only the accent's `levelScale`, so the velocity check passes unchanged, the stage included (3.3's invariant).
- The stage's `voiceFilter` keeps its 0.05 s lag on `filterFreq`, so a cutoff LFO's depth falls above about 20 Hz, to about 40 % at 50 Hz. The other four destinations aren't smoothed by the graph.

#### 3.4 Engine LFOs, scatter and the commands

**SynthDefs** (in `StrataModulation.sc`, built by one function):
- `\strataFastLfoSine`, `...Tri`, `...Square`, `...Up`, `...Down`, `...Sah`, `...Smooth`, `...Drunk`: one SynthDef per shape (about 10 control-rate UGens each, against about 35 for one def selecting among eight). Controls: `out` (one channel), `rate`, `depth`, `offset`, `t_sync`, `syncPhase`, `gate`. A `Phasor.kr` over 0-1 at `rate × ControlDur`, reset by `t_sync` to `syncPhase`; the shape of §2.2 from the phase (s&h, smooth and drunk latch a `WhiteNoise.kr` draw, or a `Dbrown` step through `Demand.kr`, on the phase's wrap); `depth` lagged 20 ms; an ASR envelope through `openedGate(gate)` with 20 ms attack and release, `doneAction: 2`; `Out.kr(out, ((2u − 1) × depth + offset) × env)`.
- `\strataScatter`: 35 `LFDNoise3.kr` at `rate × (0.8 … 1.2)` (a fixed spread per channel, drawn at build, so voices and destinations never move together), each times a gain from a 35-element control (`gains`: depth × the destination's and the voice's enable bits), through `openedGate(gate)` with 0.5 s attack and release, into `Out.kr(fastModBus index, 35 channels)`.

**State and lifecycle:**
- At most one node per LFO (`areaState[\modulation][\lfos][n]`) and one scatter node, all in `fastModGroup`. A shape change replaces the LFO's node with `Synth.replace` (the old node goes in the same server step), as 3.2's spectral writer swap does; nothing else creates a node.
- They can end on their own (their release), so every message to them goes through `sendToSynth`. When an LFO's node ends (`onFree`) the engine sets its channel to 0 (`/c_set`) unless another node of the same LFO now writes it; other writers on that channel write it again in the next block, so the sum stays right. Scatter's end zeroes all 35 channels the same way, and every remaining LFO writes its own again in the next block.
- `stop` (the area's) frees every node; `free` frees the buses.

**Commands** (rows for AGENTS.md's table, added by 0c):

| Lua Command | SC Signature | Purpose |
|-------------|--------------|---------|
| `engine.setFastLfo(lfo, voice, destination, shape, rate, depth, offset)` | `"iiiifff"` | Engine LFO 0-5 on voice 0-6's fast destination 0-4 (pitch, position, size, cutoff, amp), for the whole voice (note slot 0 in 3.4): shape 0-7 (sine, tri, square, up, down, s&h, smooth, drunk), rate 0.003-50 Hz, depth 0-1, offset -2..2 (half ranges). Creates the LFO's node if it has none, else sets it; a new shape replaces the node, a new voice or destination moves it and zeroes the old channel. Never more than one node per LFO |
| `engine.stopFastLfo(lfo)` | `"i"` | Release LFO 0-5's node (20 ms) and zero its channel once it ends. No-op without one |
| `engine.syncFastLfo(lfo, phase)` | `"if"` | Set LFO 0-5's phase (0-1) now. No-op without a node |
| `engine.setScatter(depth, rate, destinations, voices)` | `"ffii"` | Scatter: depth 0-1 (0 releases the node over 0.5 s), rate 0.01-1 Hz, destinations a 5-bit mask (bit d = destination d), voices a 7-bit mask (bit v = voice v). Creates the node when depth rises above 0 |

Out-of-range indices are ignored with a console line, as the crossmod commands ignore an unknown slot; the Lua engine stub rejects them.

#### 3.5 How Lua stays the owner

- The grain synth's and the stage's own controls hold Lua's values, set by `setGrainParam` and the voice sync exactly as now. The buses hold only offsets, which the engine never folds into those controls. The matrix's offset node is the one bus writer whose values Lua computes, and they too are offsets only. Restarting a voice, switching its source or recalling a scene sends the base values as always; the offsets carry on from the bus.
- The engine keeps no base values of its own, so there is nothing for Lua to resync after a switch: a replacement grain synth reads the same bus.
- What Lua shows (the screen, the arc, the grid faders) is the stored value, as with Lua LFOs.

The tightest is synth → buffer → synth on high, about 400 KB above the floor counting the late allocations; the earlier estimate's tightest was the all-buffer case (255-285 KB above), which leaves the most now, since it has no voice synths and no spectral writer.

**Per synth** (3.3.0 measured, plus 3.4's additions above; 1a re-measures): `\strataGrainBuf` 67.4 + about 1.5 = about 68.9 KB, 19.1 KB under the 88 KB limit decided on 2026-11-08; `\strataGrainRing` 58.1 + about 1.5 = about 59.6 KB, 28.4 KB under it; and `\strataVoiceMix` 34.5 + about 2.25 = about 36.75 KB with 3.4's offsets, 3.25 KB under 3.3's 40 KB check for it.

**What is measured and what is estimated.** Every 3.3 figure is 3.3.0's, measured at the release in full runs of the engine check, four on SuperCollider 3.13.0 in Docker and two on 3.14.1 (`docs/notes/engine/memory.md`). All six agree except the two high cases with a recording, which read 20 KB higher in one 3.14.1 run; the table takes the other five runs' figures. The memory area run alone reads 100-120 KB more free in every case, and each def about 0.8 KB less; the cause was not found, and the full-run figures are the ones used. The free figures are printed to 10 KB. Every 3.4 figure is estimated at 250 bytes per UGen, which 1a measures. The sums above are unrounded; the rest of this design rounds them to 10 KB. A stage is 3.3.0's measured 34.5 KB plus 3.4's cutoff and amp offsets (about 9 UGens, 2.25 KB): about 36.75 KB. 3.4's offsets don't push it past 3.3's 40 KB check, so `vfilt` and `amp` stay in the stage as designed and nothing moves; 3.4 keeps that check and needs none of its own. 3.25 KB of headroom is left (the offsets could cost about twice the estimate and still fit), and if 1a measures the stage above 40 KB it reports that before going on.

**What 3.5 gets.** 3.3's §0c.6 counts extra notes per voice that fit in what the high worst case leaves with 3.4 and one recording, 1 MB kept back, at about 1490 KB a note. The mixed worst case is now synth → buffer → synth: 4190 − 112 (the late allocations) − 97 (3.4) − 30 (one recording, measured at 3.3.0: 4350 against 4320 KB at the spaced case) = 3951 KB, and two extra notes (2 × 1490 = 2980 KB) leave 971 KB. So **three notes per voice leave about 970 KB at the mixed worst case, just under the 1024 KB kept back** (the estimate said about 1.07 MB, at the spaced case, which now leaves 4111 − 2980 = 1131 KB). The all-buffer worst case leaves 4813 − 2980 = 1833 KB at three notes (the estimate fitted two there). The 970 KB rests on §0c.6's 1490 KB a note, built from 0c's estimated streams (about 59 KB on average), and is the figure #229 and `docs/notes/engine/memory.md` use. 3.3.0's measured streams with 3.4's additions are larger: about 59.6 KB a ring stream and 68.9 KB a buffer stream, two to one as at the mixed worst case, (2 × 59.6 + 68.9) / 3 = about 62.7 KB. If only the streams grew and the voice synths' share of a note stays §0c.6's, a note is 1490 + 21 × (62.7 − 59) = about 1568 KB; if the whole note grows with them, it is 1490 × 62.7 / 59 = about 1583 KB. That is about 1570-1580 KB a note, and 3951 − 2 × (1568 to 1583) leaves about 780-820 KB at three notes. 3.5's design re-measures both the base and the note before it settles the count; a lifecycle that keeps no voice synth in the cutting set gives back the margin.

- Eight recordings at once must still leave 3 MB on both tiers (3.3's rule); 3.4 adds no recording-time memory, so its 97 KB counts there once.
- The `memory` check area runs the worst case with six engine LFOs and scatter running on every voice, on both tiers, against the decided floors, 3.3's all-buffer worst case (21 `\strataGrainBuf`, one recording) and synth → buffer → synth (#502, the tightest) included. 1a measures before anything else is built and reports if an estimate was wrong.
- The check area also asserts that no SynthDef of the area has a `LocalBuf`, a delay line or a table sized from a control.

**CPU** (a control-rate UGen costs about 0.1 µs per block on a CM3, at 750 blocks a second; to be measured on #6):
- worst case: 21 grain synths × 6 + 7 mix stages × 9 + 6 LFOs × 10 + 72 + 37 (the offset node) = 358 control-rate UGens, about 27 ms a second, about 2.7 % of one CM3 core;
- typical (seven running voices, two engine LFOs, scatter off): 7 × 6 + 7 × 9 + 20 + 37 = about 160 UGens, about 1.2 % (the stages and the offset node always run);
- against that, every engine-run LFO saves up to 96 sclang commands a second, each of which costs sclang more than this whole budget costs scsynth per block.

### 4. Macros

#### 4.1 Model

- 16 macros (`NUM_MACROS` 16, cross-release decision 6), each a param `macro_<m>` (m 1-16; control 0-1, default 0, shown in percent) in a group `strata_macros` ("macros", 16 params, added in one loop) after the LFO groups. They form four banks of four (`MACRO_BANK_SIZE` 4; bank b holds macros 4b − 3 .. 4b), a view only, as scene banks are (§4.6).
- Each macro has up to eight targets (`MACRO_TARGETS` 8): `S.macros[m].targets[k] = {id = <param id or "">, min = 0, max = 1}`, min and max being fractions of the target's normalized range (its controlspec's warp); min above max inverts.
- **Applying** (`S.lib.macros.apply(m)`): the param's action schedules it for the next clock tick (`clock.run` with `clock.sleep(0)`, coalescing repeated moves of one macro into one apply), and no sooner than 1/30 s after that macro's last apply (`MACROS.apply_hz` 30), so a fast arc ring or a CC stream never applies one macro more than 30 times a second. One coroutine serves every macro (a pending set and a next-due time per macro), never one per macro, so 16 macros cost one of the 100 clock slots. norns' `params:read` runs every param's action before it calls `action_read`, so an immediate apply during a PSET load would use the old targets; the PSET hook (§4.3) cancels every pending apply, then loads the PSET's targets, and the targets' own values come from the PSET itself. Then, for each target with an id, `params:set(id, spec:map(min + v × (max − min)))`. So the targets' actions send to the engine, the screen shows the values and scenes capture them. A macro with no target does nothing.
- **Silent sets.** A scene recall, a transition and morph set `macro_<m>` without applying it (`S.lib.macros.set_silently(m, v)`, `params:set(id, v, true)`), because the scene already holds each target's value. Only a hand move (encoder, arc, grid fader), a MIDI CC, a PSET load and a gesture recorder apply.
- **Messages:** one per target and apply: an encoder at 30 detents a second on a full macro is 240 a second, the rate of turning eight knobs. Every apply's target sends are deferrable (Question 29, §4.7).

#### 4.2 What a macro can target

`S.lib.macros.target_ids()`: every LFO destination (§2.3) except the macros themselves, plus each LFO's `strata_lfo<n>_rate` and `strata_lfo<n>_depth`, plus each routing's `matrix_<k>_depth` (Question 38). Never another macro, the morph params, a discrete param or a non-control param, so a macro can't start a loop or flip a source. `v<i> main` resolves when the macro applies, so it follows the voice's source. An LFO on a macro moves only part of this list (§4.4).

#### 4.3 Storage

- **Values** are params: PSETs store them, MIDI CCs map to them through PARAMS > MAP, and scenes store them as continuous globals set silently.
- **Targets** are not params (Question 5): they are stored by param id.
  - In scenes as a block (`data.macros`, registered by `lib/macros.lua`: discrete, applied at a transition's end before the macro values), sparse: `data.macros[m][k]` only for targets with an id, so a scene with no targets stores an empty table and a full one (16 × 8) about 6 KB.
  - Per PSET in `<pset path base>.macros.data` (a PSET hook writes, reads and deletes it), sparse the same way (at most about 6 KB).
  - Live in `data/strata/macros.data`, saved at cleanup and loaded at init like `scenes.data` (with its `pcall` and move-aside rules), so the macros survive a restart without a PSET.
- An unknown id (a param a later or earlier release doesn't have) is kept verbatim with a console line, never a failed file, and re-saving keeps it, as the matrix's unknown parts are (§10.2). While it is unknown it does nothing: an apply and an LFO on the macro skip it, it doesn't count as a target for the bank's mark (§4.6), and MOD draws it at level 4 with the stored text cut with `fit_text`.

#### 4.4 What reaches a macro

- **MIDI CC** through PARAMS > MAP (it is a control param).
- **LFOs** (`macro <m>` destinations, Lua-run). An LFO never `params:set`s its destination, so it can't move the macro param. Instead it moves the macro's targets around their stored values: each step it computes the macro at `v + offset` and, per target, the difference from the target at `v`. The targets' engine values move; their params don't. **It skips LFO-param and matrix-depth targets** (`strata_lfo<n>_rate`, `strata_lfo<n>_depth`, `matrix_<k>_depth`): LFO n on a macro that targets `strata_lfo<n>_depth` would modulate itself, on a matrix depth it is what Question 38 forbids, and neither has an engine sender for an extra offset. Those targets still follow the macro's own value; only the LFO's offset leaves them alone. A target on a Lua destination takes the difference as its extra offset (`set_extra_offset("macro<m>", id, ...)`, §0.1). A target on a fast destination (§2.3's five) goes into the `setFastOffset` sum instead: `S.lib.matrix.set_fast_extra("macro<m>", id, m)` (1h), with `m` the difference in half ranges from `S.lib.lfos.m_for(id, base, value)` (1b, §3.3's law for each destination), so that Lua's clamp and the engine's clip never both act on it (§10.2). This counts up to eight sends per step against the cap (§2.5).
- **Gesture recorders** record and replay macro moves (§7).
- **Morph and scenes** set macro values silently (above).

#### 4.5 Learn

On MOD's target view, ALT+K3 arms learn for the cursor's target row: the next control param touched by hand (the `param_touch` registry, §0.1) that is a valid target becomes the row's id, with min 0 and max 1, and the flash shows `M12 > v3 pitch`. A touched param that isn't a target flashes `not a macro target`. K2, a page change or 10 s (`MACROS.learn_seconds`) cancels.

#### 4.6 Banks (cross-release decision 6)

- **One shown bank** (Question 28): `S.lib.macros.bank()` (1..`NUM_MACROS / MACRO_BANK_SIZE`, 4) and `S.lib.macros.set_bank(b)`. MOD's list (§10), MOD's arc rings, the 256's rows 10-13 and the 128h and 128v macro fader pages (§11) all show it, and changing it from any of them changes it for all, with a flash (`macros 5-8`), `request_redraw()` and `grid_redraw()`.
- It is session state: not a param, not in scenes or PSETs, bank 1 at start. A bank change moves no macro and sends nothing.
- `S.lib.macros.bank_macros(b)` lists bank b's four macro numbers; every surface draws and edits through it, so no surface computes 4b − 3 itself.
- **Bank LEDs and marks**: a bank "has targets" when any of its four macros has a target with an id; the grids light such a bank's key at 6 and an empty one at 2, the shown one at 15.

#### 4.7 Memory and message rates with 16 macros

- **Real-time memory: none.** Macros are Lua only; the engine sees only their targets' ordinary sends.
- **Lua memory and files:** 128 target records (16 × 8, a few KB of heap); `data/strata/macros.data` and each PSET's side file at most about 6 KB; `scenes.data` grows by at most about 100 KB with all 16 scenes holding 16 full macros (1e's test loads such a file and times `tab.load` on the stub; 3b times it on a CM3).
- **Message rates** (Question 23, revised): one macro moved by hand is still at most 8 targets × 30 applies = 240 a second. The worst cases, before the budget:
  - every macro moving at once (an E16 in 3.6 sweeping all 16; today, CCs from any 16-knob controller): 16 × 8 × 30 = 3840 a second (four macros: 960);
  - gesture recorders: a recorder holds whatever hand moves it recorded (four arc rings at once, for example), so four recorders looping four macros each: 4 × 4 × 8 × 30 = 3840 a second (the approved figure was 960, one macro each);
  - LFOs on macros: unchanged, inside Lua's 576 a second (12 sends per step).
- **The budget stays 1500 a second** (`ENGINE_BUDGET.messages_per_window` 150 per 100 ms). Because every macro fan-out is deferrable (Question 29) and a deferred send keeps only its id's newest value, the macros' demand can only delay their targets, never add sclang traffic: 128 target ids at most, about 480 deferrable ids in all with morph's 270, the LFOs' and the matrix's (§10.2), so every pending value goes out within four windows (400 ms) at the worst case, and within one when only macros move (128 < 150). The macro param itself, every target's stored value, the screen and the grid change at once.
- **Unchanged:** hand edits of a target (not through a macro), steps, notes, locks and recalls are never deferred. The 3b soak adds 16 macros moving at once, from a recorder and from CCs, to its list.
- **Lua's local limit:** `lib/macros.lua` keeps its constants in `MACROS` and builds the 16 params, banks and LFO destinations in loops, so no per-macro local exists; the new constants are fields of `lib/constants.lua`'s returned table, not locals; the locals probe's 30 free locals hold.
- **CC mapping:** `macro_1`..`macro_16` are control params, mapped through PARAMS > MAP as any param; 3.6's OXI E16 layout maps its 16 encoders to them one to one, and its rings read each macro's modulation source (§10.1).

### 5. Scene morph

#### 5.1 Params

| Param | Kind, range | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `morph` | control 0-1 (percent) | 0 |
| `morph_a` | number 1..`NUM_SCENES` | 1 |
| `morph_b` | number 1..`NUM_SCENES` | 2 |

In a group `strata_morph` (3 params) after the macros. They are params, so PSETs store them and CCs reach them; scenes don't store them (a scene holding the morph between two scenes would be circular). Morph is not an LFO destination or a macro target (Non-goals).

#### 5.2 Interpolation

`S.lib.morph` (`lib/morph.lua`, **(new)**) builds a plan whenever A, B or either slot's contents change (a save, a load, a migration): `scene_entries` of both (§0.2), validated, paired by id.
- **Continuous entries** (exactly the ones a transition fades: voice, grain and crossmod values, the registered extras that crossfade, the continuous globals, LFO rate, depth and offset, the matrix depths, except a depth whose routing changes, §10.2) are set to `A + morph × (B − A)` in raw values, as a transition's steps are. Number params round to whole numbers, as transitions round the LFO depths. Entries equal in A and B are left out of the plan.
- **Discrete entries** (sources, mod sources, envelope shapes and grain modes, voice active and lock states, tempo, filter type, mutation and LFO settings, the registered discrete keys and fields, patterns, step data, macro targets, matrix routings and their on/off, the recorders' run states; a routing that changes switches through §10.2's ramp through 0) take A's value below 0.5 and B's above, with hysteresis (Question 6): from A they switch to B when morph reaches 0.52, from B back to A when it falls to 0.48 (`MORPH.hysteresis` 0.02). The first apply after a plan change takes A below 0.5 and B at or above it. A switch applies the discrete entries in recall order through `apply_entries`, so voices that stop stop first, sources come before their params, discrete extras after their source and voices that start start last (Question 7); one full voice sync per running voice whose source changed, as a recall does.
- Macro values are continuous and set silently.

#### 5.3 Applying

- The `morph` action records the position; a clock (`S.morph_clock`) applies the latest position at most 10 times a second (`MORPH.apply_hz`), so a fast turn is coalesced, and once more when the position stops moving (Question 8). A continuous entry is skipped when it moved by less than 1/1000 of its range since the last apply.
- At most 120 values per apply (`MORPH.max_values_per_apply`, Question 23): with more in the plan, each apply takes the next 120 round robin, so every value is reached within a few applies and a still morph converges. So morph sends at most 1200 messages a second, against about 2700 for every field of seven running voices at 10 Hz, and its sends are deferrable (§0.1).
- Moving the morph cancels a running transition (`cancel_scene_transition`) and the scene sequencer's wait moves on, as after a manual recall. A recall doesn't move the morph param; the next morph move re-applies from the recalled state.
- `S.current_scene` becomes 0 when the morph applies (the state is no longer a stored scene), so the header shows no scene; saving to a slot makes it current again, as now.
- An empty or invalid endpoint makes the morph do nothing and flashes `S5 empty` once.
- Morph never runs `recall_scene` or `recall_scene_instant`, never clears the randomize undo by itself (each param it sets goes through the actions, which do) and never starts a transition.

#### 5.4 How morph differs from a transition

| | Transition | Morph |
|---|---|---|
| driven by | time (`scene_transition_time`) | the `morph` param, any speed, any direction |
| ends | in `recall_scene_instant`: exact values, discrete keys at the end, `S.current_scene` set | never: discrete keys at 0.5 (with hysteresis), `S.current_scene` 0 |
| steps | 20 | at most 10 a second, only fields that differ |
| result | a recalled scene | params, which can be saved as a new scene |

#### 5.5 XY morph (stretch goal)

Four corners (`morph_a`, `morph_b`, and new `morph_c`, `morph_d`) and two params `morph_x`, `morph_y` (0-1). Continuous entries take the bilinear blend of the four corners; discrete entries take the corner with the largest weight (ties go to A, then B, C, D), with the same hysteresis on the weights. On the 256, page 3's rows 11-16 × columns 11-16 are a 6×6 pad (§11). It is built only after everything else (package 3a).

### 6. Scatter

- **Params** (group `strata_scatter`, 8 params, after morph):

  | Param | Range | Default |
  |---|---|---|
  | `scatter` | binary toggle | 0 (off) |
  | `scatter_depth` | 0-100 % | 10 % |
  | `scatter_rate` | 0.01-1 Hz, exp | 0.1 Hz |
  | `scatter_pitch`, `scatter_pos`, `scatter_size`, `scatter_cutoff`, `scatter_amp` | binary toggles | pitch and pos on, the rest off |

- **What it does:** every unlocked voice gets slow smooth random modulation on each enabled destination, independent per voice and destination, at depth × half the destination's range (§3.3). It is one engine node (`\strataScatter`), no messages per step (Question 9).
- **Sends:** `setScatter(depth / 100 (0 while off), rate, destination mask, voice mask)` from each param's sender and whenever a lock changes (through `S.register_lock_change`, §12). The voice mask is the unlocked voices: a locked voice is left alone by scatter, as by drift and mutation.
- **Scenes** store all eight params (depth and rate crossfade; the toggles and on/off switch at the end). MOD shows them (§10).

### 7. Gesture recorders

#### 7.1 What they record

- Four recorders (`NUM_GESTURES` 4), `S.lib.gestures` (`lib/gestures.lua`, **(new)**), each a `lib/pattern_time` pattern (`S.gestures[k].pattern`) plus Strata's state: `state` (`empty`, `recording`, `playing`, `stopped`), `quantize` and, for a quantized loop, its length in beats.
- **Events** are `{id, value}`: the param's value after a hand edit, so playback is absolute and repeatable (Question 10). They come from the `param_touch` registry (§0.1): encoder edits (`enc_param_delta`), arc rings (`arc_sweep_param`, `delta_with_detent`) and grid faders (`set_param_from_column`), for **control params only** (`params.tCONTROL`): macros and morph included, sources, options and binary params never.
- Not recorded: a recorder's own or another recorder's playback (it writes with `S.gesture_playing` set, which the touch hook skips), LFOs, scatter, mutations, drift, scene recalls and transitions, morph's writes (only the morph param itself, if touched by hand).
- One recorder records at a time: starting one stops another's recording, which then plays.
- At most 1024 events per recorder (`GESTURES.max_events`); the recording stops at the cap and flashes `G2 full`. A recording also stops at 10 minutes.

#### 7.2 Keys

`S.lib.gestures.press(k)` (MOD's K3, the grid keys, the arc button on a gesture row):
- empty → recording (it records from the first event, so a silent lead-in is not part of the loop; the loop's length runs from the first event to the stop);
- recording → playing (a free loop at once; a quantized loop from the next bar or beat);
- playing → stopped; stopped → playing.

`S.lib.gestures.clear(k)` (ALT+K2 held 0.5 s on MOD, a 0.5 s grid hold) stops and empties it. `S.lib.gestures.overdub(k)` (ALT+K3 on MOD) toggles overdub while playing. A free loop overdubs through pattern_time's own `set_overdub`, which relies on its metro's `step` and `prev_time`. A quantized loop is played by Strata's coroutine, which pattern_time's `overdub_event` knows nothing about, so Strata overdubs it itself: an event touched while overdubbing is inserted into the loop's beat list at `(clock.get_beats() − loop_start) mod length`, kept sorted, under the same 1024-event cap.

#### 7.3 Quantization

- `gesture_<k>_quantize` (option: free, beat, bar; default free) per recorder, in a group `strata_gestures` (8 params) after scatter: the four `gesture_<k>_quantize`, then the four hidden `gesture_<k>_play` (§7.4). Hidden params still count in a group, so the stub's group check sees eight.
- **Free:** pattern_time plays it with its metro, as recorded.
- **Beat or bar:** when the recording stops, the loop's length in beats (seconds over `clock.get_beat_sec()` at the stop) rounds to the nearest whole beat or bar (at least one), and each event's time is scaled by the same factor and stored in beats. A clock coroutine (`S.gestures[k].clock`) plays it: `clock.sync(length_beats)` at each loop's start, so every loop starts on its bar, then each event after its offset in beats (`clock.sleep(offset × clock.get_beat_sec())`, re-read at every event, so it follows tempo changes). It stays locked to Link and MIDI clock. Changing the quantize of a recorded loop converts it the same way (free → beat or bar), or back to seconds at the current tempo.
- Playback conflicts: a hand edit of a param the recorder plays wins until the recorder's next event for it.

#### 7.4 Storage

- **Per PSET:** a PSET hook writes `<pset path base>.gestures.data` (`{version = 1, recorders = {...}}`: each recorder's events, times, quantize and length; `tab.save`), reads it after a PSET load (stopping every recorder first) and deletes it with the PSET.
- **Live:** `data/strata/gestures.data`, saved at cleanup and loaded at init (stopped), like the macros.
- **Scenes** store only whether each recorder plays: four binary params `gesture_<k>_play`, hidden from the PARAMS menu, kept in step with each recorder's state and registered as discrete scene fields (`S.register_scene_field`). A recall that sets 1 on an empty recorder does nothing; setting 0 stops it.
- Cleanup stops every recorder's metro and clock (a new cleanup step, §12).

### 8. The step sequencer

#### 8.1 Data

- **Levels stay `S.patterns[voice][step]`** (0-15), so every grid layout, the SEQ page and `step_brightness` keep working.
- **Step attributes:** `S.pattern_steps[voice][step]`, nil for a plain step, or a table with any of:

  | Key | Range | Default (nil) | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `prob` | 0-100 (%) | 100 | the chance the step plays, drawn once per step |
  | `ratchet` | 1-4 | 1 | hits within the step |
  | `notes` | a list of 1-8 whole numbers -14..14 (`SEQ.max_step_notes`) | none | a chord of scale degrees from the voice's pitch (§8.4); 3.4's voices play the first, its step outputs get them all |
  | `pos` | 0-1 | none | locks `buf_pos` |
  | `pitch` | 0.25-4 | none | locks `grain_pitch` |
  | `size` | 0.01-0.5 | none | locks `grain_size` |
  | `bookmark` | 1..`NUM_BOOKMARKS` | none | recalls a 3.3 bookmark |

  The lock ranges are the params' specs; the arc and grid draw them through `param_value_fraction` of the matching param.
- **Per voice** (a registered voice-param record, `seq`, with no grain keys, discrete scene keys and no VOICE rows): `v<i>_seq_length` (number 1-16, default 16) and `v<i>_seq_division` (option: `1/32`, `1/16t`, `1/16`, `1/8t`, `1/8`, `1/4`, `1/2`, `1 bar`; that is 1/8, 1/6, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1, 2 and 4 beats; default `1/16`).
- Scenes store the step attributes as a block (`data.steps`, registered by `lib/sequencer.lua`: `steps[voice] = {[step] = {...}}`, sparse), switched at a transition's end with the patterns. The chain (§8.8) loads the same block. `notes` is a list in every one of them (cross-release decision 2), so 3.5's chords need no new version or migration.

#### 8.2 The clock

- One coroutine per voice (`S.pattern_clocks[voice]`) plus the transport's (`S.pattern_clock`, which keeps counting 16ths for the 8×8's half blink and the chain's bars). Start: every voice plays its step 1 at the start beat `b0` (now, or the next beat or bar under quantized actions, §8.9), then `clock.sync(division, b0 mod division)`, so its steps fall on `b0 + k × division` whatever the division.
- Each voice wraps at its own `v<i>_seq_length`; a length change takes effect at the voice's next wrap if the position is past the new length, else at once.
- **Per-voice playheads:** `S.lib.sequencer.sounding_step(voice)` is the voice's sounding step (nil while stopped and before its first step); with no argument it is the transport's, as today. `step_brightness(voice, step)` uses the voice's own, so every layout's playheads follow each voice's length and division without layout changes.
- Stop cancels every coroutine and calls every step output's `stop` (§8.10); cleanup (step 90) does the same.
- A transport start or stop (§8.11) starts, restarts or stops them as the play key does.

#### 8.3 A step, and why it creates no nodes

When voice v's coroutine reaches step s with level L > 0, the sequencer draws the probability once, then builds one event per hit and hands it to every registered step output (§8.10) in registration order. Steps 2-6 below are the `voices` output's `play`, which `lib/sequencer.lua` registers first; the probability draw and the ratchet timing are the sequencer's, so every output plays the same hits.
1. **Probability:** one `math.random()` against `prob`; a miss plays nothing and changes nothing, on every output.
2. **Locks:** for each of `pos`, `pitch`, `size`, `set_base_override(v<i>_<param>, value)` (§0.1), which sends it through the param's sender (`setGrainParam`) with the LFO offsets on top. Each lock the previous played step of this voice had and this one lacks is cleared (`clear_base_override`, which sends the stored value back). Only a hand edit of the param clears its override early (Question 14); drift, morph, macros and gesture playback change the stored value under it.
3. **Bookmark:** `S.lib.bookmarks.recall(v, n)` (3.3), if `is_set(v, n)` and the bookmark lies inside the voice's current loop (`v<i>_loop_start`, `v<i>_loop_length`); outside it the lock does nothing, because 3.3's recall would reset the loop for good (Question 25).
4. **Note** (§8.4): a base override on the voice's pitch param.
5. **Hits:** `engine.retrigger(v − 1, L / MAX_STEP_LEVEL)` at the step's start, then, for a ratchet r, r − 1 more at `division / r` intervals (`clock.sync(division / r, b0 mod (division / r))` inside the step, in the sequencer's coroutine, which calls every output's `play` at each hit), each with the same velocity. Ratchets that would fall closer than 20 ms (`SEQ.min_ratchet_seconds`) are dropped from the end, for every output alike. Locks, the bookmark and the note apply at the first hit only.
6. A stopped voice is started with `toggle_voice` instead of the first hit, as today; the overrides apply after its sync.

Every message here is `retrigger`, `setGrainParam` or `setVoiceParam` to nodes that exist. The only node-creating call is `toggle_voice` for a stopped voice, exactly as in 3.1. Worst case: seven voices at `1/32` with four hits at 300 BPM would be 160 retriggers a second per voice; the 20 ms rule caps it at 50, so 350 a second for all seven, plus at most four lock sends per step.

#### 8.4 Notes per step

- A note is a scale degree relative to the voice's own pitch: degree 0 is the stored pitch, degree k the k-th tone above it in the global scale (3.2's `scale` and `root`; `S.lib.variation.scale_mask()`, chromatic while the scale is off), so ±14 is two octaves of a heptatonic scale (Question 13).
- Applied as a MIDI note would be (`docs/notes/lua/midi.md`, `lib/midi_in.lua:121-136`), but as a base override for this step only: a synth voice's `v<i>_freq` (`setVoiceParam`) and a buffer voice's `v<i>_buf_rate`, each times `2 ^ (semitones / 12)` of its stored value and clamped to its range. An input voice, which MIDI notes leave alone, takes the note on its `v<i>_grain_pitch`, multiplied onto the step's `pitch` lock when it has one (Question 24). The next step without a note clears it.
- **Chords** (cross-release decision 2): `notes` is always a list, ordered as entered, duplicates kept, an empty list stored as nil. **Until 3.5, a step with several notes plays its first note on Strata's voices**, exactly as a one-note step, and hands the whole list to every step output (`event.notes`, §8.10). 3.5 plays the whole list through its note allocation and adds chord editing; the data needs no migration.
- **3.4's UI edits one note** (SEQ's `note` row, the step editor's ALT ring 2): it reads and writes `notes[1]` and keeps any further entries; `off` (or ALT+K2 held 0.5 s on the row) clears the whole list. 3.4 itself never makes a list longer than one, but loads, validates, saves, chains and morphs longer ones unchanged (`validate_scene`: at most `SEQ.max_step_notes`, §9.2).

#### 8.5 Euclidean fill

The SEQ edit view's `euclid` row holds hits k (0..length) and rotation w (0..length − 1) for the selected voice (`S.seq_euclid[voice]`, not stored). Changing either writes the voice's first `length` steps from `er.gen(k, length, w)`: hits at level 15, the rest at 0 (Question 22). Step attributes are kept. While a scene recall is in progress nothing fills.

#### 8.7 Editing a held step from the grid

- `S.lib.grid_core.step_key` toggles a step on release instead of press, unless the press was consumed: by the level gesture (a second key on the row, as now) or by an edit while held (Question 11).
- A step key held for 0.3 s (`GRID_HOLD.step_edit_seconds`, through `on_hold`) opens the **step editor**: SEQ's edit view for that voice and step, pushed as a modal owner (§0.1) so it shows on any page and takes E2/E3 and the arc. - Arc rings while it is open: 1 `pos`, 2 `pitch`, 3 `size`, 4 `prob` (each from `off` at the stored value; a turn below the range's start back to `off`), drawn through `param_value_fraction`; with ALT: 1 `ratchet`, 2 `note`, 3 `bookmark`, 4 `level` (whole steps, 20 ticks each).

#### 8.8 Pattern chaining

Params (group `strata_seq`, 7 params): `seq_chain` (binary, 0), `seq_chain_from` (1-16, 1), `seq_chain_scenes` (1-16, 2), `seq_chain_bars` (1-16, 4), `quantize_actions` (§8.9), `seq_division_reset` (trigger: every voice back to step 1 at the next bar), `seq_follow_transport` (§8.11). While the chain is on and the patterns play, every `bars` bars (counted by the transport, on the bar) the sequencer loads the next scene's patterns-only block (`S.patterns`, `S.pattern_steps`, each voice's length and division) from the range `from .. from + scenes − 1`, wrapping, skipping empty scenes. Nothing else of the scene is applied: no params, sources or voice states. PSETs store the chain; scenes don't (they are its contents).

#### 8.9 Beat-quantized actions

- `quantize_actions` (option: off, beat, bar; default off). `S.lib.quantize.schedule(key, fn)` (`lib/quantize.lua`, **(new)**) runs `fn` at once while off, otherwise at the next beat or bar (`clock.sync(1)` or `clock.sync(4)`); a new action with the same key replaces a waiting one, and the header flashes `S3 at bar`.
- Quantized: scene recalls from SCENES' keys, the grid's scene keys and the arc button (key `scene`); freeze toggles from any control (key `freeze<v>`, by a one-line wrap in `S.lib.freeze.toggle`); bookmark recalls and position jumps from keys, BUFFER and the grid (3.3's calls in `lib/positions.lua` and `lib/pages/buffer.lua`, one-line wraps by 1f, keys `bookmark<v>` and `position<v>`; a step's bookmark lock is already on the grid); pattern start (key `patterns`). Not quantized: the scene sequencer (time-based), steps, LFOs, morph.
- Cleanup cancels every waiting action (a new step, §12).

#### 8.10 Step outputs (cross-release decision 3)

The sequencer never plays a voice itself: it fires each hit through the registered outputs, so 3.6's MIDI out, Just Friends and W/ over ii, and nb are one registration each, with no sequencer change.

**Registration** (`lib/state.lua`, 0a; one line in the owning module, as the other `S.register_*` registries):

```lua
S.register_step_output({
  name = "voices",                  -- unique; refused if taken
  play = function(event) end,       -- one call per hit
  stop = function() end,            -- optional: the patterns stopped
  available = function(voice_idx) return true end,  -- optional: false skips this voice
})
```

**The event** (one table per hit, read-only for the outputs):

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `voice` | 1..`NUM_VOICES` |
| `step` | 1..`PATTERN_LENGTH` |
| `hit`, `hits` | 1..r and r, the ratchet count after the 20 ms rule |
| `first` | true for the step's first hit |
| `level`, `velocity` | L (1..`MAX_STEP_LEVEL`) and L / `MAX_STEP_LEVEL` |
| `notes` | the step's list of scale degrees, or nil (the whole chord, §8.4) |
| `semitones` | the same through the global scale at the hit (`S.lib.variation.scale_mask()`), or nil |
| `locks` | `{pos, pitch, size, bookmark}` as stored, or nil (Strata's own; other outputs may ignore them) |
| `beat` | `clock.get_beats()` at the hit |
| `gate_beats` | the hit's length in beats (`division / hits`), for outputs that end notes |

**Rules:**
- Outputs run in registration order; `lib/sequencer.lua` registers `voices` in its constructor, so it is always first. The list is read at each hit (after init), so a module's include order never matters.
- `play` never yields (no `clock.sync` or `clock.sleep`): it runs inside the voice's coroutine, between its syncs. An output that must end notes later (3.6's MIDI note-offs) runs its own clock, one per output, with a cleanup step.
- `voices`' `play` creates no nodes (a `retrigger`, or `toggle_voice` for a stopped voice, as in §8.3); the velocity invariant and "steps never create nodes" are about it. Other outputs send nothing to the engine.
- Each call is protected (`pcall`): a failing output prints `step output <name>: <error>` once and is skipped until the patterns next start, so a broken integration never stops the sequencer or Strata's voices. The unit tests make an output fail and check both.
- `stop` runs on every stop of the patterns (the play key, a transport stop, cleanup), in registration order, protected the same way.
- MIDI notes from `lib/midi_in.lua` do not go through the outputs: they are input, not the sequencer.

#### 8.11 Following the clock transport (cross-release decision 5)

- **Param** `seq_follow_transport` (binary, default **1, on**; name `follow transport`), in `strata_seq`. PSETs store it (a 3.3 PSET lacks it and loads with the default); scenes don't. SETUP shows it as a `transport` row (`follow` / `off`).
- **Installing:** a start step (`START_ORDER.transport`) sets `clock.transport.start` and `clock.transport.stop` (norns `lua/core/clock.lua:129-139`) to Strata's handlers, keeping whatever functions it found, which Strata's handlers call after their own; a cleanup step (`CLEANUP_ORDER.transport`) puts them back unless something installed later replaced Strata's, exactly as `install_tempo_change_handler` and `restore_tempo_change_handler` do (`lib/params_setup.lua:55-71`). norns' `clock.cleanup` also clears both when the script ends (`clock.lua:92-93`). The handlers stay installed while the param is off and do nothing then, so the param's action installs nothing.
- **When norns calls them** (matron's `clock.cc:114-127`, only for the active clock source): internal, when PARAMS > CLOCK > reset or any script runs `clock.internal.start()`, and on `clock.internal.stop()`; MIDI, on the first clock tick after a MIDI start (0xFA), at beat 0, and on a MIDI stop (0xFC), while MIDI continue (0xFB) calls nothing; Link, on the session's start and stop, only while PARAMS > CLOCK > `link start/stop sync` is enabled; crow, never. At a start norns resets the beat to 0 and reschedules every pending `clock.sync`.
- **Start** (follow on): every voice back to step 1 at the new beat 0, at once, never through `quantize_actions` (the transport has just defined the bar); if the patterns were playing they restart (Question 27), and a running chain restarts at `seq_chain_from`. Clocked engine LFOs resync at once instead of at the next bar (§2.6). Quantized gesture loops and waiting quantized actions follow by themselves, since norns reschedules their syncs.
- **Stop** (follow on): the patterns stop as from the play key (§8.2): coroutines canceled, playheads cleared, every step's overrides cleared, every output's `stop` called. Voices keep sounding, as after the play key.
- **Strata never leads** (Question 26): the play key, SEQ, the grid's play keys and the 64's row-8 key start and stop the patterns locally and never call the clock's start or stop. With follow on, an external start still restarts a pattern started by hand.
- **What changes for 3.3 users:** nothing with the internal clock unless something resets it; with MIDI clock, or Link with start/stop sync, the patterns now start and stop with the transport. The release notes say so, and SETUP's row turns it off.
- **Tests** (1d): a new `test/stub_transport.lua` gives the clock stub `clock.transport` and the helpers `context.clock.transport_start()` and `transport_stop()` (beat reset to 0 and syncs rescheduled, as matron does); the tests cover start, restart while playing, stop, follow off, the chain restart, the handlers kept and restored, and a stop with ratchets pending.

#### 8.12 Gates (Question 41, proposed 2026-11-11)

A gated voice is heard only while a gate is open. Everything else about a step (probability, ratchets, locks, notes, the outputs) is unchanged, and a voice whose gate switch is off plays exactly as §8.3 says.

- **Params,** in the registered `seq` voice record (§8.1), which grows from two params to five:

  | Param | Kind, range | Default |
  |---|---|---|
  | `v<i>_seq_gate` | option: off, on | off |
  | `v<i>_seq_gate_length` | control 0.05-16 steps, exp | 0.5 |
  | `v<i>_seq_gate_release` | control 0.005-4 s, exp | 0.1 s |

- **Step attribute** `gate` (0.05-16 steps, nil for the voice's length), in `S.pattern_steps`, `data.steps` and the chain, edited on SEQ's edit view.
- **In the engine** (the per-voice mix stage, through `voiceMixMod`; no new command): three stage controls, `seqGate` (0 or 1), `gateSeconds` and `gateRelease`, set with `setGrainParam` through handlers of the modulation area, as the stage's `filterFreq` is set. The stage's whole output is multiplied by `1 − seqGate × (1 − env)`, where `env` is an envelope the stage's own `t_trig` starts (the trigger `retrigger` already sends for the accent): 5 ms up, `gateSeconds` held, `gateRelease` down, restarted from its current level by a new trigger. With `seqGate` 0 the factor is 1 and the graph folds to 3.3's. The mirror (the crossmod source of a buffer or input voice) is not gated, as a synth voice's crossmod signal is not; the voice's tap is, so a recording of a voice holds what is heard. `velocity` never reaches the gate, so the velocity invariant holds.
- **From Lua:** the record's sync and senders send `seqGate` and `gateRelease`; the sequencer sends `gateSeconds` (the gate's length in steps × the voice's step in seconds ÷ the step's hit count) only when it differs from the value last sent for the voice, before the hit's `retrigger`. A step on a stopped gated voice starts it and then retriggers it. So a gate costs no message per hit beyond today's, and at most one more when its length changes.
- **Outputs:** an event's `gate_beats` (§8.10) is the gate's length in beats for a gated voice, and `division / hits` otherwise.
- **Scenes:** `seq_gate` is a discrete key (it switches at a transition's end), `seq_gate_length` and `seq_gate_release` crossfade; migration fills off, 0.5 and 0.1; `validate_scene` checks the three and a step's `gate`.
- **On screen:** SEQ's edit view gains `gate`, `gate len` (`0.50 st`) and `gate rel` (`0.10 s`) in its `voice` section and `gate` (`2.00 st`, `off`) in its `step` section; the pattern view marks a gated voice's row. A gated voice with no step playing is silent while MAIN shows it `ON`; the SEQ page is where the gate shows.
- **Memory** (estimated; 1a measures): about 8 control-rate UGens a stage, about 2 KB, 14 KB for the seven stages, so 3.4 takes about 111 KB in all (§3.6's 97 plus 14) and a stage about 38.75 KB of 3.3's 40 KB check. If 1a measures a stage above 40 KB it reports before going on; the check's limit is then the maintainer's to raise (the tightest case keeps about 380 KB above its floor) or the gate's to shrink.
- **Not in 3.4:** a gate per note (3.5's note allocation), an attack param, a gate that follows a held MIDI note's length (MIDI notes open it for the voice's gate length, as a step does).
- **Tests** (1a, 1d, 2b): with `seqGate` 0 the stage equals 3.3's within 1e-6; a gated voice is silent before a trigger, at its ungated level 50 ms after one and silent again after `gateSeconds` and the release; a trigger during the release reopens the gate from its level; the mirror is not gated and the tap is; a hit on a gated voice sends `gateSeconds` only when it changed (0.0625 s for 0.5 steps of 1/16 at 120 BPM, 0.03125 s under a ratchet of 2, 0.25 s for a step `gate` of 2), and a stopped gated voice is started and then retriggered.
- **Cost if wrong:** if silent-until-played surprises (Question 41c), the switch gains a third option whose gate opens while the patterns are stopped; the engine part is unchanged. If 5 ms attacks click on some sources, an attack param is one more control.

#### 8.13 Swing (Question 42, proposed 2026-11-11)

- **Param** `v<i>_seq_swing` (number 50-75 %, default 50), the `seq` voice record's sixth param, a discrete scene key (migration default 50).
- Every second step of the voice (steps 2, 4, 6, ...) plays `(swing − 50) / 50 × division` late: 50 is straight, 66 is close to a triplet feel, 75 puts the step three quarters of the way through its pair. The voice's coroutine syncs its odd steps on twice its division and its even steps that much after the middle, so swing never drifts against the clock, Link or MIDI.
- A swung step is shorter and the step before it longer by the same amount: a step's ratchets are spaced over its own actual length (and the 20 ms rule reads that length), while gate lengths keep to the nominal step.
- On SEQ's edit view, a `swing` row (`58%`) in the `voice` section. It costs no message and no engine work.
- **Tests** (1d): at 120 BPM and 1/16, step 2 plays at 0.125 s with swing 50, 0.165 s with 66 and 0.1875 s with 75, and step 3 at 0.25 s in every case; a ratchet of 2 on a step swung to 66 spaces its hits by half of the 0.085 s left.
- **Cost if wrong:** a global swing is one param in place of seven and the same sync arithmetic.

#### 8.14 Mute and solo (Question 43, proposed 2026-11-11)

- **Params,** two more in the voice mix's registered record (`lib/voice_mix.lua`), each a binary: `v<i>_mute` (default 0; a discrete scene key, migration default 0) and `v<i>_solo` (default 0; never a scene key). VOICE's `filter & sends` section gains the two rows.
- **A voice is silenced** while it is muted, or while any voice is soloed and it is not. `S.lib.voice_mix.silenced(voice)` is the one answer; a change of any mute or solo sends `setGrainParam(v − 1, "voiceMute", 0 or 1)` to each voice whose answer changed, and the voice sync sends it too.
- **In the engine:** one more stage control, `voiceMute`, lagged 20 ms, in the same factor as the gate (§8.12): the stage's whole output is multiplied by `(1 − mute)`. The mirror is not muted (a muted voice still modulates the others, as a gated one does), the tap is, and the sends come after it, so tails ring out. About 3 control-rate UGens a stage, under 1 KB, counted with the gate's estimate.
- **What stays as it is:** a muted voice keeps running, its pattern keeps stepping and its params, LFOs and scenes keep acting on it; a lock (`[L]`) is unrelated. Randomize, mutations and the fader pages never touch mute or solo.
- **On screen and grid:** MAIN's voice list shows `m` in place of `+` for a silenced running voice and `s` for a soloed one; the voice keys of every grid layout light a silenced voice at 6 (running 12, stopped 2); the 256's page 1 row 9 columns 5-11 are mute keys for voices 1-7 (a press toggles the mute; held 0.5 s it toggles the solo; LEDs: muted 2, soloed 15, otherwise 8), between the recorders and the bank keys. The other layouts mute from the screen and MIDI.
- **Tests** (1a, 1j, 2c): `voiceMute` 1 silences a voice within 0.1 s whatever its gate, and the mirror never; muting voice 3 sends `voiceMute` 1 to voice 3 only; soloing voice 2 sends 1 to every other unmuted voice and unsoloing sends 0 to them; a scene recalls mutes and leaves solos; a muted voice's steps still retrigger it.
- **Cost if wrong:** if solos should clear with a scene recall or at start, that is one line; if a mute should also stop the voice's pattern, the sequencer reads `silenced`.

#### 8.15 What 8.12-8.14 change elsewhere

§8.1's step attributes gain `gate`; the `seq` record has six params (§8.1's two, the gate's three, swing); §8.6's edit view gains their rows; §9.2's migration defaults and `validate_scene` rules gain the new keys; §3.6's memory gains about 14 KB for the seven stages; §14's packages gain the engine part in 0c and 1a, the sequencer part in 1d, the rows in 2b, the grid keys in 2c, and two small packages of their own, mute and solo (1j) and the input level (1i, §10.3).

### 9. Scenes: 16 in two banks, with names

#### 9.1 Slots

- `NUM_SCENES` 16, `SCENE_BANK_SIZE` 8: bank A is 1-8, bank B 9-16. A bank is only a view (Question 15).
- `scene_sequence_length` becomes 1-16 (default 4); `morph_a`, `morph_b` and the chain take 1-16.

#### 9.2 File format and migration

- `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` 5 (3.3's is 4). Files of any earlier version load: slots 9-16 are filled empty, and the migrations below run in order after 3.3's.
- **Migration defaults** (each reproduces 3.3):

  | Field | Default |
  |---|---|
  | slot name | `""` (and an old default `scene <n>` becomes `""`) |
  | LFOs 1-3 | converted from the library's fields (§2.9) |
  | LFOs 4-6 | every param's default, off |
  | `macro_1`..`macro_16` | 0 |
  | `data.macros` | 16 empty target lists |
  | `matrix_1_depth`..`matrix_16_depth`, `matrix_1`..`matrix_16` | 0, 1 (on) |

  | `gesture_1_play`..`gesture_4_play` | 0 |
  | `voices[i].extras.seq_length`, `seq_division` | 16, `1/16` (through the registered record's `scene_defaults`) |
  | `data.steps` | empty (every step plain) |

- **`validate_scene` covers every new field:**
  - `name`: a string of at most `SCENE_NAME_MAX` (12) printable ASCII characters;
  - each LFO's 13 values: the binary and option params integers in range (a mod source within 1..n), the controls numbers in range;
  - `macro_<m>` (m 1..`NUM_MACROS`, 16): numbers 0-1; `data.macros`: `NUM_MACROS` tables, each sparse, keyed by whole numbers 1..`MACRO_TARGETS`, of `{id = string, min, max}` with min and max numbers 0-1 (an unknown id is kept verbatim with a console line, not rejected, §4.3);
  - the scatter fields: binaries 0/1, depth 0-100, rate in range;
  - `matrix_<k>_depth` (k 1..`MATRIX_SLOTS`, 16): numbers −100..100; `matrix_<k>`: 0/1; `data.matrix`: a sparse table keyed by whole numbers 1..`MATRIX_SLOTS` of `{source = string, dest = string}`, each at most 32 characters (an unknown key or id is kept verbatim with a console line, not rejected, §10.2);
  - `gesture_<k>_play`: 0/1;
  - `seq_length`: an integer 1-16; `seq_division`: an integer 1-8;
  - `data.steps`: `NUM_VOICES` tables keyed by whole steps 1..`PATTERN_LENGTH`, each `prob` an integer 0-100, `ratchet` 1-4, `notes` a list (keys 1..n, no holes) of 1 to `SEQ.max_step_notes` (8) integers -14..14, `pos`, `pitch`, `size` numbers in their param's range, `bookmark` an integer 1..`NUM_BOOKMARKS`; anything else rejects the scene.
- Patterns stay 7 × 16 integers 0-15, as now.
- `seq_follow_transport` and the shown macro bank are not scene fields (§8.11, §4.6).

#### 10.1 Modulation sources (cross-release decision 4)

Every modulator registers itself, so MOD lists sources it was never written for, and 3.5's MPE pressure and slide and 3.6's crow CV and MIDI CC inputs appear with one registration each.

**Registration** (`lib/state.lua`, 0a):

```lua
S.register_mod_source({
  key = "lfo3",                    -- unique; refused if taken. Also the extra-offset key
                                   -- (set_extra_offset, §0.1) if the source moves Lua params
  name = "L3",                     -- MOD's row label, at most 4 characters
  section = "lfos",                -- MOD's section; a new name is a new section
  value = function() return 0 end, -- the value now, within range
  range = {-1, 1},                 -- min < 0 marks a bipolar source (drawn from the center)
  describe = function() return "v3 pitch~ 25%" end,  -- the row's text (destination, state)
  visible = function() return true end,  -- optional (macros: in the shown bank)
  row = {                          -- optional: MOD's actions on the row
    enc = function(d) end, fine_enc = function(d) end,
    press = function() end, alt_press = function(n) end,  -- K3; ALT+K2 held 0.5 s / ALT+K3 (a quick ALT+K2 randomizes the section, MOD's own)
    hints = function(alt) return "K3:lfo E3:depth" end,
  },
})
```

**Rules:**
- `value`, `describe` and `visible` are cheap, never send and never yield: MOD calls them at up to 15 frames a second per shown row, and 3.6's readers (crow outputs, MIDI CC out, the E16's rings) at their own rates.
- `value` is in its source's own units and range; a reader that needs 0-1 or −1..1 maps it through `range` (`S.lib.mod_sources.fraction(key)` and `normalized(key)`, the only places that do). `lib/mod_sources.lua` (**(new)**, 0a) holds the readers: `get(key)`, `list(section)` (registration order, visible ones only), `fraction(key)` (0..1) and `normalized(key)` (−1..1 for a bipolar source, 0..1 for a unipolar one, §10.2; 0 for a value that isn't finite, so a bad source can't send one).
- A source with no `row` is read-only on MOD (E3 and K3 do nothing, the hint row shows none): 3.5's pressure and slide are such sources.
- Read at init or later, never at include time, like every registry; MOD rebuilds its rows when the page is entered.
- 3.4 registers: `lfo1`..`lfo6` (section `lfos`, range −1..1, the LFO's shape value 2u − 1 before its own depth and destination, as §2.4's inputs read it, and 0 while the LFO is off, 1b); `macro1`..`macro16` (section `macros`, 0..1, visible in the shown bank, 1e); `gesture1`..`gesture4` (section `gestures`, the loop's phase 0..1, 0 when not playing, 1f).
- The registry lists and reads sources; it routes nothing itself. Routing is each source's own (an LFO's destination, a macro's targets) and the matrix's (§10.2), which takes its sources from here.

#### 10.2 The modulation matrix (Question 30, decided 2026-11-08)

A fixed set of routings, each from any registered source to one destination with a bipolar depth. Like the LFOs, the matrix adds offsets around stored values and never `params:set`s a destination. It lives in `lib/matrix.lua` (**(new)**, `S.lib.matrix`, constants in `MATRIX`).

**Routings** (Question 31): `MATRIX_SLOTS` 16. Routing k (1-16):

| Part | Where | Kind, range | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| source | `S.matrix[k].source`: a key of `S.registry.mod_sources` (§10.1), or `""` | side table, by key | `""` (empty) |
| destination | `S.matrix[k].dest`: a param id from `S.lib.matrix.destination_ids()`, or `""` | side table, by id | `""` |
| depth | param `matrix_<k>_depth` | control −100..100 %, step 1, center detent (as the LFO offset) | 0 |
| on | param `matrix_<k>` | binary | 1 (on) |

- The 32 params are a group `strata_matrix` ("matrix", 32 params, added in one loop) after `strata_scatter`. They are params (Question 32), so CCs (PARAMS > MAP), PSETs, scenes, morph and macros (Question 38) reach them.
- Source and destination are stored by key and id outside the params (Question 32), for §4.3's reason: the source registry grows with 3.5 and 3.6, the destination list with every `register_lfo_destination`, and an option param's stored index would then point at another entry.
- A routing plays while it is on, its source and destination are both set and known, and its depth isn't 0. A MOD edit (the routing view, its arc rings or learn, through `S.lib.matrix.edit_routing`) that completes a routing at depth 0 sets the depth to 25 % (the LFOs' default), so a new routing is audible. `set_routing`, which a recall, morph or PSET load uses, never does.
- **Storage**, as the macro targets: in scenes a block (`data.matrix`, sparse: `data.matrix[k] = {source, dest}` only for routings with either set, discrete, applied at a transition's end before the depths); per PSET `<pset path base>.matrix.data` (a PSET hook writes, reads and deletes it; the depths and on/off come from the PSET itself); live in `data/strata/matrix.data`, saved at cleanup and loaded at init with `scenes.data`'s `pcall` and move-aside rules. An unknown source key or destination id (a scene from a later release, or a source not registered in this build) is kept verbatim with a console line, never a failed file, and re-saving keeps it: the routing doesn't play, and MOD draws it at level 4 with the stored text cut with `fit_text`.

**Destinations** (Question 33): `S.lib.matrix.destination_ids()` is the LFO destination list (§2.3: the 68 built-in entries, then the registered ones, `v<i> amp` included) without the `macro <m>` entries, in that order, by param id. `v<i> main` resolves at each step, so it follows the voice's source, as for LFOs and macros. Not destinations: macros (they are sources), LFO params and matrix depths, so no source's value can depend on a routing and the matrix forms no loop (Anti-goals). Any later `register_lfo_destination` becomes a matrix destination too.

**Relation to the LFOs and macros** (Question 34): an LFO's own destination and a macro's targets stay as §2 and §4 define them. The matrix is a third layer whose offsets join the same per-destination sum. Nothing migrates: old PSETs and scenes have no matrix, and load with 16 empty routings, depth 0 and on.

**A routing's offset.** With the source's value v in its range {min, max}:
- s = 2 (v − min) / (max − min) − 1 for a bipolar source (min < 0; −1..1), and s = (v − min) / (max − min) for a unipolar one (0..1) (Question 37), from `S.lib.mod_sources.normalized(key)` (§10.1);
- m = depth / 100 × s, in half-range units, exactly an LFO's `m` (§2.2) at the same depth and shape value with offset 0, so a routing from LFO n at depth d sounds like LFO n at depth d on that destination (an LFO used only through the matrix keeps its own destination at `none`, as a modulator does, §2.4);
- m becomes a param offset through `S.lib.lfos.offset_for(param_id, m)` (1b), the LFOs' own mapping: ± half the range, except the voice cutoff (exponential) and amp (§2.3). The matrix computes no ranges of its own.

**Summing and clamping.** For each destination, every step:
- **A Lua destination** sends `clamp(B + Σ Lua-run LFO offsets + Σ extra offsets + Σ matrix offsets, the param's range)`, where B is `engine_base_value(id)` (the stored value, or a step lock's override, §0.1). The matrix's sum enters as one extra offset, `set_extra_offset("matrix", id, Σ)`, so it adds with the LFOs' and the macros' LFO offsets (§4.4) in `with_lfo_offsets` and is clamped once, after every term: two routings of +100 % and −100 % cancel instead of clamping each other. The order of the terms doesn't matter, since it is one sum and one clamp.
- **A fast destination** (Question 35): the five fast param ids (`grain_pitch`, `buf_pos`, `grain_size`, 3.2's `filter_freq`, the voice level) resolved for voice v and destination d. Lua keeps sending B exactly as now and sends Σ m of the routings on (v, d) and the macro LFOs' fast extras (§4.4) to the engine with `setFastOffset(v − 1, 0, d, Σ m)` (note slot 0 in 3.4). The engine's offset node writes it onto channel `fastModChannel(v, 0, d)`, where it adds with the engine LFOs and scatter in the same block, and the grain synth (or, for the cutoff and amp, the mix stage) applies §3.3's formula and clip once. A fast destination's routings never send `setGrainParam`.
- `v<i> main` takes the path of the param it resolves to.
- An LFO and a routing on the same fast destination therefore sum on the bus; an LFO and a routing on the same Lua destination sum in `with_lfo_offsets`. Either way the stored value never changes.

**The offset node** (`StrataModulation.sc`, 1a): `\strataFastOffsets`, one node at the head of `fastModGroup`, created by the area's start and freed by its `stop`. Controls: `offsets` (`numVoices × fastModNoteSlots × numFastDestinations` values, 35 in 3.4, each −2..2 half ranges, default 0) and `lag` (0.05 s); `Out.kr(fastModBus index, Lag.kr(offsets, lag))`, one `Out.kr` for the whole bus. It never ends on its own, so its key is in the area's `effectKeys` and messages to it go plainly, as to the effects chain, and a failure there stays reported. Lua refreshes a destination at 24 Hz at the worst case (a 42 ms step, Question 31), so the lag is at least 1/24 s: a shorter one leaves audible steps on amp and cutoff. About 37 UGens, about 10 KB (§3.6). 3.5, with more note slots, sizes `offsets` to the slots or splits the node per slot; the command's meaning stays.

**The command** (a fifth row for AGENTS.md, registered by 0c):

| Lua Command | SC Signature | Purpose |
|-------------|--------------|---------|
| `engine.setFastOffset(voice, noteSlot, destination, offset)` | `"iiif"` | Set the Lua-computed offset (−2..2 half ranges, clamped) on voice 0-6's note slot (0 in 3.4; 0..`fastModNoteSlots` − 1) and fast destination 0-4, through the offset node, lagged 50 ms. Out-of-range indices and a non-finite offset are ignored with a console line. Creates no node |

**Evaluation and messages** (Question 36):
- The matrix steps in the Lua LFO clock (§2.5), after the six LFOs, through `S.lib.lfos.on_step(fn)` (1b), so a routing from an LFO reads that step's LFO value.
- For each destination whose matrix sum moved by at least 1/1000 of its range (Lua) or of its half range (fast) since its last send, the matrix queues it with `S.lib.lfos.mark_due(key, send)` (1b) in the LFOs' round-robin queue. The 12 sends per step (`LFO.sends_per_step`) cover both, so Lua LFOs and the matrix together never send more than 576 messages a second, however many routings run. With the six LFOs and 16 routings on 22 different destinations, every one is sent within two steps (24 Hz). A routing from an LFO to a fast destination therefore moves at Lua's step rate; the LFO's own destination is the way to run it in the engine at up to 50 Hz, and the LFO page says so for an LFO that is also routed.
- Every matrix send is deferrable (§0.1): the clock sets `S.deferrable_write` around them, as for the LFOs.
- A routing that is cleared, turned off, set to depth 0 or left with an unknown part drops out of its destination's sum at the next step; a destination whose sum returns to 0 is sent once more (Lua: the base value with the other layers' offsets; fast: `setFastOffset(..., 0)`), as `restore_lfo_destination` does.
- Cleanup (`CLEANUP_ORDER.matrix`, before the LFOs' 120) sends every fast offset to 0 and clears the matrix's extra offsets, sending each Lua destination back.

**The message budget, revised.** Deferrable demand at the worst case: Lua LFOs and the matrix 576 a second together (one cap), morph 1200, recorders 3840, macro fan-out 3840: about 9460, all under the 1500-a-second budget. Distinct deferrable ids: 128 macro targets, morph's 270, the LFOs' up to 48 and the matrix's up to 16 Lua destinations and 16 fast channels, about 480, so every pending value goes out within four windows (400 ms) at the worst case. Hand edits, steps, notes, locks and recalls are still never deferred. The 3b soak adds 16 routings (eight on fast destinations, eight on Lua ones, from LFOs, macros moving and a recorder's phase) to the run with everything else.

**Scenes and morph.** `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` stays 5: the depths and on/off are scene globals (depths crossfade, on/off switch at the end), `data.matrix` is a discrete block, and migration fills 16 empty routings with depth 0 and on (§9.2). Morph interpolates depths and switches routings and on/off at 0.5 with its hysteresis (§5.2).

**A routing that changes source or destination ramps through 0.** Depths crossfade, but `data.matrix` switches at a transition's end and at morph's crossing, so a routing whose source or destination differs between A and B would push its old destination toward B's depth. So for such a routing the depth goes A → 0, the routing switches at the midpoint and the depth goes 0 → B: with f the transition's fraction or the morph position, the depth is `A × (1 − 2f)` below 0.5 and `B × (2f − 1)` above, and the source and destination switch once at 0.5 (in morph, at §5.2's 0.52 and 0.48, the depth staying 0 until the routing has switched). `lib/matrix.lua` declares these through its scene block's `through_zero` (§0.1, §0.2). A routing whose source and destination match in A and B crossfades its depth as above. A recall, a morph or a PSET load goes through `set_routing` and never applies the 25 % rule.

#### 10.3 The input level as a source (Question 44, proposed 2026-11-11)

- **A source** `input` (name `IN`, section `audio`, range 0..1, unipolar) registered by `lib/input_level.lua` (**(new)**, `S.lib.input_level`): the louder of norns' two input level polls (`amp_in_l`, `amp_in_r`), times `input_follow_gain`, clamped to 0..1. MOD lists it in a section of its own, read-only apart from the gain.
- **Param** `input_follow_gain` (control 1-30, exp, default 4) in a group `strata_input` (1 param): a line-level input rarely reaches an amplitude of 0.3, so the raw level would move a destination little.
- **The polls.** A norns poll has one callback, and BUFFER's input meter owns these two today, running them only while it shows. `lib/input_level.lua` takes them over: it runs them from a start step to a cleanup step at 30 Hz, keeps the last two levels, and BUFFER's meter reads `S.lib.input_level.levels()` in place of its own polls. 60 poll messages a second arrive whether or not a routing uses the source.
- **Use:** any matrix routing (§10.2). A routing from `input` to `v<i> amp` at a negative depth ducks the voice under the input; to `v<i> vfilt` it opens the filter with it. The matrix refreshes a destination at 24-48 Hz and the level arrives at 30 Hz, so this is an envelope follower for swells and ducking, not for transients.
- **Not in 3.4:** followers of the voices' own levels (seven new engine polls), an attack and release for the follower, the input level as an LFO rate or depth input.
- **Tests** (1i): the source's value is the louder channel times the gain, clamped; `normalized("input")` is 0 with no input; BUFFER's meter shows the same levels as before; a routing from `input` to `v1 amp` at −50 % with a level of 0.5 sends `setFastOffset(0, 0, 4, −0.25)`.
- **Cost if wrong:** if the polls' traffic matters on a CM3, they run only while a routing uses the source or BUFFER shows.

## Cost if wrong

- **The stage's two level lines (Question 40).** If 3.3's 0c builds them in instead, 3.4's 0c only moves the hook; if neither does, the amp destination can't reach the stage and would have to go back to the grain synths.
- **Own LFOs (Question 1).** If keeping norns' library matters more (for example another script's PSET habits), the library can come back for the Lua-run LFOs, with the new shapes and LFO-to-LFO computed around it (Approach B). The engine-run part doesn't change.
- **LFOs on pitch, position and size move to the engine.** They become smoother; old scenes keep their depths and rates. If an old patch relied on the stepping (a 10 Hz LFO stepping at 48 per beat), it sounds cleaner, not louder. The destination rule is one table (`FAST_DESTINATIONS`).
- **High-tier memory (Question 3, decided; re-planned 2026-11-11 from 3.3.0's measured figures, #229).** With the 3.5 MB high floor and 3.3's stage, three notes per voice on 3.5's high tier leave about 970 KB at the mixed worst case, now synth → buffer → synth (the estimate said about 1.07 MB), just under the 1 MB kept back (3.3's §0c.6), and about 1.83 MB at the all-buffer one, where the estimate fitted two notes (§3.6). If 3.5 needs more room, the engine LFOs could share one node, and a lifecycle that keeps no voice synth in the cutting set gives back the margin; 3.4 removes nothing of 3.2.
- **The message budget (Question 23).** If 1500 a second proves too low (LFOs that step under a morph) or too high (#131 on a CM3), it is one constant (`ENGINE_BUDGET`), set from the 3b soak test.
- **Bookmark locks inside the loop only (Question 25).** A lock on a bookmark outside the loop is silent; if performers want the jump, 3.3's recall would need a variant that keeps the loop.
- **48 Hz Lua steps (Question 17).** A 10 Hz Lua LFO is steppier than 3.3's at 120 BPM. `LFO.step_hz` is one constant; raising it raises the cap's message rate with it.
- **Absolute macros (Question 4).** A macro move overwrites a hand edit of its target. Relative macros would be a change to `apply` and the LFO path only; the data stays.
- **16 macros (cross-release decision 6).** If four banks prove hard to follow on hardware, `MACRO_BANK_SIZE` could become 8 (two banks of eight on MOD, the arc and grids still showing four at a time); the params, scenes and side files stay. If the deferred fan-out sounds late under a full E16 sweep, the 3b soak sets `ENGINE_BUDGET` and `MACROS.apply_hz`.
- **A shared bank (Question 28).** Per-surface banks are a field per port and per arc instead of one; the macros stay.
- **The bus per note slot (cross-release decision 1).** With one slot, nothing in 3.4 costs anything; if 3.5 finds per-note blocks too costly (a second `In.kr` per note for a shared voice block), it keeps the formula and changes `fastModBlock` only.
- **Step outputs (cross-release decision 3).** One indirection per hit; if a profile ever shows it, `voices` can be called directly before the loop. A 3.6 output that needs more than the event asks for a new field, never a change to an existing one.
- **Transport following on by default (cross-release decision 5, Questions 26-27).** A user whose MIDI clock source sends start and stop for other gear now gets patterns that follow; SETUP's row turns it off, and the default is one value.
- **The matrix (Question 30, decided; Questions 31-39).** If 16 routings run out, `MATRIX_SLOTS` is one constant (32 halves the refresh rate under the shared cap). If routings from fast LFOs to fast destinations sound stepped at Lua's 48 Hz, the LFO's own destination stays the engine path. If one model is wanted later, LFO destinations and macro targets can be converted to routings by a migration; the matrix's data is already the target. If the shared cap starves destinations, `LFO.sends_per_step` rises, costing messages linearly.
- **Sources listed generically (cross-release decision 4).** MOD loses a little hand layout per section; a section can still draw its own rows (`bank`, morph and scatter do), and a source can add `row` actions.
- **Morph hysteresis and voice switching (Questions 6-7).** A performer who wants a hard 0.5 or morphs that never start voices: one constant, or a filter on the discrete entries.
- **Morph at 10 Hz (Question 8).** A fast morph sweep sounds stepped at 100 ms; raising `MORPH.apply_hz` costs messages linearly.
- **Scatter on five destinations (Question 9).** Scatter on other params means a Lua scatter with its own message budget, a separate feature.
- **Gestures record absolute control values (Question 10).** A loop recorded on a param and replayed after a scene recall sets that param back to the recorded values; that is the point of absolute playback. Relative recording would be a new event kind.
- **Step keys toggle on release (Question 11).** If live step entry feels late, step keys can toggle on press again and the editor open only on steps that are already on.
- **Temporary locks and relative notes (Questions 13-14).** If permanent p-locks are wanted, the step writes the param instead of an override; the data is the same.
- **Chaining through scenes (Question 19).** If performers want chains independent of scenes, a chain list is a new param set and the loader is unchanged.
- **Scene numbers 1-16 (Question 15).** A1-B8 labels are a display change only.
- **MOD after LFO (Question 16).** One line of `UI_PAGES`.
- **Two engine LFO models (Lua's draws differ from the engine's for random shapes).** If the dot's mismatch bothers anyone, random shapes' Lua values can be fed from a seeded generator shared with the engine; nobody should hear the difference.

## Calls made in this design

- Strata's own six LFOs, one 48 Hz Lua clock, exact phases (free from `util.time()`, clocked from `clock.get_beats()`), eight shapes, LFO-to-LFO rate (±2 octaves) and depth (×(1 ± amount)) inputs from lower-numbered LFOs only, evaluated in Lua for every target.
- The destination decides where an LFO runs: `v<i> pitch`, `pos`, `size`, `vfilt` and the new `v<i> amp` in the engine, everything else in Lua; `v<i> main` always in Lua.
- The same offset math in both places (± half the range, clamped), except the voice cutoff (exponential) and amp (the crossmod amplitude law), both new to 3.2 or 3.4.
- At most 12 Lua LFO sends per step, round robin; engine LFOs send only setting changes, rates deduped at 0.5 % and depths at 1/1000; clocked engine LFOs resynced once a bar.
- One 35-channel control bus for the fast destinations, read by one `In.kr` in every grain synth; engine LFO and scatter nodes in a `fastModGroup` at the head of the engine group; one SynthDef per LFO shape, replaced with `Synth.replace` on a shape change; channels zeroed when their last writer ends.
- Five commands: `setFastLfo`, `stopFastLfo`, `syncFastLfo`, `setScatter`, `setFastOffset`; the area `modulation` at order 160.
- A modulation matrix of 16 routings (source by key, destination by id, depth and on/off as params), destinations the LFO list without macros, offsets summed with the LFOs and clamped once, fast destinations through a Lua-written offset node, sends inside the LFOs' cap and deferrable, LFO destinations and macro targets unchanged.
- Macros: absolute, normalized-range min and max, silent sets from scenes and morph, targets stored by id outside the params, learn through the touch hook, LFOs on macros as extra offsets on the targets, no macro on macros or morph.
- Morph writes params at most 10 times a second, only the differing fields, discrete keys at 0.5 with 0.02 of hysteresis, in recall order through the same functions a recall uses; `S.current_scene` 0 while morphed; never a recall.
- Scatter: one engine node, smooth random per voice and destination, unlocked voices only, depth 10 % and 0.1 Hz by default with pitch and position on.
- Gesture recorders: control params only, absolute values, from hand edits only; free loops through pattern_time's metro, quantized loops converted to beats and clocked per bar; per-PSET and live side files; scenes store only whether each plays.
- Cross-release (2026-11-08): the fast-modulation bus addressed by (voice, note slot, destination) with one slot per voice; the cutoff and voice-level amp offsets in 3.3 0c's per-voice mix stage; steps store chords as lists and 3.4 plays the first note; the sequencer plays through registered step outputs; modulators register as sources that MOD lists; patterns follow norns' clock transport by default and never lead it; 16 macros in four banks of four with one shown bank, fan-out always deferrable.
- Sequencer: levels stay in `S.patterns`; sparse step attributes; per-voice length and division as registered voice params; one coroutine per voice; temporary locks as base overrides; relative scale-degree notes applied like MIDI notes; ratchets at least 20 ms apart; Euclidean fill destructive; chaining through scene ranges every N bars; quantized actions through one scheduler.
- Scenes: 16 numbered slots in banks of eight, 12-character names through `textentry`, old default names dropped.
- The MOD page after LFO; the LFO page shows a window of three LFOs; the 256's page 1 rows 9-13 hold the gesture recorders and the macros (row 8 stays clear for 3.3's page-5 key); step keys toggle on release and a 0.3 s hold opens the step editor on any page.
