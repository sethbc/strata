## Decided in the brainstorm (2026-11-12)

1. **A shared pool of extra notes**, not a fixed limit per voice. Every running voice keeps its one note as today; the notes beyond it come from one pool that all seven voices share, with at most eight notes on a voice. The roadmap's "2 on standard and 6 on high" per voice does not fit: one more note on every voice costs about 1570 KB at the worst case (`docs/design/3.4-design.md:498`).
2. **Lifting a finger: latch and release, both kept** as the two behaviors of `midi_note_off`. Latch (today's `ignore`): a chord keeps sounding, and the next chord, starting with the first key after all are up, replaces it. Release (today's `voice off`): each note fades when its key lifts, and the voice stops with the last. Latch is the default for plain MIDI and release once an MPE mode is on.
3. **A note is whatever makes the pitch** (approach 1 below): on a synth voice another voice synth on the voice's bus, grained by the voice's one grain synth; on a buffer or input voice another grain synth at its own rate. Every note of a voice shares its one mix stage.
4. **Lua allocates.** A new note module picks the slots and decides what is stolen; the engine never stacks inside a slot.
5. **Extra notes have a lean lifecycle.** A source switch or a steal cuts them with the 50 ms fade; only the voice's own note crossfades. A pool slot holds at most one live and one cutting synth.

## Questions for review

**All decided 2026-11-12 by the maintainer, each asked with its alternatives: every one as recommended, except Question 8** (ring streams, not the recommended refusal). The body is written to the decisions.

Every choice below could reasonably go the other way. Each has a recommendation.

1. **Per-note expression is set on the note's own synth, and the fast-modulation bus keeps one block per voice.** Cross-release decision 2 planned the opposite: raise `fastModNoteSlots` and write pressure and slide into a block per note. Looking at the built code, that costs more than it gives. Block 0 already does two jobs, the voice's block (LFOs, scatter, the matrix, the stage's cutoff and amp) and the only note's block, so notes would need blocks of their own beside it (9 per voice). `\strataScatter` and `\strataFastOffsets` are sized from the bus's channel count (`lib/engine/StrataModulation.sc:236`): at 9 blocks the offset node alone grows from 11 KB to about 90 KB, and scatter has to be rewritten to skip the note blocks. Every grain synth would need a second bus read, and voice synths, which are the notes of a synth voice, read no bus at all. The alternative: five controls on each note's synth (`noteBend`, `noteAmp`, `notePos`, `noteSize`, `noteMain`), set by one command per note, lagged in the SynthDef. It costs about 1 KB per synth, changes nothing in the modulation area, and one message carries a note's whole expression. What it gives up: per-note values can no longer be set on a node that outlives the note (the engine keeps them per slot instead), and a later per-note engine LFO or per-note matrix routing would need its own path. **Recommended: controls on the note's synth** (§3.3). `FAST_MOD_NOTE_SLOTS` stays 1, and `setFastOffset`'s note slot stays 0.
2. **The pool's size.** A maximum of 16 extra notes on both tiers, held by the memory check; a default of 8 on high and 4 on standard; the setting kept per device in a data file, not in PSETs, since it is the device's capacity. Estimated: a full pool of 16, every slot holding a live and a cutting buffer stream on top of the tightest 3.4 case, leaves about 1.5 MB on high and about 2.2 MB on standard. **Recommended: 16, 8 and 4, with a floor of 1.25 MB (five 256 KB probes) at the full pool on both tiers**; if package 0b's measurement falls short, the maximum drops to 12. Standard's default is a guess about the CM3's CPU, which nothing measures before the 4.0 hardware check: SETUP shows `may xrun` beside a pool above the default there, as it does for the high tier on a CM3 (§1, §2.5).
3. **A target that exists once per voice takes the largest value among the voice's held notes.** The voice filter is one per voice, grain density is one per voice, and a synth voice has one grain stream, so pressure on cutoff cannot be per note. The alternatives are the average (a new soft note pulls the filter down) and the newest note (the filter jumps at every key). **Recommended: the largest**, lagged like every offset: the strongest finger wins, and adding or lifting a soft finger changes nothing (§6.3).
4. **Slide is measured from where the touch began.** A LinnStrument's Y starts near the middle of the cell, an Osmose's CC74 starts at 0, an Exquis's at its center. Read as an absolute value, slide would move the target the moment a note starts, by a different amount on each controller. **Recommended: relative**, the note's CC74 less its value at note on, so every note starts on the stored sound; the modulation source reports the absolute value (§6.2).
5. **Polyphony is on for every voice from the first start.** With the per-voice cap at 8 and the pool at its default, a second key held on a plain MIDI keyboard now adds a note where 3.4 re-pitched the one note. That is the release's point, but it changes existing MIDI setups. A voice's `note cap` at 1, or the pool at 0, plays 3.4's way exactly. **Recommended: on by default** (§2.1).
6. **Voices gain attack and release time params.** A new note on a synth voice fades in over its SynthDef's fixed attack, 2 to 8 s for every voice but Karplus (`lib/engine/StrataVoiceDefs.sc`), and the accent multiplies an envelope that is still near zero, so fast playing on six of the seven synth voices would be nearly silent. 3.4 left "an attack param" to a later release (`docs/design/3.4-design.md:815`). **Recommended: two params per voice, `attack` and `release`, in seconds, defaulting to each voice's fixed times of today** (so every scene sounds the same), acting on every note of the voice, its own included (§3.5).
7. **The voice's own note parks; it is never freed while the voice runs.** In release mode the key that started the voice can lift while others are held. Freeing the voice's own synths then would leave a running voice without the grain synth that freeze, the scan, the playheads and a dozen engine paths address (`grainKey`). **Recommended: the own note's synth fades to silence and keeps running** until the key returns or the voice stops. It costs the CPU of one silent note while parked and one envelope per synth (§3.2).
8. **Extra notes on a frozen synth voice.** Its ring writer is paused, so more voice synths on its bus would be paid for and never heard. The recommendation was to refuse them in 3.5. The alternative makes a frozen synth voice's notes streams of its frozen ring, each at its own rate, as an input voice's notes are streams of the frozen copy; that is a second kind of note on a synth voice, started at a freeze and cut at an unfreeze. **Decided 2026-11-12: ring streams** (not as recommended), so a frozen sound can be played as chords on any source (§2.4, §3.4).
9. **MIDI notes set an input voice's grain pitch**, note 60 at 1, as they set a buffer voice's rate. Today an input voice takes no pitch from MIDI (`lib/midi_in.lua:207-224`), and 3.4 left this to 3.5 (its Question 24). Without it a chord on an input voice has no meaning. **Recommended: yes** (§5.2).
10. **Split zones are a zone and a note range per voice.** Each voice has a zone (off, lower, upper) and a lowest and highest note; a note plays every voice whose zone and range hold it, so ranges that overlap layer voices. They are edited in one SETUP editor, by encoder or by playing the lowest and highest note. Defaults: voice 1 takes the lower zone's whole range and voice 2 the upper zone's, which is a LinnStrument's two splits with nothing set. The alternative is note ranges alone, with the zone ignored. **Recommended: zone and range, with learn** (§5.4, §9.3).
11. **Pressure and slide as modulation sources: one pair for all voices, and a pair per voice.** `PRES` and `SLID` are the largest pressure and slide among all held notes; `PR1`..`PR7` and `SL1`..`SL7` are each voice's. The per-voice ones show on MOD's list only while their voice holds a note that has sent expression, and are always choosable in the matrix. **Recommended: sixteen sources, read-only** (§6.5).
12. **What pressure on amp does.** Two readings are both common. As a swell, pressure adds level above the voice's own (`1 + depth × pressure`): zero pressure is the stored sound, nothing ever goes silent, and it suits voices that drone. As a level, pressure is the loudness (`1 - depth × (1 - pressure)`): a note is silent until pressed, which plays like a bowed instrument but silences an aftertouch keyboard's held notes whenever its pressure rests at 0, and starts every note silent on a controller that sends pressure 0 before its note on. **Recommended: both, as two targets, `amp` (the swell, and pressure's default at 100 %) and `level`**, so the default never takes sound away and a player who wants the second reading picks it (§6.2).

## Intent

3.5 makes each voice playable as an instrument:
- **Chords inside a voice.** Each of the seven voices plays up to eight notes at once on its own sound, from a pool the voices share.
- **MPE.** A LinnStrument, an Osmose or an Exquis plays a chord on one voice with each note's own pitch bend, pressure and slide.
- **Expression that reaches the sound.** Per voice, pressure and slide each drive a chosen target with a depth.
- **Chords from the sequencer.** A step's notes, stored as a list since 3.4, sound together, and SEQ edits them.

Everything defaults to 3.4's sound: a voice that nothing plays chords on, and every scene and pattern saved in 3.4, sounds the same.

## Goals

0. A restructure that changes no behavior: the Lua hooks the note module needs, the engine's notes area with its four commands as stubs, and the measurement of every voice SynthDef, which no check has measured yet.
1. Up to eight notes per voice (`NOTE_SLOTS` 8): the voice's own and seven from a shared pool of at most 16 (`NOTE_POOL.max`), sized on SETUP and kept per device.
2. An engine that never stacks: a slot holds at most one live and one cutting synth, the pool's tails are trimmed oldest first, and the pool's worst case is in the memory check on both tiers.
3. One allocator in Lua for every player of notes (MIDI, MPE, the sequencer), with one stealing rule.
4. MIDI polyphony with the latch and release behaviors, poly aftertouch and channel pressure.
5. MPE: lower and upper zones, the configuration message, per-note bend at a settable range (48 semitones by default), pressure, slide, and the high-resolution prefix (CC87) the Osmose sends.
6. Two MPE assignment modes beside the two plain ones: every note to the selected voice (the default MPE mode), and split zones.
7. Expression mapping per voice: pressure and slide each to one of amp, the main param, grain size, grain density, position and the voice filter's cutoff, with a bipolar depth. Per note where the target is per note.
8. A bounded message rate: a note's whole expression is one message, and at most 16 are sent per 48 Hz step.
9. Sequencer chords through the same allocator, with no change to the pattern or scene format, and chord editing on SEQ and the arc.
10. MAIN shows each voice's note count; VOICE gains a `notes` section; SETUP gains the MIDI and MPE settings, the zones editor and the pool; MOD lists pressure and slide.

## Non-goals

- More than seven voices, or voice banks (the roadmap's Non-goals).
- Per-note matrix routings and per-note engine LFOs. The matrix, the engine LFOs and scatter act on the whole voice, as in 3.4.
- A filter per note. The voice filter stays one per voice (3.3).
- Chord entry from the grids. A held step opens the screen's step editor, as in 3.4; no layout gains note keys.
- An arc page for pressure (the roadmap's stretch).
- MIDI out, and MPE out (3.6).
- Sustain pedal, sostenuto, portamento and MPE's note-off velocity.
- An arpeggiator or strum (3.8 lists a grain arpeggiator).
- Tunings other than twelve equal steps (3.8). Every note's frequency or rate is worked out in Lua, in one function, so 3.8 has one place to change.
- A velocity curve, and velocity as a modulation source.

## Anti-goals

- **Stacked nodes.** No slot ever holds two live synths, however fast notes, steals, source switches and stops arrive.
- **A pool that the engine trusts Lua to keep.** The engine counts its own notes and refuses or trims past the pool.
- **Breaking a CM3 by default.** The standard tier's default pool is small, and a pool of 0 is 3.4.
- **Old scenes and PSETs that sound different.** Every new param defaults to today's sound, and expression is neutral until a controller sends it.
- **Messages that grow with the controller's rate.** An MPE controller can send thousands of messages a second; Strata sends at most 768.
- **A voice that changes under a held chord.** A source switch, a scene recall or a param turn while notes are held leaves every held note sounding on what the voice now is.
- **Hidden state.** The note count shows on MAIN; the pool, the zones and the MPE settings show on SETUP.

## Constraints

**Platform:**
- **scsynth's real-time pool is 8192 KB**, and norns never raises it. At 3.4.0 the tightest case, synth to buffer to synth with spectral freeze on every voice, leaves 4120 KB on high (about 4000 counting the spectral writers' late allocations); the same case leaves 4790 KB on standard. `\strataGrainBuf` is 69.1 KB, `\strataGrainRing` 59.0 KB, a stage 40.9 KB.
- **No voice SynthDef has ever been measured.** `test/engine_check/memory.scd` probes the two grain defs, the stage, the record synth and the modulation nodes. On record are two estimates: Karplus's two 0.1 s `Pluck` lines at about 64 KB, and seven cut voice synths costing 160-170 KB together.
- **The engine's bookkeeping holds one synth per key.** `synths` and `releasingSynths[voice]` are Dictionaries keyed `\voice<i>`, `\grain<i>` and `\mod<i>` (`lib/engine/StrataUtil.sc:10-12`), and `gateVoice`, `releaseVoice`, `voiceRunning`, `forgetFailedStarts` and the check's `voiceTrio` are built on those three keys. `fadingSynths`, `cuttingSynths`, `grainSlots` and `ringReaders` are sets or keyed by synth and already hold any number per voice.
- **`watchVoiceSynth` restarts a synth that frees while live, and any key it does not know starts a mod synth** (`lib/engine/StrataLifecycle.sc:210-218`).
- **Every per-voice send reaches one synth.** `setGrainParam`, `setVoiceControl`, `retrigger`, the `envShape`, `history`, `bufRate`, `scaleNote`, `t_scanReset` and `freeze` handlers and `pointAtCopyRing` send to `grainKey` or `voiceKey`; `bufferChanged` treats every grain synth that is not `synths[grainKey]` as releasing (`lib/engine/StrataBuffers.sc:191`).
- **The fast pitch offset is linear in the rate and is quantized**: it enters `pitchShift` before `quantizedOffset`. Only `bend` and frequency crossmod bend a quantized voice (`lib/engine/StrataVariation.sc:36-51`). A ring grain's rate is clipped to ±4.
- **matron polls carry one float**, and every command passes through sclang.
- **MPE** (the MIDI Association's specification): a zone is a master channel (1 for the lower zone, 16 for the upper) and member channels counted away from it; the configuration message is RPN 6 on the master channel with the member count; a member channel's pitch bend range defaults to 48 semitones and the master's to 2; a controller sends a note's first bend, pressure and CC74 before its note on. A LinnStrument sends RPN 6 and puts its left split on the lower zone and its right split on the upper. An Osmose sends CC87 just before a channel pressure or CC74 as its low seven bits.
- **norns' own CC handler sees every message** whatever Strata does (`lua/core/menu/params.lua`), so a PARAMS > MAP mapping of CC74 moves its param while an MPE controller slides.

**Project:**
- Everything in AGENTS.md, in particular: Lua as the only source of values; replace, never stack; `sendToSynth` for synths that can end on their own; `openedGate`; one persistent stage per voice; the real-time memory rule (a new node per voice needs the budget re-measured); `startPlacedGrainSynth`; engine files add methods and never variables; the command table and the engine stub; one owner per file per wave; the 200-local limit.
- Three AGENTS.md invariants change on purpose, each rewritten by the package that changes its code: "Pattern steps and MIDI notes never create nodes" (an extra note creates exactly one), "One grain path" (a grain SynthDef gains a per-note gain and accent) and the real-time memory rule's bound on what a voice holds.
- AGENTS.md is 7471 of its 8000 words. 3.5 adds four command rows and about six invariant lines, so every added line keeps to the two-line rule and the release package trims elsewhere.
- The largest scenes file is asserted at exactly 7394 tables (`test/unit/matrix_test.lua:1394`). 3.5 adds numbers to `voices[i].extras`, which add no tables.
- `lib/performance.lua` is loaded by the engine stub with nothing but `{constants}`, and `set_setting` rewrites `performance.data` with exactly two keys. The pool does not go there.

## Approaches

### What a note is

1. **Whatever makes the pitch** (picked). A synth voice's note is a voice synth, about a third the memory of a grain stream and no grain CPU, with its own pluck and its own main param; a buffer or input voice's note is a grain stream. Two mechanisms, and on synth voices grain size and position stay per voice.
2. **Every note a grain stream.** One mechanism, and pitch, position, size and amp per note everywhere; but a synth voice's chord is one sound transposed (the timbre shifts with pitch, one Karplus pluck sounds at every note), slide cannot reach the main param per note, and every note costs a full stream.
3. **Chords from the grain intervals.** Free, but no per-note pressure, slide or envelope, which is the point of MPE.

### How many notes

A fixed limit per voice gives 3 on high at best, a ten-finger chord cut to three. A shared pool (picked) puts the notes where they are played, since MPE playing concentrates on one or two voices; chords sequenced on every voice compete for it.

### Who allocates

The engine could take a note id and pick slots itself, which keeps the count beside the nodes. Lua (picked) keeps every value in the `voices` table's owner and puts the stealing rule under the unit tests; the engine still enforces the bounds, so a Lua mistake costs a refused note, never memory.

### How per-note values reach the engine

More note slots on the fast-modulation bus, or controls on the note's own synth: Question 1.

### 2. Notes in Lua

#### 2.1 The model

- A voice has eight **slots**. Slot 1 is the voice's own note: it exists while the voice runs, its pitch is the voice's params (`v<i>_freq`, `v<i>_buf_rate`, `v<i>_grain_pitch`), and nothing but the voice's own players use it.
- Slots 2-8 hold **extra notes**. Each is one engine synth, has a pitch of its own that no param stores, and counts against the pool.
- A voice's `note cap` (a param, 1-8, default 8) bounds its slots. A cap of 1, or a pool of 0, is 3.4: every note re-pitches the own note.
- A note has an **owner** (`"midi"` or `"seq"`) and a **key** unique to that owner (a MIDI note's channel and number; a chord note's index in its step).
- Extra notes are never stored: not in params, scenes or PSETs. A scene recall leaves held notes held.

#### 2.2 `S.lib.notes` (`lib/notes.lua`)

The only module that calls `noteOn`, `noteOff`, `noteExpression` and `setNotePool`.

- **`play(voice, note)`**, where `note` is `{owner, key, pitch, velocity, set_own, park, expression}`: `pitch` a MIDI note number, fractional allowed, already snapped to the scale by its player; `set_own(pitch)` the player's way of pitching the own note (MIDI sets the param as today; the sequencer sets a base override as today); `park` whether the own note parks when this note is released under others (MIDI: yes; the sequencer: no); `expression` the note's first `{bend, amp, position, size, main}`, neutral when absent. Returns the slot, or nil when the note was refused. The branches, in order:
  1. **The same owner and key already held:** that slot again, re-pitched and accented, no node.
  2. **Slot 1 is free** (no note held on the voice, or the own note parked): slot 1. The voice starts if it is stopped (`start_voice`); `set_own(pitch)`; then `retrigger`, exactly today's messages. A parked own note gets `noteOn` on slot 1 in place of the `retrigger` (§3.2); a frozen synth voice gets it before the `retrigger`, to carry the rate (§2.4). *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 6, 15, 18, 19, 47):* one rule for slot 1: `set_own(pitch)` first, then the start of a stopped voice (`toggle_voice`), then the note's expression where it differs from the engine's, then `noteOn` on slot 0 when the own note was parked or the voice is a frozen synth voice (to a running voice only), then `retrigger`: the `noteOn` never stands in for the `retrigger`, since the stage's step gate and accent open only on it, and in the engine it fires no accent on an unfrozen voice. The note record gains `quiet`: slot 1 then only calls `set_own` and sends that `noteOn`, and its caller starts and accents the voice (the sequencer). On a stopped voice slot 1 is always free, so every play there takes this branch, and branches 3 to 6 run only on a running voice.
  3. **A free extra slot under the cap, and room in the pool:** the free slot whose last note ended longest ago, so a tail has the longest time to fade. The note's first expression goes first (`noteExpression`), then `noteOn`.
  4. **A free slot but the pool is full:** the oldest held extra note on any voice is stolen with `noteOff(voice, slot, 1)`, the 50 ms cut, and the new note starts as in 3.
  5. **No free slot under the cap:** the voice's oldest held extra note is stolen in place (its slot re-pitched and accented, no node).
  6. **Nothing can be stolen** (a cap of 1, a pool of 0, or every candidate protected by the rules below): slot 1 is re-pitched with `set_own` and accented, whoever holds it. This is 3.4's behavior, the newest note winning the one note.
- **`release(voice, owner, key)`**: `noteOff(voice, slot, 0)` for an extra note. For slot 1: when other notes are held on the voice and the note asked to `park`, the own note parks (`noteOff` on slot 1); otherwise nothing is sent, and the player decides whether the voice stops (MIDI) or its override ends (the sequencer). *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 39, 40):* a release, a steal or a freeze's cuts that leave a running voice with no held note under its parked own note unpark it (`noteOn` on slot 0 at velocity 0, after a neutral `noteExpression` when the engine's is not neutral): a running voice never stays parked and noteless. `forget_owner(voice, owner)`, a player's reset, releases the owner's extra notes and drops its own note with nothing sent and nothing parked.
- **`release_owner(voice, owner)`**, **`release_all(voice)`**: every such note; `release_all` runs from the voice-stop registry and sends nothing, since `voiceOff` releases every note in the engine.
- **`set_expression(voice, slot, values)`**: stores `{bend, amp, position, size, main}` and marks the note due for the flush, which this module runs (§6.4).
- **`held(voice)`** (the count MAIN shows), **`slots(voice)`**, **`pool()`**, **`pool_used()`**, **`set_pool(n)`**.
- **`note_values(voice, pitch)`**: the one place a pitch becomes what the engine plays, `{note, freq, rate}`: `freq` from `musicutil.note_num_to_freq`, clamped to the voice's freq range; `rate` `2 ^ ((pitch - 60) / 12)`, clamped to 0.25-4, or, for a frozen synth voice, `2 ^ ((pitch - reference) / 12)` from the voice's freeze reference (§2.4). 3.8's tunings change this function.

**Slot 1 has one holder at a time, and the newest wins.** When MIDI holds slot 1 and a step plays the voice, the step's notes are extra notes with pitches of their own, so a step no longer hides a held MIDI note behind its override, as it does in 3.4; only branch 6 lets one player re-pitch the other's own note. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 32, 35):* a step never takes anything from another owner: while another owner holds slot 1, the step's first note too takes only a free extra slot (`extra_only`), so a step no longer re-pitches a held MIDI note by branch 6, while a MIDI note may still take a sequenced one's place. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 55, refining 35):* only a key physically down holds slot 1 against a step: a latched MIDI note whose key is up gives way to the step's first note, as in 3.4 (MIDI forgets it, sending nothing, and the step re-pitches and accents the own note); a first note left in an extra slot when MIDI lets go of slot 1 moves back to slot 1; and a MIDI note that takes slot 1 from a step ends the step's note override at once.

**What is never stolen:** slot 1; a note of a locked voice (`S.frozen`), for another voice; and a note that started less than the cut time (50 ms, `NOTES.cut_seconds`) ago. The last rule bounds the cutting synths: a burst can only steal notes that were already sounding before it, so at most the pool's number are ever cutting at once, which is the worst case §1 budgets. A step that asks for more chord notes than the pool holds gets the pool's number and the rest fall to branch 6, never a churn of notes stolen as they start. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 26, 32):* a step's further notes are played `extra_only` (only branch 1 or 3) and are refused past the cap or the pool, never branch 6: the chord keeps its first notes.

**The engine's tails are not Lua's.** Lua counts held notes only. A released note's tail still sounds in its slot, and the engine trims the oldest tail when a new note needs the room (§3.4). Lua needs no timer and no poll.

**Registrations:** a start step that loads `notes.data` and sends `setNotePool`; a cleanup step; `S.register_voice_stop` (the voice's notes forgotten); `S.register_freeze_change` (a synth voice's extra notes released when it freezes, §2.4); the invariant `notes` (each slot's record is whole; held extra notes never exceed the pool or a voice's cap; a stopped voice holds none).

#### 2.3 The pool setting

- `notes.data`, through `S.lib.data_files`: `{version = 1, pool = <n>}`. Missing or unreadable: the tier's default (`NOTE_POOL[S.performance.tier]`).
- `set_pool(n)` clamps to 0..`NOTE_POOL.max`, writes first and changes state only when the write succeeds (as `S.lib.grid_core.set_rotation` does), releases the newest held extra notes past a smaller pool, then sends `setNotePool`.
- It is device-level: a PSET or a scene never changes it.

#### 2.4 Freeze, locks and sources

- **A frozen synth voice plays its notes as streams of the frozen sound** (Question 8). What sounds is the voice's ring, so a note's pitch is a rate: the note against the voice's **freeze reference**, the pitch its own note had when it froze (`S.lib.notes` takes it from `S.lib.engine_sync.engine_base_value("v<i>_freq")` in the freeze-change registry of §0.1, and when a voice starts frozen). A key at the reference plays the frozen sound as it is; a key a fifth above plays it a fifth up. The reference is never stored. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built:* the hooks keep it: taken at a running voice's freeze, at every start and at a running voice's source change (the grain-restart hook), dropped at an unfreeze and at a stop; a stopped voice keeps none.
  - `play` works as for any voice. Slot 1's `set_own` still sets `v<i>_freq`, as today, which is what the voice plays once it unfreezes; its rate goes with `noteOn` on slot 1.
  - While frozen the voice's `scaleNote` is the reference, not the moving freq, so quantize measures from the frozen sound.
- **At a freeze**, a synth voice's extra notes are cut (`noteOff` with `cut` 1, from the registry) and forgotten: their sound is in the frozen ring, and their voice synths would be paid for unheard. Keys still down do nothing more.
- **At an unfreeze**, the engine cuts the voice's ring-stream notes and restarts each held one as a voice synth at its kept `freq` (§3.4); Lua's records stand.
- A buffer or input voice needs none of this: its notes are streams already, and an input voice's read the frozen copy.
- A source switch needs nothing from Lua: the engine restarts each held extra note on the new source from the values it keeps (§3.4). A source switch unfreezes first.
- The lock (`S.frozen`) stops nothing: a locked voice can be played.

#### 2.5 What SETUP shows

A `note pool` row: 0-16, E3, kept in `notes.data`. On standard a dim `may xrun` note shows under a pool above the tier's default.

### 3. Notes in the engine

#### 3.1 The commands

Four, in `StrataNotes.sc`. Each ignores an out-of-range index or a non-finite value with one console line, as 3.4's five do, and clips its continuous arguments. Slots are 0-indexed in the engine: slot 0 is the own note, 1-7 the extra ones (Lua passes `slot - 1`).

| Command | Signature | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `noteOn(voice, slot, note, freq, rate, velocity)` | `"iiffff"` | Start slot 0-7's note: `note` 0-127 (the scale reference), `freq` 8-20000 Hz (a synth source plays it), `rate` 0.25-4 (a buffer source plays it as the note's `bufRate`, an input source as its `pitchShift`, a frozen synth voice as its stream's `noteRate`), `velocity` 0-1. Slot 0 unparks the own note and takes no pitch, but for a frozen synth voice's `rate`. Never stacks |
| `noteOff(voice, slot, cut)` | `"iii"` | End slot 0-7's note: `cut` 0 releases it, 1 cuts it in 50 ms. Slot 0 parks the own note (`cut` ignored). No-op on an empty slot |
| `noteExpression(voice, slot, bend, amp, position, size, main)` | `"iifffff"` | Slot 0-7's expression, kept for the slot and set on its synth: `bend` a ratio 0.0625-16, `amp` a gain 0-2, `position`, `size` and `main` offsets of -1..1 half ranges. Creates no node |
| `setNotePool(size)` | `"i"` | The pool, 0-`notePoolMax`; trims past a smaller one |

`voiceOn`, `voiceOff`, `retrigger` and every other command keep their signatures. `voiceOn` resets slot 0's kept expression to neutral, as it resets `bend`. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 46):* the engine keeps `noteOn`'s `note` and reads it nowhere yet: a buffer note's `scaleNote` comes from the voice's reference note and the note's rate, the same today, and 3.8's tunings will need the note itself.

#### 3.2 The SynthDefs

Every per-note control is engine-owned (`engineOwnedControls`): `setVoiceParam` and `setGrainParam` refuse it.

**The seven voice defs** gain `noteGate = 1`, `noteBend = 1`, `noteAmp = 1`, `noteMain = 0`, and, by Question 6, `attackTime` and `releaseTime` in place of each def's fixed ASR times:
- `noteGate` drives a second ASR with the def's attack and release and no `doneAction`: the own note's park. Its output multiplies the signal.
- `voiceModulation` takes the bend as `bend × noteBend.lag(noteLag)`.
- `noteAmp.lag(noteLag)` multiplies the level inside `voiceLevel`'s clip.
- `noteMain.lag(noteLag)` offsets the def's expression control by its half range, clipped to the control's range: FM `index`, Folder `fold`, Sub `drift`, Pulse `width`, Karplus `decay`, Ring `ratio`, and Resonator `rq`, since a Resonator's MAIN param is its pitch. The ranges are literals in `StrataVoiceDefs.sc`, compared with Lua's param specs by a docs test.

**The two grain defs** gain `noteGate = 1`, `noteBend = 1`, `noteAmp = 1`, `notePos = 0`, `noteSize = 0`, `velocity = 1` and `t_accent = 0`. A buffer or input note's pitch needs no new control: a buffer note's is its synth's own `bufRate`, with a `scaleNote` of its own, and an input note's is its synth's own `pitchShift` (an absolute rate, note 60 at 1, as `v<i>_grain_pitch` is the own note's since Question 9), under the voice's one `scaleNote`. `\strataGrainRing` also gains `noteRate = 1`, which multiplies `pitchShift` before quantize: the rate of a frozen synth voice's note, whose `pitchShift` stays the voice's (Question 8).
- `noteBend.lag(noteLag)` multiplies `bendRate` outside its `rateLevel` term, so it bends a quantized note and acts on every grain synth, a frozen synth voice's streams included, where `bend` and crossmod do not reach.
- `notePos` and `noteSize` add to the fast offsets `fastModPosition` and `fastMod` apply, before their clips.
- `noteAmp × (1 + voiceAccent(t_accent, velocity))` and the `noteGate` envelope multiply `grainSignal`'s `fade`: a per-synth gain before the voice filter, as 3.3's §0c.6 planned. The velocity check's rule becomes: in a grain def, `velocity` feeds only the accent's `levelScale`.
- The synth-source grain synth, which grains a voice's whole chord, keeps every one of these at its default.

With every per-note control at its default, each def plays 3.4's values within 1e-6 (a check, as 3.4's for the fast offsets).

**Messages go one way per kind of name:**
- **Voice-wide** names reach every note of the voice: through `setVoiceSynths(voice, pairs)` and `setGrainSynths(voice, pairs)` **(new, `StrataNotes.sc`)**, which send to the own synth and every live extra one. Of the sites in Constraints' list, these use them: `setGrainParam`'s plain sets, `setVoiceControl`, `envShape`, `history`, `t_scanReset`, `freeze` and `pointAtCopyRing`; `bufferChanged`'s live set is built the same way.
- **Per-note** names reach one synth and are never fanned out: a voice synth's `freq`; a buffer grain synth's `bufRate` and `scaleNote`; an input grain synth's `pitchShift`; `noteRate` and the other `note*` controls, `velocity` and `t_accent`. So the param that is a voice's own pitch moves its own note only: `freq` on a synth voice, `buf_rate` on a buffer voice, `grain_pitch` on an input voice. On a synth or buffer voice `grain_pitch` is voice-wide and transposes the whole chord. An input voice's `scaleNote` is voice-wide.
- `retrigger` keeps accenting the voice: the own voice synth and the stage, and one grain on every grain synth of the voice in triggered mode.

#### 3.3 Why controls and not bus blocks

Question 1. The fast-modulation bus, its five commands, its nodes and its checks are untouched: 35 channels, `fastModNoteSlots` 1. A voice-wide offset reaches every note because every note's synth reads the voice's one block (grain notes) or passes through the stage (synth notes), which is 3.4's behavior for free.

#### 3.4 The lifecycle

State, in `areaState[\notes]`: per voice and extra slot a record `(synth, cutting, state, note, freq, rate, velocity, expression, order)`; the pool size; a counter for `order`.

**`noteOn` on an extra slot:**
- **Live:** the pitch is set on its synth and its accent fired. No node.
- **Releasing:** the tail is cut (it becomes the slot's cutting synth; an earlier cutting synth of the slot is freed at once) and a new synth starts.
- **Empty:** a new synth starts.
- Before a new synth starts, if live and releasing extra notes already number the pool, the oldest releasing one anywhere is cut. If none is releasing the command is refused with one console line: Lua never sends that.
- A synth-source voice: `Synth(voiceSynthDefs[voice], ...)` at the head of `voiceGroup`, from `voiceControls[voice]` with its own `freq`, `velocity`, `t_trig` and expression. A buffer or input voice: a grain synth through `startGrainSynth(voice, voiceMixGroup(voice), \addToHead)`, from `grainControlPairs(voice)` with its own `bufRate` and `scaleNote` (a buffer voice) or `pitchShift` (an input voice) and its expression. Placement is never relative to another note's synth.
- **A frozen synth voice:** a `\strataGrainRing` reading the voice's ring through `grainReadPairs(voice)`, as the voice's own grain synth does, placed at the head of `processGroup` and writing the synth path, with `noteRate` the note's `rate` and `rateLevel` and `pan` at 0.
- **A new buffer note starts its scan at the voice's position**, as a voice start does (every grain start restarts the scan); it does not join the own note's scan phase. The playheads show the own note.
- **Cutting note synths never exceed the pool.** Lua's rule (§2.2) keeps them there; if a cut would pass it, the oldest cutting note synth is freed at once.

**`noteOff`:** `cut` 0 sends `gate 0` (the synth frees itself after its release; the slot is releasing until then); `cut` 1 cuts it. Both through `sendToSynth`.

**Slot 0:** `noteOff` sets `noteGate 0` on the synth that makes the own note's sound: the own voice synth (a synth source), or the own grain synth (a buffer or input source, and a frozen synth voice). `noteOn` sets it to 1 and fires that synth's accent; on a frozen synth voice it also sets the own grain synth's `noteRate` to `rate` and fires its accent whether or not it was parked. `voiceOn` starts every voice unparked. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 44, 47):* on an unfrozen voice the slot-0 `noteOn` fires no accent and no trigger: the `retrigger` Lua sends after it accents (and plucks Karplus) once. On a frozen synth voice it sets the own grain synth's `noteRate` and fires that synth's `t_accent` at `velocity`. On a synth voice the park is always on the own voice synth, frozen or not, and while frozen on the own grain synth too: the freeze parks nothing, and the unfreeze opens the grain synth's park again.

**The voice's own commands:**
- **`voiceOff`** releases every extra note with the voice. Their tails stay in their slots and the pool until they free.
- **`voiceOn`** on a voice still releasing re-gates its own synths as today and leaves the extra tails to end.
- **`setGrainSource`** on a running voice: the own note crossfades as today. Every extra note is cut, and each that was live restarts on the new source in the same bundle from its kept values; a releasing one is not restarted. So a slot holds a live and a cutting synth at most. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 45):* the restarted notes start with no accent and no trigger, their velocity kept, so a switch under a held chord does not strike it again. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 53):* a ring-stream note's kept rate is against the freeze reference, so a note leaving the stream (this switch, or an unfreeze) takes its rate from its kept `note`, as Lua's `note_values` gives a buffer or input voice's note.
- **A buffer load or clear** fades every grain synth reading the slot, extra notes included: `bufferChanged`'s live set is the own grain synth and every live extra one.
- **An input freeze** points every ring grain synth of the voice at the copy ring; `ringReaders` already holds them all.
- **A synth voice's freeze** changes no note on its own (Lua cuts the extra ones, §2.4). **Its unfreeze**, in the bundle that re-points the ring and sends `freeze` 0: the own grain synth's `noteRate` returns to 1, a parked own note's park moves back to the voice synth, every ring-stream note is cut, and each that was live restarts as a voice synth at its kept `freq`. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 44, 45, 48, 49):* the park never left the voice synth, so nothing moves back, and the restarts are silent. Under an own note parked during the freeze, the grains after the unfreeze replay the ring's audio from just before the freeze for the reader's lookback, the note's included, for at most `ringReadMargin` + `posSpread` × `ringSpreadSeconds` + a grain (about 0.3 s at the defaults): the voice's ordinary ring tail, displaced to the unfreeze, accepted and bounded in time by the check, since removing it would cut a held chord at every unfreeze.
- `stopVoices` and `free` end every note synth with the voices' groups.

**Watching.** A note synth is watched with a watcher of its own, never `watchVoiceSynth`'s restart rule: when it frees, its slot empties (or its cutting entry clears), and nothing restarts it. A start the server never confirms is forgotten as `forgetFailedStarts` does (#497), so a failed start frees its slot.

**Bounds the check holds** (`test/engine_check/notes.scd`): no slot ever has two live synths; live and releasing extra notes never exceed the pool; cutting synths never exceed one per slot, nor the pool in all; after `voiceOff` and the longest release every slot is empty; `engine.synths` holds no note key the voice does not have.

#### 3.5 Attack and release

By Question 6, each voice def's `Env.asr(A, 1, R)` takes `attackTime` and `releaseTime`, controls whose defaults are the def's times of today (Resonator 4 and 8, FM 5 and 10, Folder 3 and 6, Sub 8 and 12, Pulse 2 and 4, Karplus 0.01 and 2, Ring 3 and 5). They are ordinary voice params, sent with `setVoiceParam` and fanned out to every note. The grain defs keep 0.1 and 2 s. A switch's forced release still uses `grainRelease`.

#### 3.6 The memory check

`test/engine_check/memory.scd` gains:
- the seven voice defs' and `\strataMod`'s sizes, each under a limit set from 0b's measurement;
- **the pool cases**, on both tiers, after the existing eight: the all-buffer worst case with the pool full of buffer notes, every pool slot holding a live and a cutting synth (each note started, then stolen and restarted inside one hold); and the synth to buffer to synth case with the pool on synth voices, Karplus's seven slots first. Each against the full-pool floor.
- `probeHeadroom` collecting the notes area's cutting synths beside `cuttingSynths[voice]`, and the runner's `stopAllVoices` cutting note synths, whose releases run up to 12 s.

The existing cases run with the pool empty and keep their floors. The grain defs' 88 KB limit stays.

### 4. The sequencer

- **The `voices` output plays the whole list through the allocator**, every note as `S.lib.notes.play(voice, {owner = "seq", key = k, pitch = <the voice's stored pitch + semitones[k]>, velocity = event.velocity, set_own = <today's base override>})`. On a voice nothing else holds, the first note takes slot 1 and is the base override of 3.4, message for message; the further notes are extra notes. A step without notes is 3.4's `retrigger`. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 13, 27, 33, 35):* the first note is played `quiet` inside `apply_step` (the override as in 3.4; the sequencer keeps its own start and `retrigger`) and the further notes `extra_only` once the voice runs; they are measured from the base the first note's override uses in that step, an input voice's pitch lock included; while another owner holds slot 1 the first note too is `extra_only`. On a running frozen synth voice a step's first hit also sends the slot-0 `noteOn`. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 55):* "holds" is a key physically down; a latched MIDI note gives way to the step's first note.
- **Every hit accents every note.** A ratchet's later hits call `play` again for each chord note (a live slot: an accent, no node) beside the voice's `retrigger`.
- **A chord ends where its override ends** (3.4's rule): at the voice's next played step, a note whose index the new step lacks is released, and one it keeps is re-pitched in place; at a pattern stop (the `voices` output gains a `stop`: `release_owner(voice, "seq")` for every voice), `replace_pattern`, a shorter length and the wrap.
- **A stopped voice starts at a step's first hit**, as today, then takes its chord.
- **Notes that do not fit** follow the allocator's rule like any note: under the cap and the pool the oldest is stolen, so a step's eight notes on a voice capped at four leave its first note and its last three sounding. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 26, 32):* a chord takes only free room and never steals: past the cap or the pool it keeps its first notes at every step, so eight notes at a cap of four sound the first four.
- The step gate and mute are the stage's and gate the whole chord. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 54):* an extra note's start, a player's or a step's (never the engine's own restart), opens the step gate as `retrigger` does, with no accent (the stage's `t_gate`), so a key added to a gated voice's chord sounds.
- No format change: `S.pattern_steps`, `data.steps` and the chain already hold the lists.

### 5. MIDI

#### 5.1 Params

Group `strata_midi`:

| Param | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `midi_device` | unchanged | |
| `midi_channel_mode` | `per voice`, `omni to selected`, `mpe selected`, `mpe split` | `per voice` |
| `midi_note_off` | `auto`, `release`, `latch` | `auto` |
| `mpe_lower_members` | 0-15 | 15 |
| `mpe_upper_members` | 0-15 | 0 |
| `mpe_bend_range` | 1-96 semitones | 48 |

- `midi_channel_mode` keeps its two indices; the MPE modes are appended. Every `== 2` test in `lib/midi_in.lua` is reviewed.
- `midi_note_off` keeps its indices' behavior: index 1, `ignore` until now, is `auto` (latch in the plain modes, which is `ignore`; release in the MPE modes), and index 2, `voice off`, is `release`. So no PSET changes meaning and `PSET_FORMAT` stays 2.
- The two zones never overlap: while both have members, setting one lowers the other so their sum is at most 14 (each zone keeps its master channel). A zone with 0 members is off, and the other may have 15.
- Group `strata_zones` **(new)**, per voice: `v<i>_zone` (`off`, `lower`, `upper`), `v<i>_zone_low` and `v<i>_zone_high` (0-127). Defaults: voice 1 `lower` 0-127, voice 2 `upper` 0-127, the rest `off`.

#### 5.2 Notes

Every note on becomes one `S.lib.notes.play` per target voice, with `park` set, its first expression from `S.lib.expression.note_values` (§6.2), and `set_own` today's `params:set` on `v<i>_freq` or `v<i>_buf_rate`, and now `v<i>_grain_pitch` for an input voice (Question 9). A quantized voice's note is snapped first (`S.lib.variation.midi_note`), per note.

- **Release:** a note off releases its note; when the voice holds no MIDI note any more, the voice stops (today's `voice off`).
- **Latch:** a note off changes nothing. A note on arriving with no key down on its voice first releases the voice's latched extra notes (`release_owner(voice, "midi")`), then plays as slot 1; further keys add notes.
- A note off always goes to the voices its note on played (`note_voice` becomes a list of voices per channel and note).
- The panic (CC 123 and 120) releases every MIDI note and stops what MIDI started, as today.

#### 5.3 Expression in the plain modes

- Pitch bend: the voice's `bend`, ±2 semitones, as today. It bends every note of the voice.
- Poly aftertouch (key pressure): that note's pressure.
- Channel pressure: the pressure of every note the channel holds.
- CC74 is not read in the plain modes; PARAMS > MAP keeps it.

#### 5.4 MPE: `lib/mpe.lua` (new), `S.lib.mpe`

A module of pure functions and one state table, called by `lib/midi_in.lua`.

- **Zones.** The lower zone is master channel 1 and members 2 to `1 + mpe_lower_members`; the upper is master 16 and members `16 - mpe_upper_members` to 15. A channel in neither is ignored in the MPE modes. A plain keyboard on channel 1 plays the lower zone through its master channel.
- **Configuration.** RPN 6 on channel 1 or 16 sets that zone's member count (the param, with a header flash); RPN 0 on a member channel sets `mpe_bend_range`, on a master channel that zone's master range (kept in state, 2 by default).
- **Per channel** the module keeps the bend, pressure and slide last received, whether or not a note is down, since a controller sends them before the note on. A note on takes its channel's values as its first expression.
- **Bend:** a member channel's bend × its zone's master bend, as one ratio per note.
- **Pressure:** channel pressure on a member channel. **Slide:** CC74 on a member channel. Both are ignored on a master channel.
- **High resolution:** CC87 on a member channel is kept as the low seven bits of that channel's next channel pressure or CC74, then cleared. A controller that never sends it is read at seven bits.
- **Assignment.** `mpe selected`: every note to the selected voice. `mpe split`: every voice whose `zone` is the note's zone and whose range holds the note (Question 10).
- A mode, zone or port change releases what is held, as a port change does today. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 14, 37, 40):* so does a change of `midi_note_off`; a zone or member count change reaches MIDI through `S.lib.mpe.on_layout_change`, and acts only in the MPE modes. A reset forgets MIDI's notes without parking anything (`forget_owner`).

The user page says which CCs not to map while an MPE controller is in use: 74, 87 and the RPN controllers 6, 38, 100 and 101.

### 6. Expression

`lib/expression.lua` **(new)**, `S.lib.expression`.

#### 6.1 Params

Per voice, registered with `S.register_voice_params` into VOICE's `notes` section (§9.2):

| Param | Values | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `press_target` | `off`, `amp`, `level`, `main`, `size`, `density`, `position`, `cutoff` | `amp` |
| `press_depth` | -100 to 100 % | 100 % |
| `slide_target` | the same list | `main` |
| `slide_depth` | -100 to 100 % | 50 % |

They are scene keys (the targets discrete) and PSET params. `position` shows for a buffer voice only.

#### 6.2 From a controller's value to a note's

`S.lib.expression.note_values(voice, raw)` turns a note's `{bend, pressure, slide}` into the five values of its `noteExpression`; `S.lib.expression.update(voice, owner, key, raw)` does the same for a held note and hands the result to `S.lib.notes.set_expression`. `lib/midi_in.lua` calls both. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (rulings 12, 30, 36, 38):* with `latch(voice, owner, key)` (a lifted key's note keeps the highest pressure it reached; a note never updated keeps what it has) and `forget(voice, owner, key)` (a new touch), and a new record starts from what the allocator holds for the note (`S.lib.notes.expression(voice, slot)`).

- **Pressure** is 0-1. **Slide** is the note's CC74 less its value at note on, -1..1 (Question 4).
- **A latched note** keeps the highest pressure it reached and its last slide.
- **`amp`** (Question 12): a gain of `1 + depth × value`, clipped 0-2. Zero pressure is the stored level.
- **`level`:** a gain of `1 - depth × (1 - value)` for a depth above 0 and `1 + depth × value` below, clipped 0-2. A note has no pressure until its first pressure message, and until then `level` is neutral. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 34):* this is pressure's formula; slide on `level` acts as on `amp` (`1 + depth × slide`), since a relative slide is 0 at every touch's start and cannot be a loudness.
- **Every other target:** an offset of `depth × value` half ranges.
- Both mappings on one target multiply (the gains) or add (the offsets).

#### 6.3 Where each target acts

| Target | Synth voice | Buffer voice | Input voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amp`, `level` | per note | per note | per note |
| `main` | per note (`noteMain`: the def's expression control) | per note, as `position` | per note, as `size` |
| `size` | the voice | per note | per note |
| `position` | hidden | per note | hidden |
| `density` | the voice | the voice | the voice |
| `cutoff` | the voice | the voice | the voice |

- **A frozen synth voice** has `amp`, `level` and `size` per note (its notes are streams) and no `main`, whose control is on the voice synths the ring no longer hears.
- **Per note** values go into the note's `noteExpression`.
- **The voice** takes the largest value among its held notes (Question 3), as an offset beside the matrix's: `size` and `cutoff` through `S.lib.matrix.set_fast_extra("expression", param_id, m)`, which joins the matrix's sum for that fast destination, and `density` through `S.lib.engine_sync.set_extra_offset("expression", "v<i>_grain_density", offset)`. Both exist since 3.4 and send through `S.lib.lfos.mark_due`. No held note: the offset is cleared. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 31):* the largest is the value furthest from 0, so with a negative depth the hardest-pressed note still wins.

#### 6.4 The flush

Expression arrives far faster than it can be sent. Every arriving value is stored (per channel in `S.lib.mpe`, per note in `S.lib.expression`) and handed to `S.lib.notes.set_expression`, which marks the note due; nothing is sent on arrival. The flush is the note module's, since it alone sends `noteExpression`:

- A step hook on the 48 Hz LFO clock (`S.lib.lfos.on_step`), registered by `lib/notes.lua`, sends one `noteExpression` per due note, at most 16 a step, in round-robin order so no note starves; a note due twice in a step sends its newest values once.
- A note's first expression is sent at once, before its `noteOn`, outside the cap.
- The sends are counted in the engine budget's window through `count_sends` (§0.1) and never deferred.
- The voice-wide maxima of §6.3 are worked out in a hook of `lib/expression.lua` on the same clock.

#### 6.5 Modulation sources

Sixteen read-only sources in a section `notes` (Question 11): `PRES` and `SLID`, the largest pressure and absolute slide among all held notes, and `PR1`..`PR7`, `SL1`..`SL7` per voice, each 0..1. The per-voice ones are `visible` while their voice holds a note that has sent expression. They register from `lib/expression.lua`, included after `lib/input_level.lua`, so the section lists after `audio`.

### 7. What is not per note

The voice filter, the sends, the step gate and mute, crossmod (the mod synth follows the chord's sum on the voice bus), the engine LFOs, scatter and the matrix all act on the whole voice. A chord is louder than a note: the stage has no limiter, and the master's limiter is the bound. No level is scaled by the note count.

### 8. Scenes and PSETs

- New scene keys, all in `voices[i].extras`: `press_target`, `press_depth`, `slide_target`, `slide_depth`, and by Question 6 `attack` and `release`. `SCENE_DATA_VERSION` becomes 7, with one migration giving older scenes the defaults; a scene saved in 3.4 recalls the same sound.
- `note_cap`, the MIDI and MPE params and the zones are PSET-only, as the record params are.
- The pool is neither (§2.3).
- The largest scenes file keeps its 7394 tables.

#### 9.1 MAIN

#### 9.2 VOICE

#### 9.3 SETUP

After `channels`: `note off` (the param, on SETUP for the first time), `mpe & zones` (an action row, K3) and `note pool`. The three rows are always present, so no field moves when the mode changes.

`mpe & zones` opens a modal editor through `S.lib.ui_modal.push` (`lib/ui/zones.lua` **(new)**, `S.lib.ui_zones`): rows `lower members`, `upper members`, `bend range`, then one row per voice with three columns, as MOD's target view has:

```
SETUP ·········
lower members             15
bend range             48 st
v1   lower     C-1      G9
v2   upper     C-1      G9
v3   off       C-1      G9
K2:back K3:column E3:value
```

E2 moves between rows, K3 between a voice row's columns, E3 changes the value, K2 closes. ALT+K3 on a voice row learns its range: the next two notes played set its lowest and highest, with a header notice (`learn v3 low`) and a 10 s timeout, as MOD's learn has. *Amended 2026-11-13, as built (ruling 28):* notes are named as norns names them (60 is C3), so a whole range shows `C-2` and `G8`, not the mock's `C-1` and `G9`.

#### 9.4 SEQ and the arc

The step section's `note` row becomes one row per chord note: `note`, `note 2` .. `note 8`, then `add note` while the step has one to seven notes. The edit view's cursor already keeps its row when rows come and go.

- E3 on a note row moves that degree. Turned below -14 it removes that note and closes the gap; today that clears the whole list.
- E3 on `add note` adds a note, starting from the last note's degree.
- ALT+K2 held on a note row removes that note.
- `S.lib.pages_seq.turn_step(voice, step, "notes", d, {index = k})` is the one entry point; the arc's step editor, ALT ring 2, edits the note under the screen's cursor, or the first.

#### 9.5 MOD, grids

MOD lists the `notes` sources with no change. No grid layout changes: a held step opens the step editor, where the chord rows are.

## Cost if wrong

- **Controls instead of bus blocks (Question 1).** If a later release wants per-note engine modulation, the bus's addressing is still there: `fastModNoteSlots` can rise then, with the scatter and offset-node work this release avoids. Nothing 3.5 builds blocks it.
- **The pool's maximum (Question 2).** One literal on each side. If the CM3 cannot carry the standard default, it is one number in `NOTE_POOL`.
- **The largest value (Question 3).** One function in `lib/expression.lua`.
- **Relative slide (Question 4).** One subtraction; an `absolute` option could join the mapping.
- **Polyphony on by default (Question 5).** The `note_cap` default.
- **Attack and release params (Question 6).** If they are refused, fast playing on the slow voices is near silent; the fallback is a fixed faster attack for extra notes only, which makes a chord's first note differ from its others.
- **The park (Question 7).** A parked own note costs one note's CPU while a chord is held without its first key. If that hurts on the CM3, the own note could free and restart, at the cost of the lifecycle work this design avoids.
- **Frozen synth voices play ring streams (Question 8).** It is the one place a synth voice holds two kinds of note, switched at a freeze and an unfreeze, and so the likeliest home of a lifecycle bug in 3.5. If it proves too costly to get right, refusing extra notes on a frozen synth voice is one branch in `play` and one in `noteOn`, and nothing else depends on it.
- **Chords are louder than notes (§7).** If the master limiter works too hard under eight-note chords, a per-voice gain by note count is one multiply in the stage.
- **Pressure on amp (Question 12).** Two formulas and one option; the default is one value.
- **A new buffer note scans from the voice's position (§3.4).** If a chord should read where the own note has scanned to, the grain defs need a start phase and the engine the live one: a later change to two defs and `noteOn`.
- **Young notes are never stolen (§2.2).** If dropped chord notes are worse than a click, the 50 ms can shrink to a block and the engine's cap on cutting synths takes over.
- **Lua does not track tails (§2.2).** If the engine's trimming cuts tails that still matter, Lua can prefer slots by a release time it estimates; the engine's bounds do not change.
- **The 48 Hz flush with a 20 ms lag (§6.4).** If bends sound stepped, the lag and the rate are two constants; the cap of 16 a step is the third.
- **The memory estimates (§1).** Every figure for a 3.5 node is an estimate. 0b measures before anything is built on them, and the maximum follows the measurement.

## Calls made in this design

- Slot 1 is the voice's own note and is never stolen; notes are allocated to the free slot whose last note ended longest ago; a locked voice's notes are never stolen for another voice.
- The engine trims its own tails, oldest first; Lua counts held notes only.
- A releasing slot's tail is cut for a new note, never re-gated at a new pitch.
- A source switch restarts held extra notes on the new source from values the engine keeps.
- A note younger than the 50 ms cut is never stolen, so cutting synths never exceed the pool; the engine frees the oldest past that.
- A parked slot 1 is the first choice for a new note. Slot 1 has one holder; the sequencer's own note never parks.
- A new buffer note scans from the voice's position, not from where the own note has scanned to.
- An input voice's `grain_pitch` is its own note's pitch, and an extra input note carries its own `pitchShift`.
- A frozen synth voice's notes are pitched against the pitch its own note had when it froze; extra notes held at a freeze are cut, since the frozen sound holds them, and notes held at an unfreeze restart as voice synths.
- `retrigger` stays the voice's accent; `noteOn` on a live slot is a note's.
- Per-note pitch is absolute (a frequency or a rate from Lua), not an interval from the voice's pitch, so the own note can change under a held chord.
- A latched note holds its highest pressure and its last slide; the `level` target is neutral until a note's first pressure arrives.
- The Resonator's expression control is `rq`; a buffer voice's `main` is its position and an input voice's its size.
- Master-channel pressure and CC74 are ignored; master-channel bend applies to the zone.
- Overlapping split zones layer voices.
- The pool is a device setting in `notes.data`; the zones and MPE settings are PSET params; the note cap is PSET-only.
- A sequenced chord's notes share its step's velocity, and every ratchet hit accents every note.
- No level is scaled by the note count.
- No grid layout and no arc page changes beyond the step editor's ALT ring 2.
